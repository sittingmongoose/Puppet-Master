Notes

What stayed

  • Pip's babble: one syllable for each group of vowels, typed and said together. It now has consonants, stress, pauses and a moving mouth.
  • One kit for each look, Retro Dark and Retro Light apart, and NieR its own.
  • The rules that came out of earlier listening: nothing is held (no hum), no two tones a hair apart (no wobble), nothing switched on and off fast (no buzz), no low thump on a page change, no moaning vowels in NieR. tools/measure.mjs measures the first three.
  • Nothing sounds before the first click or key, and the speaker switch stops everything.

Where Concept 3 comes from

From "Marionette's Dream" (Tennon) only its quiet passages were used as a model: a key being wound, a clock's tick, a waltz's oom-pah-pah and a slow music-box line in D minor. Its heavy, fast sections were left alone. For NieR, "A Beautiful Song" gave D minor with a raised seventh, a lilting six-eight and the feeling of one voice on a stage; here there is no voice, choir or drum, the tune is on a soft bell over a harp, and it turns to D major at Ready. No melody from either piece is quoted.

The stage

Pip's mouth follows its voice. Pip nods on the beat and the light behind it breathes on the bar. Notes drift up from whatever plays the tune. Each look has more in it (a sun or moon and bunting, prisms, a drawing's title block and an oscilloscope line, a spectrum on the tube, a tempo strip on the paper, broken columns), and each concept dresses the stage: a music box, the band's instruments, or theatre curtains and footlights. Under Reduced Motion all of it is shown still.

How it was checked

The model that made this cannot hear. Every tune was checked on paper (each melody note against the chord under it), then rendered and measured: loudness (BS.1770), peaks, hum, wobble and buzz. Levels are matched across the eighteen looks by measurement: a spoken line sits at −26 LUFS, a full tune at −30. How it actually sounds is for a person to judge.
