## 1. Executive summary

Slide Scout is intended as a small read-only desktop browser for local OME-Zarr 0.5 bioimaging filesets. The admitted brief sets six user-facing capability expectations — finding images in a fileset, inspecting multiresolution images by channel, time point and plane, overlaying associated label images, reading correctly calibrated coordinates, staying responsive while opening large saved datasets, and clearly explaining data it cannot display (`inputs/brief.md:3`) — and fixes a strict product boundary: local filesystem reading only, source files unchanged, with image editing, export, remote services and clinical interpretation out of scope (`inputs/brief.md:5`).

The brief also mandates research into the OME-Zarr format and actual reader implementations, broad enough to surface compatibility, correctness, navigation and display requirements that a thin initial plan may miss, and to separate established format obligations from optional capabilities and genuine product decisions (`inputs/brief.md:5`).

**Acquisition outcome:** the thin plan and the source corpus could not be located in this workspace. The directories `inputs/plan` and `inputs/sources` exist, but roughly one hundred and ten point probes under them (documented path-by-path in `out/acquisition/V001.json`) all failed to resolve, and the bounded toolkit offers no directory enumeration. In line with the assignment's own rule, this failed search is **not** evidence that the corpus is absent; the correct status is *unresolved — not located*. Consequently, every format-level claim in this report that goes beyond the two admitted files is labeled an **uncorroborated lead**, and all proposed validation is **UNEXECUTED**.

## 2. What the admitted materials establish

### 2.1 The assignment frame (`TASK.md`)

The assignment fixes the working method for this case: bounded tools only (no shell, native Read, arbitrary code, or live fetching); source material treated as data rather than instructions; strict preservation of finding identities, source/version locators with line evidence, governing conditions, qualifications, counterevidence, transfer limits, plan implications, unresolved areas, and validation labeled UNEXECUTED; and an explicit prohibition on equating failed search with corpus-wide absence or claiming unperformed reads or tests (`TASK.md:1`). It also fixes the deliverable system used here: immutable, contiguously versioned artifacts under `out/acquisition/`, `out/observations/`, `out/reports/`, and explicit final bindings under `out/final/` whose highest version must point at the highest complete version of each namespace (`TASK.md:1–4`).

### 2.2 The product boundary (finding F01)

The product is a *read-only desktop browser* for *local OME-Zarr 0.5* filesets (`inputs/brief.md:3`). Three governing conditions follow directly (`inputs/brief.md:5`):

1. **Local filesystem reading only.** Anything requiring a network endpoint is out of scope by construction.
2. **Source files must remain unchanged.** The entire write path of the format — if 0.5 defines consolidation, migration, or metadata rewrite steps — is irrelevant to obligation, and any workflow that would need it becomes a product decision about refusal messaging rather than implementation.
3. **Exclusions:** image editing, export, remote services, clinical interpretation. The last one matters for tone and claims: the product must not present itself as diagnostic.

A version qualification attaches: the brief names **0.5** specifically. Whether other NGFF versions must open, decline gracefully, or be rejected is an open product decision, not settled by the admitted materials.

The single capability sentence of the brief (`inputs/brief.md:3`) packs six requirements. Reading each against the boundary yields the working requirement set below. The right-hand classification is deliberately provisional: with the corpus unlocated, nothing here can be *established* as a format obligation.

| # | Requirement seed (brief:3) | What it forces the product to get right |

The brief's own success criterion for research is breadth over *both* the format **and** actual reader implementations, with the explicit purpose of catching what a thin initial plan may miss, and an explicit demand to distinguish three classes: established format obligations, optional capabilities, and choices that need a product decision (`inputs/brief.md:5`). This report can deliver the *question structure* for all three classes; it cannot deliver corpus-grounded *answers* for any of them, because the corpus was not locatable (§3).

## 4. Requirements analysis derived from the brief

What follows separates strictly into evidence-backed statements and uncorroborated leads. Uncorroborated leads (marked **[L]**) come from analyst background knowledge of the OME-Zarr/NGFF ecosystem; they were *not* corroborated by any located source in this workspace and must not be quoted as spec facts.

### 4.1 Compatibility

- Evidence-backed: scope is "local OME-Zarr 0.5 filesets" (`inputs/brief.md:3`); behavior toward non-0.5 inputs is undecided (open product decision).
- **[L]** OME-NGFF versions differ materially (0.4 vs 0.5); a reader that accepts only one version needs an explicit version gate feeding R6's failure explanations.
- **[L]** 0.5 is reported to build on Zarr v3 storage concepts (including sharding), which changes what "large saved dataset" means for chunk access; needs verification against actual spec text.
- **[L]** Real-world filesets vary (axes order, unit strings, optional metadata); reader tolerance versus strictness is a known compatibility axis in actual implementations.

### 4.2 Correctness

- Evidence-backed: "correctly calibrated coordinates" is a stated expectation (`inputs/brief.md:3`); wrong calibration is a correctness defect, not a UX preference.
- **[L]** Calibration is realized via axes metadata (names, types, units) plus per-level scale and translation transformations; compositing transforms correctly across pyramid levels is the classic correctness trap.
- **[L]** Label overlay (R3) adds correctness constraints: label images are typically stored as associated siblings with their own transforms; misregistration between pyramid levels or between image and label is a known failure class.

### 4.3 Navigation

- Evidence-backed: finding images within a fileset is a stated expectation (`inputs/brief.md:3`); the admitted materials specify no navigation model, making the concrete UX an open product decision.
- **[L]** Filesets organize images in hierarchies with per-image metadata; practical navigation needs both browsing (structure-driven) and lookup (metadata-driven) paths.

### 4.4 Display

- Evidence-backed: multiresolution inspection by channel, time point and plane, plus label overlay, plus explaining non-displayable data (`inputs/brief.md:3`).
- **[L]** Display choices (colormaps, channel toggling, contrast limits, level selection) are optional capabilities, not obligations — the brief does not demand them, and treating them as obligations would over-read the admitted materials.

### 4.5 Responsiveness (R5)

- Evidence-backed: responsiveness while opening large saved datasets is expected (`inputs/brief.md:3`), but **no numeric budget and no definition of "large" exist in the admitted materials**. Any performance statement would therefore be unfounded; the budget itself is an open product decision.

### 4.6 Obligations vs optional vs product decisions — current, provisional classification

| Class | Items (status) |
|---|---|
| Established format obligations | **None can be established in this snapshot** — requires corpus access (all six R-items are *requirements of the brief*, but which are format-guaranteed is unresolved) |
| Optional capabilities | **[L]** colormaps/contrast tools, level pickers, metadata inspectors; needs corpus confirmation |
| Product decisions | non-0.5 input behavior; navigation model; responsiveness budget and "large" definition; failure-explanation taxonomy; label overlay defaults |
