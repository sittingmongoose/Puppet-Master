## The open question: should exact duplicate node rows be rejected?

Short answer: the gate is right as it stands and should not be changed on this branch, but the criterion
itself should probably be tightened, and that is Jared's call rather than the gate author's.

**Why the branch is right to stop where it does.** SCS-017 forbids two nodes sharing a `node_ref` *while
carrying different `revision_ref` values*. A gate that rejected agreeing duplicates would be enforcing
something canon does not say, which is the same move as picking a parent bound the owner had reserved. The
author enforced the criterion exactly, said so in the bundle, and pinned the boundary with a test
(`test_repeated_node_ref_agreeing_on_revision_stays_within_the_owner_rule`). That is the correct discipline.

**Why I think the criterion should be tightened anyway.**

1. *It fights the count rule this very branch just enforced.* `page.returned_count` must equal the number of
   emitted nodes, and `nodes` is capped at 200. If a page may repeat a node, the count is honest about rows
   and dishonest about nodes: a page reporting 200 could be showing 120 distinct revisions. The page-size
   budget stops meaning what a reader takes it to mean.
2. *It undercuts two `const true` promises.* `virtualization.stable_node_identity` and
   `selection_anchor_preserved` are both fixed true. With duplicate rows, a `node_ref` no longer addresses one
   row on the page, so "the selected node" is ambiguous as a position even when the two rows are semantically
   identical — and `selected` is a per-node boolean with no single-selection rule, so two rows could both
   claim it.
3. *Nothing wants to emit them.* A revision that appears twice in a history graph is one node reached by two
   edges; that is what `edges` is for. A duplicate row is redundancy, not expressiveness, so forbidding it
   cannot break a legitimate producer.

**Why it is nonetheless an owner question and not a defect.** The research witness was the disagreeing case;
full in-page uniqueness goes one step past the evidence, exactly the kind of step the adjudication reserved
elsewhere. So: ask, do not assume.

**If Jared says yes, the change is small.** SCS-017's criterion becomes "`node_ref` is unique within one page";
the gate's `revision_by_node_ref` dict becomes a plain seen-set with the comparison dropped; and one negative
fixture covers the agreeing-duplicate case, with the existing disagreeing one kept so the stronger rule still
catches the original witness. One line of canon, three lines of gate, one fixture — and the boundary test
inverts rather than disappears.

**Jared answered during the review**, verbatim: "Yes, forbid exact duplicates too, fold it in." The fifth
commit implements it. One thing worth recording before I check that commit: I pre-scanned every fixture pack in
the repository for a `source_graph_projection` page that repeats a `node_ref`, and there are none outside the
two negatives written for this rule. So the tightening could not invalidate any existing positive — it closes a
gap that nothing was standing in.
