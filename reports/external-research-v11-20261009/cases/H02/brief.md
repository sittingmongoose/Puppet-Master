# H02

A small shellfish farm works with twelve harvest crews and two packing sheds across a broad coastal inlet. Supervisors assign beds, pickup windows, and packing-line capacity, while crews record harvest quantities and lot labels on waterproof cards. The farm has $14,000 for an initial system and spends part of each year working where mobile coverage is weak. Buyers need reliable lot-level handoffs, but they should not see crew phone numbers or internal quality notes. Operations change when weather, buyer demand, or locally issued closures affect an area; the farm wants its own authorized notices represented without assuming the software decides whether harvest is permitted. Several crew members speak different first languages, and the shed uses a shared touchscreen with gloves. Managers want to see which lots are waiting for a handoff, packed, or held for review, and to correct a transcription without erasing who changed it. They are considering starting with one species and one shed. It is still undecided who may release a held lot, which outside notice should be linked to a bed, and whether buyers need direct access or a paper record is sufficient.
