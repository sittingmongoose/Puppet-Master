## 1. Recommendation and scope

The brief calls for scheduling roughly 180 volunteers whose availability changes, recurring shifts, predictable coordinator coverage, mobile access, accessible reminders, privacy-limited roster sharing, and occasional roster imports. It explicitly says not to invent legal requirements or assume equal availability. The evidence does not include shift counts, roles, minimum staffing, eligibility rules, scheduling horizon, geographic timezone, historical assignments, how volunteers confirm availability, jurisdiction, budget, or an existing identity/roster source. These remain open inputs; this plan does not assign values to them.

Use a hybrid workflow:

1. Coordinators define shift roles, staffing targets, site timezone, a recurrence series, signup window, and qualifications.
2. The system creates dated shift occurrences from that series. It preserves published and past occurrences and permits a coordinator to edit one occurrence or end/change the future series without silently rewriting history.
3. Volunteers maintain availability and may sign up for eligible open slots if the food bank chooses self-scheduling. Treat “cannot attend” as a hard exclusion only when the volunteer means it as a commitment; treat “prefer” as a soft signal. Do not infer availability from silence.
4. The schedule shows coverage gaps clearly. A coordinator resolves gaps, conflicts, substitutions, and role requirements. A candidate optimizer may suggest assignments, but no optimizer is necessary for a first release if a suite can support this workflow.
5. Share only the schedule information each person needs. Make the visibility of peer names a deliberate setting, separate from the filled-slot count and private availability.
6. Stage occasional roster imports for review before committing them; do not assume one vendor’s CSV/XLSX importer updates existing records or imports availability and assignments.

This approach keeps coverage and human control visible while leaving the allocation method and fairness definition open for an explicit decision. Public vendor pages indicate that volunteer-specific schedule, mobile, reminder, self-signup, and privacy controls exist in commercial products, but these are product claims and help-page descriptions, not a verified fit or purchase recommendation. Better Impact describes recurring shifts, volunteer web/app access, self-scheduling, and email/text reminders; its admin workflows are browser-oriented. [S01](https://www.betterimpact.com/solutions-volunteer-impact?hsCtaAttrib=171143047728) [S02](https://siteguide.betterimpact.com/en/articles/9893058-navigating-the-app-and-mobile-interface) Volgistics describes openings, coordinator scoping, optional self-scheduling, configurable sign-up caps/windows, and a volunteer choice to hide their name while leaving a slot visibly filled. Its cited recurring-entry page concerns an individual volunteer’s repeating assignment, not recurrence of an opening/template; do not use that page to establish opening-series behavior. [S04](https://www.volgistics.com/help/schedule/schedule-overview/) [S05](https://www.volgistics.com/help/schedule/getting-started-with-self-scheduling/) [S06](https://www.volgistics.com/help/schedule/self-scheduling-settings-for-vicnet-and-victouch/) [S07](https://www.volgistics.com/help/vicnet-portal/volunteer-privacy-settings/)

Commercial volunteer-management suites are plausible candidates because their stated feature sets include recurring assignments, volunteer sign-up, reminders, and coordinator administration. A custom scheduler may merit evaluation if a product demo fails an essential requirement or if the food bank has a strong reason to own maintenance, data protection, accessibility, support, and migrations. Neither product was purchased, trialed, or independently benchmarked in this research.

## 3. Candidate scheduling and fairness model

If custom scheduling or a vendor’s rules need evaluation, represent each candidate assignment as a Boolean decision: volunteer v is assigned to occurrence s or is not. Create hard exclusions only for policy rules the organization has explicitly defined, such as confirmed unavailability, missing required qualification, or overlapping commitments. Keep a preferred time or requested shift as a soft objective. OR-Tools’ release-pinned example shows these as separate modeling primitives: Boolean assignments, coverage, hard/soft bounds, weighted requests, and solver statuses. It selects exactly one modeled shift option per employee/day, including an Off option; work-shift coverage is separately lower-bounded and excess can be penalized. It does not force every employee to work each day and does not supply this food bank’s rules. [S12](https://github.com/google/or-tools/blob/551ad10d94835c99e5e1e684500d3db398c0e345/examples/python/shift_scheduling_sat.py)

First decide whether “coverage” means an inviolable minimum or a target. Under the inviolable interpretation, the solver must mark a schedule infeasible or leave an explicit gap for a coordinator when the available qualified volunteer pool is insufficient. Under a target interpretation, it may produce a partial roster with a visible shortage penalty. It must not silently treat a volunteer who did not respond as available or meet minimum staffing by assigning someone who cannot attend. Timefold’s hosted product makes hard, medium, and soft levels explicit; active soft weights default to 1 and can be set as high as 1,000,000,000,000, so equal defaults should not be mistaken for food-bank priorities. Its versioned docs describe balance-time-worked as a soft rule for cases with at least two eligible employees, and shifts still receive assignments when the rule is broken. [S13](https://docs.timefold.ai/employee-shift-scheduling/1.35.x/employee-resource-constraints/fairness/balance-time-worked) [S14](https://docs.timefold.ai/employee-shift-scheduling/1.35.x/user-guide/constraints) [C11](sources/critic-solvers.md#c11--timefold-hosted-employee-shift-scheduling-fairness-docs-135x) [C12](sources/critic-solvers.md#c12--timefold-hosted-constraint-priorities-and-weights-docs-135x)

No fairness measure is implied by a count of 180 people. Options for the food bank to compare include:

- **Equal shift count** among people eligible for comparable shifts. Easy to explain, but ignores duration and opportunity differences. A Timefold quickstart implements count balance as a soft score and includes employees with zero shifts; this is an example mechanism, not evidence that the policy fits volunteers. [S16](https://github.com/TimefoldAI/timefold-quickstarts/pull/517) [S17](https://github.com/TimefoldAI/timefold-quickstarts/commit/77b1c335ce47c2026ba2873a646582cfbc091d58.patch)
- **Equal minutes or workload** among volunteers eligible for comparable assignments, possibly including prior-period history. This accounts for shift duration but depends on a justified cohort and period. The hosted Timefold API’s FTE-adjusted minutes and standard-deviation formula are product-specific; the documentation notes the relevance of comparable availability. Do not assign volunteers employee FTE by assumption. Its soft rule applies when at least two employees are eligible for the same shifts, and shifts still receive assignments when this fairness rule is violated. [C11](sources/critic-solvers.md#c11--timefold-hosted-employee-shift-scheduling-fairness-docs-135x) [S13](https://docs.timefold.ai/employee-shift-scheduling/1.35.x/employee-resource-constraints/fairness/balance-time-worked)
- **Opportunity-normalized distribution**, such as assignments relative to eligible shifts the volunteer actually offered to take. This can avoid treating people with very different available hours as interchangeable, but only if availability and eligibility are current, well-defined, and not inferred from silence.
- **Preference fulfillment**, such as honoring the share of feasible requests met, with clear limits on request volume and tie handling.
- **Coverage first, fairness among feasible eligible choices**, followed by explicit gap reporting. This makes coverage priority visible but can reduce fairness when volunteers are scarce.

The food bank should choose the fairness cohort, time window, workload unit, and tie-break rule before optimizing. Exclude people who could not serve a particular occurrence from fairness comparisons for that occurrence. Report understandable outcomes (coverage, gaps, assignments/minutes by eligible cohort, and preference fulfillment) before applying them. Do not permit a fairness score to override a hard qualification or an explicitly confirmed absence.

The source-evolution chain is informative but bounded: Timefold issue #492 requested a fairness quickstart; PR #517 merged the soft shift-count implementation and tests; that work required solver PR #980, which fixed Python load-balance casts in the v1.13.0 milestone. [S15](https://github.com/TimefoldAI/timefold-quickstarts/issues/492) [S16](https://github.com/TimefoldAI/timefold-quickstarts/pull/517) [S18](https://github.com/TimefoldAI/timefold-solver/pull/980) [S19](https://github.com/TimefoldAI/timefold-solver/commit/d6546cc9720df0578c67ec91956ca7a46eaf36c8.patch) This is a real issue-to-change-to-fix evolution chain in public code. It is not a production outage, a release guarantee, or proof that equal shift counts are fair for this volunteer population.

### Cross-cutting disposition classes

- **Already covered by the brief:** recurring shifts, changing availability, mobile access, accessible reminders, privacy-limited sharing, and occasional roster imports are stated requirements. Their presence in the plan is not a newly discovered product feature.
- **Optional enhancements:** volunteer self-sign-up, a waitlist, signup caps, and a configurable advance window can reduce coordination effort if the food bank wants them. Public Volgistics help documents optional caps and a 1–12 month or fixed-date window; neither is inherently fair or required. [S05](https://www.volgistics.com/help/schedule/getting-started-with-self-scheduling/) [S06](https://www.volgistics.com/help/schedule/self-scheduling-settings-for-vicnet-and-victouch/)
- **User decisions:** minimum versus target coverage; volunteer-initiated swaps; peer-name visibility; fairness denominator/window; reminder channels and timing; timezone-gap handling; and which roster fields imports may update.
- **Uncertain until demonstrated:** each vendor’s current tier, price, defaults, accessibility, exact import/update semantics for this food bank’s data, and compatibility with its coordinator workflow. The product sources are mutable descriptions and no live configuration was checked.

## 5. Privacy, mobile use, reminders, and accessibility

Mobile access can be a responsive browser, a vendor app, or both. A browser path avoids making app installation a prerequisite; an app may offer an easier volunteer home screen. Better Impact’s guide describes mobile assignment and profile access but does not show tested offline or assistive-technology behavior. Its page characterizes admin operations as browser-oriented, so confirm whether coordinators need to change schedules from phones. [S01](https://www.betterimpact.com/solutions-volunteer-impact?hsCtaAttrib=171143047728) [S02](https://siteguide.betterimpact.com/en/articles/9893058-navigating-the-app-and-mobile-interface)

Decide separately: (a) whether peers see names, (b) whether the slot appears filled when a name is hidden, (c) who can see availability, (d) which coordinator roles can view or edit which assignments, and (e) how much shift information appears in email/SMS or a lock-screen notification. Volgistics documents separate volunteer name-hiding and coordinator access controls. [S07](https://www.volgistics.com/help/vicnet-portal/volunteer-privacy-settings/) Use individual messages or authenticated, filtered views rather than a full email roster. Let volunteers manage available reminder preferences and record a consent/status for each channel. Choose reminder timing and whether it includes address/contact details; the available product source does not identify the food bank’s desired values.

The brief requires accessible reminders but supplies no conformance level. If selected as an internal product target, test the responsive web scheduling and account flows against relevant WCAG 2.2 AA criteria: keyboard operation; readable error/status announcements; reflow at 320 CSS pixels; and pointer target sizing subject to WCAG exceptions. Review email and SMS separately for readable text, clear shift date/time, meaning that does not rely on color, privacy, and channel opt-out. The W3C Recommendation provides testable web-content criteria across device types. These are proposed acceptance criteria, not a claim of current conformance or legal requirement. [S20](https://www.w3.org/TR/2024/REC-WCAG22-20241212/)

## 6. Imported roster workflow

Treat import as a controlled data change, not an incidental file upload. Public vendor guides show distinct product behavior: Better Impact describes XLSX with a named worksheet and exact ordered headers, region-specific date formats, staff handling through a secure exchange, and possible fees for certain/custom imports. Volgistics documents flat file formats, creation of new records rather than updates, pending records, mapping previews, and validation warnings. [S03](https://support.betterimpact.com/en/articles/8368729-importing-user-profiles) [S08](https://www.volgistics.com/help/volunteer-records/import-volunteer-records/) These statements are vendor-specific and cover volunteer profiles, not necessarily shift assignments or availability.

Proposed import controls:

- Require an authorized coordinator to select the data source and identify the import purpose.
- Match on a stable external volunteer ID where available. Show new, update, duplicate, no-match, multi-match, and unchanged outcomes before committing.
- Preview column mapping, normalized dates/times/timezones, roles, status, communication preferences/consent, and any recurrence or availability fields.
- Reject or quarantine ambiguous dates, unknown timezone, invalid role/status values, and duplicate identities. Never silently guess time zones or replace consent.
- Allow a test/staging import using synthetic records; capture a row-level error report and reversible batch record; confirm what becomes an update versus a new record.
- Keep uploaded data and reports accessible only to authorized staff and follow the organization’s selected retention policy.

**Open decision:** authoritative roster source and identity key; which fields an import may update; whether an import may change existing availability, assignments, or reminder consent; and who approves the batch. Do not assume the product guide’s profile-import behavior applies to a different data type.

## 7. Proposed validation plan — not executed

These checks distinguish the principal design choices and expose failure modes. They are proposals only.

| Proposed scenario | Method | Expected result or distinguishing measure |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary recurring week | Use synthetic volunteers, shifts, roles, availability, and staffing targets; create and review one week. | Every published occurrence is dated in the site timezone, roles/targets are visible, volunteers can find their assignments, and coordinator can identify gaps.
