# Independent discovery — volunteer shift scheduling

**Status:** pre-reveal discovery. This record was completed from the S08 brief, exact input map, and public primary sources. It is the source-led discovery freeze for M02. It does not compare or react to the concealed case plan. No plan-root-only material was read.

## Brief-derived scope and unknowns

The brief describes a food bank with about 180 volunteers, changing availability, recurring shifts, coordinators who need predictable coverage, mobile access, accessible reminders, privacy-limited sharing, and occasional roster imports. It asks for competing products, constraints/optimizers, and fairness options with implementation/version evidence. It does not provide number of shifts, roles, slots per shift, recurrence horizon, historical assignments, confirmed/unavailable semantics, volunteer capacity, or jurisdiction. Those are gaps, not values to invent.

The six brief obligations are: useful unfamiliar tools and materially different approaches (O1); primary implementation/default/limit/applicability evidence (O2); issue/fix/release evolution evidence (O3); exact per-clause dispositions after plan reveal (O4); retained alternatives, conditions, constraints, disagreement, and uncertainty in a coherent final (O5); and discriminating proposed validations separated from checks actually run (O6).

## Product and operating approaches found

### Volunteer-management suites

**Better Impact Volunteer Impact** is a volunteer-specific suite with a volunteer portal and mobile app. The vendor describes one-time, recurring, and event-based shifts; self-scheduling from the portal/app; and email/text shift reminders. Its help guide describes app access to assignments, profile privacy, messaging preferences, and general availability. The product page says volunteer-facing work is available in a browser and an app, while coordinator/admin workflows are designed for web browsers. This is an integrated benchmark for scheduling, mobile access, and communications, but its public page is vendor-authored; this research did not verify a configured account, accessibility, exact reminder behavior, service-level limits, or fit for this food bank. [S01](sources/products.md#s01--better-impact-volunteer-impact-product-page) [S02](sources/products.md#s02--my-impact-mobile-guide)

**Volgistics** exposes granular schedule behavior in public help. Coordinators can be given access to schedules for selected Assignments/groups. Self-scheduling requires VicNet or VicTouch and must be enabled; assignment rules select who qualifies and can restrict sign-ups to openings. Optional self-service caps and a 1–12 month rolling sign-up window exist, but caps constrain volunteer self-signups, not coordinator/operator assignments. A volunteer-specific privacy control can hide that person’s name while keeping the slot visibly filled. These are configurable patterns, not a product purchase ranking. [S04](sources/products.md#s04--volgistics-schedule-overview) [S05](sources/products.md#s05--volgistics-self-scheduling-setup) [S06](sources/products.md#s06--volgistics-self-scheduling-limits) [S07](sources/products.md#s07--volgistics-volunteer-privacy-settings)

The Volgistics recurrence model is an important caveat: Regular entries repeat by pattern, and editing/removing the series can affect past and future dates. Its help offers an ending date to retain past dates and stop future repeats; a one-date exception is handled separately. That behavior supports explicit series boundaries, occurrence exceptions, and immutable published history if recurrence is implemented locally. It should not be generalized to every vendor. [S09](sources/products.md#s09--volgistics-recurring-entry-edit-behavior)

The user-facing workflow can be volunteer sign-up into posted openings, coordinator assignment, or a hybrid. Sign-up-first reduces manual back-and-forth and lets volunteers act on their actual availability, but does not by itself guarantee equitable access or minimum coverage. Coordinator assignment supports coverage/role judgment but needs current, trustworthy availability and a clear update path. A hybrid can publish recurring openings and use coordinator intervention for coverage gaps, unfilled roles, and late changes. None of these paths should silently convert general availability into a commitment.

### Roster import is its own workflow

The vendor import patterns make occasional imported rosters a material product requirement, not a file-picker detail. Volgistics’ built-in utility creates new records and cannot update existing records; it stages them as pending and previews field mapping/validation errors. Better Impact’s profile import is a staff-processed XLSX flow with an exact worksheet/header layout and date conventions; custom and subsequent standard imports may be billable. These product-specific semantics do not prove availability, shift assignments, recurrence, or substitutions import the same way. [S03](sources/products.md#s03--better-impact-profile-import-guide) [S08](sources/products.md#s08--volgistics-volunteer-record-import)

A safe product pattern is a staged, reversible import: preserve source-file identity and import time; match records against a stable external volunteer ID where available; show duplicate/no-match/multi-match cases; preview normalized dates, time zones, contact fields, roles, availability, consent, and recurrence mappings; validate every row; let an authorized coordinator approve; and report created/updated/skipped rows. Never assume “CSV import” implies merge/update semantics. Prove the mapping with synthetic records before real data is used.

## Optimizer mechanisms, defaults, and applicability

### Explicit CP-SAT model

Google OR-Tools’ CP-SAT employee example uses Boolean assignment variables, exact coverage for its example slots, at-most-one shift per person/day, and an objective that rewards requested assignments. A release-pinned v9.15 shift-scheduling example separately models weekly demand, hard and soft sequence bounds, weighted requests, and over-coverage penalties. These are building blocks for this brief: an assignment variable can be excluded when unavailable or unqualified, overlap can be forbidden, minimum coverage can be counted, and preferences or fairness can be expressed as transparent weighted objectives. The model must encode volunteer policy; the library does not choose it. The official sample is much smaller/different from 180 volunteers and establishes no runtime or solution quality here. [S10](sources/optimization.md#s10--google-or-tools-employee-scheduling-documentation) [S11](sources/optimization.md#s11--or-tools-v915-release) [S12](sources/optimization.md#s12--or-tools-v915-pinned-shift-scheduling-code)

Distinguish **unavailability** from **preference**. Confirmed unavailability, time overlap, and qualification can be hard exclusions if those meanings are agreed. A preferred time is normally a soft reward. Coverage can be a minimum plus visible uncovered count, or a required assignment with explicit infeasibility/gap handling. A solver must not hide a shortage by assigning someone who did not confirm attendance. Validate with both a feasible week and a deliberately under-resourced week.

### Search/score-based solver and hosted schedule API

Keep two Timefold products distinct. The open-source Timefold Solver and employee-scheduling quickstart use code-defined constraints and hard/soft scores. The hosted Employee Shift Scheduling API is a vendor model with an API key and JSON schema. Its v1.35.x docs describe balance of FTE-adjusted minutes as a **soft** constraint, based on standard deviation among employees eligible for the same shifts, multiplied by 100. The docs say historical minutes may be included, but compare workers over a common period and note similar availability is important for accuracy. For API tag filters, ALL is the default. The constraints guide sets active soft weights to default 1 and a 1-trillion maximum. These defaults are consequential: a hosted model would need an agreed eligibility basis, units, historical period, weights, and privacy posture. It must not assume employee FTE or equal volunteer availability. No API account, key, usage, data submission, or billing was observed. [S13](sources/optimization.md#s13--timefold-employee-shift-scheduling-fairness-version-135x) [S14](sources/optimization.md#s14--timefold-employee-shift-scheduling-constraints-version-135x)

### Fairness options without an equal-availability assumption

There is no single “fair” distribution implied by 180 people. Options for user adjudication:

1. **Equal shift count** among people eligible for the same shift class. Easy to explain, but ignores duration and unequal availability. Timefold’s open-source quickstart uses this as a soft count-balance example.
2. **Equal minutes or burden** among people who could serve the relevant work, optionally carrying history from a comparable prior period. This counts duration and history, but requires a legitimate denominator and may penalize someone for a shift pattern they cannot take.
3. **Availability/opportunity-normalized distribution**, such as assignments per offered eligible opportunity or share of feasible requested opportunities. This avoids treating someone available one day as equivalent to someone available every day, but only if availability, eligibility, and opportunity windows are collected accurately and missing data is not inferred.
4. **Preference fulfillment**: maximize the proportion of a volunteer’s feasible requests honored rather than raw request count, with limits on how requests are collected stated clearly.
5. **Coverage-first with fairness among feasible eligible choices**: meet defined minimum staffing and skill requirements, then apply transparent fairness/preferences. If capacity is insufficient, report each gap and require coordinator action rather than quietly sacrificing a hard requirement.

A fair policy could combine a chosen workload measure, a historical window, and a volunteer-facing explanation; each remains a decision. Exclude people who could not perform the shift from that shift’s fairness cohort. Do not assume equal available hours, an FTE for each volunteer, or that a volunteer who did not respond was unavailable. Timefold demonstrates hard/medium/soft priorities and history; OR-Tools demonstrates weighted objectives. Neither selects the food bank’s rule. ## Mobile access, accessibility, and privacy choices

Mobile access can be a responsive volunteer web portal, a vendor native app, or both. A browser path avoids requiring every volunteer to install an app; an app may provide a familiar home screen and notifications. Public vendor guides demonstrate those patterns but do not establish offline use or accessible operation. Mobile coordinator work may remain desktop-first, as Better Impact explicitly says its admin work is designed for browsers and is easier on a computer. Confirm if coordinators need full schedule editing by phone.

The brief asks for accessible reminders, but gives no conformance target. An optional product target for responsive web and email content is WCAG 2.2 AA, not an assumed legal rule. Proposed checks: keyboard operation and visible focus for scheduling controls; screen-reader names/status for shift and coverage changes; mobile reflow at 320 CSS pixels; sufficiently large pointer targets; accessible error summaries on signup/import; and email/SMS content that identifies shift/date plainly and does not rely on color alone. WCAG 2.2 criteria are testable and cover mobile web, but no accessibility check was run here. [S20](sources/accessibility.md#s20--w3c-wcag-22-recommendation)

Privacy decisions include whether peers see names or only filled slots; whether availability is private to the volunteer and coordinator; which assignments each coordinator can inspect/edit; which schedule details appear in reminders/lock-screen notifications; and who can access uploaded rosters. Keep volunteer consent and role-based access independent from optimizer logic. Volgistics provides a pattern where a volunteer can hide their name while the slot remains filled and scopes coordinator access by assignment. This is a product pattern, not a legal conclusion or recommendation to share names.
