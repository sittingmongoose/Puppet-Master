# ER11 scientific revision — A-M10-A

## Scope, evidence and conclusion

This is the complete revised research disposition for the S10 data-import brief: a desktop assistant for nonprofit analysts who combine large CSVs and occasional Excel workbooks, encounter inconsistent dates and identifiers, need to understand rejected rows, and repeat transformations monthly on laptops with modest memory. It compares all six revealed plan clauses, retains the full discovery and useful alternatives, and adjudicates the critic’s findings against the declared evidence and independent checks recorded as R01–R08.

The evidence boundary was the exact brief, the declared A-M10-A research and critique predecessors, their registered source roots, and independent read-only checks of public primary sources. Source IDs S01–S29 and C01–C16 retain their original mappings. The reviser’s checks have new IDs R01–R08 in `source-map.json`. No parent, counterpart, evaluator, campaign history, unrelated experiment, local dataset, package, installer or executable was read or run.

**Conclusion:** the thin plan has useful intent, but it is not a complete import contract. Sampled inference, locale choice, reject reporting, recipe replay and counts can all be useful when their scope and semantics are explicit. The design should preserve original input, make changes reviewable, report uncertainty and keep the transformation recipe portable. The evidence does not establish one winning engine, a universal null/date/workbook policy, a supported-format list, or a quantitative memory/performance target. Treat the implementation alternatives as candidates and measure the chosen path against a selected first-release scope.

## Coherent product and data-flow proposal

Separate reading, inference, accepted transformation and validation into explicit stages:

1. **Retain a source layer.** For CSV, preserve decoded field values under an explicit encoding policy and, where invalid encoding or byte-exact audit matters, retain raw bytes or source spans. Do not silently trim, infer into, or overwrite source values. Keep leading-zero IDs and long numeric-looking identifiers as text until the analyst chooses a conversion. For workbooks, retain the source location and enough cell representation to support the chosen meaning of “original”: stored value, formatted display, formula and cached result, or a deliberate combination.
2. **Show proposals with their coverage.** Infer dialect, header, candidate types and date formats as suggestions. Label whether a value/profile result covers a preview, sample or whole file. A late anomaly omitted by a sample remains unchecked. Do not block ordinary preview solely to obtain a whole-file scan, but do not label a sample-only result as whole-file validation.
3. **Apply reviewed operations.** Make conversions and other changes explicit, ordered, inspectable and recorded in a versioned recipe. Keep raw and converted values side by side when useful. Date conversion records format/culture and distinguishes calendar dates, local datetimes and offset-bearing instants. Null mapping records configured tokens, trim-before-compare behavior and operation order.
4. **Report what happened.** Preserve failed rows by default and distinguish structural parse/decode failures from semantic conversion failures. Report issue code, field/cell when known, raw value/evidence available, message, parser/settings version and source location. Allow multiple issues per row. Expose caps/truncation and distinguish “not evaluated” from “passed.” A CSV can be a sufficient portable, durable report if its schema and completeness make those facts inspectable; a separate issue database is an implementation choice.
5. **Replay with a compatibility check.** Save machine-readable settings plus a human-readable summary. On the next monthly file, compare schema and recorded assumptions, show a useful diff, and wait for approval when meaning may change. A per-import source fingerprint identifies and audits that run. It should not, by itself, reject the expected new file in the next month. Unsupported or version-incompatible operations must be surfaced rather than silently dropped.
6. **Count at defined stages.** Record the population and unit being counted, whole-record outcomes, field evaluation coverage and issue counts separately. Counts are useful controls, not proof that values are correct.

## Implementation approaches and comparative evidence

| Approach | What it offers | Limits and applicability |
|---|---|---|
| **Native Rust readers** (`csv` 1.4.0 plus Calamine 0.36.1) | Fits a Rust/Slint application and gives the product control over source identity, proposed conversions, issues and recipes. `csv` exposes low-level delimiter, quote, header and field-width controls; `ByteRecord` permits raw non-UTF-8 records (S11–S12, C16, R08). Calamine exposes typed workbook values, errors and empty cells (S13–S16, C15). | These are readers, not a complete import assistant or profiler. The app must implement inference, conversion proposals, diagnostics, workbook representation and scaling. Byte preservation still needs a policy and framing recovery does not follow merely from using bytes. No memory or compatibility benchmark was run. |
| **DuckDB-backed profiling/import** | Offers CSV dialect/type detection, SQL profiling, reject facilities, and documented memory/temp-directory controls. Its XLSX extension may help with occasional workbook reads (S01–S06, C01–C03, R01–R02). | Its inference is sampled; DuckDB 1.5 current docs state 20,480 rows by default and beginning-only sampling for gzip/stdin, with `sample_size=-1` for whole-file sampling. Extension pre-bundling, offline first use and spill behavior are untested. |
| **Hybrid Rust UI plus DuckDB profiling** | Could combine a native workflow and retained source/issue model with analytical queries where they provide measurable value. | It is a candidate, not a proven winner. Benchmark the added dependency and packaging cost. |
| **OpenRefine as a workflow benchmark** | Useful reference for analyst-facing history and operation reuse. Its manual documents JSON extraction/reapplication for selected operations, but explicitly says some operations cannot be replayed that way (S17–S21, C07–C09, R06). | Its Java/local-server workflow is not a native Rust library dependency. Its UTC ISO-8601 date semantics and issue #6009 show that timezone-free date interpretation can surprise users; neither is a universal policy for this product. |
| **Power Query as incumbent benchmark** | Documents first-200-row or whole-dataset CSV inference, a Text choice, first-1,000-row profiling with a whole-dataset option, OS-regional defaults and a “Using locale” override (S22–S25, C04–C06, R03–R05). | These are product-specific behaviors, not recommendations for this app or a distributable component. They demonstrate that sampling and locale are user-visible choices. |
| **Frictionless as schema/report precedent** | Offers structured row/field/type/message-style reports, custom validation and Excel controls (S26–S29, C10). | Its Python runtime boundary and version-specific behavior need consideration in a Rust desktop product. The current guide demonstrates a configurable `limit_errors` control but does not establish a 1,000-error default for a pinned release (C10, R07). Do not repeat that default as fact until verified against exact tagged code/docs. |

DuckDB documentation describes a default memory limit of 80% of physical RAM and disk spill for some larger intermediate data (S04). This is a configurable engine behavior, not a guarantee that every operator spills, that temporary disk remains available, or that an end-to-end desktop import fits a target laptop. “Rust” likewise does not establish low memory use. Define the minimum hardware, file-size range, latency expectations and disk policy, then measure peak RSS, temporary disk, cancellation and cleanup on representative inputs.

### Workbook representation and formats

An XLSX cell can have stored value, number formatting, formula, cached result, sheet and address. A typed reader value is not automatically the source OOXML text or Excel’s displayed string. Decide what “original” means and show that choice in preview. Calamine 0.36.1 documents 1900/1904 date epochs, millisecond precision and no timezone (S13–S16); preserve the epoch/value where needed and do not invent a timezone or precision. DuckDB’s inspected extension reads XLSX but not XLS (S03, C03). Whether to support legacy XLS, XLSB, strict OOXML, formulas/cached values, blank rows and formula recalculation remains a product decision.

## P1–P6 plan dispositions

The exact plan clauses are reproduced below. The primary category is intentionally calibrated to the clause as written. A conditional correction is called out only if implementation turns the clause into behavior the clause does not itself require.

### P1 — “Infer column types from the first 1,000 rows.”

**Disposition: Optional enhancement.** A fixed sample is a legitimate preview tradeoff; Power Query documents sampled, whole-dataset and text-only choices, and DuckDB documents a different default and different coverage for seekable versus gzip/stdin inputs (S22–S23, C01, C04–C06, R01, R03, R05). The short clause does not say to treat that sample as whole-file truth or destructively coerce values. Preserve the warning and safeguards from discovery, but do not call the sample size inherently unsafe.

Keep raw CSV values through preview. Show a candidate type and sample/profile scope, allow override, and make conversion a reviewed step. Keep identifiers as text unless changed deliberately. A whole-file scan is needed before claiming whole-file validity, not before every ordinary preview. If sampled inference is silently presented as a whole-file result or applied destructively, that behavior is a **conditional Correction**. No numeric sample size can be selected from this evidence as universally correct; file size and latency targets are unspecified.

**Proposed discriminator:** place a type-changing value, malformed row and alternate date after both 1,000 rows and the selected engine’s default sample, then repeat with gzip. Confirm preview scope is labeled, raw values are unchanged, full-file findings include late rows, and sample-only results are never called whole-file validation.

### P2 — “Parse date strings using the machine locale.”

**Disposition: Correction, limited to an implicit, unrecorded default.** OS locale is a valid selected rule if the recipe records that exact locale and replay applies it. Power Query documents both an OS-regional default and an explicit locale override (S23, C05, R04); these support the portability risk, not one universal date policy. Record pattern/culture and whether a value is a date-only calendar value, local datetime or offset-bearing instant. Keep source text visible, report conversion failures/ambiguity, and do not invent a timezone for a date-only value. The correct interpretation of `01/02/2025` requires source context.

**Proposed discriminator:** test genuinely ambiguous `01/02/2025` plus `1/27/2025`, ISO, DMY, mixed, invalid, date-only and offset-bearing examples under two OS locales. `1/27/2025` is an MDY discriminator, not itself ambiguous between MDY and DMY. Identical explicit recipe settings should yield identical results across locales; an ambiguous value without a chosen pattern should remain visibly unresolved instead of silently guessed.

### P3 — “Convert missing values to null.”

**Disposition: User decision.** “Missing” can mean an empty CSV field, quoted empty string, whitespace, configured sentinel (`NA`, `NULL`, `N/A`), absent trailing field, blank workbook cell, or formula returning an empty string. Collapsing them may erase distinctions. DuckDB’s Excel behavior illustrates how empty cells can be typed and how `ignore_errors` can replace failed cells with NULL; Frictionless illustrates that stringification and later schema coercion are separate concerns (S03, S28).

Preserve the raw representation. Make null-token mapping explicit, ordered and configurable, including whether trimming occurs first. Keep raw value in the audit/issue output after conversion. The owner must choose defaults for empty/whitespace/sentinels, absent columns and formula results; the brief does not authorize one answer.

**Proposed discriminator:** include empty and quoted-empty CSV fields, spaces/tabs, literal NA/NULL/N/A, absent trailing fields, blank XLSX cells and formula results. Verify only configured cases map to null and replay preserves the selected order.

### P4 — “Keep failed rows in an error CSV.”

**Disposition: Optional enhancement.** Retaining failed rows is necessary and the clause already names a portable report. CSV is not inherently lossy: it can be the durable canonical report if it preserves source linkage/raw evidence, issue meaning, multiplicity and completeness. A separate database or in-memory audit model is not required by the evidence. Add structured fields and explicit coverage as a stronger design option; make it a **conditional Correction** only if the actual export drops rejected source data or cannot explain what failed.

Distinguish structural parse/decode failures from semantic conversions. When framing is known, link to source fingerprint plus logical record ordinal and available byte/record position. Include field/cell, raw value when available, stable issue code, concise message, parser/settings version and source location; allow multiple issues per record. Surface caps and truncation. A projected-away column can be “not evaluated,” not “passed.” Do not make silent `ignore_errors` the default (S02, C02, R02).

**Proposed discriminator:** combine uneven-width rows, quote/decode errors, multiple conversion errors and an error in an unselected field. Restart and inspect the exported report: raw evidence and issue multiplicity should remain, limits should be explicit, and unevaluated data must not be marked passed.

### P5 — “Save transformation settings.”

**Disposition: Optional enhancement.** The clause meets the basic monthly-replay intent. OpenRefine demonstrates replayable operation history with documented coverage limits; it does not define this app’s full recipe (S17–S19, C07, R06). Add a versioned, inspectable recipe containing app/reader versions; encoding, CSV dialect and header behavior; workbook sheet/range and selected representation; selected columns; accepted conversions; explicit locale/date and number formats; null/trim/whitespace rules; operation order; error policy; output types; and expected schema fingerprint. Include human-readable and machine-readable forms.

Use a source fingerprint to identify/audit each import run. Do not block a monthly replay solely because the new file hash differs. Compare the incoming schema and result-changing assumptions; show differences and wait for approval when meaning may change. State which steps are replayable. If an operation is unsupported or version-incompatible, stop and identify it rather than silently dropping it. Omission of a result-changing setting from a recipe that claims replayability, or silent loss of a step, is a **conditional Correction**.

**Proposed discriminator:** replay on a second monthly file with matching schema and explicit settings, then rename/reorder/add/remove columns and change date format or null token. Confirm stable replay when compatible and a clear diff/approval gate when assumptions change; the changed file fingerprint alone should not trigger a false incompatibility.

### P6 — “Compare output row counts with inputs.”

**Disposition: Optional enhancement.** Count comparison is a valid integrity check, but does not establish value correctness. The clause does not claim otherwise. Add a defined population/stage and distinguish row outcomes from field coverage and issue totals. Treat it as a **conditional Correction** only if counts are presented as sufficient proof or compared without defining what was counted.

For a pure parse stage with no row-changing operation, define the input population as parser-established logical records and count mutually exclusive **whole-record** outcomes such as accepted, rejected, explicitly excluded and not processed. Use that reconciliation only if these outcomes are exhaustive and refer to the same stage and unit. A field can be unselected/not evaluated while its row is accepted, so field evaluation coverage is a separate measure, not another mutually exclusive row bucket. Count issue records separately because one row may have multiple issues. After filter, split, deduplicate, aggregate or one-to-many transforms, report each operation’s input/output and effect rather than forcing a source-to-output partition. CSV quoted records can span physical lines; lines/errors are not automatically record counts (S02, S12, C02, R02, R08).

**Proposed discriminator:** use quoted multiline records, structural and conversion rejects, a projected field, an explicit filter and (if supported) a split. Verify stage-specific counts and dispositions; separately alter a value without changing row count and require a value-level check to detect it.

## Issue, fix, release and evolution evidence

DuckDB issue #13043 describes a gzip, tab-delimited sniffer problem against DuckDB 1.0.0 CLI on macOS arm64. PR #13083 merged a scoped sniffer correction on 2024-07-20; DuckDB v1.1.0 lists that PR (S07–S09, C11–C13). The examined research release context is v1.5.6, released 2026-09-28 (S06). This forms an issue-to-fix-to-release chain and a useful regression candidate; it does not establish general malformed/compressed-file robustness or a failure in the later release. This research did not reproduce the issue.

A 2026 DuckDB discussion reports a slash-date inference concern but does not identify an exact engine build and was not reproduced (S10). Treat it as a test lead, not a release-wide defect. OpenRefine issue #6009 records disagreement over timezone-free date interpretation; inspected 3.10.0 notes did not identify a matching correction, which is not proof no correction exists (S20–S21, C08–C09). Calamine v0.36.1 notes fixes for strict OOXML, empty XLS strings and XLSB parsing; these are maintenance/regression signals, not evidence that the release has a current bug or performance bound (S15, C14).

## Discriminating validation plan

**Execution status: every item below is proposed; none was run.** No application/runtime, performance test, usability test or fixture execution was available or performed. The compact baseline answers the stated CSV/XLSX/monthly-replay brief. Conditional cases become acceptance checks only after product scope is chosen.

### Baseline for the stated workflow

1. **Raw fidelity and identifiers:** include leading-zero IDs, 20+ digit values, Unicode, empty versus quoted-empty fields, whitespace and sentinels. Compare source preview, accepted converted output and replay; verify source values remain recoverable.
2. **Inference scope:** put a type/date/width anomaly after 1,000 rows and after the chosen engine’s sample. Repeat in gzip if supported. Compare sample and whole-file findings; require sample scope disclosure and prohibit whole-file-valid claims from sampled-only inspection.
3. **Date repeatability:** include ambiguous `01/02/2025`, MDY-discriminating `1/27/2025`, ISO, mixed and invalid values, date-only input and offset-bearing datetimes. Run under two OS locales, then replay with explicit recipe settings. Require deterministic explicit settings and visible unresolved/failed ambiguous inputs; attach no timezone to date-only values by default.
4. **Null policy:** test empty, quoted empty, whitespace, literal sentinel, absent field, blank workbook cell and formula-empty cases. Verify the chosen explicit rule maps only configured values, preserves audit values and replays in order.
5. **Reject reporting:** combine structural and semantic failures, multiple issues per row and an error in a projected-away field. Confirm raw evidence and source linkage survive restart, issue multiplicity is represented, report limits are visible, and unevaluated data is not marked passed.
6. **Framing and identity:** include a valid multiline quoted record, a broken quote followed by a known sentinel record, and invalid encoding. Verify IDs are stable only where framing is established; allow a reported uncertain region/suffix with available byte evidence rather than forcing false record mapping.
7. **Monthly replay and counts:** replay on a compatible second file and on controlled schema/date/null changes. Confirm a changed file fingerprint identifies the new run without alone blocking it, compatible settings replay consistently, incompatible assumptions show a diff/approval gate, and counts reconcile only within their defined stages and units.
8. **Workbook baseline:** for supported XLSX behavior, test chosen source representation, sheet/cell location, text and numeric identifiers, styled dates, blank values and formula/cache handling. Scope expectations to the product decision; do not assume displayed, stored and formula values are interchangeable.

### Conditional compatibility, resource and usability checks

- Add legacy XLS, XLSB, strict OOXML, unusual workbook ranges, formula recalculation and 1900/1904 epochs only if those formats/behaviors are in scope. Calamine’s release history makes targeted regression fixtures useful, not mandatory support (S14–S15).
- Add split/aggregate/deduplicate and one-to-many accounting cases only if the product supports such transformations.
- Once minimum hardware, file-size range and latency targets are selected, measure representative peak RSS, temporary disk, elapsed time, cancellation, low-disk behavior and cleanup after success/failure. DuckDB spill must be observed for chosen queries rather than assumed (S04).
- Observe analysts inspecting proposals, diagnosing rejected data and replaying the next month only after the workflow is selected. Do not treat a broad usability study or nearly-full-disk test as an unconditional first-release requirement from this brief alone.

For any future executed check, record environment, exact binary and dependency versions, fixture hashes, expected and observed results, and completion state. Until then, the validation list remains a proposal, not evidence of passing behavior.

## Evidence limits and open decisions

The sources establish documented behavior and public history for the named versions/pages, not behavior of a Puppet Master implementation. Mutable current documentation can drift; pin and verify any chosen dependency. The issue reports were not reproduced, release notes do not imply a current defect, and no specific product behavior was executed. No local files or packages were processed. Performance, memory, offline first use, packaging, Excel fidelity, UI usability and recipe replay all remain unverified. Usage/billing was not observed (`null`).

The product owner still needs to decide supported OSes and formats, encoding and duplicate-header policy, workbook stored/display/formula representation, date/locale policy, null tokens, whether export may proceed with unresolved rejects, full-file validation expectations, replayable-operation guarantees, schema-drift gates, minimum hardware and file/latency thresholds. Those decisions should bound implementation and conditional validations; this research does not supply invented defaults.
