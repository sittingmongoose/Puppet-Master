## Recommendation for planning

Keep the pilot narrow: preserve the existing bench-tag IDs and calendar, and add a phone-friendly way to record tray movement, count observations, readiness forecasts, order allocations, and fulfillment outcomes for two plant families. Model a propagation batch and its trays/cohorts as related but distinct records. Treat a forecast window as an estimate, and expose confirmed saleable count, holds, and reservations separately. A forecast must not itself create available quantity or a firm customer promise.

Offline capture can be useful in the two structures with poor wireless coverage. A disconnected phone cannot see another disconnected phone's reservation. Until reconciliation, label new counts, movements, and allocation requests as saved/pending rather than globally confirmed. Confirming availability therefore needs one online authority, physically partitioned quotas, or another conflict-safe rule the nursery actually operates. This is a design inference from the brief's coverage condition and documented synchronization semantics, not a runtime-proven result.

No product has been selected. ODK Collect plus Central is a possible rapid event-capture pilot if current Android devices and hosting are available; it should not be treated as a live shared stock lock. ERPNext v15 provides a useful actual-stock reservation comparator if an online stock ledger fits the staff workflow. farmOS offers useful agricultural entity and movement concepts, but the reviewed Field Kit compatibility and offline conflict behavior need current verification. A small purpose-built PWA could express the nursery's exact states but brings engineering, support, hosting, and cost questions. Improved paper-first reconciliation remains a viable interim/fallback path. The $5,500 cap is a constraint to validate, not an estimated fit: no quotes, device inventory, implementation estimate, or hosting price was collected.

## Exact plan-clause comparison

The disposition is the primary treatment of each exact clause. Enhancements below refine implementation and validation; they do not resolve nursery policy questions.

| Clause and exact text | Disposition | Finding and planning treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **P1:** “Link propagation batches and bench locations to projected readiness windows and customer allocations.” | **correction** | This covers the requested relationships, but “link” can blur forecast and confirmed availability. Preserve the clause's intent and clarify that allocations refer to a batch/cohort and quantity while readiness remains a separate forecast; only a verified/released count can back confirmed available quantity. Model tray/container identity and current location separately, retaining the existing tag ID and recording moves as events. If germination varies within a batch, permit split sub-cohorts and their distinct forecast windows. This makes the linkage auditable without presenting an estimate as a promise. |
| **P2:** “Record tray moves and fulfillment changes in a phone-friendly workflow that can coexist with paper tags during the pilot.” | **already-covered** | This captures the immediate adoption constraint. Use tag IDs as lookup keys; record each move with from/to, time, actor, and stable event ID. Keep a paper fallback and shared-calendar process during the current season. An optional scanner is only an accelerator after testing the actual tags, phone cameras, greenhouse conditions, and any barcode-to-batch mapping. Include local-saved, pending-sync, synced, and needs-review feedback; no wholesale relabeling is implied. |
| **P3:** “Show uncertain readiness and quantity changes without representing estimates as firm promises.” | **already-covered** | This directly states the key promise-safety requirement. Separate forecast, observed count, held quantity, allocation request, confirmed reservation, and fulfillment result. A conservative candidate is `allocatable now = max(0, verified saleable count − active reservations − safety hold)`, subject to the nursery choosing what counts as saleable and what hold is appropriate. Label forecasts as unconfirmed and make the state legible with text plus icon/shape/pattern, not color alone. |
| **P4:** “Restrict customer contact and delivery details to staff who need them for fulfillment.” | **already-covered** | This is correct but the allowed role boundary is not yet operationally defined. Keep names, phone numbers, and addresses out of ordinary seasonal-worker forms, entities, search, tag/QR payloads, shared-calendar titles, notifications, printouts, and device caches. Floor staff can use an order alias and needed plant/quantity details. Test every access path, including linked contacts/addresses, reports, exports, print and cached/offline data. Ask the nursery to define “staff arranging fulfillment,” retention, and device controls before choosing a platform. |
| **P5:** “The owner of available-quantity adjustments and the approval for substitutions remain nursery decisions.” | **user decision** | Preserve both decisions. A propagation lead could accept verified release counts, reducing conflicting edits but risking a bottleneck. Packers could enter pick shortages/outcomes close to fulfillment, but should not silently overwrite a grower's forecast or accepted count. A possible hybrid lets anyone submit observations, a designated nursery authority accept a verified count, and packers reserve/fulfill against it; this is an option for the nursery, not an adopted role split. Substitutions likewise need an approval owner and an explicit customer-consent rule. |
| **P6:** “Wholesale access and the handling of missed seasonal windows are not yet specified.” | **user decision** | Keep wholesale access deferred for this pilot; do not infer a buyer portal or exposure of restoration allocations. For a missed window, candidate policies include hold the order and request a revised range, offer an approved substitute, cancel/refund, or release stock only under a chosen customer-consent rule. The workflow can surface “window missed — decision needed” but must not automatically notify, substitute, cancel, or reallocate before the nursery sets policy. Define the responsible role, timing/buffer, and communication rules later. |

No clause is rejected. P5 and P6 correctly keep nursery-owned policy open; their unresolved state is not a reason to invent a product rule. The correction to P1 narrows the meaning of allocation and forecast while preserving the plan's intended scope.

## Findings retained from discovery

### Data and workflow semantics

- Distinguish plant family/variety, immutable propagation-batch ID, physical tray/tag ID, location, readiness forecast, count observation, order/allocation, and fulfillment. If one batch is split or trays are combined, retain parent/child lineage and quantities rather than reusing an ambiguous tag.
- Use appendable movement/count/allocation events with stable IDs and actor/time metadata. Duplicate retries should be idempotent; edits to a finalized event should create a correction or superseding event, not mutate history. Preserve two distinct conflicting observations and resolve them explicitly instead of using last-write-wins on stock.
- Keep expected germination distinct from verified healthy/saleable count, safety holds, active reservations, and packed/picked results. The sample formula above is a candidate only; the brief does not define bundle sizes, unit, saleability threshold, verification frequency, safety margin, or whether partial reservations are allowed.
- A movement reconciled against an order needs both physical lineage and an order alias. Fulfillment posts actual picked, short, damaged, canceled, or otherwise approved outcome once; cancellation should restore a reservation only through an auditable event.
- Define units explicitly. Seedlings may be counted as individual plants, trays, flats, or bundles. ERPNext displays a default stock unit of “Nos,” while whole-number enforcement is a separate UoM setting. The nursery's chosen unit and conversions need confirmation before importing counts. (S15-S18)

### Offline, concurrency, and tools

- ODK Entities can share longitudinal state across forms, but offline Entity create/update has a Central/Collect version floor of 2024.3.0 or newer. Central processes concurrent updates in receipt order and surfaces conflicts; stale out-of-order work can be held up to five days and then force-processed with a conflict. This supports offline lookup/capture with review, not a stock lock shared by disconnected devices. (S02-S03)
- ODK Collect is Android-oriented. Enketo/Web Forms have different offline behavior; queued sending may require the form to remain open after connectivity returns, and offline Entity updates must not be assumed. Form labels and media can be translated, while app menus use a separately selected UI language. The actual phones, languages, and reading preferences remain unknown. (S01-S06)
- ODK supports barcode questions, but that alone does not prove the current paper tags are machine-readable, batch-specific, or scannable under wet/glare/dirty greenhouse conditions. ERPNext item barcode fields are item-level; they do not automatically equate an existing tag with a batch ID. (S05, S15)
- ODK's public retry history includes issue #4589 and merged PR #4655 / commit `12ef1a3f4f2257591cfe912754a44305dd686a2a`, which prevents editing finalized failed submissions and includes test coverage. This supports immutable event identity and retry discipline, but it is historical product evidence, not a test of a nursery deployment. (S09-S10)
- ERPNext v15 Stock Reservation is enabled by a setting and applies to recorded stock on Sales Orders. Pinned v15.121.3 code calculates batch quantity by item and warehouse, subtracts existing reservations, and either reduces an oversized request when partial reservation is allowed or rejects the excess. It cannot reserve expected germination as existing stock. Batch identity, warehouse, UoM, negative-stock paths, permissions, and the online workflow still need sandbox and user validation. The reviewed negative-batch guard is conditional; not every call/configuration path was traced. (S13-S14, S21-S24)
- farmOS Plant Assets can represent groups or individuals, movement logs determine location, and inventory quantity logs can reset/increment/decrement in a measure and unit. These are useful domain concepts. The reviewed sources do not establish reservation/customer-promise support. Field Kit's current build/version compatibility was not pinned; older v1 material calls itself proof-of-concept; farmOS.js documentation describes last-write-wins and a farmOS 2.x scope, while current installation material is v3-oriented. Treat this as a compatibility and conflict risk, not proof that the whole platform lacks an extension. Hosting, database, SSL, operations, and cost also remain unverified. (S25-S30)
- The WCAG 2.2 Recommendation says color must not be the only visual cue. Use visible text and a distinct icon, shape, or pattern alongside color; verify phone and monochrome print views with the color-blind coordinator. (S31)

### Alternatives and conditions

1. **Purpose-built phone-first PWA:** closest fit to tag IDs, offline events, forecasts, confirmed count and reservations; requires careful queue persistence, duplicate handling, sync conflict resolution, privacy, support, hosting and cost estimates. Consider only if a fixed-scope prototype demonstrates restart-safe offline capture and the total first-season cost fits the cap.
2. **ODK Collect + Central:** a candidate for rapid offline movement and count-observation forms where current Android devices/hosting exist. Prefer coordinator-confirmed reservations and explicit conflict review; keep PII out of seasonal forms/entities and test storage/security. It is not itself an authoritative disconnected multi-device stock ledger.
3. **ERPNext v15:** a comparator when online stock ledger, sales-order reservations and fulfillment integration matter most. Prototype two plant families and validate batch quantities, partial reservation behavior, units, role permissions and all negative-stock paths. The general ERP's setup/training burden and offline field suitability are unknown.
4. **farmOS + Field Kit:** offers crop/variety, grouped plant assets, location and inventory-history concepts. Evaluate only against a pinned supported client/server combination and explicit conflict tests; do not infer that v2 client docs prove safe operation on v3.
5. **Paper tags + improved board/ledger:** viable this season and the natural fallback. Assign a daily authoritative reconciliation, visible reservation/order alias, and a missed-window review queue. Network-independent but more vulnerable to stale records and double-entry as orders grow.

No market comparison, procurement review, pricing, local infrastructure check, installation, or product benchmark was performed. Tool fit is conditional. None of these findings establishes a product selection or confirms the $5,500 budget.

## User decisions, unknowns, and conditional design choices

The brief's explicit open decisions remain open: who adjusts available quantity; who approves substitutions; what to do when a readiness window is missed; and whether wholesale buyers later receive a separate view. In addition, research cannot choose the following without local input:

- Current phone OS/models, shared vs personal device use, offline storage/security settings, and whether staff can reach a coordinator to confirm stock.
- Actual languages, literacy needs, greenhouse gloves/light conditions, current tag formats and legibility, and whether camera scanning is useful.
- Families/varieties for the pilot, batch-splitting practice, count unit/conversions, saleable criteria, safety buffer, and frequency/person for verified counts.
- Where the authoritative record lives, permitted offline reservation policy, order aliases, multi-structure stock partitioning, cancellation/partial fulfillment behavior, retention period, and fulfillment role boundary.
- Available hosting/support and full first-season cost (setup, services, migration, tag printing, training, recurring charges, and maintenance). No claim that any approach fits the $5,500 ceiling is warranted yet.

Potential enhancements that retain the plan's intent: capture count/movement provenance; keep explicit pending-sync and conflict-resolution states; support batch/tray lineage and split cohorts; distinguish units; make retries idempotent; use translated plain labels and non-color cues; test PII access through every output/cache path. These can be considered in acceptance criteria once staffing and operational decisions are known. They should not silently expand the pilot into a customer portal, full ERP, or wholesale system.

## Proposed discriminating validation; none executed

These are proposals for later discovery/build evaluation. No product, nursery site, device, sandbox, code, or runtime check was used in this research stage.

1. **No-network end-to-end walkthrough:** On the actual phones, go into both poor-coverage structures, identify tagged trays, record a movement and count, close/reopen the app, and reconnect. Verify local persistence through restart; clearly distinct saved/pending/synced/failed states; one-time application of each event; and no false confirmed quantity. Repeat with battery/app termination and an intentionally duplicated retry.
2. **Two-device contention:** Start with ten verified units. While disconnected, device A requests six and device B requests six. Reconnect in both orders. A safe workflow must never silently present twelve confirmed units, must preserve both requests/events and actor/time, and expose a human-readable resolution. Run ODK's Entity conflict scenario if selected; for another implementation, test its actual queue/merge path.
3. **Forecast and cohort split:** Use uneven germination with a subset ready earlier. Verify two forecast windows can be represented, neither becomes available stock by itself, and only an accepted count affects allocatable quantity. Check wording with coordinators and a sample customer-facing message before sending.
4. **Physical-to-order reconciliation:** Move a tagged tray from propagation to hardening/pickup, split or combine a cohort if normal practice allows, then fulfill one order partially. Verify tag identity, lineage, location, order alias, reserved quantity, actual packed quantity, and remaining count reconcile without double decrement.
5. **Missed-window policy safety:** Put an order beyond its forecast range. Ensure the system creates a decision item and does not auto-cancel, notify, substitute, or reallocate. After the nursery selects a policy and responsible role, test each permitted action and the audit record.
6. **Accessibility/language and tag use:** Test status cards and printed views in color and grayscale with the color-blind coordinator; confirm no status relies on color alone. Ask seasonal workers which languages and wording they need. Try existing tags with actual phones in greenhouse light, wet/dirty conditions, and gloves; retain manual lookup if scanning is unreliable.
7. **Privacy role test:** Create representative seasonal-worker and fulfillment accounts. Attempt to see customer contact/address in forms, entity values, list/search, notification, shared calendar, print/export/report, and locally cached/offline data. Only the defined fulfillment role should receive contact/address details. Include linked address/contact records and revoked-user/device behavior for whichever platform is considered.
8. **ERPNext comparator test, if shortlisted:** In an isolated qualified sandbox, configure two plant batches, chosen whole-number UoM, a Sales Order, and reservations. Check actual qty minus reservations, partial and excess behavior, batch movement/split, permissions, and negative-stock documentation/code paths. No source review here substitutes for a transaction test.
9. **Cost and operations gate:** Inventory current devices and hosting/support capacity; estimate setup, integration/import, tag changes, service costs, training, backups, and first-season maintenance. Confirm total cost and required coordinator time against $5,500 before procurement or deployment.

## Evidence and limitations

Public primary-source evidence and exact identifiers are kept in the source map and notes. Pinned code/version records are available for ODK Collect v2026.3.5, its identified retry-fix merge, and ERPNext v15.121.3. ODK, ERPNext, and farmOS documentation is rolling; where pages are mutable, source-map access checkpoint and locator are recorded, and changed claims should receive a new source ID rather than silent rebinding. farmOS Field Kit remains version-ambiguous. The static ERPNext negative-quantity observation is conditional and incomplete. The available retry fix supports a safe design principle but does not establish current failure prevalence.

There were no site visits, phone inventory, pricing quotes, product instances, installations, runtime code execution, or validation tests. All checks above are proposed. No arbitrary files outside this research write root were accessed or changed, and no executable or installer was downloaded. The research supports a bounded plan comparison; it does not certify production safety, security, regulatory compliance, offline correctness, or budget feasibility.
