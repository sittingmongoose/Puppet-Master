## 2. P1 — "Generate recurring shifts from a weekly template."
**Disposition: correction (kept as the core, fixed on two points).**

Food-bank operations are characterized as running "on a fixed, recurring schedule tied to warehouse operations" (Golden food-bank platforms article, secondary — exact phrasing). No fetched vendor page exposes a *named* recurring-shifts feature: the VolunteerHub features page (primary product page) names Volunteer Scheduling (volunteers self-manage shifts), Multi-Event Editor, Email/Text Messaging, Mobile App, Check-In, Advanced Permissions, Configurable Forms — and has **no recurring-shift/shift-template feature name at all**. So a first-class weekly-template engine is both domain-matched and the product-gap differentiator.

Corrections carried forward: (1) **exceptions and overrides live inside the template** — holiday skips, one-off extra shifts, mid-cycle template edits; a bare weekly generator cannot express "no Tuesday shift on Thanksgiving week" without per-instance overrides. (2) **Imported rosters land as templates or template deltas, not just shift instances** — the brief requires occasional imported rosters, and the one primary product page checked is precisely quiet there (no roster/CSV import; its integrations list is Salesforce, Blackbaud, Zapier, Sterling Volunteers). Template-level import is the reusable form.

Conditions: template expansion must be timezone-explicit (interlocks with P5); expansion horizon bounded (4–8 rolling weeks) so availability changes re-enter allocation before far-future shifts lock.

Alternatives retained: full RFC-5545 RRULE recurrence semantics (more expressive, heavier — **unevidenced this session**, no RRULE source retained; proposed standard only) vs plain per-week template rows with override dates (simpler, matches the thin plan's spirit). Uncertainty retained: whether unnamed dedicated food-bank platforms (the Golden article's category 1 names no members) already ship template+import together — settle by probe V4.

## 3. P2 — "Assign the first available volunteer alphabetically."
**Disposition: rejected as the primary allocation rule; replaced by availability-derived constraint allocation. Alphabetical retained as fallback; self-signup retained as the dominant flow.**

Why alphabetical first-fit is rejected as the *primary* rule: it is a scan order, not an allocation policy. Where several volunteers are available it resolves ties by name, which (a) chronically concentrates load at the top of the alphabet, (b) is blind to no-show history — the factor the Golden food-bank material (linked post, per m1 re-scope) frames as the strongest predictor of coverage failure, and (c) carries no fairness semantics, while the brief explicitly forbids assuming equal availability. It survives as the deterministic, explainable fallback when the solver fails or times out, and all fetched vendor products confirm the self-signup-first pattern in any case (VolunteerHub "allows volunteers to self-manage their shifts").

Replacement mechanics, from primary-documented APIs: model assignment as boolean allocation — `new_bool_var` per (volunteer, day, shift); `add_exactly_one(...)` per shift slot; `add_at_most_one(...)` per volunteer/day (OR-Tools employee-scheduling page, verified verbatim on re-access). **Per-volunteer caps are derived from that volunteer's own declared availability** (cap_v = floor(available_slots_v × target_load)): the documented hard bound `min_shifts_per_nurse = (num_shifts * num_days) // num_nurses` (max `min+1` when indivisible) assumes availability-blind, near-divisible load and is inapplicable as written under the brief's explicit no-equal-availability constraint. Fairness objective, two evidenced options: (a) Timefold `ConstraintCollectors.loadBalance(...)` inside `groupBy`, penalized via `LoadBalance::unfairness` on a BigDecimal soft score — minimax semantics (minimize the most-loaded participant's workload, next-most-loaded as tie-break), `unfairness()` dimensionless, 0 = perfect, no upper bound, scales ~linearly with dataset size, comparable only within one dataset, six-decimal rounding means ~1e-6 differences matter and integer rounding creates score traps (documented workaround: scale unfairness by a power of ten and rebalance constraint weights; may rule out 32-bit int scores); caveat: balancing raw counts under unequal availability is wrong-shaped — the load to balance should be the utilization *ratio* (assigned ÷ declared-available), which the generic key function makes expressible but which is **not a documented composition** (m4 retained; tested by V2). (b) plain CP-SAT availability-proportional caps plus a minimize-spread objective using only the primitive linear constraints — less machinery, no score-type trap. Preference weighting (per-request weights, negative weight = desire, as now verified in the located sophisticated example) can layer on top as a soft term.

Conditions: **no size-limit statement appears on the cited OR-Tools page** (M1 re-scope) — the only documented instances are 4×3×3 and 5×7×3, so behavior at 180 volunteers × multi-week horizons is genuinely unknown and V1 is load-bearing. Timefold's full constraint set beyond the quickstart ("skills, pairing employees, fairness and more") lives in the **commercial** app.timefold.ai model, not the Apache-2.0 quickstart (the quickstart use-case description covers availability and shift skill requirements; "Load Balancing" is a repo-overview "Notable Solver Concept", per m3 correction); there is no volunteer-specific quickstart — a licensing/feature boundary a build decision must respect.

User decision: suggestion-only (coordinator confirms) vs authoritative allocation — evidence does not discriminate; default suggestion-only.

## 5. P4 — "Email everyone the full roster."
**Disposition: correction (conflicts with the brief's privacy-limited sharing as written).**

Emailing the *full* roster to every one of 180 volunteers exposes all contact details and patterns to everyone. The primary product evidence shows the scoped alternatives are standard surface: per-audience "Email Messaging" and "Text Messaging", "Mobile App", "Check-In", "Advanced Permissions" (exact VolunteerHub feature names). Corrected form: per-person schedule emails (own shifts only), coordinator-visible full roster under permission control, targeted broadcast to affected volunteers only; reminders dual-channel email + SMS at designated intervals, accessibility honored by offering both channels and machine-readable text rather than image-only content. User decision retained: what "roster" means in any shared artifact (names-only directory vs full contact list) — an organizational privacy norm evidence cannot set.

Condition (release-evidence-grounded): notification sends must be **decoupled from solver callbacks**. The OR-Tools v9.11 release notes document a known issue where Python/Java/.NET callbacks "can slow down search performance a lot" such that "search will continue until the time limit is crossed" (page's own typo retained as observed); the regression→fix window around #4674 (Mac libscip linkage) and #4677 (ComputeMinSumOfWeightedEndMins) in v9.13, repaired by v9.14/v9.15, shows the release channel does surface and repair such issues. Solve, then notify, as separate passes.

## 6. P5 — "Use local wall-clock timestamps."
**Disposition: rejected as storage representation; corrected to zone-aware timestamps.**

Provenance stated honestly: **no retained source covers timezone handling** — this correction is engineering reasoning, not retained evidence (confirmed again on re-access: the OR-Tools page has no timezone/DST mention; VolunteerHub's "sent at intervals you designate" is not zone semantics). Local wall-clock storage cannot represent DST spring-forward gaps or fall-back ambiguities, mis-orders imported rosters produced in other zones, and makes interval-based reminder scheduling drift across transitions. Corrected form: persist UTC (or zone-aware datetimes) plus one IANA zone per site; render local at the edge. Uncertainty retained: whether single-site food banks ever cross zones in practice — but the brief's imported-roster requirement makes cross-zone input non-hypothetical, so the cheap fix is warranted regardless. This is a correctness issue, not a preference, which is why it stays a rejection rather than a demoted enhancement.
