## Core product hypothesis

Treat each inspection as a durable case that can be started, resumed, completed, sent, reviewed, returned for correction, resubmitted, approved, and exported. The field app must work throughout a day with no service; the office coordinator must be able to see which cases and photos are actually received, ask for a correction with a reason, and distinguish an unsent device copy from the server's accepted version.

A practical thin product is an offline-first Android capture workflow plus a small coordinator review surface. Form completion should be easy to scan and recover: an inspector sees assigned work, opens an inspection, saves/resumes drafts, captures structured answers, a location with its accuracy/source, and one or more photos, then explicitly marks it ready. A visible Outbox/Ready-to-send queue holds complete packages until network returns; per-record upload state and attachment counts make progress legible. Server acknowledgement changes the record to Received; it must not be inferred from tapping Send. Corrections should be addressed to an inspection with a clear reason, and the next edit should become a new version tied to the same stable case identity. The coordinator needs to review by status, inspector, date, and address/record name; detect missing media; comment; request changes; and see who changed what.

The important concurrency choice is ownership. Before submission, the inspector owns the local draft. After acceptance, the coordinator reviews the submitted version. If an inspector edits a case while the coordinator has an older version open, or two inspectors can edit a shared record, show that their bases differ and preserve both versions. Do not silently discard one side with last-write-wins. Choose who can merge, whether merge is field-level or whole-form, and which party gets the next edit before implementation. A small team can start with one current editor per case plus explicit correction handoff; that is simpler than a general collaboration system, while still requiring duplicate-send protection and visible conflicts.

## Product and workflow alternatives discovered

| Approach | Fit and useful evidence | Conditions and tradeoffs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **ODK Collect + Central** — open field-form client and review/server stack | Closest public workflow benchmark for offline Android forms with location and image answers. Central has review states, comments, activity history, missing-media warnings, filtering, and a CSV ZIP layout that keeps attachment folders associated by stable submission instance ID. Its evolution from v1.2 submission edits/review/comments to v1.3 diffs and audit details and later export refinements shows that review and export are separate product capabilities, not just a status field. | Strong prototype/reference if the team wants to validate the forms and correction loop before custom work. Self-hosting requires an operator, domain, deployment setup, backups and upgrades; free software does not mean zero deployment cost. Central states do not trigger workflow effects; rejected cases still export unless filtered. Verify exact current release and form authoring needs. |
| **KoboCollect + KoboToolbox** — simple enumerator-survey workflow | A useful Android workflow pattern: download the latest form before disconnecting; save as draft; finalize into Ready to send or queue sending; upload in bulk after service returns. The support guide also calls out no-internet USB transfer for devices that cannot upload. | Good candidate to benchmark straightforward form completion, training, and recovery. Its documented finalization path makes the editable-draft/immutable-ready distinction important. Investigate the coordinator's correction loop in a hands-on evaluation before deciding it covers it. |
| **QField / QFieldCloud or Mergin Maps** — QGIS and spatial-feature-first | Valuable if the inspection workflow is map-led (select building/parcel on a map, navigate to features, work with geometry). QField's offline mode packages editable layers as GeoPackage; its documented default conflict rule is Last Wins, with a manual option. Mergin recommends GeoPackage for collaborative offline editing; its selective sync can keep photos off other field devices while still exposing features. | Potentially avoid rebuilding GIS capabilities if maps/geometry are central and a QGIS-capable coordinator exists. It brings a QGIS project/workflow dependency and conflict-policy consequences. A feature can be visible without its photo on a second device under selective sync. Choose only if map-led tasks beat a plain assigned-inspection list in usability testing. |
| **ArcGIS Survey123** — mature form workflow integrated with Esri | Useful status and handoff patterns: Draft, Outbox, Sent, and optional Inbox. Offline completed forms remain editable in Outbox until sent. Its Inbox supports filters but is documented with a 5,000-parent-record cap; mixing Sent and Inbox for ongoing multi-device editing can fail to bring another device's updated response into the Inbox. | Strong comparison if the nonprofit already has ArcGIS expertise/data and budget. For a multi-user correction workflow, test the folder/edit configuration rather than assume a shared record stays current. It is a hosted feature-layer ecosystem, so test export and migration with all media before adopting. |
| **Fulcrum** — commercial general field-data benchmark | Its official docs distinguish a filtered Data Downloader from a broader full-history Exporter, include CSV/XLSX and spatial/database formats, and allow photo/history/GPS choices. The Downloader's concrete ceiling is 10 GB and/or 100,000 media files; its Exporter is faster for full history but less filterable and limited for line/polygon geometry. | Could reduce form-building and office workflow time if pricing, contract, and hosting meet constraints. This research did not establish offline reliability, self-hosting, pricing, or privacy fit; test these and a complete exit export before treating it as a solution. |

These are distinct architectures, not a feature-count contest: a form-first tool suits structured inspections; a GIS-first tool suits map navigation and spatial editing; a commercial builder may optimize speed-to-deployment. Do a narrow scenario comparison against this nonprofit's exact inspection tasks rather than selecting by market breadth.

## Design implications for capture, review, and usability

### Forms and field interactions

- Make the first screen establish where and what the inspector is inspecting. Let the inspector confirm or correct the address/building identity before recording findings. Use conditional sections so irrelevant inspection branches are not shown; allow multiple findings/photos where one building can have several independent issues.
- Distinguish required from optional fields in text. Avoid making a GPS fix or camera permission a hidden hard blocker if an inspector can continue with an explained manual location or a documented no-photo reason. Preserve all answers when the app closes, the OS kills it, or capture permissions are denied.
- Clearly separate Draft (can still change), Ready to send (complete on device, not yet accepted), Sent/Received (server acknowledgement), Needs correction, and Approved. The product must show last sync result, outstanding attachment count, and a retry path that cannot create a second case.
- For corrections, keep the original answer and reviewer note visible together; offer an inspector a direct route to the specific returned question/photo. A change history and attributed comment protect context when the office cannot reach inspectors during their offline day.
- Label every control for Android accessibility services. Use clear question text, plain-language units, and instructions near the relevant field. Show which field is invalid, state what is wrong in text, preserve the value, focus or navigate to the correction, and provide a useful fix. Do not use color alone. Use large, separated touch controls and test screen-reader order, font scaling, contrast, focus visibility, and error announcement.
- A web coordinator console should use the WCAG 2.2 CSS-pixel criterion where applicable (SC 2.5.8 uses a 24-by-24 CSS-pixel target or its spacing exception); do not carry that unit over as an Android dp threshold. Test native Android with platform tools and real devices.

### Photo and location handling

Keep the photo as a separately stored attachment with a stable ID and a parent inspection ID; the inspection record should not point to an ephemeral local path. Keep original images and a portable manifest that maps names/IDs to cases, capture time, uploader, and optional location metadata. The field UI should state when a camera image is saved locally and when it has uploaded. If photo compression or thumbnails are used, preserve the original or make the transformation an explicit user decision. Show storage pressure and let a user retry a missing upload without re-submitting the case.

Record coordinates with units/coordinate reference, capture time, and accuracy value and unit, plus whether it was GPS, a selected map point, or entered manually. Explain when location access is off or accuracy is poor. The brief asks for location; it does not specify whether a photo may expose precise residence location through embedded metadata. Make retention, photo EXIF/metadata, external sharing, and any redaction behavior a privacy/user decision. Do not silently strip or disclose it.

Selective sharing should start with least privilege: coordinator role can review; inspectors can see assigned cases; exports choose cases and fields/media explicitly; sharing to a partner is a deliberate action with an audience and expiration/access revocation if using a link. Ask who the partner is, what photos/locations they need, and how long they may retain them. A filtered case bundle is safer and more portable than an unrestricted account or a public link.

### Portability and deployment options

For routine exit, offer one self-contained export per chosen batch: UTF-8 CSV for case fields and status/version metadata, a folder of original attachments, and a manifest linking stable case IDs to each attachment. Include a field/data dictionary and export timestamp. For map-oriented recipients, consider GeoJSON or GeoPackage as an additional, tested format; keep CSV as a simple general path. Verify that a person without an account can interpret the export and rejoin photos to rows. Keep data IDs/schema independent from any hosted provider's row IDs.

A future small self-hosted deployment is a separate operating choice, not just a server checkbox. ODK Central is concrete evidence that self-hosting can be available but still requires domain/Docker/Linux skill, backups, upgrades, monitoring, storage, and a named operator. The nonprofit should first decide who operates it, acceptable outage/recovery targets, and available time/budget. Keep a deployment proof-of-concept as an option. Do not promise that an independently built server is simple or that a vendor export alone proves migration works. If an existing hosted option is used first, test a complete restore and export into an independent tool before scaling field use.

## Consequential source behavior and change history

1. ODK Central's documented review states are labels assigned by people except for automatic Received/Edited. The docs explicitly say they do not cause anything automatically; rejected submissions remain in ordinary exports unless the export is filtered. The implication is that assignment, correction notification, locking, and exclusion need explicit workflow behavior rather than a dropdown alone.
2. ODK Central's CSV ZIP export includes root/repeat CSV tables and attachment files grouped by submission instance ID. That is a concrete, portable join pattern. It is not by itself proof that the entire app's forms, access rules, review discussion, audit history, or server config can be migrated.
3. The ODK Central API changelog provides a relevant evolution chain: v1.2 introduced edit versions, comments, and review state; v1.3 added per-submission diffs and audit-log events (including export); v1.4 added richer CSV-export controls, deleted-field exports, and original form-version metadata; later changes continue to refine data geodata and actor filtering. This history supports a phased design of review, version history, and export as separable capabilities. The evidence reviewed is a documented release history, not an independently reproduced regression or source-code audit.
4. QFieldCloud documents Last Wins by timestamp as its default conflict resolution; the older overlapping attribute change is not applied but remains in history. Manual resolution is available. This is a consequential default, and its applicability is specifically the QFieldCloud data sync path, not a universal offline-sync rule.
5. Survey123's Inbox and Sent-folder caveat is a product-specific workflow constraint. Its 5,000 limit is parent survey responses, with repeats adding further retrieved records. It is not a general mobile database limit.
6. Fulcrum export caps are specific to its Data Downloader: 10 GB and/or 100,000 media files. Do not generalize the cap to every export tool.

## Discriminating validation proposals

No device, account, or runtime witness was available or exercised in this research; every item below is **proposed**, not passed.

1. **One-day disconnected field run:** Preload the current form and a small assigned case set on representative low/mid-range Android models. Capture location, several photos, and a partially completed form; force-close/reboot at defined points. Keep devices offline for a simulated full shift, then reconnect. Pass only if every acknowledged case has all expected answers/media, drafts remain editable, uploads resume after interruption, and repeated sends yield one server case and one set of attachments. Record device/OS, form build, image sizes, storage before/after, latency, and server acceptance IDs.
2. **Correction and conflict exercise:** Submit a version, have the coordinator add a note and request one correction, then have the inspector revise and resubmit. In a separate case, edit the same field on a phone and coordinator surface from the same earlier version before either syncs. Confirm the application detects stale base state, preserves both submitted versions, attributes changes, and provides one explicit resolution path; no silent overwrite. Exercise a different-field edit to see whether field-level merging is understandable and safe. Decide one-editor ownership versus concurrent edits based on this result.
3. **Field form/usability trial:** Give 5–8 representative inspectors the same scripted but unfamiliar inspection workflow. Compare a compact multi-section form with a map-first/feature-first flow only if location navigation is central. Measure task completion, omitted required evidence, correction rate, abandonment, and time; observe confusion and recovery after interruption. Include TalkBack, large text, touch, contrast, and validation-error tasks on actual target devices. Agree success thresholds with users before trial; compare rather than report ungrounded benchmark times.
4. **Coordinator workload test:** Give one coordinator a mixed queue with complete, missing-photo, duplicate-looking, and correction-request cases. Ask them to identify exceptions, write one actionable note, return it to the right inspector, verify a corrected response, and approve/export selected records. Measure missed exceptions and steps; the key discriminator is whether the queue makes unsent, received, needs-correction, and approved states unambiguous.
5. **Photo and selective-sharing check:** Generate a representative case batch and verify disk usage, local storage warnings, retry after a media-only interruption, duplicate attachment handling, original-image preservation, and privacy policy for EXIF location. Test a filtered share/export with only authorized cases and fields/media, check access revocation/expiration if links are chosen, and verify other inspectors see or do not see photos exactly as the chosen policy specifies.
6. **Portability and future deployment drill:** Export a sample batch and reconcile counts, types, coordinates/units, comments/version metadata in scope, stable IDs, and every media reference against the source. Open in a separate generic tool with no vendor account. Then deploy/restore the proposed small self-host setup from written steps using a clean environment/operator; record prerequisites, backup/restore duration, missing configuration, and recurring maintenance work. A successful CSV export alone is not a successful migration test.
