## Overall judgment

The two drafts are a strong, complementary research set. Their main corrections to the thin plan stand: browser localStorage is not justified as the sole home for all offline records and photos; generic last-write-wins is unsafe as an assumed inspection policy; a photo URL is not an offline photo store; review/export and authorization need real workflow and scope; and a network toggle is only a smoke check. Both drafts preserve the case constraints and label implementation and policy choices as unresolved where evidence cannot decide them. No P-clause disposition should be reversed on this review.

The q1 Room/API/PostgreSQL path is a **candidate baseline to prove**, while q2's ODK/Kobo/GIS/Survey123/commercial comparisons are options and workflow benchmarks. The final synthesis should keep that distinction: neither investigator has evidence to select a platform or hosting mode for the nonprofit. A product-specific comparison cannot establish measured field reliability, total cost, licensing suitability for every bundled component, or user fit.

Two material findings below should be carried into the final option assessment and validation. The remaining findings are traceability or completeness improvements; they do not undermine the principal architecture advice.

## Material findings

### M1 — Survey123's existing-image behavior is missing from the photo-heavy comparison

Q2 accurately reports the Survey123 Inbox limit (5,000 parent responses, with repeats additional) and the Sent/Inbox refresh caveat. The same official editing page also states that existing images are not downloaded when a survey is retrieved from Inbox and that offline workflows are unsupported for editing existing images [CR07]. This matters here because photos are a required part of the inspection and coordinator corrections may concern submitted cases.

Keep the limitation narrow: it concerns editing existing image attachments through this workflow; it does **not** establish that Survey123 cannot capture new photos offline. Q2's recommendation to test ongoing correction and full media export is good, but the comparison and test proposal should name a returned inspection with existing photos, re-entry/replacement of an image while offline, and what the coordinator will actually receive. Do not rank Survey123's correction loop until that path is proven on the intended configuration.

### M2 — CouchDB issue #5422 is mutable and its current status has drifted

Q1 correctly treats the reported attachment growth as one issue report, not proof of a general or current CouchDB defect. Its map gives the exact reporter version (3.4.2) and access time. At this critic's direct retrieval, however, GitHub rendered issue #5422 as **Closed**, while retaining bug / needs-triage labels and showing no explanatory activity, fix, or linked pull request [CR11]. This does not show that the reported behavior was fixed; the reported 3.4.2 reproduction remains evidence and the closure reason is unknown.

Q1's source map marks C05 mutable_source: false, even though its claim includes mutable issue status/labels/activity. Preserve the observation as a timestamped issue snapshot and mark status mutable. Update the synthesis to say the issue was reported for 3.4.2, is now shown closed without a visible resolution in the inspected issue page, and still requires exact-release reproduction or a linked fix check. Absence of #5422 from release notes is not proof either way.

## Minor findings and source-traceability notes

1. **PowerSync Room status has a mismatched citation in q1.** Q1's statement that the Kotlin Room integration is Beta and built-in Kotlin attachment helpers are Alpha is corroborated by the current official Feature Status table: Room (Kotlin) is Beta, the Kotlin SDK is GA, and Kotlin attachment helpers are Alpha [CR05]. But draft citation P06 and q1's P06 map entry point to the attachments page, which substantiates the helper but not the Room ORM status. Add a separate immutable source-map entry for the Feature Status/ORM evidence; do not merge that claim into the attachment-only locator.
2. **ODK licensing can be made more precise.** Q2's “free software” wording for self-hosted ODK Central is directionally supported: the Central repository README states its source is Apache 2.0 and says an installation includes third-party software under their respective licenses [CR10]. It is still worth distinguishing Central's license from the licenses of bundled components and from managed-hosting terms. This is procurement diligence, not a reason to reject ODK.
3. **Q1 P05 bundles two PowerSync documentation URLs.** Upload operation semantics and update-conflict defaults come from separate pages but share one url field/ID in the q1 map. Split them or use separate URL fields so each exact locator and mutable page can be audited. This does not change q1's well-scoped description of PowerSync's synchronous/idempotent backend responsibility or its example conflict policy.
4. **Q1's issue evidence state needs a timestamp, not an immutable-source label.** This is the C05 point above; the issue's reported reproduction itself remains useful historical evidence.

## Discovery, alternatives, conditions, and evidence quality

- **O1 — discovery beyond the thin plan:** Met. Q1 adds Room/local database plus custom outbox/API, PowerSync, and CouchDB. Q2 adds form-first ODK and Kobo, GIS-first QField/Mergin, ArcGIS Survey123, and Fulcrum. The distinctions are meaningful and their fit conditions (form-first, map-first, existing ArcGIS, or commercial/hosted preference) are explicit. These are documentary comparisons, not a measured bake-off.
- **O2 — consequential primary behavior/defaults/units:** Substantially met. Direct review confirms q2's Survey123 parent/repeat cap and folder caveat, Fulcrum Data Downloader's 10 GB and/or 100,000-media-file ceiling (not a ceiling on every export tool), Mergin's device-level photo omission, QFieldCloud's specific conflict default, PowerSync's current feature-status distinctions, and the web-only CSS-pixel target-size unit [CR04–CR09]. The q1/q2 source roots cover the other platform, licensing, backup, and sync details. Preserve their applicability boundaries.
- **O3 — issue/evolution chain:** Met with stated limits. Q2's ODK changelog chain is supported: v1.2 submission edits/review/comments, v1.3 granular version diffs and additional audit events, v1.4 export options/form-version metadata, and a later geodata-parsing fix [CR03]. This is release-history evidence, not a reproduced regression. Q1's CouchDB item is a useful issue report and should now include the mutable closed status described in M2. No runtime behavior was independently exercised.
- **O4 — every P disposition:** Both drafts explicitly adjudicate all six. P4 is correctly marked already-covered while still improving detail; P2 reasonably has a split disposition; P6's validation correction does not imply the simple network toggle is useless.
- **O5 — self-contained alternatives and uncertainty:** Met. The drafts retain unresolved platform, ownership, correction, access, retention, location, export, operating-responsibility, and licensing decisions. Do not turn the preferred q1 baseline or q2's one-editor starting preference into settled product policy.
- **O6 — discriminating validations:** Met as proposals only. No device/app/service tests or user sessions were run. Proposed tests are relevant, especially day-offline recovery, interrupted media, stale-version conflicts, scope/revocation, portability, and accessibility. For Survey123, add the existing-image Inbox/offline-edit case noted in M1. For CouchDB, reproduce the exact stub-reuse sequence against a named release and record actual source/target sizes; issue status alone cannot answer that question. The suggested 5–8-person usability study is exploratory; set success criteria with the nonprofit and do not treat that sample as a powered estimate.

## Scope and uncertainty for the final synthesis

The recommendation to prototype local-first capture, durable queue/file state, per-submission versions, human conflict resolution, assigned-data authorization, and a records-plus-media export follows the brief and the evidence. Room/custom API, PowerSync, ODK Central, CouchDB, GIS-first products, Survey123, and Fulcrum remain conditional candidates. Before selecting one, the nonprofit still needs to choose Android-native versus PWA, one-editor versus concurrent ownership, coordinator handoff states, access scope/revocation and cached-data retention, photo/EXIF policy, GPS requirements, export/import semantics, and who operates any self-hosted service.

No result in this critique proves reliability, security, accessibility conformance, privacy compliance, costs, licensing fit for all components, or performance on the target devices. Keep the implementation checks proposed and label their status plainly.
