# H02 research draft — plan comparison and recommended pilot

**Basis:** exact H02 brief, the pre-reveal discovery in [discovery.md](discovery.md), the frozen own-case plan in [revealed-plan.md](revealed-plan.md), and the immutable source inventory/evidence in [source-map.json](source-map.json) and [sources/index.md](sources/index.md). Discovery was not rewritten after plan reveal. No new external source or independent finding was added after reveal; clause dispositions below compare the six plan clauses to the saved discovery.

## Recommendation

Proceed with a capped, one-species / one-shed pilot that retains the waterproof harvest card as the field source record and adds a simple, explicit lot event history plus a shed view for `waiting for handoff`, `packed`, and `held for review`. Use pre-issued unique lot labels while disconnected. Record each correction and status change as an addition linked to the card and prior value; derive the current board from that history. Show saved-local, queued, and server-received separately. Keep held lots blocked until the farm names an authorized release role. Represent closure notices as source-linked facts with a human-selected bed relationship; never calculate legal harvest permission.

Use a paper handoff docket as the default buyer deliverable while the farm decides whether direct access is needed. Build a buyer projection from a field allowlist that excludes crew contact details and internal quality notes. Keep raw internal notes and buyer output separate. Before purchasing or commissioning a production platform, run a short fit exercise against ODK Collect/Central on available equipment (or an equivalent offline capture prototype) and compare it with a narrow custom event workflow. ODK is a candidate for testing, not an assumed complete traceability/scheduling solution: Collect is documented for offline enumerator capture, Web Forms are not offline, the audit and offline-sync edge cases require regression tests, and entered user identity alone is not authentication. ABALOBI MONITOR is the closest unfamiliar product reference for multilingual offline fisheries landing capture; use its demo/support route to learn, but do not assume its fishery workflow covers farm beds, sheds, holds, or buyers. Avoid selecting farmOS Field Kit v1 as production without a current maintenance/build and fit check. EPCIS 2.0.1 is an optional event/export mapping if buyers later require interoperability; a full standard implementation is outside the demonstrated need.

Treat $14,000 as a hard initial cap covering product/build or configuration, devices, labels/printing, initial data setup, hosting, backup/restore, multilingual review, training, and support. No credible total cost is established in the brief or sources. Require an itemized quote and demonstrate the critical behaviors before committing. If the offline correction history, protected buyer output, and safe hold/release behavior cannot be demonstrated inside the cap, reduce scope or continue the paper-backed board; do not conceal those features in a later phase while calling the system complete.

## Retained findings and design conditions

### Workflow and data

- Keep these original constraints attached to the implementation: 12 crews, two sheds, weak mobile coverage, waterproof cards, $14,000 initial system, restricted buyer visibility, weather/demand/local closure change, varied first languages, shared glove-operated shed touchscreen, manager's waiting/packed/held view, correctable transcription with change attribution, candidate pilot of one species and one shed.
- Separate planned assignments/capacity from observed events. Crew assignment, bed, pickup window, line capacity, actual harvest amount, packing, and handoff are related fields, but a later schedule change must not rewrite what happened earlier.
- Use farm-preissued lot IDs and per-event IDs. On reconnect, deduplicate the same event safely and surface conflicting material values for an authorized human. Do not use a last-write-wins rule for bed, quantity, hold, release, or buyer handoff.
- Capture the actual amount plus explicit unit. The pilot must decide count versus weight/volume, permitted unit, scale/rounding precision, and how card notation is retained. If supporting a physical measurement, preserve the original value/unit as entered and any normalized/exported form. GS1's fixed-count versus variable-measure split is a useful interoperability model; it does not choose the farm's unit. [S04, S08]
- Show `event happened at` separately from `recorded/received at`. A disconnected device's wall clock may be wrong; system receipt time is a separate provenance fact. Do not sort away conflicting reports or treat a timestamp as evidence of legal permission.
- Treat `held` as a blocking operational state. Keep a restricted internal reason if used; do not expose quality notes to buyers. Release permission is not to be guessed from job title, crew status, current closure feed, or user-account existence.

### Closure notices and safety boundary

Model an outside closure notice as a referenced source record rather than a generated farm determination. Store its originating authority, stable URL and/or retained copy, exact title/identifier, time retrieved/observed, any stated issue/effective/expiry times, and the human-selected bed association. Preserve subsequent corrections/supersession as events. If the official source cannot be reached, show the age of the last observed notice state and fall back to the farm's chosen offline process; do not label the bed open. If the notice-bed match is uncertain, mark it for farm review. Do not silently geofence crews or adjudicate whether a person may harvest.

Jurisdiction and species are unknown. The FDA source is not a legal answer: the page scopes the traceability rule to foods specifically listed and points to exemptions, while its top and lower sections disagree on compliance dates. If the farm operates in the United States or sells into a regulated market, confirm current official applicability with species, activities, exemptions, and customer requirements before claiming compliance. This inquiry can change required fields, but it must not make the software the closure authority. [S09]

### Access, receipt, and shared devices

Keep the internal operational view and buyer projection separate. Define and review the buyer-visible field list with the farm and buyers. The first print should have only lot identity, mutually agreed product/area/quantity/unit/time facts, and handoff acknowledgment/reference; exclude phone numbers and quality notes. Use an explicit export allowlist rather than copying a full internal record and trying to hide fields at display time. When/if direct access is chosen, require a separate authorization/revocation plan and repeat the disclosure test after every schema change.

The shed touchscreen is shared, so event attribution should ask who performed each meaningful correction, hold, release, and handoff. Do not treat a typed name as a secure credential; decide an acceptable, low-friction worker-identification approach in pilot design. Avoid locking the whole device to an identity long after a worker leaves. For weak signal, make local persistence visible and make sync receipt equally visible; distinguish queued from completed handoff to the system.

## Decisions needed from the farm

1. Which person/role can release each held lot, and what reasons/states require approval?
2. Which local authority's notices are eligible sources? Who verifies currentness and chooses the bed association? What is the farm's disconnected-operation rule when a notice source is unreachable?
3. Which species and shed define the pilot; what amount units/precision appear on current cards and buyer records?
4. Which fields may buyers receive, and is the initial printed docket sufficient? If not, who receives accounts/links and how is access revoked?
5. What existing Android/tablet, shed touchscreen, network, printer/scanner, hosting, and support capacity can be reused?
6. Which crew languages are needed, and which workers/buyers can review terminology and notice wording?
7. What must the $14,000 cap include, and what maintenance/support spend can continue after initial setup?
8. Which regulatory and buyer traceability requirements apply to the actual species, jurisdiction, growing method, processing, and destination markets?

Until decisions 1–4 are settled, keep release disabled, use only farm-approved notice entries, and default buyer delivery to the printed allowlisted docket.

## Discriminating validation plan — all proposed, none executed

The following checks are not evidence that a runtime was available. No project was deployed, no device was connected, and no cost quote or regulatory determination was obtained.

1. **Fit and cost gate:** create a 1-shed, 1-species demonstrator using a form product and a paper/custom event-board concept. Obtain itemized all-in estimates for configuration/build, hardware, label/print, hosting, backup, translation, training, support, and changes. Stop if the required offline event history and privacy boundary exceed $14,000; compare demonstrated behavior, not feature checklists.
2. **Lot identity and quantity:** issue test IDs to two crews who work offline in parallel. Enter same-looking labels, duplicates, count lots, decimal amounts, and corrections across the range observed on cards/scales. Reconcile source notation, unit, decimal precision, and normalized buyer output. Pass only if IDs remain unique and every value/unit can be traced back.
3. **Offline/sync/conflict:** on at least two crew devices plus the shed screen, disable coverage, enter events, close/reopen, submit twice, edit a later form version, skew one device clock, and reconnect after one lot was held or changed elsewhere. Verify local persistence, idempotency, visible stale state, conflict review, server receipt, and exact reconstructed history. Include deletion/update tests in any ODK Entities workflow, because #6768/#6910 demonstrates a specific offline reconciliation edge. [S05, S06]
4. **Correction/audit:** deliberately mistype a quantity, bed, date/time, or lot label; correct each; include failed validation and a deferred-validation field-list form if that interaction exists. Compare current board, original submission/card, correction event, prior/new values, reason, actor, and times. Attempt deletion and re-entry; verify the audit chain remains. Include the old #5550 scenario if using relevant ODK controls. [S02, S07]
5. **Closure/hold authority:** add a local notice with an ambiguous bed match, later amendment, stale or unreachable source, affected lot, and an unauthorized release attempt. Verify the system records the notice source, labels the match uncertain, keeps related lots held for review, and does not display “permitted” or infer open status. After the farm chooses a release authority, test allowed and denied actions with that exact role.
6. **Buyer confidentiality:** produce a sample printed docket and, only if later approved, a test buyer view. Search/inspect all visible and downloadable fields for crew numbers, internal notes, unrelated lots, and hidden metadata. Forward or revoke a test link if links are used. Pass only against an approved field allowlist.
7. **Glove/language usability:** with actual gloves on the shared target device, ask workers in each pilot language to find a lot, record amount/unit, save with no signal, identify queue state, correct an error, read a hold, and perform a handoff. Record completion time, errors, need for help, translation confusion, accidental actions, and whether users can tell queued from received. Revise and repeat failed tasks.
8. **Capacity and recovery:** publish a capacity plan, disconnect one crew, have another use the last available slot, then reconcile. Confirm no silent oversubscription and that stale plans are labeled. Restore the backup in a test environment and reproduce one lot's source, corrections, hold/release, and buyer docket. This is required before treating data retention as proven.

### Proposed pass conditions

All pilot lots have unique IDs, explicit quantity units, an origin/source-card reference, a complete correction/handoff/hold history, and a visible sync state. A held lot cannot be released by an unspecified or unauthorized person. Buyer output contains only approved fields. Crew users can complete critical actions in gloves and understand their available languages. Offline events survive restart and reconcile without lost/duplicated history. The pilot stays within an itemized $14,000 initial cap. These checks assess the bounded pilot; they do not establish unlimited production reliability or legal compliance.

## Source/result limitations and review record

The source set is limited to independently chosen public primary product/standard/regulator sources and the H02 brief. Mutable sources are explicitly marked in `source-map.json`; the FDA page has a visible date inconsistency, ODK issue state can change, and legacy farmOS v1 documentation is not a current support commitment. Source IDs are not silently rebound. No usage/billing or private provider information was observed. All source excerpts and locators remain navigable in `sources/`.

A same-thread counterread checked for: unapproved legal inference, unsupported budget estimates, conflating ODK identity prompt with authentication, conflating a form-submit public link with buyer read access, using audit logs as immutable correction truth, and treating plan open decisions as settled. The draft was revised to retain those limits and to keep paper and direct-access alternatives visible. The assignment bars nested agents; therefore this is not presented as an independent fresh-context critic/reviser pass. No runtime or qualified sandbox witness was run; the validation list is proposed work only.
