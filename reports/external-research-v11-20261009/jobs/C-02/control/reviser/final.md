# H02 complete revision — control arm

## Basis and recommendation

This is a complete revision for the H02 shellfish-farm brief. It incorporates the complete control/research draft and discovery, the revealed six-clause plan, the complete control/critic assessment, the ten existing public-source records, and the bounded critic evidence. I independently reopened only the already inventoried primary sources S01 and S02 to check the two material findings. No source ID was added or rebound. The navigable source evidence and lineage are in [sources/index.md](sources/index.md) and [source-map.json](source-map.json).

Proceed with a capped, one-species / one-shed pilot that keeps waterproof harvest cards as the field source record and adds a compact lot-event history plus a shed view for `waiting for handoff`, `packed`, and `held for review`. Pre-issue unique lot labels for disconnected work. Record corrections and status changes as linked additions; derive the current board from that history. Make local save, queued sync, and server receipt visibly different. Keep held lots blocked until the farm names a release role. Represent local closure notices as source-linked facts with a human-selected bed association; never have the software decide whether harvest is permitted.

Default to a printed, allowlisted buyer docket while the farm decides whether direct access is needed. Keep the internal operational record and buyer projection separate. Before buying or commissioning a production platform, run a fit exercise with ODK Collect/Central on available equipment or an equivalent offline-capture prototype, and compare it with a narrow custom event workflow. ODK is a candidate to test, not an assumed traceability, scheduling, authorization, or buyer-portal solution. ABALOBI MONITOR is a useful multilingual offline fisheries reference, not a demonstrated shellfish-farm fit. Before using it with live farm records, settle the organization structure, user access, and site list: its current setup instructions say data cannot move between organizations and placeholder-site records cannot be reassigned. Use the demo route or non-production data until setup is correct. [S01](sources/reviser-recheck.md#s01)

Treat $14,000 as a hard initial cap covering software/build or configuration, devices, labels and printing, setup, hosting, backup/restore, multilingual review, training, and support. The brief and sources establish no credible all-in cost. Require an itemized quote and demonstrate core behaviors before committing. If offline correction history, buyer privacy, and safe hold/release handling cannot fit inside the cap, reduce scope or continue the paper-backed board; do not omit those behaviors while calling the system complete.

## Original constraints and unknowns

The plan must continue to account for all facts in the brief: 12 harvest crews; two packing sheds across a broad coastal inlet; bed assignment, pickup windows, and line capacity; waterproof field cards; $14,000 for the initial system; weak mobile coverage during some work; reliable lot-level buyer handoffs; no buyer exposure of crew phone numbers or internal quality notes; weather, buyer demand, and locally issued closures that can change operations; farm-authorized notices represented without software deciding permission; multiple crew languages; a shared glove-operated shed touchscreen; manager states for waiting, packed, and held lots; transcription correction without erasing who changed it; and a candidate pilot of one species and one shed.

The brief does not say which species, jurisdiction, buyer rules, existing devices/network, measurement unit or precision, notice authority, pilot languages, buyer record format, release role, or budget line items apply. It also leaves open which notice should be linked to a bed and whether buyers need direct access or paper is sufficient. These remain farm decisions or unknowns. Do not fill them by assumption.

## Workflow, record, and safety conditions

### Lot events, units, and offline work

Keep planned assignments and capacity separate from observed events. A later pickup or line-capacity change must not rewrite what happened earlier. Use farm-preissued lot IDs and unique event IDs. On reconnect, deduplicate repeated event delivery and surface conflicting material values for an authorized person; do not apply last-write-wins to quantity, bed, hold, release, or buyer handoff. Preserve a source-card reference (or a farm-approved card image/scan, if chosen) for every lot.

Capture a quantity with an explicit unit and preserve the original written/entered value. The farm must choose count versus mass/volume, allowed units, and scale/rounding precision. GS1 EPCIS distinguishes fixed-count quantities from variable physical quantities paired with units; that is useful export guidance, not a unit decision for this farm. ODK’s integer widget rejects decimals and has a nine-digit limit; its decimal widget accepts decimals with a 15-character input cap. Check real card and scale ranges before adopting either. [S04](sources/S04-odk-question-types.md) [S08](sources/S08-gs1-epcis-201.md)

Keep three time facts distinct where available: (1) time written on or reported from the source card as the crew’s occurrence report; (2) client audit timestamps and within-session elapsed duration, including the clock basis; and (3) server-received time. For ODK specifically, the form-start timestamp uses the device clock; later audit timestamps add elapsed time to that start. The documentation says absolute timestamps can be inaccurate even when elapsed intervals within one form editing session are accurate. A server receipt does not correct an inaccurate client start. Skew the device clock before opening a form and across restart in the proposed test; do not label an ODK absolute audit timestamp as verified harvest time. [S02](sources/reviser-recheck.md#s02)

Keep `held` as a blocking operational state. A private hold reason may be stored internally, but it must not leak into buyer output. Do not infer release authority from job title, crew status, login presence, or a closure feed. Attribute meaningful edits and handoffs to a person using a low-friction method the farm accepts; a typed name is attribution text, not a secure credential. For disconnected work, show saved-local, queued, and server-received distinctly. Shed capacity is shared across crews, so centralize capacity changes during the pilot or visibly flag stale plans and route collisions to a human instead of silently overbooking.

### Notices and legal boundary

Record a local closure notice as a source fact: issuing authority, original title/identifier, stable URL or retained copy, observed/retrieved time, any stated issue/effective/expiry interval, and the person’s decision linking it to a bed. Preserve later corrections or supersession as events. If the source is unreachable, show how old the last observation is and follow the farm’s chosen offline procedure; do not label the bed open. If the bed match is unclear, route it for review. Do not geofence crews or determine whether harvest is lawful.

The FDA Food Traceability page is conditional U.S. context, not a compliance answer for an unspecified shellfish farm. It applies to listed foods and firms subject to its conditions and exemptions. The observed page calls January 20, 2026 the original compliance date, describes a proposed extension to July 20, 2028, and says a 2026 Act directs FDA not to enforce before that date; a lower section still states January 20, 2026 as the compliance date without that qualification. That is stale/mixed page text. Do not state that the compliance date itself was amended based on the non-enforcement text, and do not claim applicability here. If the actual species, activities, jurisdiction, or market make it relevant, check current official guidance and qualified advice. [S09](sources/S09-fda-food-traceability-rule.md)

### Access and shared-device use

Keep the internal operational record separate from the buyer projection. Have the farm and buyers approve the exact visible fields. The initial print may include only mutually approved lot identity, product/area, quantity/unit, relevant times, and handoff acknowledgment/reference. Exclude crew contact details, internal quality notes, unrelated lots, and internal identifiers that are not approved. Use an export allowlist; do not copy the full internal record and hide fields only in the screen. If direct buyer access is later selected, design separate roles and revocation and repeat disclosure tests after schema changes. ODK Public Access Links are for submitting forms; they do not establish read-only buyer viewing. [S03](sources/S03-odk-central-submissions.md)

For the shared touchscreen, use actual crew languages, short labels with icons, large high-contrast targets, no color-only status, one-tap common actions, fixed pilot units, and a numeric keypad. The worker should be able to find a lot, record an amount, save without signal, see the queue state, correct a mistake, understand a hold, and record handoff. Do not treat a shared-device user prompt as authentication. Translation and interaction design remain implementation work; the product sources do not show that any named product already meets this farm’s needs.

## Discriminating validation plan — proposed, not executed

Before running the pilot checks, record the chosen devices and app/form/build versions, network conditions, responsible observer, participating crew languages, and pre-agreed pass threshold for each critical task. Set usability and reconciliation thresholds with farm users rather than inventing a universal completion-time number. For safety/privacy properties, use direct thresholds: zero unauthorized releases, zero prohibited buyer fields, and zero lost or duplicated test events.

1. **Fit and cost gate.** Build a one-species/one-shed demonstrator with an ODK or equivalent form path and a paper/custom event-board path. Obtain an itemized estimate for configuration/build, hardware, labels/printing, hosting, backup/restore, translation, training, support, and changes. Compare demonstrated behavior, not feature lists. Stop or rescope if core history, privacy, or safe holds exceed $14,000.
2. **Lot identity and quantity.** Give two disconnected crews pre-issued IDs, including similar-looking labels and duplicates. Enter counts, decimal weights/volumes, and corrections across the range seen on cards/scales. Reconcile source notation, unit, precision, and buyer output. Pass only with unique IDs, no silent overwrite, and an exact trace from each normalized value to its source.
3. **Offline queue, sync, and capacity conflict.** On two crew devices and the shared shed screen, disable coverage, enter events, close/reopen, submit twice, edit after a form/schema update, reconnect after a lot was changed elsewhere, and inject stale line-capacity data. Verify persistence, idempotency, visible saved/queued/received states, conflict review, accurate final history, and no silent oversubscription. Include the specific owner-only Entity deletion/update pattern if using ODK Entities; #6768/#6910 motivates this check, not a universal reliability claim.
4. **Correction and audit time.** Mistype quantity, bed, time, and label; correct them; include failed/deferred validation if used. For ODK, skew device time before opening the form and across app/device restart. Compare the card-reported occurrence time, ODK absolute audit timestamps, elapsed within-session duration, central receipt, old/new values, reason, actor, and source reference. Confirm the client absolute timestamp is not presented as verified harvest time. Compare saved answers with audit events in the old #5550 flow only if that interaction is used. Pass with complete, reconstructable corrections and no lost test events.
5. **Notice, hold, and release authority.** Enter an ambiguous bed match, notice amendment, stale/unreachable source, and an affected lot; attempt handoff while held and an unauthorized release. Verify source linkage, uncertainty routing, blocked release, and no “permitted/open” inference. After the farm names a release role, test allow and deny cases against that exact role. Pass with zero unauthorized releases.
6. **Buyer confidentiality.** Print/export sample lots and inspect all fields, metadata, and unrelated rows for crew phone numbers, internal notes, and unapproved identifiers. If direct access is later selected, test authentication, link forwarding/revocation, field allowlisting, and paper/offline fallback separately. Pass with zero prohibited fields in any buyer-facing output.
7. **Glove and language usability.** On the actual shared device, have workers in every selected language find a lot, enter an amount/unit, save offline, identify queue status, correct an error, read a hold, and complete handoff while wearing working gloves. Record task completion, errors, help needed, translation confusion, accidental actions, and whether queued differs clearly from received. Before observing, agree with farm users which tasks must be completed, acceptable errors/help, and any task-time target; revise and repeat failures.
8. **Capacity and recovery.** Have separate crews compete for the last planned slot with one disconnected. Confirm stale capacity is labeled and collisions are reviewed. Restore a backup in a test environment and reproduce a lot’s source card, corrections, hold/release, and buyer docket. Keep the two-shed case out of the pilot unless selected.

These checks assess a bounded pilot. They do not prove unlimited production reliability, legal compliance, or a vendor’s all-in cost. No test above has been executed.
