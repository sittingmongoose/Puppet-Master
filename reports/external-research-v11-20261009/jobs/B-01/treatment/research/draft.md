## Recommendation in brief

Run a one-route pilot qualification with two kinds of ferry products: a ferry-specific offline system such as PDMS Compass and a ferry operations suite that describes local work and later sync, such as Access One Voyage. Use Zaui as a connected inventory and onboarding comparison. Treat FerryCloud as a lead only after confirming its current status, because the reviewed product/pricing pages show 2019 date markers and quote-only pricing. Do not choose a product because its page says “offline.” Make each supplier demonstrate the operator’s exact case: both terminals isolated, one capacity unit remaining, both desks attempting to confirm, and one late change for an accessibility need.

Before that decision, Jared’s brief leaves the critical reservation-authority rule open. There are three viable policy directions: one authority with offline entries marked provisional/pending; preallocated, non-overlapping quotas by terminal; or independent local confirmations followed by reconciliation, with an explicit risk that two confirmations may compete for one capacity unit. If strict no-overbooking is required, the first two directions need to be tested; the third cannot be sold as a guarantee. Maintain a numbered paper manifest and a named dispatch reconciliation role as a continuity path.

This is a conditional recommendation, not an award or a claim that any vendor has passed. The final choice depends on the manager’s answer to P5, actual vessel loading limits, outage duration, a written all-in quote, queue privacy behavior, and a successful exit/export review. The budget is $9,000 initial and $1,800 recurring per year ($150 per month). No comparable current public price was found.

## Retained findings and alternatives

### Product and architecture findings

1. **PDMS Compass is the strongest vendor lead for difficult ferry connectivity.** Its official page describes ferry routes, vessel and ship configuration, ticketing across booking channels, a central system, printed tickets, and port ticketing while online or offline. That is closer to a storm-affected terminal than a generic event reservation product. But the page does not say whether two offline terminals can simultaneously promise the same last capacity unit, whether local quotas exist, or how disconnected edits merge. Request a demonstration and a written description of those rules.

2. **Access One Voyage is a second offline-ferry lead.** Its vendor page describes a ferry operations suite with locally available data and synchronization for supported/authorized offline actions, Windows workstation support, Android mobile workflows, data-control claims, and possible role/audit controls. This may fit desk, loading and dispatch better than a custom app, but the allowed offline action list and duplicate-capacity rule are not specified publicly. A product statement that data belongs to the operator does not itself settle backups, export completeness, license exit, or contract obligations.

3. **Zaui is a useful connected-product comparator.** It advertises passenger, vehicle and cabin inventories, real-time manifests, route and sailing schedules, check-in, schedule notifications, reporting, onboarding and 24/7 support. It could reduce the manager’s operational support burden if connected inventory is acceptable. Its inspected page did not specify offline functionality. Treat that as a question for a demo, not proof that it does or does not support the use case.

4. **FerryCloud is a weaker currentness lead.** Its pages describe ferry SaaS, single/return and multichannel ticketing, real-time inventory, a basic tier, standard reporting and CET-business-hours support. The product and pricing pages carry 2019 date markers and request a quote rather than publishing an amount. First confirm product availability, support and current product details. Do not compare its old quote page with current pricing or infer budget fit.

5. **Manual quota or pending workflow is an active alternative, not just a failure fallback.** A numbered paper manifest with a named dispatch reconciling owner can cover a measured half-shift outage. If each isolated station must issue confirmed reservations, allot each a non-overlapping share of sailing capacity before the split. If the manager chooses one authority, paper/offline requests should remain visibly provisional until dispatcher review. Either approach should mark imported paper rows once and preserve their original IDs.

6. **Custom local-first software is technically possible, but it transfers substantial work to the operator.** CouchDB 3.5 documents independent replicas, periodic incremental replication and conflict branches; losing revisions may be kept, but the application must inspect and resolve conflicts, and normal views show a winner. RxDB 17.0.0 documents offline local writes, reconnect replication, pull/push checkpoints, and a default master-wins conflict handler. Its shown defaults include a 5-second retry time, live replication, automatic start, and one leader for multi-instance replication. Retried writes may arrive more than once, requiring idempotent backend handling. These are database-level features, not a ferry capacity protocol.

A meaningful conflict can evade a document-level detector: if both terminal replicas create different reservation IDs for one remaining vehicle space, there may be no same-document revision conflict at all. If both decrement one shared capacity document, the application still needs a business decision for the conflict. Custom sync therefore needs a reservation state machine, capacity policy, idempotent event IDs, conflict queue, reconciliation UI, monitoring, backups, update ownership, and support. With no dedicated IT employee and a $1,800 annual ceiling, this is not the default pilot path unless a named delivery/support owner and full lifecycle budget are identified.

### Issue/fix/release evidence and limit

RxDB issue #5342 documents an owner-confirmed case where a vanished WebRTC peer could stall sync. Commit 28015c6097fe1e98c902cc5a8edca87c002c94c0 and PR #5348 changed reconnect logic and tests; the issue says v15.0.0-beta.42 carried the fix. The reporter then described remaining divergent/missing updates on one of three clients after a small run of about 200 items and 20 updates, and the maintainer again characterized WebRTC as beta. RxDB’s later 15.0.0 notes say WebRTC moved out of beta. This is a useful evolution chain showing that a reconnect fix does not validate every replication scenario. It concerns one developer report and one order-processing workload; it is not a ferry-system benchmark or a general reliability estimate.

### Capacity, privacy and reports

- A route/sailing list needs the physical capacity units dispatch uses. Oversized vehicles may consume a different number of bays/length than ordinary vehicles; determine the local rule before modeling.
- Separate a held/reserved spot from a ticket and from an actual passenger/vehicle boarding record. WSF’s route-specific system illustrates this split and also shows that walk-on reservation rules vary. Do not import its policy as local law.
- Count both walk-ons and actual boarded load separately from reservations. Daily reports should include changes, cancelled/moved, no-shows if the operator uses that rule, and unresolved provisional/conflict items.
- Accessibility assistance should be a staff-only operational flag with no unnecessary passenger narrative in public. Any queue screen or printout must omit passenger names/contact details and explanatory notes.
- Use separate desk, loading and dispatch roles where possible. Shared tablets need an accountable staff identity or a shift handoff that still attributes changes. Large text should be validated on the actual tablets; a seasonal-worker handoff may be a one-page quick guide or a short guided flow.
- The one-route pilot should preserve a second-route path through route/sailing/vessel/capacity configuration and reports rather than duplicated hard-coded screens. Do not add payments, public booking, automated notifications, dynamic pricing or integrations until the operator confirms them and their costs.

## Cost and scope conditions

The repeating ceiling converts to $150 per month total. Dividing that by all 36 staff would be $50 per person per year only if all required a paid seat; actual licensing may work differently and is unknown. Compare complete quotes that include setup, license, staff/device count, support hours, hosting, backups, printer/scanner equipment and consumables, payment fees if included, training, integrations, route expansion, export/retention and exit charges. The $9,000 initial cap must also include any hardware and data migration that the vendor requires. No amount was observed from current, comparable vendor pricing; usage/billing were unobserved and are recorded as null.

The case omits jurisdiction, fare rules, passenger/vehicle capacities, device models, actual shift duration, public-display details, current ledger fields and outage frequency. Do not infer a legal retention period, safety standard, price, or current vendor availability from the supplied information. These are open facts for the operator or vendor, not reasons to block the one-route pilot plan.

## Validation plan

### Proposed, not executed

1. **Offline capacity collision:** use the actual pilot sailing with one ordinary unit and one oversized unit remaining. Disconnect terminals from one another, have both desks attempt the same final unit, then reconnect. The test passes only if the result matches the manager’s written authority rule and no reservation is silently lost. For a zero-overbook rule, a test in which both isolated terminals confirm the same last unit is a failure unless capacity was partitioned in advance.
2. **Quota behavior:** if quota policy is chosen, test each terminal at the exact limit and one request over it; verify a clear refusal/wait state, dispatcher reallocation audit and no use of the other terminal’s allotment while disconnected.
3. **Conflict and duplicate delivery:** change or cancel the same reservation at two terminals, repeat with a late assistance note, restart one tablet and restore connection after a measured half-shift. Verify all original intent is visible, no stale value silently wins, retry does not duplicate a reservation, and dispatch can resolve each case.
4. **Paper handoff:** use a printed manifest and numbered local rows through an outage; after reconnection import each row exactly once and reconcile report totals against paper.
5. **Privacy and access:** test staff view, loading view, public queue screen and printed output with names/contact details present in the private record. Public views must not leak them. Test staff-specific attribution and lost/shared-device behavior.
6. **Large text and onboarding:** use the actual shared tablets at the configured large-text setting. Have a seasonal worker after the intended brief training create/find a reservation, record an oversized vehicle, enter walk-on counts and find an assistance flag. Record observed completion and errors; do not treat this as formal accessibility certification.
7. **Daily report and export:** compare one day’s reservations, tickets if used, changes, walk-on counts and actual boarded load to the paper source. Export full history, field definitions and event trail, open it with ordinary tools, and check that a second route can be configured without rebuilding the application.
8. **Budget:** collect a written, itemized first-year and annual quote, then check it against $9,000 initial and $1,800/year recurring caps. Include 36 staff access, hardware and operating-hour support; compare payment, training, second-route and exit charges separately.

## Open decisions for the operator

1. Does “continue operating” mean staff can record provisional requests, or must both disconnected terminals independently confirm reservations?
2. Is strict no-overbooking mandatory? If yes, approve a single authority or preallocated terminal quotas before procurement.
3. What does a booking reserve: vehicle deck space only, passengers as well, ticket/payment, or some combination?
4. What physical dimensions/classes define an oversized vehicle and the capacity unit for each sailing?
5. Which passenger fields and notes are necessary, who can view assistance details, and what may appear on the public queue?
6. Which country/state and operational authority governs manifests, privacy, record retention and boarding accessibility?
7. What is the true half-shift outage length, what devices/printers are already available, and what support hours are required?
8. What historical records must survive exit, for how long, and what export representation does the manager accept?

Until those decisions are made, a useful pilot can compare defined policies and product behavior, but it cannot truthfully promise globally unique confirmed reservations during disconnection or compliance with an unspecified retention rule.
