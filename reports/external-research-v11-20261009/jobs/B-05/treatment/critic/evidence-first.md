# B-05 treatment critic — evidence-first record

**Status:** Written before opening any predecessor draft or revealed plan. This records independent discovery and the brief-derived questions to carry into the later comparison; it does not assume what the draft or plan says.

## Brief-derived problem

The cooperative is small (26 interpreters, four coordinators, three counties), but its workload has three coupled problems: short-notice schedule changes with travel gaps; parallel coordinators can offer one interpreter to different clients; and sensitive client, meeting-topic, and interpreter-availability details should be disclosed on a need-to-know basis. A phone-capable coordinator workflow matters. The requested product must preserve qualification and client-specific communication/access needs; track request, offer, acceptance, travel estimate, completion, and invoice readiness; and make “unfilled” explicit. Two unresolved policy decisions are automated client confirmations and whether accepted work may be reassigned without interpreter consent. The $4,200 first-release budget and six-week board review bound initial scope. The brief does not name jurisdiction, legal regime, existing calendar vendor, hosting preference, exact payment workflow, or a client-portal requirement.

## Independent discovery and alternatives

1. **Vertical interpreter scheduling product:** Interpreter Intelligence documents an assignment queue/scheduler with eligible-interpreter filtering, offers by email/SMS, offer responses, manual assignment, day/availability panes, mobile interpreter responses, job closure with actual times and possible incidental travel expenses. Its own documentation exposes important caveats: the scheduler timeline uses the scheduler's time zone; unset interpreter availability defaults to available at any time; and some double-booking warnings/overrides vary by job-offer-pool configuration. This is a product to evaluate against the small budget and exact privacy/access workflow, not a recommendation or proof of fit.
2. **General-purpose calendar-first approach:** Google Calendar supports free/busy-only sharing and distinct calendar/event ACL/visibility controls. However, `default` event visibility inherits calendar ACLs, and a public event can expose details even to free/busy readers. Calendar availability is not itself an interpreter qualification, request/offer/acceptance, unfilled, invoice-readiness, or safe reassignment workflow. It is a possible calendar primitive, with privacy configuration and idempotent assignment behavior requiring separate verification.
3. **General-purpose booking platform / custom workflow:** Cal.com is a discoverable general scheduler with an open issue report describing a concurrency race at confirmation for pending bookings; the report proposes transactional locking, conflict re-check, compare-and-swap, and rollback. Treat the issue as a reported failure and proposed defense, not a verified current defect or shipped fix. A thin custom coordinator queue may fit better if it deliberately has distinct request → offer → accepted/reserved → complete/unfilled states and server-enforced conflict handling; an ordinary availability check followed by a write is not enough under concurrent coordinators.
4. **Another vertical option found:** Interpreter.io's public booking capability page describes an interpreter booking lifecycle with accept/decline, assignment, completion duration, and reporting. The page returned a bot-check on open, so this remains a lead for direct due diligence, not confirmed implementation detail, price, or current availability.

These approaches are materially different: buy/configure a domain system; compose a calendar with a separate coordination workflow; or build a small purpose-specific web app. The tradeoff is not only feature count: privacy boundaries, mobile coordinator operations, configuration errors, implementation/hosting/support cost, and the reliability of concurrent assignment all need inspection. No price evidence was collected; the budget makes a quote/prototype and six-week stop/go review useful validation rather than allowing a cost assumption.

## Governing technical evidence and application

- A free/busy API can avoid disclosing event details, but returned intervals use inclusive starts and exclusive ends; timezone defaults and per-calendar errors must be handled explicitly. Google's documented caps (100 group expansion, 50 calendars per request) are ample for the cooperative's 26 interpreters if the integration were otherwise suitable. Free/busy is a snapshot/availability signal, not a reservation. A race remains possible between checking and saving; the application needs an atomic server-side claim or a serial coordinator action plus conflict re-check at commit.
- Calendar event privacy is not a substitute for authorization design. Explicitly check each role's access to each record/action; make requests, client names/topics, qualifications/availability, and invoicing fields separately visible only to their need-to-know roles. Use the narrowest external API scopes if an integration is chosen. The exact platform/provider and its security configuration are unknown.
- WCAG 2.2 is a stable W3C Recommendation. For the browser/mobile-coordinator workflow and assistive-technology users, evaluate keyboard operation, programmatic labels/roles/state and announced status, visible text errors, and avoiding drag-only controls. WCAG is a design/validation lens here; the brief does not establish which legal conformance standard applies, and no claim of compliance is made.
- A first-party Cal.com issue (#29958, opened 2026-08-13 and still shown open when accessed) ties double booking to pending records omitted from availability plus a non-atomic confirmation transition. Its proposed mitigation is locking within a transaction, rechecking overlaps, compare-and-swap on state, and rollback on failure. Related issue reports #29967 and #29968 cover pending-time visibility and idempotency. I found no merged change or release proving these issues fixed; therefore this is evidence of a failure mode and validation design, not evidence about every version or configuration of Cal.com.
- Google's release notes show an evolving permission model: `writerWithoutPrivateAccess` became generally available July 7, 2026, and a recurring-event visibility change is also documented. A design that relies on third-party calendar defaults must pin behavior/permission and regression-test after provider changes.

## Initial discriminating validations to carry forward

These are proposals only; no runtime, production system, or qualified scientific sandbox was supplied or used.

1. **Concurrent offer race:** two coordinators submit offers for overlapping assignments to the same interpreter from separate sessions; exactly one pending offer may reserve the interpreter or the second save must show a clear conflict. Repeat with simultaneous accept/confirm, and verify retries cannot duplicate assignments.
2. **Interval/travel boundaries:** back-to-back same-location jobs, different-location jobs with a travel estimate, intervals that touch at the exact end/start instant, daylight-saving transitions and different county/interpreter time zones; verify no accidental zero-buffer acceptance and show assumptions/override reason.
3. **Access matrix:** coordinator, assigned interpreter, unassigned interpreter, client (if any client interface is selected), and unauthenticated user attempt to view request list, details, topics, interpreter availability, contact data, travel estimate, completion notes, and invoice readiness. Unassigned interpreters see only the minimum offer details; client policy remains unset until the board decides.
4. **Lifecycle and policy:** request with no qualified/available interpreter becomes explicitly “unfilled”; decline, expire, cancellation, accepted-work reassignment, completion, travel estimate, and invoice readiness each have traceable states and actor/time. Do not send automatic client confirmation or permit unilateral reassignment until the board resolves the two policy disagreements; test the chosen rule once resolved.
5. **Mobile and assistive technology:** coordinator can create/edit the day's schedule on a phone without drag-only interactions; keyboard and screen-reader path can enter/offer/accept/unfilled/close, perceive conflicts/status messages and recover from errors. Verify text labels and non-color status cues.
6. **Six-week decision:** use a bounded pilot with a small number of coordinators/interpreters; record duplicate-offer attempts prevented, unfilled requests, late changes, travel-estimate error, invoice-ready completeness, access incidents, and time to schedule. Compare pilot outcomes/cost against the $4,200 cap before approving further spend.
