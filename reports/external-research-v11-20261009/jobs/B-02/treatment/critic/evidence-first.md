## Brief-derived requirements and process model

The clinic has ten staff, a USD 7,500 annual software budget, no systems administrator, two locations with occasional outages, a six-month pilot, and a no-transfer condition for full clinical records. The minimum operational object is not simply a device: it joins an individually tagged reusable device, fitting settings, a caregiver's contact preference, a return due date, possibly an interpreter flag, custody events, return/cleaning/check results, and reassignment eligibility. The staff-facing workflow must prevent a returned device from appearing assignable before cleaning and functional checks pass. A waiting-room workstation must not expose another family's record. A staff member using a screen reader must complete the core path without color-only cues. An appointment-system link is a later, unfunded possibility. Two governance questions remain expressly undecided: post-handoff editing authority for fitting notes and the retention period for identifiable loan history.

I model the minimum states as `Available → Checked out → Returned/awaiting clean/check → Cleaned and checked → Available`; failure or an incomplete inspection must retain a blocked state. `Returned` is not equivalent to `Available`. Device custody, the cleaning/functional gate, reminder delivery, fitting-note authorization and identifiable-history retention are distinct data/permissions decisions and must not be collapsed into a single generic check-in.

## Independent findings

A managed low-code app is a plausible pilot shape because it can fit a ten-person team and offers offline queues; AppSheet's published list prices are USD 5/user/month Starter and USD 10/user/month Core, while some Workspace subscriptions include Core. At ten separate staff users those list prices correspond to USD 600 or USD 1,200/year before other costs; confirm existing entitlement, licensing definition, privacy terms, administration, build support and maintenance before committing (E09). Its offline behavior has meaningful conditions: the app first needs online initialization; clients keep local data; updates queue and later synchronize; extended disconnected operation can permit stale writes to override intervening changes; and Quick Sync should not be combined with advanced security filters in the documented cases. Browser offline use depends on a loaded page, and local cache raises a workstation privacy question (E10/E11). Outage handling needs a two-location conflict test and a clear stop/reserve/paper-reconcile rule.

An asset platform such as Snipe-IT offers unique tags and a checkout/checkin custody model that disallows a second checkout until return (E04). Its custom asset fields can add attributes but do not in themselves implement clinical role separation, fitting-note change history, a cleaned/checked readiness transition, caregiver-preference-aware messages or child/family privacy (E05). Its documented expected-checkin reminder is a scheduled command, and normal checkout/checkin notices are email/Slack at category level (E06). The clinic would need to validate message audience, wording, delivery status, preferences and update schedule; a staff inventory notification is not a caregiver reminder policy.

A small purpose-built system can model the workflow explicitly but shifts support, backups, security, accessibility and change control to an owner the brief says the clinic does not have. A hosted vendor or an existing supported identity/platform contract might lower the operational load, but the vendor must be reviewed. Paper can remain a controlled downtime fallback, with reconciliation ownership, but the separate current lists already fail at return readiness.

Information in the workflow may be sensitive health information. Whether HIPAA applies depends on the clinic's status and the records in use, not on an app being called a loan tracker. If HIPAA applies, HHS describes role-based access, workstation controls, activity review, contingency planning and business-associate arrangements; the Privacy Rule minimum-necessary requirement also has specific exceptions, including treatment (E01/E02). The clinic should make an applicability/data-minimization determination and approve role, vendor, retention and workstation controls before a live pilot. For fitting settings, the default proposal pending a clinical owner decision is clinician-only edits after handoff, with attribution/timestamp and audit history; staff may record custody and cleaning without changing settings. Retention duration remains a director/privacy/records decision and is deliberately not invented.

The exact screen-reader user is a meaningful acceptance participant. Use the normative WCAG criterion that color is not the only visual way to convey state, plus keyboard/screen-reader validation of labels, errors, status updates, focus and completion (E03). Test the actual workbench arrangement: one family at a time, authenticated staff access, short idle lock, no list left exposed, no sensitive desktop notifications, and a reset state after each interaction. AppSheet local caching makes this an explicit test condition; no product's sign-in claim alone proves a family-safe kiosk.

For an issue/evolution chain, Snipe-IT issue 18750 is a report on v8.4.0 build 21690 (`g20382ea5bf`) describing a stale/reusable request after request → checkout → optional acceptance → check-in. It is a reporter's report, not established general behavior. The issue currently displays Closed, while the latest release page lists v8.8.0 (`2c466fa`) and calls out beta mobile and sync-adapter work; the viewed release notes do not show whether the issue was fixed (E07/E08). The next verification is to inspect the resolution/linked commit and reproduce the flow on the exact current hosted version. Do not use a request queue as a clinical loan state until this is resolved empirically.

## Alternatives and conditions retained

- Managed configurable app: keep the data set minimal and staff-only; use offline mode only after explicit resolution of stale custody/conflict behavior, and verify the current entitlement and support ownership.
- Hosted inventory platform: use it for tags/custody if the pilot adapts around its limits, but do not assume the asset model is a clinical workflow or use caregiver accounts without privacy review.
- Purpose-built app: reserve for a supported builder/vendor who can own deployment, updates, backup, incident response, and accessible validation.
- Existing separate lists and controlled paper log: retain as outage fallback only, with reconciliation owner, not as the clean/check gate.

The field fit and note edit policy, identifiable retention period, reminder channel/content, outage behavior, and which vendor can meet contractual/privacy needs are decisions or validations, not facts settled by the brief. Full clinical record transfer and year-one appointment integration remain out of scope. No app was configured, no runtime was accessed, and no proposed validation has been executed.
