## Decision in brief

No product is established as suitable from public evidence alone. Use a **quote-and-synthetic-demo screen**, not a winner or procurement ranking. Lend Engine is a promising low-cost lending-workflow demo lead only: before identifiable use, obtain a written answer to its child-data/age policy, hosting and contract terms, backups and deletion, and the conflicting SSL statements; then prove loan-data isolation, a blocking cleaning-and-check gate, reminder preference handling and accessible task completion. Blueprint OMS is worth a targeted check only if the clinic already licenses it. AssetTiger and hosted Snipe-IT are low-cost inventory/status leads, but reviewed sources do not establish a safe family-specific loan schema. Cheqroom has explicit availability flags and operation requests, but an open work order does not block checkout; required add-ons are unpriced and two administrative seats at listed Business starting price would total $6,600/year before those fees. AppSheet can support a normalized custom model, but same-row last-writer-wins and local cache behavior require a narrow, tested online-first/outage design; some existing Workspace subscriptions may include Core, so verify actual entitlement. myTurn merits a quote and task demonstration, but current public evidence is thin.

Keep this as a six-month pilot. No full clinical-record transfer and no appointment-system integration in the approved first-year scope. Record only what is needed for device custody, loan handoff, a plain-language reminder and readiness. The clinic must decide fitting-note authority and identifiable-history retention before live identifiable data enters a service. The reminder workflow and caregiver contact preference are required by the brief; channel, consent/opt-out implementation, interpreter path and vendor are unresolved clinic/policy decisions. Screen-reader staff must be able to complete the real core task without color-only status cues. If authoritative availability cannot be confirmed during an outage, pause new assignment or follow an approved controlled paper queue and reconcile before release.

## Exact plan dispositions

The categories below distinguish **correction**, **optional enhancement**, **user decision**, **already covered**, **rejected**, and **uncertain**. The plan text remains verbatim in `revealed-plan.md`; these dispositions state how it should guide a product decision.

### P1 — “Track each loan through preparation, assignment, return, cleaning, and availability for reassignment.”

**Disposition: already-covered intent; correction to the safety rule.** “Track” and “availability” leave an unsafe ambiguity. A returned device must remain unavailable until it has been cleaned **and checked**; incomplete or failed cleaning/checking leaves it blocked. Reservation and assignment must consult the blocked/ready state. The minimum useful transition is `Returned → cleaning → functional check → Available`; a failed check remains blocked for repair or hold. A note, separate maintenance ticket, or “returned” event alone is not readiness.

This correction follows the brief’s stated problem: staff cannot tell whether return cleaning and checking are complete before reassignment. Cheqroom documentation makes the distinction concrete: a configured availability flag may remove equipment from availability, but open work orders do not change availability. Snipe-IT pending/undeployable statuses and Lend Engine cleaning/repair routing are candidate mechanisms, not proof of a configured clinic gate ([Cheqroom workflow evidence](sources/workflow-platforms.md), [Snipe-IT evidence](sources/asset-trackers.md), [Lend Engine evidence](sources/lending-platforms.md); SRC-02, SRC-09–12, SRC-22).

**Optional enhancement, not fixed product canon:** name cleaning/checking roles, require a particular human release action, and store actor/time/location/outcome. An automatic gate or controlled staff release could implement the same safety rule; the clinic and trial should establish what is needed. Do not hard-code a role or audit schema from this research.

**Rejected:** treating a “returned” label, cleaning note, or open work order as enough to make a device available; also rejected as premature product canon is a specific required release role or audit-field schema.

**Uncertain:** whether a candidate’s purchased tier enforces this exact state transition and permission boundary. Demonstrate with a synthetic unit, including an open work order without a separate availability flag.

### P2 — “Associate loan records with the minimum patient and caregiver details needed for contact and handoff.”

**Disposition: already-covered minimization intent; correction to enumerate the brief’s fields; user decision for identity and fitting-data scope.** Each handoff involves the device identifier, fitting settings, caregiver contact preference and return date; sometimes an interpreter request. The pilot should prove that these values are attached to the approved loan/fit record for that handoff and are not exposed to the next family when the device is reused. Do not copy a full clinical chart. Do not assume the brief requires storing the caregiver’s direct phone/email when an approved minimum contact reference could suffice.

A separate loan/history record is a **safety hypothesis and validation target**, not a universal data-schema conclusion. Fitting settings could be a loan-linked encounter record or a separately governed clinician-controlled fitting record. The minimum patient identifier is also unspecified; an opaque loan reference may suffice, or the clinic may require an approved patient/appointment reference. The clinical and privacy owners must choose identifier, field visibility and fitting-note linkage. The model must prevent a later family or unauthorized staff member from seeing prior family data. Reviewed product pages do not prove a candidate supports this safely; Snipe-IT custom fields in particular are described at the asset-model level, not as loan-event fields ([Snipe-IT custom fields](sources/asset-trackers.md); SRC-02, SRC-04, SRC-05, SRC-08, SRC-23).

**User decision:** minimum identifier; where fitting settings are governed; who can view/edit them after handoff; and which reference/contact source the reminder workflow may use. Resolve with clinic owners before configuration.

**Rejected:** full clinical-record migration for this pilot, and storing family-specific information on a reusable device record without a demonstrated and approved isolation model.

**Uncertain:** all candidates’ transaction-level field isolation, history/export behavior, and prior-loan visibility. Validate by making two synthetic sequential loans on the same reusable device.

### P3 — “Provide accessible status controls, including screen-reader labels and cues that do not depend on color.”

**Disposition: already covered; make the task acceptance test explicit.** Use visible status text or another cue as well as color. The screen-reader-using staff member must be able to complete the core task with their actual screen reader and keyboard: locate a device, create/update the approved loan record, handle return, keep it unavailable during cleaning/checking, record pass/fail, release only a passing device, and handle the reminder decision. Check control names/roles/states/values, focus and keyboard order, required fields and errors, and task-relevant announcements for results, progress or errors.

W3C WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.1 supports a non-color visual cue; keyboard access and name/role/value guidance inform the workflow checks ([W3C WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/); SRC-18–20 and E03). Applicable status-message guidance does **not** require a live announcement for every screen change; excessive announcements can hinder use ([WCAG status messages](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/status-messages.html); E21). Do not prescribe a particular ARIA implementation or announce-all-updates rule.

AssetTiger’s January 2026 VPAT is vendor-authored and reports a WAVE-based evaluation; it is useful evidence for procurement but not an independent end-to-end task test ([VPAT](https://www.assettiger.com/content/vpat_template_2.5rev_wcag_2025.pdf); SRC-07). No accessibility work was performed in this research. All other candidates remain uncertain until the real task is tested.

**Rejected:** color-only status, and a blanket rule that every screen change must be announced through a live region or a particular ARIA implementation.

### P4 — “Support plain-language reminders and a staff view that limits exposure of other families' records.”

**Disposition: required scope already present; correction to make the safety outcome testable; user decisions for policy and implementation.** A plain-language reminder workflow is required in the pilot, and the caregiver’s contact preference must drive the approved channel and recipient. Message content should be minimal and must not include fitting settings or unnecessary clinical detail. The waiting-room workflow must protect one family from the next user. Test that a new family cannot see the previous family’s record through the active view, browser back/cache, an export or a printout; also check actual sightline/monitor visibility in the room.

The exact channel/provider, legal basis or consent/opt-out mechanics, interpreter delivery path, staff role matrix, inactivity timeout and reauthentication method are **clinic decisions and implementation choices**, not prescriptions this research can set. Ask the clinic/privacy owner what policy applies. Individual staff accounts, minimum access and a secure reset boundary are plausible controls to verify. HHS materials can apply conditionally: if this clinic is a covered entity and the information is PHI, minimum-necessary and security/workstation/contingency considerations may apply, subject to the treatment-purpose exception and other facts; this review did not determine coverage, jurisdiction or legal obligations ([HHS Minimum Necessary](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/minimum-necessary-requirement/index.html), [HHS Security Rule summary](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/security/laws-regulations/index.html?previewMode=true); E01–E02). No legal conclusion is made.

Lend Engine’s policy describes day-before due-date emails and says a transactional email may be stopped by removing the member’s email; the reviewed text does not establish a caregiver channel preference/opt-out workflow. The policy and price matrix conflict about SSL: the policy describes Free/Standard as non-SSL and Plus as SSL, while the current price matrix marks SSL across displayed plans. The price matrix lists backups only in Business while other policy wording needs clarification. Confirm SSL, backup/restore, deletion/retention, region and contract details in writing ([Lend Engine policy](https://www.lend-engine.com/privacy-policy), [price matrix](https://www.lend-engine.com/pricing?p=usd); SRC-01, SRC-03, E12, E14). The under-13 clause raises a material suitability question but does not, by itself, establish that staff-entered caregiver/child-linked clinic records are either allowed or prohibited. Obtain a written answer and clinic privacy review; do not enter identifiable data before that gate.

Test reminder preference, absent/wrong preference, recipient accuracy, content preview, failure handling, opt-out behavior if the clinic adopts it, and next-family reset using controlled recipients and synthetic records. A reminder product feature alone proves none of these outcomes.

**Rejected:** making reminders optional or selecting a single channel/legal-consent mechanism in this research. The brief requires a reminder workflow; channel and policy are clinic decisions.

### P5 — “The role allowed to amend fitting notes after reassignment is a clinical governance decision.”

**Disposition: already covered; user decision.** Preserve this as an unresolved clinical governance choice. This research assigns no role. The clinical owner should decide who may amend after handoff, what staff can view, whether a change updates a loan-linked fitting record or another clinical record, and how the chosen rule is represented. An attributed history or reason may be useful if the clinic approves it; append-only behavior, audit fields and role assignment are optional design choices, not universal requirements derived here. Any trial of fitting-note permissions should use synthetic values after the clinical owner defines the rule.

**Rejected:** assigning the amendment role on behalf of the director or treating system-administrator access as clinical authority.

### P6 — “Identifiable loan-history retention and any appointment-system connection remain open scope choices.”

**Disposition: retention already correctly left open and requires a user decision; correct the appointment boundary to match the brief.** Do not invent a retention duration. Before identifiable data is entered, the records/privacy owner must decide the applicable period/trigger, deletion or anonymization, backups, exports, access and records obligations. The product’s default is not the clinic’s policy. A synthetic vendor demo can proceed while that decision is pending; real identifiable records cannot.

Appointment-system integration is outside the six-month pilot and approved first-year budget. Do not include it as a deliverable, dependency or reason to import the full clinical record. Keep only a post-pilot option to revisit it with separate budget and privacy/design review. Full clinical-record transfer is out of pilot scope throughout.

**Uncertain:** clinic jurisdiction, applicable records rules, vendor backup/deletion timing and existing retention policy were not provided or determined. No legal conclusion or retention interval is proposed.

**Rejected:** appointment integration or full-record import as a current pilot deliverable, and any invented identifiable-history duration.

## Data and workflow model that a pilot must validate

A minimal conceptual model has:

- a **reusable device** with identifier, site/location and operational readiness; and
- a **loan/handoff or approved fitting-history record** linked to the device, with only clinic-approved borrower/reference data, the brief’s fitting settings, caregiver contact preference, return date and optional interpreter request.

This is not a mandate for a particular schema. The clinical and privacy owners decide the identifier, fitting-note scope/authority and view roles. The candidate must prevent a later family from seeing a prior loan’s family-linked data. Do not put those details on a persistent device record by default, and do not import full clinical charts.

The safety outcome is explicit: a returned device stays blocked until cleaning and a functional check are completed successfully. Failed/incomplete checks remain blocked. Assignment must read the blocked/ready state. A separate note or open work ticket is not sufficient where it leaves the item available. The clinic may choose a human release step or another control after demo; this research does not prescribe the actor or audit schema.

For the two locations and intermittent network, reviewed AppSheet documentation says that offline local updates can sync later, but same-row concurrent edits use last-writer-wins. That risk depends on the schema and overlap; it does not prove all offline operation unsafe ([AppSheet conflict behavior](https://support.google.com/appsheet/answer/10104702?hl=en); SRC-15/E16). AppSheet offline use also depends on an initial online launch and a loaded browser page; stale local state and shared-workstation data remain considerations ([AppSheet offline and sync](https://support.google.com/appsheet/answer/10107724?hl=en); SRC-14/E10–E11). AssetTiger says mobile scans can queue locally, but the reviewed FAQ does not explain conflict resolution (SRC-06).

Proposed initial policy: online-first allocation; if current authoritative availability is unavailable, pause new assignment. If the clinic needs continuity, use an approved numbered paper queue with minimum data, one designated custodian and explicit post-outage reconciliation. This is a proposed clinic control, not demonstrated product behavior. Test both sites and the return/cleaning boundary. Do not rely on independent stale caches for simultaneous handoffs.

## Proposed discriminating validations

All checks below are proposals, not completed tests. Use only synthetic records until the clinic approves privacy, retention and fit-note decisions.

1. **Loan data/next-family boundary:** create two sequential synthetic loans for one device. Confirm fitting data, caregiver preference, return date and interpreter request stay with the intended approved record; a later borrower and unauthorized role cannot see prior details through active view, search, exports or history. Confirm deletion/anonymization and backup/exit behavior against the approved policy.
2. **Readiness gate:** return a device, then attempt a reservation and checkout. It must remain blocked through cleaning and functional check; failed/incomplete check stays blocked; only a passing check permits availability. Separately try an open work order without the availability flag to expose the Cheqroom-style distinction. Test the actual purchased tier.
3. **Waiting-room exposure:** use the real workstation with synthetic family A then B. Test the active screen, lock/logout/reopen, back navigation/cache, downloads, exports, print preview and physical monitor/viewing angle. Determine which controls work and whether policy owners require timeout/re-auth; do not assume timeout alone solves exposure.
4. **Roles and fitting-note governance:** after clinic owners define the rule, test reception/clinician/cleaning/checking roles against approved create/view/amend/release actions. Do not use a guessed role matrix or real fitting settings during evaluation.
5. **Two-site outage/collision:** with vendor-supported test setup, simulate each location offline and a same-device/same-row overlapping assignment, delayed sync, return and quarantine. For AppSheet, target documented same-row last-writer-wins; verify whether normalized design plus online-first operation avoids a conflict. Exercise the approved pause/paper/reconciliation route. Do not claim all offline modes are unsafe or safe without this test.
6. **Screen-reader/keyboard task:** the actual staff user completes lookup, loan/handoff, return, cleaning/check, fail/pass and reminder decision. Evaluate status text independent of color, labels/names/roles/states, focus/order, required fields/errors and relevant status announcements. Avoid a blanket “announce every update” requirement.
7. **Reminder behavior:** using controlled test recipients, verify caregiver preference, missing/wrong preference, recipient accuracy, plain-language content preview, omission of clinical details, delivery failure, and clinic-approved consent/opt-out and interpreter path. Test Lend Engine’s email suppression behavior if considered; removing an address must not silently corrupt the clinic’s contact record.
8. **Procurement/privacy gate:** obtain written current quote, minimum seats/add-ons, data location/retention/delete/backups, access/role terms, age/child-linked data position, breach/support/exit/export details and all relevant agreements. Resolve Lend Engine SSL and backup inconsistencies. Obtain clinic privacy/records owner review before any identifiable entry.
9. **Version/regression checks:** if Snipe-IT is considered, confirm the current vendor-supported version and test maintenance creation/attachment/history for restricted roles and site/company scoping. If using Requestables, test issue #18750’s reporter-described stale reusable request after checkout/check-in; because the page shows Closed with no linked fix, do not assume it is resolved.
10. **Capacity/support:** confirm item/loan volume, retention-driven row/history growth, two-site operations, backup restore and named owner/support burden within the six-month pilot and $7,500 annual budget. Verify AppSheet licensing from current Workspace entitlements rather than assume all ten accounts are separately billed.
