**Case:** I02 · **Block/arm:** B-02/control · **Method:** M13 v1 · **Stage:** research  
**Written:** 2026-10-09 19:43 UTC  
**Freeze boundary:** This discovery was developed from the exact I02 brief and independent public primary sources before the case plan was revealed. No case plan, predecessor, counterpart, evaluator, or campaign history was read. The discovery is preserved as the pre-comparison record and must not be edited after reveal.

## Brief-derived problem

The six-month pilot needs one operational view across device inventory and each loan: device ID, fitting settings, caregiver contact preference, due date, optional interpreter request, return, cleaning, and functional check. Eight clinicians and two reception staff must use it at two locations. The annual software ceiling is $7,500 and no systems administrator is available. Connectivity is usually present but not guaranteed. The waiting-room workstation and the screen-reader workflow have explicit privacy and access constraints. The pilot should avoid importing full clinical records. Two policy choices are expressly unresolved: who can change fitting notes after a handoff, and how long loan history stays identifiable. Appointment-system integration is outside the first-year budget.

The safety-critical product state is not merely “returned.” A returned device must stay unavailable for reassignment until the clinic's authorized cleaning and function-check steps are recorded. The tool should make those steps visible and prevent a returned-but-unchecked device from appearing available. It must not invent clinical settings, cleaning chemicals, legal retention periods, or edit authority.

## Independent approach discovery

### 1. Lean clinic-owned loan register on a managed low-code platform

Microsoft Power Apps with Dataverse is a plausible low-code candidate for a tailored workflow. Microsoft's current documentation describes offline-first canvas apps using Dataverse, with a device-local SQLite cache, queued offline writes, and synchronization after reconnect. Canvas apps run offline only in the native Power Apps Mobile players on iOS, Android, and Windows; browser-run canvas apps cannot run offline. The simple `LoadData`/`SaveData` fallback is memory-limited and leaves merge/conflict behavior to the maker, so it is a poor basis for this multiuser loan ledger. Dataverse's offline path has material restrictions (including unsupported SharePoint/non-Dataverse connectors, cloud flows while offline, and documented relationship/function limits). See S01–S03.

This has an important deployment consequence: an online browser app on the shared workstation plus a controlled downtime procedure is the lower-exposure pilot shape if browser access is required. If staff must continue entering patient-linked data offline, use individually assigned, managed native devices, narrow the offline profile to only necessary active loans, and settle local-device access, encryption, loss, and sync-conflict procedures before enabling it. Do not preload the entire loan history. The setup documentation requires Environment Maker, System Administrator, or System Customizer privileges; creating the offline profile is real platform administration, which conflicts with the clinic's lack of an administrator unless a named clinic owner or funded implementation partner supplies it (S03).

At the published U.S. list price on 2026-10-09, Power Apps Premium is $20/user/month with annual billing through 2026-12-31 and is announced to become $22 on 2027-01-01. Ten Premium users therefore imply a list-price license subtotal of $2,400/year now and $2,640/year at the announced rate; neither figure includes tax, implementation, the clinic's existing tenant, backup/retention work, or reminder delivery. The quote must check the exact app/data entitlement and any required licenses before procurement (S04). Microsoft lists Power Apps as an in-scope service for its HIPAA BAA, while also stating that a BAA alone does not make the clinic compliant; confirm the actual tenant agreement, service configuration, risk analysis, and any downstream messaging vendor before PHI enters it (S06, S07).

A workable first-year data boundary is a new loan register rather than an EHR import: a random loan ID; device ID/model and site; issue/return/due dates; separate return, cleaning, and functional-check events; a minimal link to the existing chart only if needed; fitting settings in their original vendor-defined fields and units; contact channel/preference and interpreter need only as operationally required. Keep unrelated history, diagnoses, audiograms, and appointment records in their current systems. A fitting-note correction should append a dated, attributable amendment while retaining the handoff snapshot; whether reception may see settings and which clinicians may amend them remains a policy decision, not an assumed role rule. The program should present a due/overdue queue and simple reminder text, but automated reminders are conditional on approved contact preferences, child/caregiver authority, secure delivery, and an in-scope agreement with each service provider.

### 2. Hosted Snipe-IT as an asset registry, not the clinical loan system

Snipe-IT is a useful unfamiliar asset-management product. Its asset tags are unique; configurable statuses can mark items as undeployable, pending, or deployable, so a clinic could model “returned—clean/check pending” as nonassignable and require an explicit ready state before reassignment. Its check-in/out model prevents an already checked-out asset from being checked out again. Yet its own documentation prefers checkout to a person and discourages checkout to locations or other nonpeople; that creates a poor fit for a clinic loan record that should not create a child as an IT “user.” Its custom fields attach to asset models and are not a typed clinical-note store. Encrypted custom fields are unsearchable and unsortable. Snipe-IT can therefore be considered for device catalog/status only if it does not receive child/caregiver identifiers, fitting data, or linkable patient loan records; it does not independently satisfy reminder, interpreter, fitting-note governance, or clinic handoff requirements (S13–S15).

Current advertised U.S. hosted tiers are $399.99/year (Basic), $999.99/year (Small Business), and $2,499.99/year (small Dedicated); the self-hosted download is free but omits vendor-hosted maintenance/backups and creates patching, database, backup, and availability duties that are a poor match for a clinic with no system administrator. Prices are dynamic, and the pricing page does not establish a HIPAA BAA for the service. Do not put PHI there unless the exact service, contract, and safeguards are confirmed in writing; an asset-only register still needs a threat review because device/loan linkage can identify a child (S13, S16).

The Snipe-IT primary-source issue/fix/release chain is instructive but narrow. Advisory GHSA-qffp-xpqv-gqr4 reports that checkout API endpoints in releases through 8.6.3 could bind live assets to soft-deleted targets because user-controlled lookups bypassed the soft-delete scope without a follow-up check; the advisory classifies it as a moderate integrity issue and says the behavior is patched in 8.7.0. PR #19330 merged on 2026-07-20, adding an `exists_undeleted` validator to disallow such targets. Release v8.7.0 was tagged at `1d05a7c` on 2026-08-11. By this review, the repository marks v8.8.0 (`2c466fa`, 2026-09-30) latest. This is evidence that version-specific checkout behavior and timely upgrades matter, not evidence that the vulnerability exposed patient information or affected Snipe-IT's ordinary web checkout: the advisory describes API endpoints. Hosted-vs-self-hosted patch responsibility and any clinic-side API use must be checked (S16–S18).

### 3. Reuse a clinic-approved system, with a controlled paper downtime procedure

Before buying or building, ask whether the current appointment/EHR platform already has a loan/equipment module covered by the clinic's existing contract, access model, and support. If so, a small, manually operated register (without system integration or bulk chart migration) may offer the least operational burden. This is conditional because the current product, licensing, BAA, accessible workflow, and feature set are unknown and therefore unverified.

Whatever digital option is chosen, define a written network-outage path. For a browser-first register, a small, controlled paper packet can record the minimum active loan/action information at each site, preserve a unique loan ID and time/operator, keep fitting detail in the approved clinical source unless staff need it for care, store completed pages securely, and require reconciliation by an authorized person when online. During a conflict, neither record should silently overwrite the other; the responsible clinician resolves conflicting setting or handoff facts. If the clinic instead permits local offline electronic data, test cache scope, concurrent edits, merge behavior, sync errors, revocation, and lost-device response before use. No runtime test was executed during this discovery.

## Primary-source implications for the design

- **Minimum data / access:** If the clinic is a HIPAA covered entity and these fields are identifiable PHI, HHS guidance says internal access policies should identify workforce classes, PHI categories, and conditions based on job duties; uses should be limited as applicable, and a cloud provider maintaining ePHI on behalf of the clinic requires a BAA plus the clinic's own risk analysis. HHS also says HIPAA itself does not prescribe medical-record retention periods; state law generally controls record retention, while safeguards apply for as long as PHI is held and through disposal. Because no jurisdiction or covered-entity facts were supplied, applicability and retention are open, not conclusions (S07–S09).
- **Device handling:** FDA says hearing aids should be cleaned as instructed and warns against solvents, alcohol, or water because they can damage electronics. The model-specific manufacturer instructions and clinic infection-control policy therefore govern the clean/check checklist; the app should capture which procedure/version was followed and who completed it, not prescribe one universal disinfectant (S10).
- **Accessibility:** W3C WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.1 says color cannot be the sole visual means of status/action/error communication, and SC 2.1.1 requires all functionality to work by keyboard. Microsoft's own canvas-app guidance calls for keyboard-only testing, logical tab order, labels announced by screen readers, and lists verified combinations of screen readers and browser/mobile player. Treat this as test criteria for the actual published workflow, not an assumption that platform defaults guarantee the custom app is accessible (S05, S11, S12).

## Decisions and facts still required

1. Clinic jurisdiction, covered-entity status, and which state/federal records rules apply.
2. Existing Microsoft tenant, eligible Power Apps plan, current BAA/DPA agreement, environment ownership, and a named person/partner for setup, user provisioning, updates, backups, and incident response.
3. Whether the waiting-room workstation is behind the staff boundary; whether it is a shared PC; whether native offline devices exist; which outage tasks must continue, and whether a secure paper fallback is acceptable.
4. Reception access to fitting settings; clinician edit/append authority after device handoff; emergency correction and audit-review roles.
5. Which manufacturer model families/settings need capturing, their native field names, value types, units, and safe display/export method; the approved cleaning/check protocol per model.
6. Caregiver identity/contact and communication permissions; interpreter workflow; approved reminder channel and provider agreements; what reminder content is safe; who handles undelivered messages.
7. Identifiable history retention, linkage-key custody, de-identification/archive approach, legal hold, deletion timing, and vendor backup deletion behavior.
8. Existing platform module/support cost versus Power Apps configuration and recurring cost; confirm the ten-user count and all indirect costs against the $7,500/year ceiling.

## Discriminating validation ideas (not run)

- Walk a synthetic loan through issue → return → pending clean/check → completed clean and functional check → eligible again. Attempt every bypass path and verify a pending/failed device cannot be loaned.
- Verify the handoff snapshot remains attributable and unchanged; demonstrate an allowed correction as an appended amendment with user, time, reason, prior value, and new value. Repeat with the chosen role matrix.
- At both sites, test online browser use, an outage, the selected downtime method, and later reconciliation; if any native offline profile is enabled, create concurrent conflicting edits, test sync status/error handling, and inspect exactly what data is stored locally and downloaded per role.
- Walk away from the shared waiting-room workstation with a record open. Confirm session lock/timeout, no family data in browser history/downloads/notifications, no exposure in the waiting area, no shared login, and no access after staff sign-out or role revocation.
- With the staff screen-reader user, perform all core tasks with keyboard alone and their usual screen reader; verify field names/units, required errors, offline/sync state, return/clean/check states, confirmation messages, focus order, and that status is named in text/icons as well as color. Test at realistic zoom and resolution.
- Have caregivers with varied reading levels read the reminder; verify plain language, the chosen language/interpreter path, delivery preferences, no diagnosis or sensitive device detail in message preview/lock-screen/voicemail, and a safe response route.
- Verify a source device's field labels/units against manufacturer software/manual and the clinic protocol; have clinicians compare the stored handoff snapshot before reissue.
- Conduct a privacy/security review of identifiers, role permissions, audit logs, export/backups, breach/loss process, vendor BAA scope and subcontractors; approve a retention schedule with local counsel/records officer; test deletion and a held-record exception.
- Build a full six-month cost worksheet that includes 10 user licenses, platform/tenant, initial configuration, training, support, device/MDM needs, backups, communication charges, and routine maintenance.
