# I02 research draft after plan reveal

**Block/arm:** B-02/control · **Case:** I02 · **Method:** M13 v1 · **Stage:** research  
**Draft written:** 2026-10-09 UTC  
**Purpose:** A complete planning recommendation for the six-month loaner-device pilot. This is not an implemented design, legal opinion, purchase quote, or validation report. `discovery.md` and its source map remain the frozen pre-reveal record; they have not been rewritten after seeing the plan.

## Recommendation

Treat this as a small, clinic-owned loan workflow, not a clinical-record migration. First check whether the clinic's current appointment/EHR product already has a supported equipment-loan module under its existing service agreement. If that is unavailable, use a managed, low-code cloud platform for a narrowly scoped loan register. Power Apps with Dataverse is a candidate for a short feasibility prototype: its 10-user Premium list-price subtotal is below the $7,500 annual ceiling, Microsoft lists Power Apps as in scope for its BAA, and the platform has documented mobile offline and accessibility capabilities. Adoption is conditional on all of the following: the clinic verifies the exact purchased license and BAA terms; names an environment owner/maker or budgets an implementation partner; approves the role matrix, backup/retention controls, and breach/loss response; and accepts the actual published workflow with the staff screen-reader user.

For a workstation used in or visible from the waiting room, make the first pilot browser path online-only, behind individual staff credentials on a physically controlled staff workstation, and keep a small protected paper downtime procedure. Power Apps canvas apps in a browser cannot run offline; its Dataverse offline-first mode uses native Power Apps Mobile and stores a local SQLite-based cache on the device. The cache is the app's primary data source. Microsoft says it has no platform-set retention period or automatic expiry while the app/profile remain installed. Setting up offline mode requires an Environment Maker, System Administrator, or System Customizer role. Do not put the patient's fitting/contact data into a shared workstation cache without an approved device-control and offline-data design. If staff must keep entering linked patient data when internet is down, test native managed devices and local cache controls before the pilot, or select a different platform after a separate evidence review. This is a decision gate, not a claim that paper is risk-free (ER11-S01–S03, S19).

Do not self-host or commission a bespoke app for this pilot. Self-hosted asset software shifts updates, database, backups, recovery, and security response to the clinic, which has no systems administrator. Snipe-IT can be a useful device catalog/status registry but is not a complete family-loan workflow. Its hosted product could be explored for device-only records if vendor contract/privacy questions are answered, but it should not hold child/caregiver linkage, fitting details, or reminders unless the exact BAA and data model are approved. If neither a supported existing module nor an accountable Power Platform owner is available, use a clinic-approved minimal paper/records-system process for the pilot and defer purchasing/building until operations and support are funded.

## Design outline for the conditional candidate

### Minimum operational data

The clinic should approve a data dictionary before configuration. The following is a proposed logical model, not a vendor-specific type declaration:

- **Device:** clinic device ID, manufacturer/model family, owning location, current operational state, and link to the model-specific care/check instruction version. Keep serial number only if it is necessary for safe identification and has an approved access scope.
- **Loan:** random internal loan ID; device ID; issue and due dates; issuing/receiving clinician and site; approved caregiver contact destination/preference and interpreter request only where needed; minimal clinic-held chart reference only if needed for reliable handoff. Do not import the patient’s entire appointment record.
- **Fitting snapshot:** manufacturer-defined parameters and source units/types, plus source device/model/software/version if needed to render them faithfully. Require an identified clinician to confirm the snapshot at issue and handoff. Do not normalize, convert, or infer fitting values without clinical approval.
- **Lifecycle events:** event type; device and loan IDs; timestamp; staff identity; prior/new state; handoff/return condition; and the cleaning and functional-check outcomes. An event record should capture which approved procedure revision was used. A failed check or incomplete cleaning keeps the device unavailable.
- **Reminder event, if used:** scheduled/due date, channel selected from the current contact preference, delivery attempt/result, operator or authorized service, and a safe minimal message template. Do not store message contents beyond what the approved audit/retention policy needs.

Use a local calendar date for the promised return date and unambiguous timestamps for handoff events; select and document the clinic time zone. Have the clinical/device lead approve numeric types, ranges, units, precision, and display for every setting before real settings are entered. Use field-level and record-level permissions only after the clinic determines which staff need each category of information.

### Workflow and safety controls

Recommended state sequence:

`Ready for issue` → `Prepared` → `Loaned` → `Returned: cleaning/check pending` → `Cleaning recorded` → `Functional check passed` → `Ready for issue`.

A device with a failed check, damage, uncertain identity, or incomplete cleaning goes to a nonassignable `Hold/repair/clinical review` state. The workflow must prevent assignment from every not-ready state, not just show a warning. Cleaning and function test are separate evidence items. An authorized staff member records each event; the application must preserve who/when and reject blank required outcomes. A manual override, if the clinic chooses to allow one, needs an explicit reason, named authority, immutable audit trail, and separate validation; do not add override as a convenience default.

After handoff, lock the recorded snapshot as the state that accompanied that loan. Later note changes create an attributable amendment; the clinical governance decision defines allowed roles and any approval step. Display current status in plain text and an additional icon/cue, never only by color. Reminders should be understandable by a caregiver without clinical jargon, honor channel/time/language preference, and avoid exposing a child's diagnosis or fitting details in previews.

### Workstation, downtime, and privacy shape

The waiting-room workstation must be treated as potentially observable by families. Use an individual staff identity, no generic shared login, least-necessary record views, short idle lock, sign-out at handoff, no saved/downloaded patient exports, and a screen angle/physical boundary that prevents waiting-room viewing. Confirm these controls on the actual workstation; software session security does not replace room layout.

For a browser-first cloud workflow, use a protected, numbered paper downtime packet only if clinic operations approve it. At both sites, the packet should capture the minimal active-loan change, loan ID, device ID, operator, and local time; keep fitting detail in the approved clinical source unless needed to deliver care. Store it in a locked location and reconcile each item online by authorized staff, preserving both the paper action and the final reconciled event. Define how two-site conflicts are resolved and who may resolve fitting-setting disagreements. Do not send reminders while offline unless a separate approved offline process exists. If this is not workable, stop and test a managed native offline workflow before loading data.

### Privacy, vendors, retention

The brief suggests a U.S. setting through its currency but does not establish jurisdiction or whether the clinic is a HIPAA covered entity. Confirm both. If applicable PHI is held in a cloud service on the clinic's behalf, verify the precise product/feature is in the vendor's current BAA scope, enter the required BAA, review subprocessors/retention/deletion and service terms, and perform the clinic's own risk analysis before production data. Microsoft's current documentation includes Power Apps in its HIPAA BAA scope but makes clear that the service/BAA does not itself make the clinic compliant (ER11-S06, S07). Snipe-IT's public pricing page says nothing about a BAA (ER11-S13), so its PHI suitability is unknown. Do not infer “not covered” from silence; ask the vendor, and keep patient-linked data out unless verified.

Write a role matrix for the eight clinicians and two reception staff and periodically review it. HHS role-based access guidance must be applied with the treatment-purpose exception in mind; it is not a reason to block a clinician who needs the information for treatment (ER11-S08). Limit downloaded/local data and exports; plan backup, restore, termination, breach response, and deletion including backups. Choose an identifiable-history schedule only after identifying all applicable law, record classification, data rights, minor rules, legal holds, and contracts. If safe and lawful, a later de-identification/archive policy may separate operational history from direct identifiers; the clinic must approve how/when the re-identification key is destroyed.

## Decisions for clinic leadership before live data

1. Confirm clinic jurisdiction, covered-entity status, and who signs off legal/records/privacy questions.
2. Name the clinical governance owner for fitting-note changes after device handoff. Decide editor, approver, correction reason, emergency correction, and audit reviewer; preserve old snapshots regardless.
3. Approve the categories each clinical/reception role may view, edit, export, and act on; decide whether reception can see fitting settings.
4. Approve the minimum caregiver identity/contact and interpreter fields, how contact preferences are obtained/updated, the guardian/child communication authority, languages, and reminder wording/channel. Decide whether reminders are manual in the pilot.
5. Approve device-by-device cleaning and functional-check instructions with the audiology/device lead and infection-control lead; define failed-check/quarantine action.
6. Decide whether offline entry is required at both locations or a protected paper procedure is sufficient. If native offline is required, fund/manage individual devices and define cache, lock, loss, revocation, conflict, and synchronization rules.
7. Adopt a retention schedule for identifiable loan history, linkage keys, audit events, paper packets, and vendor backups only after local requirements and records classification are known.
8. Confirm whether an existing EHR/appointment-system module meets the need, the 10-user license count, service owner, yearly total cost, and funding for configuration, training, support, backup, devices, and communication.
9. Keep any appointment-system integration explicitly out of year one. Reconsider it only through a later scoped approval and vendor/security review.
