## Case frame and limits

H01 is a six-person, family-owned funeral home serving nearby communities. Staff coordinate rooms, transportation, officiants/providers, service setup, and handoffs. A service can change after relatives arrive from different time zones. The current wall calendar and individual notebooks leave uncertainty about what is confirmed. The owner has a $10,000 first-year ceiling, wants a private planning aid rather than a public directory, needs an independently retainable export, and wants the process to feel calm and discreet rather than like a high-volume event system. Staff need role-limited access to family contact details, preferences, faith or cultural requests, payment arrangements, and personal planning notes. One employee primarily uses a keyboard; families may need translated information. Severe weather sometimes interrupts internet, although most work happens from one office.

The owner has not decided whether relatives may edit a shared schedule, how long personal notes remain accessible, or which service types need distinct workflows. Research should preserve those choices rather than make them. The recommended pilot is about scheduling and task handoffs. It does not claim that any product provides production-grade security, regulatory compliance, safe offline conflict resolution, complete disaster recovery, or a complete restore.

## Findings that shape a pilot

### Scheduling and handoffs

Passare documents event participants and their availability, participant change notifications, service-provider/location fields, event types, and case tasks. These align with H01's coordination problem, but do not establish hard collision prevention, atomic resource reservation, simultaneous-edit handling, or recovery of a formerly confirmed service. A pilot configuration should define proposed, held, confirmed, changed, and cancelled states; one named change owner; local time zone; and a visible change trail. Those are test questions and design suggestions, not claims that Passare provides them.

Nextcloud adds a useful, bounded alternative for rooms: its Server 35 CalDAV manual says room/resource booking can enforce one-at-a-time use when an app supplies the backend. The backend controls resource definition and newly added/updated rooms may appear after a delay. This supports a plausible room-conflict mechanism but does not demonstrate the selected backend, override behavior, vehicle booking, officiant/provider exclusivity, or funeral-specific handoffs. Test overlapping room bookings against the installed app. Keep vehicle and staff/provider conflict checks separate.

A common service view should expose only information needed to coordinate: opaque service key, date/time and explicit time zone, confirmation state, location/resource, transportation/provider status, assigned staff, next task/owner, and last-change actor/time. Do not put payments, broad free-form notes, family contact lists, or sensitive faith/cultural details in a widely shared title or calendar. Staff may need some of these details in a protected case record; the owner should decide access field by field.

### Privacy, staff access, and family access

Passare's staff-role support page describes area-level permissions and read-only options, including examples for financial and case-category access. It does not publish a complete matrix for H01's family contact, preference, faith/cultural, payment, and private-note fields. Its event-level “private” setting should not be assumed to control staff access. The Calendar support page says an event popover may show decedent information, location, participants, notes, and associated events; it also documents personal-calendar connection and print. Passare's dated July 7, 2026 release note says Case ID is added to case-event notifications and participants are included in event exports. These surfaces could reveal information not visible in a calendar list, so test them all. ([PA02](sources/evidence.md#pa02--calendar-support), [PA04](sources/evidence.md#pa04--staff-roles), [PA11](sources/evidence.md#pa11--2026-release-notes))

Passare Planning Center is an invited family area, not a public directory. Its support pages describe family-facing planning features and an internet requirement. The family roles are Viewer, Contributor, and Editor; Editor is the default, has full Planning Center access, and at least one Editor is required per case. Scheduled events can appear for family viewing, but the reviewed sources do not establish relatives' right to edit the staff calendar. Keep family access disabled until the owner chooses. If the owner enables it, test each family role's exact data and action scope, invitation/revocation, notification, and recovery behavior; do not infer schedule editing from event viewing or from the portal's Editor requirement. ([PA06](sources/evidence.md#pa06--planning-center-and-internet), [PA07](sources/evidence.md#pa07--planning-center-roles), [PA08](sources/evidence.md#pa08--family-event-viewing))

Baserow's documentation says restricted views can filter rows and fields and constrain exports, but also warns that a user with broader table Editor access can enter another unrestricted view. That is a concrete configuration risk: test the full workspace/database/table/view hierarchy, not only a screen that hides data. Nextcloud read/write calendar rights govern calendar operations, not case fields. For every product, test synthetic records for two unrelated families, six operating roles, an administrative account, and role changes. Include list/search, direct links, event popovers, connected calendar/subscription output, print, mobile/web, notifications, and exports. ([BR02](sources/evidence.md#br02--restricted-view-permissions), [NC01](sources/evidence.md#nc01--calendar-user-manual-server-35))

### Outage behavior and independent export

Passare says family Planning Center requires internet; reviewed public pages did not establish a staff offline mode. Nextcloud's 10,000-entry CalDAV change-token default and differential synchronization for offline clients such as Thunderbird support a particular client-sync mechanism, not browser editing, safe multi-device conflict merges, or a complete outage process. If the Nextcloud server is self-hosted on the office LAN, continued access during a WAN outage is an architecture possibility only while server, LAN, power, and local client access remain available; it was not tested.

Passare documents category-based case exports to separate CSV files, including event information, payments, and goods/services. Its 2026 note about participants in event exports is useful, but does not establish all notes, task comments, attachments, role state, or audit history. Nextcloud documents `.ics` export and allows an administrator to disable event export; `.ics` is calendar data, not a case archive. During a pilot, define the minimum retention package, export representative data, and open it outside the service. Record relationships, IDs, time zones, participants/resources, tasks, attachments, changes, and omissions. ([PA10](sources/evidence.md#pa10--case-export), [PA11](sources/evidence.md#pa11--2026-release-notes), [NC01](sources/evidence.md#nc01--calendar-user-manual-server-35), [NC02](sources/evidence.md#nc02--calendarcaldav-administration-manual-server-35))

A secured read-only operational snapshot for the next 48 hours before a forecast storm is an optional lead from the discovery work, not a brief requirement or proven feature. Validate whether 48 hours covers this home's realistic disruption, limit fields, name who can access it, and set expiry/disposal. If connectivity or power fails, a single numbered paper reconciliation log and one named coordinator may be safer than two writable schedules. Test WAN interruption with office LAN/power available separately from local LAN/power loss.

### Retention defaults and unresolved policy

P6 remains an owner decision. The current Nextcloud Server 35 admin manual documents a default 30-day trash-bin purge for calendars, events, and tasks, configurable through `calendarRetentionObligation`. Baserow's current Advanced pricing page lists 180 days of row-change history. These are different product mechanisms and data types; neither is the owner's rule for personal planning notes, attachments, deleted records, backups, exports, audit data, or legal holds. Passare's relevant note/history/deletion behavior remains unverified in this source set. The owner and vendor must separately set and demonstrate retention for active notes, deleted objects, change history, attachments, exports, backups, and legal holds. The type-specific defaults are mutable, so recheck them before procurement. ([NC02](sources/evidence.md#nc02--calendarcaldav-administration-manual-server-35), [BR01](sources/evidence.md#br01--hosted-pricing))

### Issue, fix, release, and code evolution

Nextcloud Calendar issue #2572 described an import-picker problem for a mixed event/task file in an old Calendar 2.0.4 / Server 19.0.2 context. PR #7876 merged on June 4, 2026 as immutable commit `02fb072fee39e83746b09582481f63c87505f14b`; it made unsupported destination calendars visible but disabled with an explanation. The picker change clarified why a destination was ineligible; it did not itself add support for the missing components. The mutable Calendar 6.6.0 changelog dated July 28, 2026 records the supported-component and picker updates closing #2572. This is a historical import compatibility and UI-evolution example, not evidence of a current defect or funeral-workflow readiness. Validate the actual export/import components for any selected system. ([NC04](sources/evidence.md#nc04--calendar-issue-2572)–[NC07](sources/evidence.md#nc07--calendar-changelog))

A second immutable Nextcloud Calendar code commit, `ea4d703419d9a7b7fc467071c760d7541af7af14`, reads create, modify, and delete ACL capabilities separately and has corresponding tests. This is useful evidence that a calendar's rights are not always one read/write Boolean; it is not a case-field privacy control. ([NC08](sources/evidence.md#nc08--calendar-acl-code-commit))
