## Decision summary

Run a short, synthetic-data comparison of (1) a funeral-specific Passare demonstration and (2) a version-supported Nextcloud Calendar plus Calendar Resource Management pilot. These are evaluation candidates, not product selections. No product has been installed or tested. Passare's vendor pages describe a closer funeral workflow, but its current price, role granularity, outage behavior, and complete export remain unknown. The earlier Passare Manage page read is historical: the critic's re-open of the exact URL returned 404, so its calendar, notes/checklists, and permissions claims need a current demonstration. [S01–S02]

Nextcloud offers a plausible staff schedule and resource-booking path, but the observed app listing reaches server 35 while the consulted Calendar manual is edition 36. The exact compatible, supported server/app pair is not established. More significantly, upstream issue #196 reports that a resource booking linked to a private event can expose event details to resource-calendar viewers. Do not rely on the ordinary private-event flag to protect linked resource bookings. A comment in that issue describes a narrower “show only as busy” setup using a real user-owned calendar shared from that account; that is a separate configuration to test only if it fits the chosen stack, not a general fix for Calendar Resource Management. Keep real family data out of the pilot until the approved role matrix passes synthetic-user tests. [S03–S07]

A central office-LAN authority is a useful outage hypothesis because most work occurs in one office. It is an inference, contingent on local network, server, power, and name resolution surviving a WAN outage. A custom PouchDB/CouchDB client could support local work, but replication retry does not arbitrate competing room or vehicle reservations; that option adds application and maintenance work and is not a turnkey small pilot. Keep the existing paper schedule as a controlled fallback for local server/LAN/power loss. Defer LibreBooking from deployment consideration until its announced 2026-10-13 high-impact security release is public and reviewed. This deferral is due to the unresolved announcement, not because v7.0.0 remains affected by the earlier patched issue. [S09–S16]

Require a written first-year total within the owner's $10,000 ceiling before procurement. Include setup, six staff, one location, hosting or hardware, support, training, backup, updates, storage, export, taxes, and renewal terms. No quote or total cost was observed. No family editing, retention period, or service-type workflow split is decided here.

## Context and retained constraints

The setting is a family-owned funeral home serving several nearby communities. Six staff share duties. A service can change after relatives arrive from different time zones. Staff coordinate rooms, transportation, staff/officiant availability, service setup, and memorial materials, currently using a wall calendar and individual notebooks. A confirmed room, vehicle, or officiant is not always clear. The owner wants a private planning aid, not a public directory; family contacts, preferences, faith or cultural requests, and payment arrangements must not be visible to every staff member by default. Some family information may need translation, and one staff member primarily uses a keyboard. Severe weather can interrupt internet access, though most work occurs in one office. The first-year ceiling is $10,000. The requested pilot is short and covers service scheduling and task handoffs, with an export retainable independently.

The owner has not decided whether relatives may edit a shared schedule, how long personal planning notes remain accessible, or whether different service types need separate workflows. The process should feel calm and discreet, not like a high-volume event system. These are preserved constraints and open decisions, not inferred product features or decisions.

## Evidence-backed behavior and limits

- **Passare (S01–S02):** vendor-authored material presents a funeral-specific case/task/service and family-collaboration lead. No price, exact role/field matrix, outage contract, or complete export was found. The critic's re-open of `/manage` returned 404, so its earlier specific calendar/notes/permissions statements require current vendor confirmation. Vendor claims are not product tests.
- **Nextcloud resource model (S03–S04):** the Calendar Resource Management v0.12.2 README is pinned to commit `d017e31aab5c59d401ad87cd2cd5579e95c0d7e4`. It describes building → floor/story → room plus general resources and vehicles, group restrictions, required parent IDs, unique UID/email values for resources, and background-job publication to Calendar. Omitted boolean flags default to false. The consulted README names `capacity`, vehicle `range`, and `seating-capacity` without establishing their numeric types or units; do not infer a mileage unit or business definition. The app-store stable table lists 0.12.2 for server 34 and 35, with 35 the highest row shown; absence of a 36 row is not proof of incompatibility.
- **Calendar permissions and export (S05–S06):** the edition 36 Calendar manual describes rejection of overlapping resource bookings, admin-configured resource back ends, read-only or write calendar sharing, and write access as create/edit/delete. It describes event `.ics` export. The edition 35 admin manual says `calendar:export` defaults to iCalendar with xCal/jCal alternatives; it documents import validation modes 0/1/2 and error modes where 0 continues and 1 stops on first error. This is not evidence that tasks, cases, attachments, notes, or audit history are included in an export.
- **Nextcloud resource privacy (S07):** the issue is an open feature request. Its reporter says a resource event created from a private event appears in the resource calendar. A comment describes a narrower user-owned-calendar setup with “show only as busy.” This is issue/comment evidence, not a code audit, support guarantee, or exact-pair test. Reviser reopened the exact URL and found the issue open and the comment at the recorded locator. No behavior was executed.
- **Nextcloud task integration (S08):** the live Calendar frontend README mentions Tasks/Deck items in Calendar, but it is mutable and does not establish assignment, escalation, handoff workflow, or behavior in the selected release.
- **LibreBooking security evolution (S09–S11):** GHSA-3356-vjx2-5pg8 names versions `<7.0.0` as affected by the legacy migration path and names 7.0.0 as patched; the advisory describes endpoint/access and attacker-controlled database connectivity conditions. The pinned 7.0.0 release is dated 2026-10-06 and removes web migration (commit `422b4b0223335d18c7d5f005ffcdd93719575c20`). The mutable `develop` README observed Oct 9 announced another high-impact security release for Oct 13. This is a dated adoption condition, not a complete security review.
- **PouchDB offline behavior (S12–S16):** current mutable project guides say live replication stops after connection loss unless retry is configured; retry mode exposes paused/active events. Concurrent offline edits of one document may leave competing revisions while a deterministic winner is selected. Different resource documents may still encode incompatible overlapping holds. Release 9.0.0, commit `b2882c1bd006bc2aaeb6fdc83ea632c9c923b9b1`, changes `.find()`'s default limit to 25. Historical issue #3179 records a regression/fix timeline; release 3.3.1 names a live replication fix. This history motivates regression tests but is not a current 9.0.0 defect claim.
- **Accessibility (S17):** WCAG 2.2 Recommendation criteria 2.1.1, 2.1.2, and 2.4.3 provide proposed keyboard/focus checks. No candidate conformance was assessed.

## Validation plan — proposed, none executed

1. **Exact versions and support:** identify currently supported candidate releases and a compatible server/app pair. Install only in a qualified test environment. Record build identifiers, enabled apps, permissions, backup route, and configuration. Do not treat a missing catalog row as proof of incompatibility.
2. **Role privacy and inference:** create synthetic records for contact, preferences, faith/cultural instructions, payment arrangements, and a neutral pseudonymous key. Map all six staff identities to owner-approved roles. Inspect event titles/descriptions/attendees, resource calendars, tasks, notifications, search, exports, and relevant browser/download or DAV/mobile paths. Ask each role whether permitted date/time, room, vehicle, officiant, or community lets them identify the family or infer a sensitive request. Pass only if visible facts and inference risk match the owner's approved matrix. For Nextcloud, reproduce a private source event linked to room/vehicle and inspect what resource viewers see. If testing the user-owned-calendar workaround, configure the real account-owned resource calendar and “show only as busy” sharing exactly as described, then repeat all relevant paths. Any protected-detail exposure fails. No real family data until remediation and retest.
3. **Booking, changes, and transport feasibility:** have two staff request overlapping times for one room and one vehicle. Require one clear confirmation, a visible pending/rejected state for the other, and an assigned resolver. Separately schedule back-to-back services in different communities using owner-approved travel, loading/setup, service, return, and turnaround intervals. Record whether the system models these or whether explicit buffer holds and manual reconfirmation are needed. Include staff/officiant availability and a service-time change after relatives arrive; verify old-slot release, new-slot confirmation, handoff owner, timezone conversion, DST, and notifications. Repeat with disconnected clients; no local hold counts as confirmed until the authority rechecks it.
4. **WAN and local failure:** disconnect only the WAN while office LAN/server/power remain up; repeat lookup, create/update, handoff, and export; restore connectivity and check for one authoritative schedule. Separately stop/isolate local server, LAN, or power and verify the designated fallback and reconciliation process without duplicate confirmations. Record remote-family limits during WAN loss.
5. **Independent export and restore:** agree roles and data scope first. Export events, status, task owner/due/completion, minimum case link, and only owner-approved notes/files to an owner-controlled location outside the product. Inspect for completeness and privacy leakage. Restore into a clean test account/system and compare counts, identifiers, local times/time zones, task states, attachments/notes, and omissions. For Nextcloud CLI import, explicitly choose strict validation/error behavior rather than relying on defaults. Pass only if the copy is independently usable and restore time meets the owner's requirement.
6. **Keyboard and discreet use:** have the keyboard-first staff member complete search, booking, conflict resolution, change, handoff, and export without a mouse. Record focus order/visibility, traps, error/status comprehension, and unavailable functions against WCAG criteria. With synthetic entries, ask staff whether today's service and change view is calm and understandable without public-directory search, high-volume event metrics, or unnecessarily loud notices.
7. **Language and family materials:** ask families which languages and materials are needed. Test accented/non-Latin names, date/time format, line wrapping, print/share outputs, and human-reviewed family text. Evaluate UI translation separately from translated materials and staff-only notes.
8. **Written cost and vendor evidence:** obtain written first-year and renewal totals for six staff/one location, including setup, subscription, hosting/hardware, support, training, storage, export, backup, updates, taxes, and renewal. Demonstrate the same synthetic role/privacy, family-access, travel, outage, and exit-export scenarios. Do not procure above the $10,000 ceiling unless the owner changes it.
