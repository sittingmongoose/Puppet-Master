# H01 independent discovery (pre-reveal)

**Stage:** ER11 C-01 / treatment / research. **Written before plan reveal:** 2026-10-09 UTC. The exact assignment and H01 brief were read. No case plan, parent, counterpart, evaluator, history, or predecessor material was read. The source inventory below is independently selected from public vendor, project, standards, release, and issue records. Source IDs are stable; see [the source index](sources/index.md) and [source-map.json](source-map.json). Observations are bounded to public material accessed on 2026-10-09 and are not a security, accessibility, procurement, or implementation audit.

## Decision context from the brief

This is a six-person, one-office pilot for scheduling services and handing off tasks. The coordination problem spans several shared, conflict-prone resources (at least rooms, vehicles, officiants), while names/contact details, service preferences, faith/cultural requests, and payment arrangements need limited visibility. Internet loss is an operational condition; an authoritative export must remain available outside the chosen product. Families editing the schedule, personal-note retention, and workflow boundaries are intentionally unresolved. The design should therefore distinguish the shared operational schedule from sensitive case details, keep family access out of the pilot until decided, and avoid treating an offline cache as a confirmed resource booking.

## Independently found approaches

### A. Funeral-specific case system: Passare

Passare describes an all-in-one funeral-home platform with case management, staff/team calendar, checklists/notes, user permission settings, and online family collaboration/Planning Center. Its Manage page also advertises reminders, reports, and collaboration across users/devices. This matches the funeral-specific terminology and handoff/family collaboration needs more directly than generic room schedulers ([S01–S02]).

This is a vendor lead for a time-boxed demo and quote, not a validated selection. Public pages do not state a first-year price, outage/offline behavior, whether its permission model can separately protect contact, faith/cultural, and payment fields from different staff, what external export contains, or whether family access can be read-only and scoped to a single service. Treat performance and security claims as vendor statements. Ask for a six-staff/one-location, one-year total (setup, training, support, storage, export, tax, renewal), role-by-role screen share using synthetic records, outage workflow, and a full data export before evaluating the $10,000 ceiling.

### B. Modular staff groupware and resource booking: Nextcloud Calendar + Calendar Resource Management

The resource-management app is a Nextcloud CalDAV back end for buildings/floors, rooms, general resources, and vehicles. Its CLI configuration includes capacity, room type, accessibility flags, vehicle type/make/model, seating capacity, unique UID/email values, and restrictions to groups; omitted boolean flags default `false`. It requires parent entities before child resources and a configured background job to publish them to Calendar ([S03–S05]). The current stable app-store row reports version 0.12.2 for Nextcloud 35 and 34; Nextcloud 36 is the current manual edition in the consulted docs, so compatibility of this exact extension with 36 is unverified. A conservative experiment would pin a currently supported, app-store-listed server/app pair rather than infer cross-version compatibility.

Nextcloud Calendar documents room/resource availability and rejection of overlapping bookings. Its sharing model is calendar-level: read-only or write; write enables creating, editing, and deleting events. Its event calendar is therefore useful for the visible operations schedule, but does not establish fine-grained field-level case permissions. Calendar export supports independent iCalendar `.ics`; the server CLI also exports iCalendar by default and xCal/jCal when requested. An `.ics` schedule is not automatically a complete case/task export, nor proof that attachments, notes, or audit history are included ([S05–S06]).

A material limitation is documented in open upstream issue #196: adding a resource to a private event exposes the event in the resource calendar to users who can see that resource calendar. The requested “show busy only” privacy behavior is a feature request, not an established capability ([S07]). For this brief, never put family name/contact, faith/cultural requests, or payment information in any calendar/resource event shared broadly. If a pilot uses this stack, separate restricted case notes from the general schedule and use a neutral operational label; verify what a non-authorized staff account can see in both event and resource views before entering real data. Do not rely on the “private” checkbox to hide linked resource-event details.

Tasks and Deck cards can appear in Calendar, but that integration does not itself prove assignment, escalation, or granular permissions. A pilot should observe a real handoff cycle (assigned owner, due point, completion, reassignment, service change) and check whether the selected task surface supports it without duplicating sensitive notes ([S08]).

### C. Purpose-built resource booking: LibreBooking

LibreBooking is an open-source resource-reservation system and is a relevant direct alternative for room/vehicle/officiant contention, but the read evidence did not establish funeral-specific case workflow or privacy behavior. Its security advisory GHSA-3356-vjx2-5pg8 says releases before 7.0.0 permit unauthenticated administrator takeover through a legacy migration endpoint; v7.0.0 removes the live migration and makes that endpoint inert. The project released 7.0.0 on 2026-10-06. As of this research, its repository also warns that a further high-impact security release is scheduled for 2026-10-13, with details withheld until release. The maintainer says fixes are not backported ([S09–S11]).

This is a meaningful issue/fix/release chain and a reason to defer any deployment of LibreBooking until the later security release is public, reviewed, installed, and its deployment verified. An open-source license does not remove patching, backups, exposure, or support costs. A README/security-policy version discrepancy was visible during discovery; prefer the dated GHSA and current release evidence, and re-check upstream before any adoption.

### D. Office-local authority versus per-device offline replication

Because most work occurs in one office, an on-premise or office-LAN authoritative server is a materially different outage strategy from a cloud-only service or an offline-first app on each device. **Inference to validate:** if the local LAN, power, DNS, and server remain available while only the internet uplink fails, a browser client reaching that server locally could continue to read/write the same authoritative schedule; it will not give remote families access, and it will not survive a LAN/server/power outage. Confirm with an actual WAN-disconnect trial before relying on it.

A custom PouchDB/CouchDB client is another path for disconnected devices. PouchDB replication retries after dropped connections when `live:true,retry:true` is configured, but it does not eliminate conflict decisions: concurrent same-document writes create competing revisions and the deterministic winner is arbitrary unless the application surfaces and resolves the other branch. Separate offline reservation documents can also encode two competing resource holds without a same-document conflict. Therefore an offline-first pilot must mark new holds unconfirmed until reconnected and atomically checked against authoritative reservations; connectivity restoration alone cannot safely confirm room or vehicle availability. PouchDB 9.0.0 additionally changed `.find()` to default to a 25-document result limit, a backwards-incompatible query default, so any custom list/search would need explicit tested pagination/limits ([S12–S16]).

This approach is technically interesting but not a turnkey pilot. It adds app development, privacy/security design, conflict handling, sync monitoring, device loss and backup policy, and accessibility/localization work. With no build/runtime or cost data, its first-year fit is unknown and should not be presumed below $10,000.

## Cross-cutting findings and limits

- **Discreet by construction:** show only date/time, operational resource, status, and a neutral service key to broadly authorized staff. Store personal contact, preferences, cultural/faith instructions, and payment arrangements in separately permissioned case material. Test what each of the six staff roles can see; verify group membership and external export paths. These are proposed controls inferred from the brief and observed product permissions, not completed configuration.
- **Family participation:** no family account or shared editable calendar in the pilot unless the owner chooses a specific access model, fields, authentication route, and revocation approach. “Read-only” still exposes content; a public calendar link is not a confidential family view.
- **Retention/workflow decisions:** do not invent a retention interval for personal notes or a required funeral workflow taxonomy. Keep notes separable from the time/resource record so the owner can choose the period and test deletion/export. Begin with only the service-scheduling and task-handoff slice named in the brief; list optional workflows without treating them as required pilot modules.
- **Keyboard and language:** WCAG 2.2 SC 2.1.1 requires functionality to be keyboard-operable and SC 2.4.3 addresses meaningful focus sequence ([S17]). This is a validation target, not proof that any candidate conforms. Have the keyboard-first staff member complete the core flow using only keyboard, including conflict correction and export. Ask families which translated information/languages are needed; distinguish a translated product interface from translated family materials, consented human review, and correctly rendered names/characters.
- **Export and continuity:** define an export contract before pilot: which schedule, task, and case fields; format; who can run it; where independent copies are held; frequency; restore/round-trip procedure. Test that no hidden/private fields leak into a broadly stored export. A standards-compliant `.ics` export is useful for events but not sufficient evidence of full data portability.
- **Cost and service fit:** only Passare is funeral-specific among the leads; pricing and support terms were not public on inspected pages. Open-source licensing for the other paths does not cost out hosting, hardware, secure access, training, backup, patching, translation, and any implementation. No vendor quote or cost fit has been verified; price is `null`/unknown, not zero.
- **Evidence boundaries:** no system was installed, run, configured, or accessibility-tested. All validation below is proposed. Public repository pages and vendor feature pages are mutable; their exact versions, commits where available, issue/release state, and observation time are recorded in `source-map.json`. Current compatibility, security posture, prices, complete export coverage, and offline behavior must be rechecked against a selected exact release before implementation.

## Proposed discriminating checks (not executed)

1. **Role/privacy walkthrough:** use synthetic cases and six role accounts; inspect schedule, resource view, task list, notifications, search, export, and browser/download artifacts. For Nextcloud, specifically reproduce issue #196 with a private event plus a room/vehicle resource. Fail if an unauthorized role sees any protected field or if an event title reveals identity; do not enter real data until this passes.
2. **Contention and change test:** two staff attempt the same room and vehicle at overlapping times; verify exactly one confirmed booking, explainable rejection, correct time-zone display around DST, and one clear reschedule/handoff after relatives arrive. Repeat after two disconnected clients each draft a hold. A PouchDB/custom hold must remain pending until authoritative arbitration.
3. **WAN outage:** disconnect only the internet uplink while preserving local office network; complete lookup, scheduling, handoff, and export. Then separately simulate local server/LAN loss and document the fallback (e.g. controlled paper schedule). Record what becomes unavailable and how changes reconcile.
4. **Export/restore:** export the agreed scope to a separate owner-controlled destination, inspect its contents for both completeness and privacy, restore/import into a clean test, and compare counts, identifiers, time zones, task state, and attachments/notes. The tested scope must be named; `.ics` alone is not a pass for tasks/cases.
5. **Keyboard and calmness:** keyboard-only staff member books, changes, assigns, and exports a synthetic service; no mouse-only function, focus trap, lost focus, or unlabeled conflict state. Ask whether status language and notification frequency feel discreet and understandable; record their corrections.
6. **Language:** use selected family language samples and accented/non-Latin names; check date/time ambiguity and rendering. Have a qualified human reviewer confirm family-facing translation before use.
7. **Procurement gate:** get a written first-year and renewal quote for six users/one location, data-export statement, patch/support window, outage behavior, permissions walk-through, and family-access controls; compare total first-year cost to $10,000, not just subscription/licence.
