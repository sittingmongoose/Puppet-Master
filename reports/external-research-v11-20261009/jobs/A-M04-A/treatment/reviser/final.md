# Maker-inventory: recommendation and exact thin-plan comparison

**Case:** S04, maker-inventory  
**Stage:** ER11 A-M04-A / treatment / reviser  
**Scope:** Small community makerspace with two sites, unreliable Wi-Fi, tool reservations and custody, consumables, repairs, and inconsistent legacy spreadsheets. This is a design recommendation and evidence review, not a product audit, implementation, deployment plan, or production-assurance claim.

## Recommendation

Keep a shared calendar as the member and staff interface, but treat it as a view and request surface. A reservation becomes globally confirmed only after one authoritative write checks the target tool or capacity pool and atomically rejects an overlapping active commitment. Keep reservations, physical checkout/return, maintenance availability, consumable quantities, and site transfers as separate records and state transitions.

For the first design pass, a small central application and transactional store is a leading hypothesis, not an established lowest-risk answer. It may suit the brief’s wish to avoid complex hosting and can make cross-site confirmation understandable, but it may be unreachable during the actual Wi-Fi failure. PostgreSQL is one documented way to enforce interval overlap in storage; another single-authority store can work if its tested transaction protects the complete write invariant. First establish what link fails, whether offline checkout is required, who can operate and back up the service, and which tools are shared between sites.

If members or staff must continue work during disconnection, distinguish local or queued activity from a globally confirmed reservation. Possible policies include one authorized offline staff writer with a journal, exclusive per-site inventory quotas, or time-bounded exclusive leases with explicit expiry and fencing rules. A replicated offline-first design such as CouchDB can preserve independent local writes, but replication conflict handling does not itself prevent two offline reservation records from promising the same physical tool. Do not use device timestamps as proof of priority or exclusivity.

No vendor, database, language, hosting topology, approval workflow, price, support contract, or offline guarantee can be selected from the supplied brief alone.

## Product and mechanism findings

1. **MyTurn — lending-library workflow.** The vendor describes check-in/out, common USB/Bluetooth barcode scanning, multiple locations and transfers, reminders, and reservations. This is a close workflow comparator for lending tools. A PG&E-hosted program was previously observed to use member requests followed by staff approval, a stated two-day lead time, and staffed pickup/drop-off appointments. On the critic’s later review the program page returned HTTP 403, so that detail remains a single scoped operational example recorded by the earlier source review; it was not independently re-confirmed here. It is not a universal MyTurn default. The inspected pages do not establish atomic overlap protection, offline operation, repairs/consumables coverage, migration fidelity, current price, or contract terms. (S01–S02)
2. **Fabman — makerspace member and machine workflow.** The vendor describes machine/room/desk bookings alongside memberships, training/access privileges, machine integration, and optional billing tied to booking or use. Its stable 1.2.10 API index lists a Booking resource. This is worth comparing if training eligibility and machine access matter. The inspected material does not establish concurrent booking guarantees, offline behavior, inventory import, consumable tracking, hosting, or cost. (S03)
3. **LibreBooking — reservation-focused alternative.** The project README describes multi-resource bookings and waitlists, role-based access, quotas/credits, reporting, and calendar integration. Manual deployment calls for PHP, Apache, and MySQL or MariaDB; Docker is also documented. It may merit a bounded reservation-workflow fit screen, not an assumption that it replaces tool custody, repair tracking, consumables, or messy CSV migration. As independently checked on 2026-10-09, the mutable develop-branch README announced a high-impact security release planned for 2026-10-13, without disclosing the issue until release. The same README warned that versions before 7.0.0 had a critical unauthenticated administrator-takeover vulnerability; the release page identifies v7.0.0 at short commit 422b4b0, dated 2026-10-06. The announced issue’s scope or effect on v7.0.0 was not stated, and none is inferred. Do a fresh security and release review before any trial or deployment. (S10)
4. **Snipe-IT — asset identity and current custody.** Its official guide uses unique asset tags, check-in/out, status classes, and distinct accessory/component/consumable concepts. A checked-out asset cannot be assigned to another person until it is returned. This informs serialized identity and custody, not future time-slot availability or a complete maker maintenance process. Its importer documentation illustrates product-specific limits: custom fields must already exist and match names; the history importer expects existing users/assets and particular columns, ignores extra history fields, and expects ascending dates. Treat these as migration warnings, not universal importer requirements. (S04–S05)
5. **InvenTree — location-scoped quantity and units.** Its moving stable/latest documentation distinguishes in-stock, allocated, and available quantity, with available defined as in-stock minus allocated. Stock is location-specific; allocation is a commitment, not physical removal until consumption. Its Pint-backed unit conversion supports compatible units and custom units, with case-sensitive symbols and common piece units. This is a useful consumables model, but general unit machinery has vocabulary and setup costs and says nothing about calendar reservations. (S06)
6. **PostgreSQL — authoritative interval enforcement.** PostgreSQL 18 documents exclusion constraints for non-overlapping ranges. Its example uses a GiST overlap operator and demonstrates combining resource equality with range overlap after installing btree_gist. The example’s tsrange is a range of timestamp without time zone; the same documentation defines tstzrange for timestamp with time zone. PostgreSQL’s date/time documentation says timestamp-with-time-zone input is converted to UTC, and the originally stated time zone is not retained. This strengthens the case for an explicit site-time and instant policy, especially around daylight-saving transitions and two locations. Choose and test the representation; do not copy the tsrange example without deciding how member-entered local times become instants and how the site zone is retained for display. A constraint only protects the rules actually encoded, including which reservation states block and what counts as the same resource. (S08, S11)
7. **CouchDB — offline-first replication alternative.** The official 3.5 documentation describes incremental replication and conflict-preserving replicas that may continue independently while disconnected. Concurrent edits can require application-specific merge/resolution. This is a genuine availability-oriented alternative, but a deterministic document winner or preserved conflict is not a global scheduling invariant. Use it only with provisional status, exclusive site/resource authority, quotas, or another explicit conflict policy. (S09)

These products and mechanisms represent different approaches, not a ranking: lending-library loans and approval; member eligibility plus machine booking; current-custodian asset assignment; location-scoped quantity allocation; a reservation scheduler; one authoritative transactional database; and offline replicated data. Vendor pages establish described features only. No product trial, pricing/contract review, security audit, availability test, or fit assessment was performed.

## Domain model and policy

Keep independently addressable facts rather than overloading one status or number:

- **Reusable equipment:** stable ID or barcode, descriptive name/category, home and current site/location, condition and availability, and optional service interval or training/access eligibility. Display names are not identity.
- **Reservation:** member, individual serialized tool or bounded-capacity pool, requested start/end, state such as requested/pending, confirmed, cancelled, or expired, and decision actor/time. Only the chosen live confirmed states block another confirmation. A reservation is not a checkout.
- **Checkout and return:** actual custody, handoff and receiving actors, expected and actual return, condition, and late/lost follow-up. Do not infer possession from a future reservation. Check-in is an event; damage can create a separate repair/unavailable state.
- **Repair and maintenance:** typed reason or category, opened/closed time, reporter/owner, unavailable or restricted state, and optional actions, cost, and parts. Keep free text for symptoms and context. A full work-order subsystem is optional; a free-text note alone must not leave a broken tool bookable.
- **Consumables:** part and site/location, base unit and allowed precision, quantity, receipts/count corrections/usage events, and optional allocations. Counted items can use integral “each”; allow compatible fractional units only where the material justifies them. Commit decrements atomically and enforce the chosen lower bound.
- **Site transfer:** dispatch/in-transit and receipt with actor, time, source/destination, and item or quantity. Do not silently change a location and make stock appear at both sites.
- **Operational history:** record reservation, checkout, return, repair, count, adjustment, transfer, import, and conflict-resolution events with event ID, actor or explicitly unknown actor, time/source, target, prior/new state or quantity delta and unit, and a reason where useful. Make corrections explainable. Retention, deletion rights, export, and restore expectations are user policy, not established legal requirements.

A half-open interval, [start,end), is a reasonable candidate: a booking ending at noon can be followed by one starting at noon, before any turnaround buffer. Decide that boundary, site time-zone conversion, daylight-saving ambiguity, pickup/return grace, cleaning/preparation time, and whether a pooled resource has capacity greater than one. Decide how overdue returns or repair reports interact with future reservations: notify staff/member, block new bookings, and arrange rebooking or approval without silently erasing either event.

## Exact disposition of every frozen plan clause

| Exact plan clause | Disposition | Assessment and treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **P1: “Use a shared calendar for reservations.”** | **Already covered as a view; correction if intended as the safeguard; user decision on workflow.** | Keep the calendar as an availability/request interface. It cannot serialize competing confirmations by itself. Store a dated reservation against the actual tool or pool and reject overlap at the authoritative commit. Decide serialized-tool versus pool booking, time-zone policy, interval endpoints and buffers, who may request/confirm, and whether all bookings need staff review or some may auto-confirm. MyTurn/PG&E supplies one approval-gated workflow example; Fabman supplies a makerspace booking comparator; PostgreSQL supplies one possible interval invariant. (S01–S03, S08) |
| **P2: “Keep stock quantities in a database row updated by each client.”** | **Correction; reject unconstrained stale read-modify-write; user decision on the client/server boundary and units.** | Keep a shared quantity by part and site where that is the correct stock fact. “Updated by each client” could mean a client requests a safe server-side write, so it does not by itself prove an unsafe design. Require one authoritative transaction or constrained database operation that checks permissions and the lower bound, records an idempotent stock event/delta, and commits the resulting quantity atomically. Reject updates based on a stale read that can over-consume or lose another client’s write. InvenTree’s location-scoped stock vocabulary and the reported take-stock race/fix/test/release chain show why serialization matters; they do not require PostgreSQL or a particular API architecture. Pick each/fractional units by consumable class. (S06–S08) |
| **P3: “Permit offline checkout and reconcile by timestamp.”** | **Uncertain whether offline checkout is required; reject timestamp-only reconciliation; user decision on offline authority.** | “Unreliable Wi-Fi” does not say whether the failure is handset-to-AP, site internet, or intersite connectivity, nor whether a disconnected member must receive a globally confirmed reservation. Device timestamps, especially skewed clocks, cannot prove which of two disconnected users was authorized to reserve or take a shared item. Minimum safe mode: cached browsing and queued/pending requests or local checkout events with stable operation IDs for idempotent retry; label them pending/local, and show global confirmation only after authority commits. If checkout must continue offline, consider one authorized site terminal with a journal and visible exception workflow, exclusive site quotas/assets, or explicit leases with expiry/fencing rules. Reconcile conflicts for staff; never silently select a timestamp “winner.” CouchDB replication is an alternative only with its application-level conflict policy. (S02, S08–S09) |
| **P4: “CSV import overwrites matching names.”** | **Reject name as identity and blind overwrite; retain import as a required capability; user decision on migration scope.** | The brief requires support for inconsistent old spreadsheets, but names may collide or change. Use stable IDs/barcodes or a reviewed crosswalk; normalize site/category/status/user/unit values; preview creates, updates, skips, duplicates, and ambiguous rows; report row errors and totals; make reruns idempotent; preserve source-file hash and row reference; back up and define rollback or compensating actions. Treat missing actors/dates as unknown, not fabricated. Decide whether old loan history is imported or only current inventory and how duplicates are resolved. Snipe-IT demonstrates that importer headers, pre-created fields, entity matching, and ignored columns are implementation-specific, supporting a staged reconciliation process rather than schema copying. Hashing, provenance, and rollback are proposed migration safeguards scoped to the data risk, not claims imposed by Snipe-IT. (S05) |
| **P5: “Tool maintenance is a free-text note.”** | **Correction; keep free text as supplemental; user decision on detail and scope.** | A note cannot reliably make a tool unavailable, support a maintenance queue, or explain a reservation affected by downtime. Add typed maintenance/unavailable state and opened/closed events, dates, responsible person, and condition; retain free text for symptoms/work. Decide whether preventive intervals, parts/costs, notifications, or training lockout matter. Do not build a full work-order system unless staff need it. Snipe-IT status classes and InvenTree stock history are analogies, not proof of a maker-specific repair workflow. (S04, S06) |
| **P6: “Test normal reservation and check-in flows.”** | **Already covered as a basic smoke test; correction/addition of discriminating validations.** | Keep ordinary reservation and check-in tests. Add simultaneous overlapping confirmations, concurrent quantity decrements, cancellation/edit/repair/late-return interactions, offline partitions and reconnect, permissions, transfer, import ambiguity/idempotency/rollback, and audit/recovery checks. The InvenTree regression test uses separate threads/connections to expose a race that ordinary flows miss; this is a model for a discriminating check, not evidence that this proposed system or even the upstream test ran here. (S07) |

## Alternatives, conditions, and open decisions

- **Reservation policy:** member request followed by staff approval; immediate confirmation for eligible tools after an atomic server check; or a hybrid based on training, hazard, or tool class. Approval exposes staff review but creates delay; self-service is faster but requires eligibility and conflict rules. The brief does not choose.
- **Hosting and connectivity:** one central instance reduces distributed consistency work but may be unavailable during a site link failure; an on-site server/AP may preserve local access during an internet outage but only if that is the actual failure; two synchronized site databases improve local continuity while making shared-resource decisions harder; offline replicas preserve local writes but add reconciliation and support complexity. Confirm who hosts, backs up, updates, restores, and owns the service.
- **Cross-site inventory:** block cross-site confirmation while unavailable; partition shared/high-demand tools into site-exclusive ownership; hold a transfer in transit until receipt; or add an explicit lease protocol with expiry, fencing, and clock assumptions. These are policy/engineering alternatives, not simultaneous guarantees of unrestricted disconnected writes and immediate global exclusivity.
- **Inventory and history scope:** choose integer versus fractional units by category; current inventory only versus historical loans; minimum useful event log versus detailed audit and retention; and staff correction/export rights. Existing sheets, legal obligations, retention rules, and staff effort were not supplied.
- **Product evaluation:** MyTurn and Fabman are commercial workflow comparators; Snipe-IT and InvenTree are open-source domain comparators; LibreBooking is a reservation-focused candidate; PostgreSQL and CouchDB illustrate different consistency mechanisms. Screen whether one integrated product, combined tools, or a small bespoke application fits before selecting. No price, support, export, security posture, or vendor contract was evaluated. Recheck LibreBooking’s security/releases before trial or deployment.
- **Questions for the makerspace:** (1) What fails when Wi-Fi drops: device-to-AP, site internet, or intersite link? (2) Must a reservation be globally confirmed offline, or are pending requests and a checkout journal enough? (3) Which tools are shared between sites and how do they move? (4) Which bookings require approval or training? (5) Which consumables are fractional and in what base units? (6) What identifiers, columns, date formats, and duplicate patterns exist in old sheets, and must past loans be imported? (7) Who can host, back up, update, and restore the service, and what capacity/budget exists? (8) What history must be retained and who may correct it?

## Proposed validations — not executed

1. **Product fit screen:** use a small representative set of serialized tools, pooled items, consumables, training rules, two-site transfers, and legacy sheets to compare an integrated lending/makerspace product, reservation scheduler plus inventory workflow, and a small central app. Record gaps, hosting tasks, export/recovery behavior, and unanswered security/pricing/support questions. Do not infer fit from feature lists.
2. **Same-tool overlap race:** two independent sessions submit identical or overlapping active intervals simultaneously. Exactly one confirmation should commit; the loser should receive a clear conflict/retry path. Repeat with adjacent endpoints, chosen time-zone/DST cases, buffers, edit, cancellation/rebooking, and repair blocks.
3. **Concurrent consumable decrement:** from quantity q, submit two concurrent operations whose total exceeds q. Accepted quantity must stay within the chosen lower bound, and each accepted operation must have one idempotency/event ID and one auditable delta. Repeat per site after transfer.
4. **Offline boundary and reconnect:** disconnect the user-selected link and submit booking/checkout activity at both sites. Verify that local/pending/global-confirmed labels match actual authority. Reconnect, retry duplicate operation IDs, inspect all conflicts and merged records, and prove no silent overwrite or false global confirmation. Include out-of-order and skewed device clocks.
5. **Reservation, custody, repair, and transfer transitions:** request/approve/deny/cancel/expire; checkout/return/overdue; repair block/close; damage affecting a future booking; dispatch/receipt. Check actor, site, time, state history, and member/staff permissions at each transition.
6. **CSV dry run and migration:** fixtures with renamed headers, BOM/encoding/quoted commas, duplicate or missing names/IDs/barcodes, unknown users/sites/statuses, ambiguous date formats, case-mismatched units, decimals/negative quantities, partial history, and rerun. Reconcile accepted/rejected/ambiguous rows and before/after counts by site; prove no unintended overwrite, duplicate, or irreversible update.
7. **Audit and recovery:** verify correction/append semantics, permissions, retention/export rules, and source-row provenance. Restore a backup in a clean environment and reconcile inventory, reservations, custody, maintenance, and imported references.
8. **Units and stocktake:** test compatible conversions, invalid dimensions/case, count adjustment, and recount from two terminals; compare site and aggregate totals.

All eight are proposals gated by the selected product and policies. No application/runtime/database was implemented or exercised here. No product trial, upstream test, or qualified sandbox witness was run. The upstream InvenTree issue/test/fix/backport/release history was inspected as source evidence, not executed as an independent test.
