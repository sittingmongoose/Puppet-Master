# Discovery — maker-inventory (ER11 A-M04-A, before plan reveal)

**Scope and method.** This is a self-contained first-pass research record from the S04 brief only. I read the supplied assignment and exact input map, then inspected public primary product documentation, official product pages, database documentation, and an upstream change/release chain. I did not inspect the case plan or any other campaign/history/evaluator/counterpart material. The conclusions below are scoped to a small, two-site community makerspace, not to unlimited enterprise or safety certification. Source identifiers are immutable and indexed in `source-map.json` and `sources/index.md`.

## Findings that should shape the design discussion

### Product patterns worth considering

- **MyTurn (lending library / Library of Things)** is unusually close to the borrow-and-return part of this brief. Its own product page describes check-in/check-out, barcode scanning with ordinary USB/Bluetooth scanners, managing/transferring stock across multiple locations, reminders, and reservations. That supports investigating a small “loan record + reservation request + site/location” workflow before inventing every library operation. It is vendor documentation and does not prove conflict guarantees, offline operation, total cost, or fit for a makerspace’s repair/consumables workflow. A real program page on a MyTurn-hosted tool library (PG&E) shows a distinct **staff-reviewed reservation** path: a request receives confirmation, then staff approval/acknowledgement; it quotes two days of lead time and scheduled pickup/drop-off appointments. That is an operational example, not evidence that every MyTurn deployment or reservation uses this policy. (S01, S02)
- **Fabman (makerspace member/resource operations)** describes machine/room/desk bookings alongside member plans, training/access privileges, and equipment integration, with billing tied to bookings/use as optional product scope. Its API documentation exposes a `Booking` class/object in version 1.2.10. This is a relevant pattern if training eligibility or access control for machines matters. The inspected sources do not establish how it prevents overlapping reservations, works offline, tracks consumable stock, imports messy historical CSV, or what deployment/price terms apply. Do not infer those properties from its marketing page. (S03)
- **Snipe-IT (asset lifecycle / checkout)** separates unique tagged assets, non-serialized accessories, components, and consumables. The product guide says an asset checked out to a person cannot be checked out again until returned, with deployable/undeployable/archived/pending status classes; checkout/check-in and condition/repair can be reflected in status. Its model is useful for one-custodian-at-a-time custody, scanning, basic lifecycle history, staff-mediated checkout, and CSV migration; a current-custodian lock does not itself describe future time-window bookings. (S04)
- **InvenTree (parts and quantity inventory)** models physical stock by part and location, and distinguishes physical “in stock,” allocated quantities for orders/builds, and available as in-stock minus allocated. It also has part stocktake, tracking/adjustment history, stock locations, API permissions, and physical-unit conversion. This maps especially well to consumables, count corrections, and transfer/traceability questions, but its allocation vocabulary is for stock commitments rather than evidence of tool-calendar booking. (S06)

These are materially different approaches, not a ranked vendor recommendation: (1) lending-library loans and staff approval; (2) member authorization plus time bookings for machines; (3) current-custody asset assignment; (4) location-scoped quantities and order allocations. A compact bespoke app could borrow only the relevant workflow ideas. Licensing, pricing, hosted service terms, data export completeness, long-term support, exact offline behavior, and actual feature parity remain open product-evaluation questions.

### Core data distinction and safe availability model

Reusable tools, consumable quantities, repairs, and reservations should not be flattened into one “inventory status.” At minimum, a stable tool ID/barcode maps to a site/location, condition/maintenance state, custody/checkout event, and possibly one or more date-bounded reservation records. Consumables instead have a part, unit, location, on-hand quantity, and stock adjustments; if the product reserves them, a separate quantity allocation should be released/consumed with a defined trigger. A repair block must make an asset unavailable while preserving the repair event and prior custody/reservation context. Transfer between the two sites should be an explicit in-transit/received operation, not a location edit that silently makes the item appear at both places.

For concurrency, a read-before-write “is this free?” UI check is only advisory: two users may both observe availability. The confirmed write needs one authoritative serialization point and a storage invariant/transaction over the relevant resource and interval or stock quantity. PostgreSQL’s primary documentation shows range overlap (`&&`) rejected by a GiST exclusion constraint, including a resource-equality-plus-time-overlap pattern using `btree_gist`; a unique scalar key alone cannot express overlapping intervals. Its example uses `tsrange` half-open interval notation, so endpoint convention, time zone, and any checkout/cleaning buffer still need a product decision. This is a mechanism option, not a claim that PostgreSQL is required; an equivalent atomic transaction/constraint can work in another store. (S08)

Unreliable Wi‑Fi creates a real policy choice. The lowest-complexity safe baseline is to let disconnected clients browse cached information and record *unconfirmed requests* or idempotent local events, while reserving global “confirmed” status for the authority’s successful atomic commit. If members must keep borrowing while disconnected, a single offline staff terminal/site writer can record a sequenced journal for later reconciliation; staff must see sync state and conflicts, and the system must not silently turn a request into a confirmed reservation. For two independently writable sites with no contact, there is no shared fact at the moment of booking that can keep one mobile/shared asset from being promised twice. The alternatives are to require central reachability for cross-site confirmation, partition the inventory into site-exclusive quotas/assets during disconnection, or adopt time-bounded exclusive leases with fencing and clock-expiry rules (more machinery and operational policy). This is a systems inference from the stated two-site partition condition; CouchDB documents that replicas continue independently and preserve conflicting document revisions for application-specific reconciliation, not that they enforce a global non-overlap constraint. (S09)

CouchDB is therefore a useful unfamiliar offline-first alternative only if offline writes are a first-class requirement. Its docs describe incremental bidirectional replication and offline laptop/remote-office replicas; simultaneous edits form conflicts, and the application must merge/resolve them. The deterministic “winning” revision is not an application-level guarantee that two independently created reservation documents for the same physical tool do not overlap. An offline-first architecture therefore improves availability but raises the conflict-resolution and user-decision burden; it must explicitly mark reservations provisional or assign exclusive booking authority/quotas. (S09)

### Units, state, audit, and migration evidence

InvenTree’s current documentation says `pint` is used to convert compatible physical units, with case-sensitive symbols (`kg` works while `KG` does not), built-in dimensionless item units such as `piece`, `each`, `dozen`, and `thousand`, and custom unit definitions. Its current `stock/availability` page defines allocated ≤ in-stock and available = in-stock − allocated, with the physical amount unchanged until the allocation is consumed. This is a concrete way to distinguish quantity/unit policy from serialized tool custody. Its stock-tracking docs and global settings also show that deletion/retention of history is configurable; before borrowing that pattern, the makerspace should decide how long checkout, repair, stock-count, and import provenance must remain visible. (S06)

CSV import should be a migration workflow, not “trust every column.” Snipe-IT’s official instructions require pre-existing custom fields and exact header matching; unmatched fields are skipped. Its history importer requires the assets and users to exist first, matches unique asset tag plus user name, recognizes the exact `Date`, `Asset Tag`, and `Name` columns, ignores extra columns, and expects ascending dates. This makes field mapping, stable IDs, duplicate detection, date/number/unit normalization, preview, row-level errors, and retained source row/provenance directly relevant. Preserve unknown or ambiguous values for staff review; do not invent actor/time/location to make an old row look complete. A safe migration proposal is: preserve original files read-only; classify row types; map old IDs/site/status/unit vocabularies; preview create/update/skip/ambiguous/duplicate rows; import first to a reversible staging copy; reconcile counts and histories by site; and only then accept a reviewed batch. Exact batch rollback semantics need specification. (S05)

Audit should capture who/what/when/site/item, event kind, relevant prior/new state or quantity delta and unit, source (UI/import/sync), and a reason/note where useful. That is a proposed minimum for useful operational accountability, not a legal-compliance guarantee. Historical spreadsheet entries may have incomplete/ambiguous dates or identities; represent that uncertainty explicitly and preserve the original source row rather than fabricate data. Consider whether corrections should append compensating events and whether administrators can edit/delete audit entries; confirm retention/backup/CSV export policy with users.

### Issue/fix/release evolution evidence

A directly relevant stock-concurrency regression is documented in InvenTree PR #12718 and backport #12719. The maintainers describe a `StockItem.take_stock` race: the method capped requested quantity against a potentially stale in-memory quantity before locking and refreshing the row. Two concurrent full-quantity removals could therefore both use the old amount; the later call could record a phantom removal / success even though nothing remained. The patch moves the cap after the database row lock/refresh and adds a two-thread, separate-connection transaction regression test asserting concurrent removals do not both succeed. The upstream master PR merged commit `2dca27f`; #12719 backported it as `4446c95` to `1.5.x`; release 1.5.3 at short commit `1930db0` lists the race fix. The cross-release path and patch are stronger than a release-note-only claim. The cited test was upstream evidence, not executed in this research assignment. This concerns quantity depletion, not calendar booking, but it demonstrates why serializing the write and testing a truly concurrent race matter for inventory. (S07)

## Decision and uncertainty register (before plan comparison)

1. **Reservation semantics:** decide whether “reserve” is a member request, staff-approved hold, immediate confirmed time booking, or a mixture by asset class. PG&E’s approval/lead-time example demonstrates a workable policy; the brief does not choose one.
2. **Offline boundary:** decide whether offline use is browse/request-only, staff checkout event capture at a single site writer, or globally confirmed booking without Wi‑Fi. The last conflicts with strict cross-site uniqueness absent exclusivity/lease rules.
3. **Reservation scope:** decide whether tools are reserved as individual serialized units or as a pool with quantity, how pickup/return grace and repair/cleaning buffers affect overlap, and whether future bookings are blocked by estimated/late return.
4. **Site transfer:** decide whether users can request cross-site transfers, who confirms dispatch/receipt, and when an item changes authoritative site.
5. **Consumable precision:** choose per-category base units and allowed fractional quantities; most counted pieces are integers while wire, sheet, resin, fasteners, or bulk goods may have compatible fractional units. Do not apply engineering-unit complexity to all items if simple each-count works.
6. **CSV policy:** confirm old spreadsheet examples, identifiers, date formats, duplicate handling, historical event availability, and whether staff want staging/rollback. Only the task brief asserts inconsistent inputs; the exact schema and data are unknown here.
7. **Audit/retention:** choose mandatory event fields, repair notes, permissions to correct history, import provenance, retention/export, and backup frequency.
8. **Hosting/product choice:** MyTurn/Fabman are useful commercial workflow comparators; Snipe-IT/InvenTree are open-source domain comparators; PostgreSQL and CouchDB are mechanism comparators. No prices, vendor contract terms, operating budget, existing hardware, staff IT capability, member volume, or required uptime were supplied, so final product selection/deployment topology is not settled.

## Validation ideas (proposed, not executed)

- **Double-booking race:** launch two simultaneous confirmations for the same serialized tool and overlapping interval from independent sessions/connections; exactly one should commit, with the losing member receiving a clear retry/alternative result. Repeat with adjacent endpoints, cancellations, edits, repair block, and same/different tools.
- **Quantity race:** run concurrent consumable removals against a known count; total accepted removal cannot exceed count, with one atomic audit delta per accepted operation.
- **Offline/site split:** disconnect both sites, submit the same-item overlapping booking from each, and prove that neither can falsely display a global confirmation; restore connectivity and inspect explicit conflict handling and idempotent retry behavior. Separately test the chosen allowed offline workflow.
- **Lifecycle/audit:** reserve → repair block → checkout → overdue/return → transfer → receive; verify state transitions, site, actor, event order, and old events remain queryable.
- **CSV migration:** fixtures with alternate date formats, BOM/encoding/quoted commas, mismatched headers, blank/unknown user and site, duplicated asset tags, mixed unit/case symbols, negative/fractional quantities, malformed statuses, ambiguous dates, and partial history. Dry-run counts should reconcile accepted/rejected/ambiguous rows and create no unintended duplicate or destructive update.
- **Units/stocktake:** compatible conversion, invalid dimensions/case, count adjustment, and recount from two terminals; compare per-site and aggregate totals before/after.
- **Recovery and operation:** restore a backup to a clean environment, compare inventory/event checksums, verify schema migration and two-site transfer reconciliation. These checks are recommendations only; no application/runtime was available and none was run here.
