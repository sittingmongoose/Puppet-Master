# Research draft — maker-inventory (S04)

## Scope and recommendation

This draft compares the six revealed thin-plan clauses with the user’s complete brief: tool reservations; consumables; repair status and checkout; two sites with unreliable Wi-Fi; simple hosting; messy legacy CSVs; double-booking safeguards, offline behavior, audit history and extension options. This is a research/planning deliverable, not implementation evidence. Discovery was frozen before reading the plan in [`discovery.md`](discovery.md); primary-source notes and exact URLs, versions/commits, locators and access operations are in [`source-map.json`](source-map.json) and [`sources/`](sources/README.md).

**Recommended direction, conditional on the offline policy decision:** keep one authoritative booking decision point and make the app useful during outages through cached inventory and queued, idempotent nonexclusive actions. Confirm a firm tool/time reservation only when it reaches that authority. Represent an offline reservation as visibly tentative until accepted. Use a transaction plus a database invariant for final overlap enforcement; the calendar is a view of confirmed booking state, not the concurrency safeguard. Keep consumable quantities, tool custody, repairs, and reservation intent as separate concepts with an append-only activity history. Start with an auditable CSV preview/import keyed by stable IDs.

If both sites must make binding promises while fully disconnected, the design needs a user-approved rule such as site-specific equipment/time entitlements or post-sync reassignment. A replicated offline database can preserve both sides of a conflict but cannot make two disconnected sites share one current answer for the same unique tool/time. Do not promise strict no-double-booking and unrestricted firm offline bookings together.

## Exact per-clause disposition

| Plan clause (verbatim) | Disposition | Research disposition and necessary correction |
|---|---|---|
| **P1: “Use a shared calendar for reservations.”** | **Correction; optional enhancement; partly already covered.** | Retain a shared calendar as a member/staff view if useful. The brief already asks for reservations and double-book safeguards, but “shared calendar” does not say which states hold equipment, what interval boundaries mean, how staff override a conflict, how location/pickup works, or how two simultaneous writes are serialized. Define reservation states and a database-backed no-overlap rule per unique tool. Show tentative/offline requests separately from confirmed reservations. A conflict override, if offered, should be role-controlled, explicit and recorded. Cheqroom provides a useful documented pattern: draft does not reserve, booked does; pickup location is distinct from the item's storage site; role permission controls conflict overrides. Do not assume its products' specific rules or plans apply here. |
| **P2: “Keep stock quantities in a database row updated by each client.”** | **Correction; uncertainty about which items are quantity stock.** | A shared row is a storage detail, not safe multi-client update semantics: concurrent read-modify-write can lose decrements or oversell. Apply an atomic transaction/compare-and-update or append a stock event and derive/materialize the balance; protect nonnegative quantity in the same authority. Record actor, time, source, location, unit, before/after or delta, and correction reason. Use integer counts for ordinary consumables unless real measured units are required. Store each quantity at a site/location. Treat serialized tools separately from bulk consumables. InvenTree is a reference for this distinction and for unit/stock tracking, not proof a single mutable row is sufficient. |
| **P3: “Permit offline checkout and reconcile by timestamp.”** | **Reject as written; user decision required; uncertain exact outage tolerance.** | Timestamp ordering does not resolve two disconnected users claiming the same unique tool/time; clocks may differ and a later-arriving event may represent an earlier action. Replication may preserve divergent revisions while choosing one visible winner, which is not a reservation policy. Decide whether offline work is limited to queued check-in notes, repair reports and stock counts, or whether both sites need binding bookings offline. Recommended default: offline checkout may be recorded as a local action against an item already assigned to that site, but cross-site/global availability and new firm reservations need server confirmation; show offline status clearly and reconcile conflicts explicitly. If immediate firm bookings at both sites are mandatory, choose site entitlements, a single always-reachable authority, or a revocation/reassignment policy and get member acceptance. This changes the product behavior, so research cannot decide it for the owner. |
| **P4: “CSV import overwrites matching names.”** | **Correction; import scope remains uncertain.** | Do not use mutable or nonunique names as update keys. Require a stable asset/part ID or a staff-resolved match; keep source row number and original values; preview create/update/skip decisions; flag duplicates and ambiguous rows; normalize synonyms, site names, dates, units, blanks and encodings explicitly; report row-level errors; support dry run, backup and idempotent rerun. Import current inventory separately from historical activity, and never fabricate old event actors or dates. Snipe-IT docs show asset tag/ID matching and pre-created custom fields; imported audit dates may not make audit events, while its history importer requires existing assets/users and a constrained CSV. InvenTree’s staged mapper validates and confirms rows but warns imports are non-reversible and separates create from update. These are patterns to assess, not claims that either handles the makerspace’s specific legacy sheets without a migration trial. |
| **P5: “Tool maintenance is a free-text note.”** | **Correction; free text remains an optional detail.** | The brief explicitly needs repair status. Give a tool a structured availability/maintenance state that blocks reservation while unavailable, and a repair case/event with reporter, time, reason/symptom, status, assigned staff, resolution and return-to-service time. Keep notes for context but preserve state changes as history. A flagged or in-repair tool must be excluded from both future reservations and check-out; returning to service should be an explicit transition. Cheqroom’s issue/maintenance workflow and Snipe-IT’s non-deployable status labels are useful patterns. |
| **P6: “Test normal reservation and check-in flows.”** | **Correction; baseline already covered; expand with discriminating tests.** | Keep ordinary reserve/check-out/check-in as baseline coverage. The brief's hard risks need tests for simultaneous overlapping bookings, location and role overrides, offline split-brain, unit/count integrity, row-level CSV ambiguity/idempotency, repair blocking and audit retention. Separate proposed tests from executed ones; none were run in this research stage. The InvenTree #12478 case shows why a check-availability-then-write sequence needs a race test and a database/transaction guard. |

## Product and architecture findings retained

### Useful products and approaches

- **Cheqroom** is the strongest workflow reference for timed tool reservations, pickup location, check-outs, partial returns, repair/maintenance, and usage reports. Its documentation makes a key distinction between future booking and physical custody. Draft reservations do not hold items; finalized reservations do. A check-out records the item leaving; partial check-in releases only returned items. Location means pickup point in a booking, and mixing items from locations can be rejected unless a privileged user overrides it. Treat this as product-specific evidence. Its help-center content is being migrated, and researched sources did not establish offline reservation semantics, pricing or current plan availability. Those are open facts to verify before a commercial evaluation. Sources: [reservation lifecycle](https://help.cheqroom.com/en/articles/9515148-how-to-create-and-manage-reservations), [conflict permissions](https://help.cheqroom.com/en/articles/1884304-preventing-booking-conflicts), [location conflicts](https://help.cheqroom.com/en/articles/3803891-why-am-i-unable-to-create-a-booking-across-multiple-locations), [checkout lifecycle](https://knowledge.cheqroom.com/helpcenter/how-to-create-and-manage-check-outs-and-check-ins), [reports](https://help.cheqroom.com/en/articles/6193193-generate-reports-from-cheqroom).
- **Snipe-IT** is useful for tagged asset registration, statuses, people/location assignment, consumable tracking and CSV migration caveats. It is not a complete borrowing calendar: check-out expresses current custody, and consumables are issued rather than returned. Current source snapshot was v8.8.0, released 2026-09-30 at commit `2c466fa`; its release notes require PHP 8.2 or newer and mention a CSV mapping/preview wizard. Evaluate it if the site can use current custody/status rather than member-facing future reservations. Sources: [overview](https://snipe-it.readme.io/docs/overview), [asset import](https://snipe-it.readme.io/docs/importing-assets), [history import](https://snipe-it.readme.io/docs/importing-history), [releases](https://github.com/grokability/snipe-it/releases).
- **InvenTree** is useful for consumable quantities, hierarchical sites/locations, stock movement history, supported units, and plugins. It has order/build allocation but no evidenced date-window equipment booking in the reviewed sources. Use it as a stock-control reference or a candidate if the owner accepts allocation/unavailable semantics; do not conflate its order reservation with timed tool borrowing. Its latest/stable docs are mutable and should be pinned to a tagged release before implementation. Sources: [stock/locations](https://docs.inventree.org/en/stable/stock/), [allocation](https://docs.inventree.org/en/stable/manufacturing/allocate/), [history](https://docs.inventree.org/en/latest/stock/tracking/), [units](https://docs.inventree.org/en/latest/concepts/units/), [import](https://docs.inventree.org/en/latest/concepts/data_import/), [plugins](https://docs.inventree.org/en/latest/plugins/develop/).
- **Local-first replication** (PouchDB/CouchDB pattern) is an optional approach where local devices remain usable while disconnected and later sync. Divergent changes are retained but the visible winner is deterministic, not a business-approved reservation winner; the app must surface and resolve conflicts. An append-only activity log improves history and mergeability but cannot enforce global one-tool/one-time exclusivity while partitions are disconnected. Sources: [replication](https://pouchdb.com/guides/replication.html), [PouchDB conflict handling](https://pouchdb.com/guides/conflicts.html), [CouchDB 3.5 conflict model](https://docs.couchdb.org/en/stable/replication/conflicts.html).
- **Central relational authority** is the safer path to firm no-overlap guarantees when clients can reach one service. PostgreSQL 18 documents an exclusion constraint over a time range with the overlap operator; combined with tool identity, it can reject an overlapping reservation at commit. Half-open intervals `[start,end)` permit one booking to begin when another ends. This requires confirming database/hosting choice and cannot finalize a reservation from a disconnected site. Source: [PostgreSQL 18 range constraints](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/rangetypes.html).

### Issue → fix → release lesson

InvenTree PR [#12478](https://github.com/inventree/InvenTree/pull/12478) documented two concurrent allocations against different orders and the same five-unit stock item both succeeding, creating ten units of allocations. The merged patch [locks stock rows, recalculates availability and adds a cross-transaction regression test](https://github.com/inventree/InvenTree/pull/12478/files). It merged 2026-07-28 and appears in release 1.5.0; the latest release seen during research was 1.5.6 (2026-09-26). A separate stock-taking race fix appeared in 1.5.3. Applicability caveat: the allocation regression test is guarded by database support for `select_for_update`, so the row-lock behavior must be checked for the chosen backend. The transferable rule is to test adversarial concurrent writes and enforce the invariant within the database transaction, rather than relying only on a calendar or a preflight availability query. This research did not execute that test or validate any proposed application.

### Data model and extension notes

Represent a unique tool, an interchangeable pool/quantity, a reservation request, a confirmed reservation, an actual checkout/return, a repair case, and a consumable stock event distinctly. A tool may be reserved for a period without being checked out yet. A repair state blocks availability even if no person has checked it out. A transfer between sites changes pickup eligibility and should be recorded. For consumables, store quantity by site and unit; avoid counting the same decrement twice on retry by using a unique operation ID. Prefer soft retirement plus a retained event ledger for items with history value. Set a retention rule explicitly; some systems let history be deleted by age or import dates without normal audit events.

Optional extensions include a shared availability calendar, barcode scanning, utilization/issues reports, recurring bookings, notification reminders and a plugin/API surface. They should follow core guarantees and actual member/staff needs. Do not add a broad plug-in system solely because a competitor has one; extensions increase deployment, versioning and support work. Export/import APIs and a documented event boundary may be enough initially.

## Conditions, decisions and uncertainty to carry forward

1. **Offline promise (owner decision):** Are reservations allowed to be firm during a complete disconnection at either site? If yes, can each site reserve only its own assigned pool, or can a booking be revoked after sync? Who decides and how are affected members notified?
2. **Network/hosting (owner decision):** Is one small service at the main site acceptable, with remote access required at the second site? Is managed hosting acceptable, or must this run locally? What downtime/backups can staff support? Exact hosting costs and product plans were not researched.
3. **Reservation unit:** Is each tagged machine unique, or can a member reserve one of N equivalent items? Are reservations time ranges with pickup buffers, opening hours and transfer time between sites? None are specified in the brief.
4. **Legacy data scope:** Which files, row counts, historical events and identifiers exist? Are staff expected to migrate only current inventory, or old reservation/check-out history too? No example spreadsheet was supplied to this research task.
5. **Audit requirements:** What history must be retained, who can correct it, and can records ever be deleted? Product docs establish examples, not the makerspace's retention policy.
6. **Implementation choice:** Product use vs custom build; relational database; local-first libraries; number of users; account model and integrations. The source review cannot select these without the owner’s priorities and a small proof of concept.
7. **Source drift:** Cheqroom help articles redirected or were inaccessible in direct opens; InvenTree `stable`/`latest` docs are mutable. Re-check feature details against current version/plan before adopting. Snipe-IT release information is tied to the observed v8.8.0 snapshot and may change after access.

## Proposed discriminating validations (not executed)

1. **Reservation race and interval semantics:** send two simultaneous independent requests for the same unique tool and overlapping half-open intervals; require one confirmed booking and one conflict response. Repeat with adjacent intervals (both should pass), different tools and nonoverlapping windows. Verify the database invariant, retry behavior and a human-readable resolution. This distinguishes true concurrency protection from a UI-only precheck.
2. **Outage semantics at two sites:** sync both sites, disconnect them, reserve the same tool at overlapping times, then reconnect in both orders with dropped/retried messages. Verify tentative/confirmed status is honest; the chosen policy is deterministic and visible; idempotent replay avoids duplicate checkout/quantity events; and a rejected claim is not silently deleted. If the policy uses site entitlements, try exceeding the local entitlement.
3. **CSV migration trial:** use staff-selected representative messy files, including missing/duplicate IDs, similar tool names, alias locations/categories, multiple date formats, different separators/encodings, blank/zero quantities and unit conversions. Compare row-level preview against a reviewed expected result; ambiguous matches must require a staff decision; errors must preserve source rows; repeated import must not duplicate; rollback/restore must be demonstrated. Run current inventory and old history as separate jobs.
4. **Stock and custody ledger:** concurrently decrement a consumable quantity from two clients near zero; assert no negative count, no lost decrement and no duplicate on retry. Verify the actor/time/site/unit and reason are queryable. Reserve, check out, partially return, report damage, transfer to repair, resolve, and retire a durable tool; verify it remains unavailable during repair and all state changes stay in history.
5. **Role/site behavior:** as member, front desk, repair staff and administrator, attempt overlaps, cross-site pickup, repair-state booking, unauthorized edits and privileged overrides. Confirm every override records user, time and reason.
6. **Acceptance pilot:** ask staff and members to walk through a real week of representative scenarios, including Wi-Fi outage; compare paper/manual fallback, expected statuses and notification/reassignment policy. Capture disagreements rather than interpreting a successful demo as acceptance.

**Executed checks:** none. The research stage inspected public documentation, official source code/PR diffs, issue and release history. There was no application/database runtime available or invoked, no installation or product account access, and no local build or test. Every validation above remains proposed.
