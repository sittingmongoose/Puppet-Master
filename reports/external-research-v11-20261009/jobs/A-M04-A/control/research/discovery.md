# ER11 research discovery — maker-inventory (S04)

**Scope:** The six research obligations in the supplied brief for a small community makerspace with individually reserved tools, staff-managed consumables, repair status and checkout, two sites, intermittent Wi-Fi, simple hosting, and messy legacy spreadsheets. This discovery was written before the case plan was revealed. It is standalone evidence synthesis, not a finalized plan disposition. Sources, versions, exact locators and access operations are indexed in [`source-map.json`](source-map.json) and summarized in [`sources/README.md`](sources/README.md).

## O1 — Useful products and materially different approaches

The evidence surfaces three distinct product patterns, not a single product that can be assumed to solve every part of the brief.

1. **Equipment booking and custody: Cheqroom.** Its workflow separates a future reservation from actual custody. A reservation has a date/time window, person/contact, equipment and pickup location. Draft reservations do not lock equipment; finalized bookings do. On pickup, a reservation can become a check-out. Check-outs capture that equipment physically left, its due date, and later item-by-item returns. Partial check-in is meaningful: returned items become available while the rest remain out. The tool also documents repair/maintenance and operational reports, including item issue history and utilization by location. This is the closest workflow reference for makerspace tools.
2. **Asset register with quantity categories: Snipe-IT.** This is a better reference for tagged durable assets, accessories, components and consumables than for future booking. It has check-in/out, status labels for unavailable/repair states and locations. However, its documented consumable model is one-way issuance to a person, not reservation and return. Current deployment would also entail PHP 8.2 or newer per v8.8.0, which matters for a self-host decision. Snipe-IT demonstrates both useful importing support and a caution: current value imports and historical activity imports are distinct, and an imported date may update a field without generating an audit event.
3. **Parts/quantity inventory: InvenTree.** Its locations, stock quantities, serialized items, allocation to orders and trackable stock movements are a better match for consumables and stock take. It supports nested locations for multiple sites and an extension API/plugin framework. But its allocations are order/workflow reservations, not a future calendar for borrowing one drill from 14:00 to 16:00. It complements an equipment booking model or is a candidate only if the makerspace can accept a simpler “allocated/unavailable” stock state instead of timed self-service bookings.

Two approaches to the offline and consistency problem differ fundamentally:

- **Local-first replicated data (PouchDB/CouchDB style):** each device/site can keep a local copy and accept disconnected edits. Replication is incremental, but edits can diverge. The database keeps both conflicting revisions while exposing one deterministic winner; the application must detect and merge/surface the hidden branch. Append-only event/delta records preserve history and reduce overwrite conflicts, but two disconnected sites can still independently create overlapping reservations for the same unique machine.
- **One authoritative relational reservation service:** a database range exclusion constraint can reject overlapping windows for the same unique tool at commit, even when concurrent online requests race. A client without a connection to that authority cannot receive a final global booking confirmation. A PWA may still cache information and queue repair notes, counts and tentative booking requests. Firm offline booking requires an explicit partitioning/override policy.

## O2 — Consequential behaviors, defaults, types and applicability

### Booking, physical check-out and repairs

Cheqroom’s conflict policy depends on permissions: ordinary users cannot override a booked item or a different pickup location when the role’s conflict override is off; selected privileged roles can ignore the conflict. This is a useful fail-closed baseline and makes any override attributable to a role decision. It also means “two sites” has a physical implication: a single booking location is a pickup point, and items stored at two sites cannot be picked up simultaneously without a location change or override. A makerspace can instead support transfer workflow, require pickup at one site, or split a booking into site-specific requests.

Distinguish **reserved**, **checked out**, **returned**, and **under repair**. A future booking should block that time window but must not imply the tool has physically left. A broken tool should be blocked from booking independently of custody. A repair record should capture who flagged it, when, reason, repair status, and re-open time; that repair history is not established merely by a mutable “condition” field. The product docs establish Cheqroom patterns but are not evidence that its offline behavior fits this case.

### Quantity, units and event history

InvenTree’s stock item is a part quantity tied to a location; quantity may be untracked (e.g. stock of bolts) or an individual serialized item. Stock movement entries can capture actor, time and quantity delta, and locations can be nested. Optional units support compatible conversions via Pint; default blank units represent dimensionless pieces, and unit spelling is case-sensitive. For the brief, the first release should likely use integer counts for ordinary consumables and only introduce length/weight units if a concrete consumable demands it. Spreadsheet unit aliases and purchase-pack sizes must otherwise be reconciled before counting stock.

Audit-like records have retention semantics. InvenTree’s latest docs say tracking entries can be retained when an item is deleted; its stable stock docs only promise history while the stock item remains. Its tracking-entry deletion option is off by default, and when enabled, the documented interval defaults to 365 days. Therefore any “audit history” promise needs an explicit keep/retire/delete policy and a versioned verification. A safe small product can use soft-retirement plus append-only event records, with corrections recorded as compensating events rather than editing old events.

### Importing inconsistent CSVs

The exact importer details matter more than a generic “CSV import” checkbox:

- Snipe-IT identifies asset updates by asset tag unless a database ID is supplied; custom fields must exist first. Some supplied dates bypass normal audit notifications/logging. Its historical importer needs current assets and people first and accepts only a small set of fields in ascending order. That makes it a reference, not a turnkey historical migration for arbitrary sheets.
- InvenTree creates an import session, maps columns, stages rows, validates and shows row errors, then asks the user to confirm each row. Creation and update are separate jobs, and update requires a unique identifier. The import is non-reversible, so it recommends backup.
- Snipe-IT v8.8.0 release notes mention a CSV mapping/preview wizard and clearer errors. Version is materially relevant: the current release observed here is v8.8.0 (2026-09-30, commit `2c466fa`), and release notes say PHP 8.2 or newer is required.

A useful importer for this brief should preserve the untouched source file and row numbers; allow per-file mapping of synonym columns; normalize whitespace, case, dates, categories and site names; treat blank vs zero vs unknown distinctly; flag duplicate tags and ambiguous matches; preview create/update decisions; report errors by row; and support a safe dry run and repeatable/idempotent re-import. For historical activities, keep a separate event migration with actor/date/asset matching and an explicit “unknown/missing legacy history” marker. Do not silently map records by fuzzy names when two tools are similar.

## O3 — Issue/fix/release evolution chain

InvenTree PR #12478 is a directly relevant concurrency lesson. In its issue description, concurrent allocations against different orders but the same five-unit stock item could both validate before commit and create ten units of allocations. The fix locks shared stock-item rows in a consistent order, rechecks available quantity while holding the lock, and adds a multi-thread/multi-connection regression test requiring one of two full-quantity attempts to be rejected. This merged on 2026-07-28. The release history lists it in v1.5.0; subsequent v1.5.3 notes a separate race fix for stock removal, and the latest release observed is v1.5.6. The test uses a backend feature check for `select_for_update`; it does not establish the same row-lock behavior for every database engine.

The lesson transfers to equipment booking: a read-availability-then-insert sequence is not a double-booking guard. A transaction/unique exclusion constraint or equivalent serialized authority must arbitrate the final write. An application-level availability check can provide a friendly early warning but should be backed by a database invariant. The cited PR tested InvenTree allocation code, not this proposed makerspace application; no local runtime or product test was run.

## O4/O5 — Design choices, useful alternatives and uncertainty

The exact product decision depends on what “unreliable Wi-Fi” means in practice. Three coherent options should be carried into plan comparison:

- **Online authoritative bookings, offline-safe operations:** confirm tool/time bookings only while connected; permit cached catalog lookup and locally queued check-in notes, consumable counts, repair flags, and tentative requests. On sync, replay actions idempotently and surface rejected bookings. This is easiest to reason about and avoids false firm promises.
- **One local server plus shared remote access:** host one small application/database at the staffed site and expose it to the second site, with careful backups. All firm bookings still require that server; loss of inter-site WAN affects only remote booking, not local site operations. Ask whether hosting and network operation at the main site is acceptable.
- **Offline firm booking at both sites:** pre-assign specific machines or capacity/time windows to a site, or choose a conflict-resolution rule that can revoke a request after sync. This adds operational constraints and user-visible uncertainty. If people cannot tolerate that, true two-site offline writes and a strict no-double-book guarantee are incompatible without pre-allocation or another always-reachable authority.

Potential alternatives to evaluating as full product candidates: Snipe-IT for tagged assets when a calendar is optional; InvenTree for quantity stock and traceable movements when timed reservations are optional; Cheqroom as a commercial managed service and workflow benchmark; and a small custom application with either centralized SQL constraints or a local-first sync model. The public sources do not establish Cheqroom pricing, offline reservation semantics or exact plan gating; do not choose it on assumed feature availability.

Uncertainty that should remain visible: whether the two sites share a dependable LAN/WAN link; whether bookings may be tentative offline; whether users can accept post-sync reassignment; whether reservations apply to a serial tool, interchangeable tool class, or consumable quantity; whether every consumable is integer count; how much legacy history is valuable; and whether staff can accept a hosted service or prefer one locally maintained server. No provider runtime was available or invoked. No third-party software was installed, no repository or product canon was touched, and no experiment was executed.

## O6 — Discriminating validations proposed (none executed)

1. **Concurrent booking invariant:** using two independent clients released by a barrier against the same unique tool and overlapping `[start,end)` intervals, require one commit and one conflict; require adjacent intervals to succeed; require different tools and nonoverlapping times to both succeed. Inspect database enforcement as well as the UI message. This distinguishes a real invariant from a race-prone availability precheck.
2. **Two-site offline split-brain:** disconnect both clients after syncing the same tool; reserve overlapping times at both sites; reconnect in both orders and repeat with delayed retries. Verify that offline status is visibly tentative, the winner is deterministic or selected by policy, loser handling is humane, duplicate replay is idempotent, no accepted claim disappears silently, and the audit history retains both attempts.
3. **CSV adversarial sample:** import one representative sheet with duplicate/missing IDs, inconsistent headers, ambiguous names, alternate dates, blank/zero quantities, decimal values where pieces are expected, aliases for sites/categories, and CSV delimiter/encoding differences. Compare preview and committed row totals; ensure ambiguous rows stop for staff resolution; rerun input and check idempotency; verify backups/rollback procedure.
4. **Event and repair lifecycle:** create a tool, reserve it, check it out, partially return a kit if applicable, flag it broken, move it to repair, resolve it, and retire it. Confirm actor/time/before-after data, no availability during repair, correct re-open behavior, and history still visible after retirement.
5. **Authorization and site rule:** test member, front-desk, repair staff and administrator roles; attempt an overlapping booking and mixed-site pickup under normal and override roles; record every override in history.

**Executed checks:** no application or database runtime was available or invoked. Browser research inspected public product documentation, code diffs, issue/pr history and release pages only. All five items above are proposals, not executed test results.
