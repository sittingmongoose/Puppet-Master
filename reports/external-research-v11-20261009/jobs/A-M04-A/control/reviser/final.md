# Maker-inventory research — final reviser report (S04, A-M04-A)

## Scope and recommendation

This is a complete research and planning report for the brief: a community makerspace needs reservations for tools, staff tracking for consumables, repair status and physical checkout, two sites with unreliable Wi-Fi, straightforward hosting, and import from inconsistent legacy spreadsheets. It must address double-booking safeguards, offline behavior, audit history, and extension options. The six exact plan clauses and their dispositions appear below. This report is not implementation or runtime evidence.

**Recommended starting point, subject to owner decisions:** keep one reachable authority for firm reservations of a unique tool. A shared calendar can display that state, but cannot arbitrate simultaneous writes. A database-enforced overlap rule should apply to confirmed/booked holds only. Keep a draft or offline tentative request visibly separate from a confirmed hold, and keep future reservation intent separate from physical checkout and return. If sites must make binding promises while disconnected, require an explicit policy such as site-exclusive equipment pools or a user-approved post-sync reassignment rule; ordinary multi-master replication does not supply a domain-correct winner.

During an outage, the interface can show cached information and queue nonexclusive actions such as repair observations, counts, and check-in notes. An offline physical checkout is a different question from a new future reservation. If the owner permits it, staff should verify the tool is physically present, serviceable, and not already in another person's custody. If a transfer, earlier checkout, or current custody cannot be established from the local evidence, queue the event as unconfirmed for reconciliation. Give each queued action a unique operation ID so replay cannot duplicate a checkout or stock change. New firm cross-site reservations remain unconfirmed until the authority accepts them, unless a chosen partition policy says otherwise.

For unique tools, PostgreSQL 18 is one grounded candidate for online enforcement: an exclusion constraint can combine tool identity equality and range overlap (`&&`), using `btree_gist` for the scalar identity operator class. Apply the invariant only to rows that actually hold the tool, such as confirmed/booked reservations; drafts, tentative requests, canceled rows, and expired rows should not block a new hold. Require valid, nonempty intervals and one enforced `[start,end)` convention. PostgreSQL's two-argument range constructor defaults to `[)`, but timestamp ranges are continuous and can express other bounds, so the application must validate rather than assume all inputs were canonicalized. The database and hosting choice remain open; a central constraint only arbitrates writes that reach that authority. If a privileged overlap exception is offered, treat it as a user decision: record the actor, reason, affected holds, and the visible conflict, and do not claim that the no-overlap guarantee still holds for that exception. The safer default is no override.

Represent repair as structured availability state and a repair case/event, not only a note. It blocks new booking and checkout. If a tool is reported unsafe after future holds were confirmed, surface each affected hold, block invalid checkout, and give staff a recorded cancel/reassign/contact path. Do not silently erase the booking or make the tool appear available.

Keep unique tagged tools distinct from interchangeable quantity stock. Quantity changes need an atomic authoritative update or a transactional event-ledger design with the same invariant: concurrent decrements cannot be lost or drive stock below zero, and an operation retry cannot apply the same event twice. Store site, unit, actor, time, and reason. Start with integer counts for ordinary consumables; add measured units only for a demonstrated need. For CSV migration, use stable IDs, preview and row-level decisions; names are mutable and ambiguous matches must stop for staff review.

## O1/O2 — Products, approaches, and consequential behavior

### Equipment booking and custody: Cheqroom

Cheqroom is the strongest reviewed workflow reference for timed tool reservations, pickup location, checkouts, partial returns, maintenance/issues, and usage reports. Its documentation distinguishes a draft reservation from a finalized booked reservation that holds equipment; a checkout records physical custody, and a partial return releases only the returned items. The pickup location can create a conflict for items stored at different sites, and documented conflict overrides depend on role permission. These are Cheqroom-specific behaviors, not universal rules. The sources do not establish its offline reservation semantics, current plan gating, or price. Its help content was being migrated; some material was available only through indexed results and some direct opens redirected or failed. Recheck current behavior before a product choice. (Primary sources are indexed under `CHQ-RES`, `CHQ-CONFLICT`, `CHQ-LOCATION`, `CHQ-CHECKOUT`, and `CHQ-REPORTS` in the inherited source map.)

### Asset register and request-calendar features: Snipe-IT

The earlier draft's description was too narrow. Snipe-IT v8.8.0, released 2026-09-30 at observed commit `2c466fa`, adds a unified `/calendar` that includes reservations. Its Requestables Overhaul says a request can carry start/end dates, appear on that calendar, and link to fulfillment. The official requestables documentation also says assets can be made requestable. Thus Snipe-IT v8.8.0 has at least dated request/calendar functionality and belongs in a borrowing-workflow comparison; it should not be characterized only as current custody.

That evidence does **not** establish that a dated request automatically holds an asset, how overlapping requests are approved or rejected, whether concurrent requests are race-safe, or what location and role rules apply. Do not call it a proven reservation or double-booking system until those behaviors are tested. Its asset check-out still expresses current custody; its consumables are issued rather than returned. It remains useful for tagged assets, status/location assignment, and import patterns. The release also requires PHP 8.2 or newer (8.4+ recommended in the observed notes) and describes a CSV mapping/preview wizard. The import documentation says ID takes precedence, otherwise Asset Tag matches an update; Name can change; some imported audit dates do not create audit events; custom fields must exist first. Current inventory import and historical-activity import are distinct jobs, and the history importer has prerequisites and constrained fields. Sources: [v8.8.0 release](https://github.com/grokability/snipe-it/releases/tag/v8.8.0), [requestable assets](https://snipe-it.readme.io/docs/requestable-assets), [asset import](https://snipe-it.readme.io/docs/importing-assets), and [history import](https://snipe-it.readme.io/docs/importing-history). The version and exact access notes are in the reviser's source map; older source IDs remain unchanged in their inherited maps.

### Quantity inventory and extensions: InvenTree

InvenTree is a useful reference for quantity stock by location, nested locations for sites/rooms, serialized items, movement history, supported units, staged CSV imports, and an API/plugin model. Its order/build allocations reserve stock for a workflow; the reviewed sources do not evidence future date-window borrowing of one tool. It is a stock-control reference or a candidate only if the owner accepts allocated/unavailable semantics instead of timed equipment booking. Its `stable` and `latest` documentation paths are mutable; pin a release and verify the relevant behavior before implementation. Unit names can be case-sensitive, and the docs describe blank units as dimensionless pieces. Import sessions stage and validate rows but are non-reversible; backup and row-level review matter. Plugin capability is an option, not a reason to add plugin infrastructure before a concrete need. (Inherited source IDs include `INV-STOCK`, `INV-ALLOC`, `INV-TRACK`, `INV-IMPORT`, `INV-UNITS`, and `INV-PLUGIN`.)

### Offline replication and central authority

PouchDB/CouchDB-style replication lets local clients record changes while disconnected and replicate later. Concurrent edits can leave multiple revisions; the system deterministically exposes one visible winner while retaining discoverable conflict branches. That deterministic choice is not a makerspace reservation policy. Append-only delta records can avoid overwriting the same document and preserve actions, but cannot stop two disconnected sites from independently promising the same unique tool and time. A local-first interface still needs sync operations, conflict handling, backups, and an accountable authority; it is not operationally free. The offline owner decision remains whether tentative requests are sufficient or a firm-offline policy is required. (Primary sources: [PouchDB replication](https://pouchdb.com/guides/replication.html), [PouchDB conflicts](https://pouchdb.com/guides/conflicts.html), and [CouchDB 3.5 conflict model](https://docs.couchdb.org/en/stable/replication/conflicts.html).)

A central relational authority is a different option: a database constraint can arbitrate concurrent online booking writes. PostgreSQL 18 documents range exclusion using `&&`; a scalar tool ID plus overlap can use `btree_gist`. Its range constructor's two-argument form uses `[)`, while timestamp ranges are continuous. This is a viable candidate, not a database selection or a global offline guarantee. Source: [PostgreSQL 18 range types, §§8.17.6 and 8.17.10](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/rangetypes.html).

## O3 — Issue, fix, and release chain

InvenTree PR #12478 is a relevant concurrency analogue, not a timed-equipment booking example. Its report describes two concurrent allocations to different orders against one five-unit stock item both succeeding, resulting in ten units allocated. The bounded source inspection in the research evidence reports a merged fix that locks stock rows in a consistent order, recalculates availability under lock, and adds a cross-transaction test requiring one of two full-quantity attempts to fail. The test is guarded by database support for `select_for_update`; do not generalize its locking guarantee to an unsupported backend. The change merged 2026-07-28, appears in InvenTree 1.5.0, and the evidence notes a separate stock-taking race fix in 1.5.3. The critic independently confirmed the PR page's race report and merge date but could not fetch the diff during that pass; its patch details rely on the inherited bounded diff/test note, not on that independent page check. The transferable lesson is to arbitrate the invariant inside a transaction and test competing writes, rather than relying on a preflight availability query. No makerspace application or test was run. Sources and provenance: inherited `INV-ALLOC-PR` and `INV-RELEASES` records and `../research/sources/inventree-concurrency.md`; [PR #12478](https://github.com/inventree/InvenTree/pull/12478).

## Data model, audit, and extension choices

Keep separate records or concepts for a unique tool, a quantity pool, a reservation request, a confirmed hold, physical checkout/return, site transfer, repair case, and consumable stock event. A confirmed reservation blocks only its time window; it does not mean the tool has left. A repair blocks use independently of custody. If interchangeable capacity can be booked as a quantity, it needs a capacity transaction/invariant rather than a unique-tool exclusion rule.

An append-only activity history is a useful design only with explicit correction and retention rules. Prefer corrections as compensating events that point to the original event rather than editing the old event; use a unique operation ID for idempotent replay. Soft retirement can preserve history, but indefinite retention is not established by the brief and must not be assumed. Decide who can correct, what remains visible, and whether/when any history can be deleted.

Optional extensions include barcode scanning, utilization/issue reports, recurring bookings, reminders, and a documented API/event boundary. A shared calendar is also optional as a view, though reservation safety is not optional. Add extensions only after core booking, custody, repair, import, and offline policy are accepted. A broad plugin system raises install, upgrade, versioning, and support burden; an export/API boundary may be enough initially.

## Owner decisions and unresolved uncertainty (O5)

1. **Offline promise:** may either site make a firm reservation during a full disconnection? Are tentative requests acceptable? If firm offline booking is required, are tools/pools/windows partitioned by site, or may a booking be revoked after sync? Who resolves it and contacts affected members?
2. **Physical offline checkout:** can staff verify physical presence and current custody locally? What happens when a transfer or prior checkout may be stale? Is an offline checkout an unconfirmed event until sync, or can a bounded local policy accept it?
3. **Hosting/network:** is one small service at the staffed site acceptable, with remote access from the second site? Is managed hosting acceptable, or must staff operate a local server? Compare service effort, backups/restore, and WAN failure against managed products before claiming either is simpler. Costs and plan entitlements were not established.
4. **Reservation unit and interval:** is each tool individually tagged or can a member reserve one of N equivalent items? What time zone, opening-hour, pickup buffer, transfer time, and empty-interval rules apply? Which states hold an item, and will an exception ever override a conflict?
5. **Repair impact:** who decides whether existing holds are canceled, reassigned, or retained after a repair report, and who must be contacted? No source establishes the makerspace's policy.
6. **Legacy migration:** what files, row counts, identifiers, and historical events exist? Migrate current inventory only or prior reservation/checkout history too? No representative workbook was supplied.
7. **Audit and correction:** which events must be retained, who can correct them, and can any records be deleted? No indefinite retention rule is implied.
8. **Product/implementation choice:** managed product versus custom build, relational database, local-first libraries, users/accounts, and integrations remain open until priorities and a small proof of concept are known.
9. **Product verification:** Snipe-IT v8.8.0 request conflict/hold semantics and location/role rules need a trial. Cheqroom help content was migrating; price, offline behavior, and plan availability were not established. InvenTree `stable`/`latest` docs are mutable and should be pinned to a release. Release and docs can change after the observed snapshot.

## Discriminating validations proposed (O6; none executed)

1. **Booking race, state scope, and intervals:** send two simultaneous requests from independent clients, released by a barrier, for the same unique tool and overlapping valid `[start,end)` ranges. Require one active confirmed hold and one conflict at the authority. Adjacent intervals should both pass; different tools and non-overlapping windows should pass. Verify that draft/tentative rows do not block, confirmation enters the invariant, cancellation/expiry releases it, malformed/empty ranges are rejected, and authorized override (if selected) leaves a visible audited conflict. Inspect the database rule and retry behavior, not just the UI. Repeat on the chosen database/backend.
2. **Two-site outage, stale custody, and replay:** sync both sites, partition them, create overlapping tentative requests, and reconnect in both orders with delay, dropped messages, and duplicate replay. Confirm no tentative claim is shown as globally accepted. For physical checkout, test a tool physically present and in staff-verified custody; then test stale transfer, earlier offline checkout, repair/unserviceable state, and missing evidence. Queue uncertain events unconfirmed. Verify unique operation IDs prevent duplicate checkout/count changes and that neither accepted nor rejected claim disappears from the event history. If site entitlements are chosen, exceed one locally and verify rejection.
3. **Snipe-IT v8.8.0 request-window trial:** for one requestable asset, create overlapping and adjacent dated requests; try a different asset; inspect which roles can see/manage requests; test fulfillment and cancellation; and test requests across two site locations. Determine whether requests hold inventory and how simultaneous approval works. Treat calendar display alone as insufficient. No such product trial was run here.
4. **Repair after confirmation:** confirm a future booking, then report a repair interval that overlaps it. The system should surface an affected hold, block new holds and unsafe checkout, preserve the history, and require a recorded cancel/reassign/contact resolution. Verify the staff and member views do not imply that the unsafe tool remains available. Repeat with repair ending before/after adjacent reservation boundaries.
5. **CSV migration:** use staff-selected representative files with missing/duplicate IDs, similar names, aliases for locations/categories, date formats, delimiters/encodings, blank versus zero quantities, decimal values where pieces are expected, and unit/pack-size variants. Compare preview to a reviewed expected result; ambiguous matches must stop for staff resolution; errors retain source row and original values; rerun does not duplicate; demonstrate backup/restore or rollback. Keep current inventory and historical activity as separate jobs.
6. **Stock and custody ledger:** concurrently decrement near-zero stock from separate clients. Require no negative balance, no lost decrement, and no double-application on retry; confirm actor, time, site, unit, reason, and operation ID are auditable. Exercise reserve, checkout, partial return if relevant, damage, repair transfer, resolution, and retirement; confirm state history remains visible under the adopted retention policy.
7. **Role/site behavior:** as member, front-desk, repair staff, and administrator, attempt overlap, cross-site pickup, repair-state booking, unauthorized edits, and any permitted override. Verify permissions and that every exception records actor, time, reason, and affected holds.
8. **Acceptance pilot:** have members and staff walk through representative real-week scenarios, including a Wi-Fi outage and the paper/manual fallback. Compare expected statuses, notification and reassignment behavior, and staff operation burden. Record disagreement; a successful demo is not by itself user acceptance.
