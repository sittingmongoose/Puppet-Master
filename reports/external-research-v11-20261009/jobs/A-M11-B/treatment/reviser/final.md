**Scope:** The museum-search brief, exact clauses P1–P6, declared research and critic predecessors, their declared evidence roots, and independently reopened public primary sources.

## Recommendation

Begin with a public lexical search over a reviewed, versioned allowlist of fields. Preserve exact identifiers, recognizable collection language, and a curator-facing correction/export trail. Keep semantic retrieval optional until a museum-specific evaluation and data-transfer decision support it; if tested, embed only an explicitly selected template of public, approved text. Decide visibility for images, captions, records, and individual fields with the responsible curators and affected communities. Rights metadata, community protocols, and technical authorization are separate inputs.

A nightly index refresh can remain a propagation mechanism, but it should not be the sole control for mutable restrictions. Define the policy owner, effective time, acceptable revocation delay, and behavior for unknown or ambiguous status. Then test the routes and cached copies that the actual implementation has. A nightly delay may be an explicit owner-approved risk; it is not automatically adequate or unacceptable without the museum's policy and threat model.

For enrichment, use staged, human-reviewed proposals with original values and provenance retained. Compare a local lexical path, a constrained hybrid option, and materially different infrastructure only if the museum’s current data store and operating capacity make them plausible. Static image derivatives may suffice; IIIF is useful when interoperability, zoom, or compound-object presentation justifies the added service and authorization work. No production stack, hosting pattern, provider, visibility class, cost, performance, or policy outcome can be selected from the supplied brief alone.

### P1 — Keep public discovery separate from internal data

A public keyword baseline remains useful for accession numbers, names, dates, exact phrases, and specialist terms. Use approved title/alternate-title, creator, date, place, material, object type, subject, and description fields only where the museum has approved each field for public discovery. Public labels, synonyms, or crosswalks can help visitors without overwriting collection terminology.

“Searchable” and “filterable” settings are not an authorization policy. Review snippets, suggestions, facets, result counts, exports, and identifiers as well as full record text. For restricted material, the museum must decide whether even existence, neutral identifiers, or aggregate counts may be exposed. Those are product and governance decisions, not defaults supplied by Meilisearch or another engine. The public-field allowlist and side-channel checks are recommendations inferred from the brief’s changing rights and restricted cultural material.

### P2 — Keep embeddings selective and evidence claims bounded

Reject whole-record embedding as the default. The brief does not identify which text is public, whether remote processing is allowed, what languages matter, or which model/provider and retention terms are acceptable. Meilisearch’s current guide describes a documentTemplate that selects fields and a provider example that sends document text for embedding; it recommends a short template. Its v1.6 article’s 0.5 semanticRatio default is historical and should not be assumed for a later target release. Choose an explicit ratio and pinned engine/model for evaluation. (See [Meilisearch current hybrid-search guide](https://github.com/meilisearch/documentation/blob/main/capabilities/hybrid_search/getting_started.mdx), [v1.6 release article](https://www.meilisearch.com/blog/meilisearch-1-6), and inherited evidence [C03–C06](../critic/sources/01-search-and-enrichment.md).)

The draft’s concern that restricted text could influence retrieval or disclose membership is a **threat hypothesis**, not a demonstrated Meilisearch leak. Open issue #6591 reports a version- and configuration-specific hybrid ranking-score change after adding a null searchable field; it does not report extraction of restricted text and was not reproduced here. Before any adoption, use restricted/public fixtures to test whether schema changes or embedding inputs alter ranking, snippets, membership inference, or other selected visibility outcomes. Review provider data-flow and terms separately. If those conditions are not acceptable, retain keyword-only search or assess local/self-hosted embeddings.

### P3 — Treat image and caption publication as policy decisions

The brief supplies no item-level rights, image classes, reuse terms, or community directions. Resizing does not remove sensitive content, and a caption can itself disclose restricted information. Decide by approved policy or item which images, previews, sizes, captions, attribution statements, and protocols may be presented, how replacement or revocation works, and who approves the change.

IIIF Image API 3.0 standardizes image requests and permits static pre-generated resources; Presentation API 3.0 describes presentation rather than replacing the catalogue/search layer. Its requiredStatement must be rendered, whereas its rights property is informative and may be rendered. IIIF Authorization Flow 2.0 connects a viewer to an existing access system; it does not choose the museum’s rules. A static derivative path is a valid lower-operations alternative when its access and invalidation behavior meet policy. Use IIIF where deep zoom, compound objects, or viewer interoperability justify its server, profile, monitoring, and authorization work. See inherited evidence [S08–S10](../research/sources/03-image.md) and [C08–C10](../critic/sources/02-image-and-access.md), with this turn’s IIIF check in [V06](sources/01-reviser-verifications.md#v06).

If Local Contexts applies, do not recommend an unspecified Notice generically. The FAQ distinguishes Disclosure and Engagement Notices, which may be used without community approval, from Collections Care Notices, which are to be used only after engagement with Indigenous community authorities. Labels are community-controlled and customizable; an institution should not author or alter one. A Notice is not a substitute for a community Label. The institution must identify the applicable Notice type and follow its conditions; communities decide whether and when to apply Labels. The Hub distributes Labels and Notices; it is not the museum’s content repository. Any API/static snapshot choice has account, project-visibility, and freshness conditions. See [Local Contexts FAQ](https://localcontexts.org/support/frequently-asked-questions/) and [V02](sources/01-reviser-verifications.md#v02).

### P4 — Define current policy and its propagation

A nightly search-index removal alone can leave an exposure window and does not establish control of details, exports, suggestions, direct image URLs, IIIF manifests or image information, derivatives, caches, or any other copy the actual system has. The source material supports this as a design concern, not as a finding that the museum already has each named endpoint or a demonstrated leak.

Set an authoritative policy source, owner, effective time, review/expiry behavior, and measurable maximum revocation delay. Apply policy at each relevant data and media boundary; invalidate or version derived indexes, embeddings, manifests, and caches as the implementation requires. If Meilisearch tenant tokens are selected, signed filters constrain search queries but changes to permissions stored on documents may require reindexing; they do not authorize direct image, detail, or export routes. IIIF Auth is a viewer flow to the institution’s existing access controls, not an access-policy engine.

“Fail closed when status is missing or ambiguous” is a defensible sensitive-material default, but it is a **proposed museum policy**, not a vendor or standards default. Ask the museum to choose deny, neutral substitute, or another controlled outcome, with an owner and review path. Likewise, a nightly propagation window is an owner-approved risk decision only if its maximum delay and exposure are measured. The route list in the validation below is a proposed coverage matrix: include only surfaces that exist in the chosen implementation and add any that do.

### P5 — Preserve source values and human decisions

OpenRefine reconciliation is a curator-side candidate workflow, not authority to overwrite the source catalog. The current manual says matching is semi-automated and requires human judgment; the reconciled cell can retain the original string alongside the entity link, but a value-only copy can lose the link. Candidate scores differ by service, so there is no shared approval threshold. It recommends iterative work; large reconciliations can be slow and the operation has no configurable request delay. (See [OpenRefine reconciliation manual](https://openrefine.org/docs/manual/reconciling), [C01–C02](../critic/sources/01-search-and-enrichment.md), and [V05](sources/01-reviser-verifications.md#v05).)

A proposed staged record should include original and proposed value, stable source record ID, selected authority URI, vocabulary/service and version, match score as reported by that service, reviewer and decision, timestamp, rejected/unmatched/ambiguous status, change history, rollback data, and export provenance. These are design recommendations for museum workflow, not fields mandated by OpenRefine. Pilot by field/entity type. Public reconciliation against Getty ULAN/AAT/TGN or Wikidata and a museum-maintained CSV-backed authority are alternatives; compare remote terms, availability, language/coverage, and handling of local or culturally specific terminology. No authority service was queried and no 40,000-record throughput was measured.

### P6 — Evaluate user success, visibility, and threshold behavior

Retain curator-authored searches as one slice, but include visitors who do not know museum vocabulary. Stratify the set by exact ID, common and specialist terms, spelling and name variants, languages and scripts actually in scope, sparse descriptions, ambiguous names, no-result tasks, image-only items, restricted items, and culturally preferred terms where relevant. Curators and visitors should judge task success and top results. Compare lexical baseline, aliases/crosswalks, and any constrained hybrid candidate. Measure exact-ID success, judged top-k usefulness, recall at a selected rank, no-result behavior, latency, facet behavior, accessibility, and leakage against the museum’s approved policy. Set size and stopping criteria from collection risk, available participants, and unresolved failure classes; neither ten queries nor a fixed 50–100 count is a supported universal benchmark.

The Meilisearch rankingScoreThreshold is a tuning parameter, not a calibrated relevance guarantee. Issue #5274 documents a threshold/count limitation on v1.12 and a later fix marked for v1.16.0; a contributor specified that the fix applies to page/hitsPerPage, not limit/offset, and describes accurate totalHits and facet counts for the former starting in v1.16. The issue’s earlier maintainer explanation describes how limit-dependent unscored candidates could overestimate totals and facets. This is historical, version- and pagination-specific evidence; it does not establish a current defect. If the selected UI uses thresholds and exact counts/facets matter, pin the release and test threshold on/off, realistic limits, filters, pagination modes, totalHits, estimatedTotalHits, and facet distributions. Keep result semantics visible in the UI and account for latency if an exhaustive path is considered. See [issue #5274](https://github.com/meilisearch/meilisearch/issues/5274) and [V01](sources/01-reviser-verifications.md#v01).

## Retained approaches and alternatives

| Approach | Use when | Tradeoff or condition |
|---|---|---|
| **Curated lexical baseline, aliases, and visitor-facing facets** | Initial search for exact IDs, names, dates, titles, and known subject terms | Requires approved fields, vocabulary work, and relevance/accessibility evaluation; does not guarantee conceptual discovery. |
| **Constrained hybrid retrieval** | Local user tasks show lexical search misses useful plain-language descriptions | Requires explicit public template, pinned model/engine/ratio, data-transfer and retention approval, language/ranking evaluation, and cost/operations review. Keyword-only remains a valid fallback. |
| **OpenRefine + public authorities** | Curators need reviewable reconciliation for names or subjects that fit a public authority | Matching is service-specific and semi-automated; verify terms and coverage, review ambiguous cases, and keep source text. |
| **Local CSV-backed authority/reconciliation** | Local terms, remote-transfer limits, or locally controlled vocabulary matter | Museum maintains the vocabulary and service; throughput and quality were not measured. |
| **PostgreSQL full-text search** | PostgreSQL already holds the catalogue or a single-store lexical path is a priority | PostgreSQL 18 docs describe normalized tsvector/tsquery, language configurations/dictionaries, ranking, and GIN/GiST; GIN is preferred for regular text search. This does not establish museum language quality, facets, relevance, or performance. Evaluate it against a dedicated engine only if the current database makes it plausible. See [PostgreSQL docs](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/textsearch-intro.html) and [V07](sources/01-reviser-verifications.md#v07). |
| **Apache Solr** | The museum wants a separate schema-driven search platform and can support its operations | Solr 10.0 guide documents query/filter and faceting behavior; schema and field representation affect literal facet terms. It adds a separately operated index and release lifecycle. No fit, speed, or cost was established. See [Solr searching and faceting](https://solr.apache.org/guide/solr/latest/getting-started/searching-in-solr.html) and [V08](sources/01-reviser-verifications.md#v08). |
| **Static image derivatives** | Modest fixed display sizes suffice and low runtime complexity matters | Less flexible for zoom/crops; policy checks and revocation still apply to files, URLs, and caches. |
| **IIIF with a dynamic server such as Cantaloupe** | Zoom, crop, multi-page objects, or viewer interoperability justify the service | Adds server, cache, authorization, monitoring, release, and staffing work. The documented Cantaloupe 5.0.3 HTTP 401 delegate exception was confirmed by a maintainer and fixed in 5.0.5 release notes. At the 2026-10-09 research/critic source snapshot, v5.0.7 was listed as latest; recheck release/security/support status before adoption. This is a historical denial-path regression chain, not evidence a current build is defective. See [Cantaloupe discussion #536](../critic/sources/02-image-and-access.md#c11), [v5.0.5 notes](https://github.com/cantaloupe-project/cantaloupe/releases/tag/v5.0.5), and [C12–C13](../critic/sources/02-image-and-access.md). |
| **Local Contexts live API/embed** | The institution and relevant communities choose it and authorized access/update behavior fits the workflow | API key, account type, project visibility, mapping, and update dependency must be checked; it does not confer permission to publish museum content. |
| **Local Contexts static snapshot** | A print/offline or low-dependency workflow is needed | Track snapshot version and refresh obligations; it does not replace technical authorization or community decisions. |
| **CARE-informed governance** | Decisions concern Indigenous data, rights, purpose, authority, or collective benefit | CARE is governance guidance, not a policy engine or rights adjudication. Apply with relevant people and institutional authority. |

## Proposed validation; none of these checks ran

1. **Search usefulness:** create a stratified query/task set with curators and non-expert visitors, including exact identifiers, plain-language descriptions, variants, sparse records, languages in scope, ambiguous names, image-only records, and no-result cases. Compare a lexical baseline and aliases with any explicit hybrid configuration. Report task success, exact-ID success, judged top-k utility, recall at a chosen rank, and latency; choose size and stopping criteria from unresolved risk and participant capacity.
2. **Field and side-channel visibility:** use policy-approved fixtures for public, restricted metadata, restricted image, culturally governed, changed-rights, and revoked cases. Test only existing surfaces: results, snippets, counts, facets, autocomplete, detail, export, manifest, image-info, previews, image sizes, direct URLs, cached copies, or mirrors as applicable. Assert the museum-selected behavior for existence, identifiers, neutral substitutes, and revocation delay.
3. **Embedding scope and ranking:** if considered, pin engine/model/template/ratio; verify every embedded field is approved, record provider/data flow and retention terms, and test public/restricted fixtures for ranking effects, output leakage, model/schema changes, and cost/latency. For #6591, compare fixed queries/configurations before and after adding null/sparse searchable fields; classify as reproduced, not reproduced, or inconclusive. No leakage is assumed.
4. **Thresholded count/facet semantics:** if the chosen Meilisearch release and interface use rankingScoreThreshold, compare threshold on/off under realistic filters, result limits, both page/hitsPerPage and limit/offset modes, and inspect returned IDs, totalHits, estimatedTotalHits, and facets. Assert the UI’s intended count semantics and measure any exhaustive-count latency. Treat #5274 as historical regression context, not a current defect claim.
5. **Image authorization and revocation:** for the actual chosen static or IIIF routes, test allowed, missing, expired, and revoked access; image/metadata replacement; direct URLs; warmed caches; and any viewer flow. For Cantaloupe or another server, assert a defined denial response rather than an exception and test the selected release. Verify attribution/required display statements and the museum’s chosen rights/protocol wording.
6. **Enrichment and correction workflow:** sample by entity type/field; measure service latency/failures and match ambiguity; record human false positives; verify original text, authority identifiers, reviewer decisions, rollback, role checks, and export round-trip. Confirm exact service terms/rate constraints. Do not compare scores across services as if calibrated.
7. **Authority/governance decisions:** ask curators and relevant community authorities which fields, images, captions, Labels/Notice types, display locations, owners, appeal/review paths, and update delays are appropriate. If a Notice is proposed, identify its type and conditions; Collections Care requires engagement with Indigenous community authorities. UI success cannot substitute for that consultation.
8. **Alternative evaluation:** only if the actual architecture makes it relevant, compare PostgreSQL full-text search with a dedicated search index or assess Solr’s schema/operations. Use the same fixture and query judgments. Do not infer fit, performance, or cost from 40,000 records alone.
