# Research draft — community oral-history collection

**Scope.** Plan P1–P6 were compared against the exact brief: a searchable public collection for 2,000 hour-long recordings, volunteer transcripts, multilingual speakers, consent restrictions, staff correction of words and speakers, preserved source audio, time-linked excerpts, and withdrawal of a recording or restricted segment. The brief’s six equal obligations are addressed below. This is a complete research/planning deliverable, not an implementation specification for unlimited production guarantees.

Source IDs resolve through [source-map.json](source-map.json); concise source notes are in [sources/README.md](sources/README.md). The larger pre-plan finding set is preserved in [discovery.md](discovery.md) and is frozen before plan comparison.

## Plan disposition

### P1 — “Batch automatic transcription then publish text and audio.”

**Disposition: correction; automatic transcription is an optional capability, with a publication decision required for each language/workflow.** Keep batch processing as an efficiency option after consent review, but change the sequence to: permission and access classification; approved transcription method; draft creation; volunteer/staff correction; curator review; rights and accessibility checks; publication. Publish transcripts only when the recorded release and community policy permit it. Do not publish raw ASR output automatically.

The OHA archival guidance treats ASR as a rough draft, advises human review for correctness, names and dates, and permits an index where a transcript is not feasible [S09]. Whisper’s own model card reports variable language/accent/dialect performance, hallucinated and repetitive text, and inadequate evaluation of speaker diarization [S10]. Its word timestamps are off by default and marked experimental; the previous-window context default and timestamp parameters affect output [S11]. The v20230306 word-timestamp/repetition report, merged fix attempt, and v20230307 release are an issue/fix/release chain, not proof of universal accuracy [S12–S14].

For 2,000 hours, evaluate local ASR and the hosted ASR4Memory option only on consented representative samples. Keep each original recording unchanged; save ASR model/version, language, processing settings, and draft as provenance; separate corrected transcript versions from machine output. Offer language-specific alternatives: volunteer transcription, bilingual review, subject indexing, or a catalog-only record when ASR quality or consent is inadequate. A correction UI should support speaker labels and uncertain passages instead of treating diarization output as speaker truth.

### P2 — “Keep transcript edits as one replacement text file.”

**Disposition: correction; plain-text export can remain an optional convenience.** Make transcript edits versioned and attributable, with segment-level text, speaker identity, language, timing, correction status and reviewer history. A replacement file can serve as a publication/export format, but it should not erase the prior approved transcript, volunteer contributions, or correction provenance. Maintain a link from each transcript version to the audio master and its time base. Keep translations as separate, aligned language variants with their own authors and review status. Preserve speaker names/aliases as entities rather than free-text labels alone.

This preserves correction of words and speakers, multilingual review, time-linked excerpts and withdrawal of selected spans. It also allows a user to export simple TXT, VTT, SRT or other agreed interchange files. OHD’s listed upload formats and per-passage editing show concrete alternatives; however, its current help says transcripts are uploaded per media file and mass upload remains in preparation [S06]. Test exports and round-trips before relying on any product’s editor.

### P3 — “Search full text with a general database search index.”

**Disposition: correction; retain a general database index as a viable implementation option after access-aware and multilingual validation.** Search interview metadata, approved transcript text and controlled index terms. Preserve language and script information, speaker names, synonyms and community-approved terms. Return a transcript passage and an authorized time link, not only an interview hit. Filter each query and result snippet by the same rights policy that gates audio and transcripts; promptly remove restricted text from search indexes and caches. Search should not index raw ASR drafts or private consent documents.

A relational database full-text feature may be sufficient for this scale; no evidence justifies requiring a separate search platform. OHD demonstrates a broader alternative that searches transcripts, translations, index entries and related metadata and takes the reader to a passage, but full-text search requires registration and archive access [S07]. Mukurtu’s protocol model offers a different way to expose public metadata while restricting media [S01–S03]. Choose anonymous public search or account-gated search with community/staff input. Measure multilingual recall, spelling variants, search latency and access revocation. Preserve a public catalog or index-only fallback for interviews without transcripts.

### P4 — “One consent flag hides the public recording.”

**Disposition: correction, with a required community/legal policy decision.** One interview-level flag can be a simple publication status but cannot express the brief’s consent restrictions and restricted segments. Store permissions and access decisions at interview, media asset, and time-bounded segment scopes, with allowed viewers/actions and effective dates. Support states such as review, public, controlled, available on request, withdrawn from public access, and destroyed under the documented agreement. Keep metadata visibility separate from media/transcript visibility. Enforce the rule in audio delivery, transcript/translation, index, search snippets, exports, player links, embeds and caches. Recheck access after revocation.

Mukurtu distinguishes open and strict protocols, defaults multi-protocol sharing to All, permits Any, and warns that item and media access can diverge [S01–S03]. Its v4 README says access control depends on correctly configured private files [S04]. OHD reports interview-level access states and archive grants [S05–S07]. OHMS’s cache rule blocks direct HTTP access to XML only when administrators install the corresponding web-server configuration; it does not secure an external audio URL or all derivatives [S16, S17]. None of the reviewed product evidence proves that arbitrary portions of a single recording can be restricted safely.

A community authority must define what “withdraw” means for a recording or segment. A reversible public takedown during review is an operational default; whether the preservation master and restricted copies are retained or destroyed depends on the actual release and legal obligations. OHA advises honoring release terms and narrator intent throughout the lifecycle but does not impose a universal deletion rule [S09]. Preserve a rights-change event and verify propagation to every public copy; do not convert uncertainty into silent retention or deletion.

### P5 — “Keep WAV originals and MP3 listening copies.”

**Disposition: already-covered intent for separate originals and listening copies; correct the fixed WAV/MP3 assumption and add preservation controls.** Keep the received source file as the unchanged authoritative master, whether it arrived as WAV or another supported format. Create an MP3 or other streaming derivative only when the measured browser, bandwidth and quality needs justify it; record derivative lineage, encoding parameters and rights. Do not overwrite a master during cleanup, transcription, redaction or format migration. If the archive adopts a normalized preservation format such as WAV/BWF, preserve the received original as required by its policy and record the new file as a derivative/migration event.

For transfer, BagIt 1.0 can package payload and documentation, and its manifests support integrity checks with SHA-256/SHA-512 (SHA-512 is recommended as the new-bag default). BagIt does not create backups or access controls [S18]. PREMIS provides a model for digest identity, fixity-check events/outcomes and time-bounded rights restrictions [S19]. Require separate verified replicas and a tested restore path. Confirm actual incoming formats, average and worst-case sizes, storage/egress costs and permissible derivatives; none were observed in this research.

### P6 — “Validate word accuracy on a few English clips.”

**Disposition: correction; a few English clips are only a smoke test.** Define an evaluation corpus and acceptance thresholds with the community and staff before selecting a transcription workflow. Stratify approved samples by every collection language, dialect, code-switching, recording era/quality, overlap, number of speakers and long silences. Compare against human-reviewed reference text. Report word or character error by language and speaker group, speaker-attribution errors separately, timestamp alignment error, repeated/hallucinated text, correction time, and consent/privacy handling. Keep failure cases and uncertainty visible. A model that fails for one language/class must not block an index-only or human-assisted path for that material.

The Whisper model card warns that language and dialect performance differs and diarization is not robustly evaluated [S10]. Its code exposes defaults and second-valued timing options that can alter behavior [S11], while the history contains a concrete timestamp/repetition defect and subsequent fixes [S12–S14]. Validate each candidate version and configuration using actual community-relevant recordings. A human sign-off, not a model score, remains the publication gate.

## Retained product alternatives and conditions

- **Mukurtu CMS:** strongest fit among reviewed options when community-defined protocols, roles and self-hosting matter. Use a supported release and confirm v4 readiness, private-file behavior, update operations, accessibility, staff capacity, backup/restore, segment-specific media/text controls and total operating cost before adoption [S01–S04, S20]. The pinned code README lists runtime and private-file requirements but does not label the code a development build. The Mukurtu 4 manual home page says the manual is still in development; code release maturity and current support should be confirmed rather than inferred from that manual status.
- **Oral-History.Digital:** useful hosted portal alternative with transcript editing, multilingual search/translation and archive access controls [S05–S08]. Proceed only if the collection is eligible, public access requirements match its registration model, bulk ingest is workable, contracts/export and data location are acceptable, and the deployed component/version is identified. Ask for a segment-withdrawal demonstration.
- **OHMS Viewer plus separate archive:** useful when the archive already has media storage and needs synchronized transcript/index access [S15–S17]. It requires a separate PHP web deployment, exported XML and configured media/player hosting. Its README’s PHP 5.3+ floor is not evidence of current runtime support. Install cache-block rules, then test public and restricted URLs, index links and player behavior. OHMS is a viewer, not the preservation master store.
- **Human-first transcription/indexing:** preserve volunteer agency and quality controls with a style guide, bilingual review, speaker correction and explicit uncertainty. Use thematic indexing or metadata-only access when transcript quality, consent, labor or language coverage is insufficient [S09].
- **Whisper or another ASR:** optional local draft generation following permission, language-specific evaluation and correction. Compare with a hosted service only after reviewing data processing, retention and cost terms. Do not assume a single recognizer handles all languages/speakers equally [S06, S10–S14].

No one option can be selected from evidence alone. The decision depends on who controls the archive, current staff/hosting capacity, intended anonymous-public versus account-based search, eligible language coverage, interpretation of withdrawal and consent documents, and actual service/hosting cost. These are user/community decisions; product fit should be tested against them.

## Cross-cutting design decisions

Represent one interview separately from its files and timed content: stable interview identifier; source/master audio; access derivatives; transcript versions by language; translations; speaker/person entries; time-bounded transcript and index spans; rights/restriction record; editorial revisions; preservation events. Use a declared, consistent media time base; store start/end values as numeric time with a documented unit and transform only at export. The brief does not decide whether the collection needs word-level or sentence/subject-level timing. Prefer sentence/segment timing for the initial searchable catalog, then add finer timing where volunteers and use cases justify it.

Use role-based editorial review: volunteers can suggest corrections and speaker labels; authorized reviewers approve text, rights state and publication. Separate “unverified”, “needs language review”, “restricted”, “approved”, and “published” states so draft text cannot leak through the public search index. Keep internal consent documentation restricted. For each access change, log actor, reason, scope, effective time and propagation status.

Preservation policy should specify accepted source formats, checksums, replicas, fixity schedule, restoration owner, derivatives, rights basis and incident workflow. Preserve originals and permission records together under controlled access. Keep public access copies separate from preservation masters. A low-cost deployment is not low-cost if one person must manually restore files, update dependencies, or discover that withdrawn text remains indexed.

## Validation status

No product was installed or run. No test audio, account, sandbox, billing information or live collection was available. All experiments below are proposals, not executed checks.

1. Test a representative permission matrix: anonymous visitor, public member, each approved community/protocol role, editor, reviewer and administrator. Cover public metadata with private media, all/any membership, hidden segment, direct media URL, search snippet and export. Revoke a user and measure how quickly every old link stops working.
2. Withdraw a whole recording and separately restrict a timed segment. Verify player, derivative, transcript, translation, index, search engine, old share URL, caches, exports, backups and restore behavior. Record whether each test case requires retention or destruction.
3. Edit a word, speaker label, uncertain phrase and translated line; verify timing and revision history, export/import integrity and unchanged master hash. Include a long recording, boundaries and silence at the end.
4. For each ASR candidate/language, compare reviewed reference text and speaker/time labels on consented stratified clips; calculate language-specific errors and volunteer correction effort. Retest after model/version changes.
5. Load 2,000 representative records in a staging copy; measure ingest throughput, full-text response, one-hour playback/seek, concurrent correction, backup/restore and monthly storage/egress. Obtain current hosting quotes and service terms before a cost decision.
6. Validate BagIt manifests and SHA-512 fixity on ingest, after replica transfer and after restore; corrupt a disposable copy to verify detection and recovery. Record rights/restriction dates and fixity outcomes in the chosen PREMIS profile.
7. Have volunteers with different language and technical backgrounds correct content, assign speakers, add timecodes and submit a restriction. Observe time, errors, accessibility and whether publication can be blocked pending approval.
