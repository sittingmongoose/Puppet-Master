# Independent critique — community oral-history collection

**Stage:** A-M08-B/control/critic, method M08.  
**Scope:** The complete research draft, frozen discovery, exact brief, revealed P1–P6 plan, its declared source map/evidence notes, and independently re-opened primary sources. This is an independent review only; no product was installed or run, and no candidate artifact was changed. Source identities and access notes are in [source-map.json](source-map.json); bounded evidence is indexed in [sources/README.md](sources/README.md).

## Assessment

The draft is unusually complete for this small brief. It correctly makes ASR optional, rejects automatic publication, treats rights and segment withdrawal as policy plus enforcement problems, preserves database full-text search as a viable option, distinguishes preservation masters from derivatives, keeps uncertainty around product versions and prices, and labels every runtime/product validation as proposed. The frozen discovery’s product comparisons and Whisper issue → PR → release chain are carried forward. I found no factual basis for rejecting the central architecture or requiring a particular hosted service, database, ASR model, audio format, or preservation standard.

Four material changes should be considered before calling the research complete. First, the publication workflow should name narrator review and formal authorization as a gate, not rely on the more general “recorded release and community policy” wording. Second, withdrawal needs an explicit limit for content already copied, quoted, or indexed outside the archive. Third, privacy review should cover identifying metadata, named third parties, pseudonyms, translations, and snippets as well as the audio and consent form. Fourth, segment-level permissions are a policy model, not evidence that a product can securely enforce time ranges in a downloadable or seekable one-hour file; the draft should give a safe fallback and state that implementation remains unproven.

The new primary source used for these findings is the OHA-hosted 2025 compiled *Principles and Best Practices* PDF. It includes sections adopted in 2018, 2019 and 2022; the source note preserves those section dates instead of treating the compilation date as a new adoption date. It recommends narrator review and approval before public release, formal authorization, privacy-aware description, and warns that material already public may not be fully removable. These are field-ethics recommendations, not jurisdiction-specific legal advice or proof that any product implements them [S21].

## Material findings

### M1 — Make narrator review and authorization an explicit publication gate

**Applies to:** O2/O4; P1, P2, P3 and P4.  
**Status:** Material correction; a workflow/user decision is needed for existing and future recordings.

P1 is corrected in the right direction: raw ASR output is not automatically published, and human review, rights checks and curator approval are required. The remaining gap is that “the recorded release and community policy permit it” does not explicitly require an authorized narrator to review/approve the recording and transcript (or another valid documented authorization) before the material is exposed to the public. The OHA guidance in the 2025 compilation recommends giving the narrator an opportunity to review and approve the recording and/or transcript before archive deposit or public access; it says not to make an interview public until formal authorization, and describes options to correct, restrict, redact, delay, or decline public release [S21, pp. 10–13]. The same guidance asks projects to consider access and preservation before interviewing, and to explain potential uses so that promises match what can actually be controlled [S21, pp. 10–12, 21–22].

**Recommendation:** Add an explicit publication state such as `awaiting narrator/community authorization`, with a recorded authority, scope, date, allowed public uses, and any restriction or expiration. Apply it to audio, approved transcript, translation, index, excerpts, and search snippets rather than audio alone. Decide how to handle legacy recordings with missing, ambiguous, or unreachable signatories; until adjudicated, an index-only or restricted record may be safer than publication. Translate the consent/review/withdrawal information for participants and offer a workable review route in their preferred language. Keep the release interpretation, copyright/licensing questions, and community protocol as distinct records; this critique does not infer a jurisdiction’s legal result.

### M2 — State what “withdrawal” can and cannot promise after release

**Applies to:** O4; P4, with consequences for P3 and the public-use model.  
**Status:** Material clarification and community/user decision.

The draft already handles platform-side propagation well: it calls for whole-recording and segment tests across derivatives, transcript/translation, indexes, snippets, caches, exports, backups, and old links. It also correctly avoids a universal delete/retain rule. It does not address the boundary the OHA now calls out: once an interview has been made public or entered an archive, a repository may be unable to remove every copy, quote, or reference made by others; it recommends explaining when withdrawal becomes difficult or impossible [S21, pp. 21–22]. That is material because the brief explicitly asks to withdraw a recording or restricted segment.

**Recommendation:** Define withdrawal in two parts: the archive-controlled response (for example, block new public access, remove public derivatives/text/snippets and revoke known links within an agreed time, then document the state of preservation copies under the release) and the limits outside the archive’s control (prior downloads, quotations, search-engine or third-party copies). Tell narrators this before release; set a published request route and target response time. Avoid promising complete erasure from the public internet. Test and report the boundary honestly; an archive-side test cannot establish that all downstream copies were found.

### M3 — Include privacy and third-party review in discoverability decisions

**Applies to:** O2/O4; P2–P4 and speaker/person metadata.  
**Status:** Material omission, especially for sensitive interviews; scope is a community decision.

The draft treats internal consent paperwork as restricted and calls for access filtering at the search, snippet, transcript and media layers. That is good. It gives less attention to the fact that a public catalog, name/alias field, translation, controlled term, time-linked index, or search snippet can itself reveal a narrator or a third party even when audio access is blocked. OHA’s guidance in the 2025 compilation says description, categorization and access should respect the narrator’s personhood and desired privacy, and advises considering third parties discussed in the interview, anonymity, protection procedures and possible harm [S21, pp. 12, 35–39]. It also recommends consulting participants about translator choices and the final linguistic form of the transcript [S21, pp. 35–37].

**Recommendation:** Add a public-description/privacy review to release and withdrawal workflows. Separate a controlled identity record from the participant-approved public name or pseudonym; allow a record to omit names, locations, sensitive keywords and transcript terms from anonymous discovery. Review translations and index terms for the same disclosure risk. Add test cases where metadata or a snippet is restricted while a non-identifying catalog stub remains public. This preserves the draft’s `public metadata/private media` alternative while preventing “metadata public” from being treated as an automatic safe default.

### M4 — Keep segment restrictions explicitly unproven until the delivery path is tested

**Applies to:** O2/O4; P3 and P4.  
**Status:** Material implementation uncertainty; no product correction is justified from the available evidence.

The draft properly says no reviewed product evidence proves that an arbitrary time range of one file can be safely restricted. It also proposes enforcement across player, downloads, transcript and index. However, the prescription to store rules at segment scope could be mistaken for an implementation capability. Mukurtu’s documented protocol and media controls distinguish item and media assets but do not demonstrate sub-file access control [S02–S03]. OHMS’s security release blocks direct HTTP access to its XML cache only when the operator installs the matching server rule; it does not secure external media or every derivative [S16–S17].

**Recommendation:** Retain a segment restriction as the policy data model, but label its enforcement **unverified** for every considered product. During validation, attempt direct URL/range requests, downloads, player seeking, old signed links, transcript exports, translations, indexes, and caches. If the platform cannot enforce a restricted range against those paths, require a separately redacted/split public derivative and matching redacted text/index, while keeping the authoritative master under the governing agreement. Do not rely on a hidden player cue or transcript display rule as a security boundary. Choosing between segmented derivatives, authenticated streaming, or suppressing public access is a technical and community decision after a sandbox demonstration.

## Minor and conditional findings

1. **Make “low cost” discriminating.** The draft rightly says no current price, file-size or billing data was observed and proposes quotes/storage/egress tests. Add decision criteria before the pilot: a cost ceiling or comparison window, ongoing staff/operator hours, translation/review labor, restore responsibility, and a minimum public-access service level. Without those, “low cost” remains a user requirement without a pass/fail test.
2. **Keep product ranking provisional.** “Strongest fit among reviewed options” for Mukurtu is supportable only as a conditional pilot preference where community protocols and self-hosting are priorities. No evidence compares actual eligibility, accessibility, total cost, maintainability or segment-control support across deployments. The draft already says no option is selected; keep that qualification adjacent to the ranking.
3. **Apply export/import checks to restrictions.** The draft proposes round-tripping transcript edits and rights/timing. Add a check that export, migration and restore preserve restriction scope and remove hidden text from public derivatives; otherwise a format conversion can re-expose a withdrawn span.
4. **The draft correctly narrows historical-product evidence.** Mukurtu v4’s pinned README supplies dependencies/private-file requirements but does not establish production maturity; the separate manual’s development status is not evidence about the code. OHD’s displayed software version and repository release are not assumed to describe the hosted deployment. OHMS’s old PHP minimum is treated as historical documentation. Keep these as unresolved deployment-version questions rather than corrections [S04–S08, S16–S17, S20].
