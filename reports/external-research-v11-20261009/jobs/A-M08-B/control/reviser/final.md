# Community oral-history collection: complete revised research

**Scope.** This is a complete research and planning deliverable for a searchable public collection of 2,000 hour-long recordings, volunteer transcripts, multilingual speakers, consent restrictions, staff correction of words and speakers, source-audio preservation, time-linked excerpts, and withdrawal of a recording or restricted segment. It addresses the six equal obligations in the case brief and compares the exact P1–P6 clauses in the revealed thin plan. It is not an implementation specification for unlimited production guarantees.

The complete source identities, exact URLs, versions/commits, locators, access times, observed operations, and drift are in [source-map.json](source-map.json). Bounded evidence notes are indexed in [sources/README.md](sources/README.md). The full pre-plan discovery is preserved at [discovery.md](../research/discovery.md). No product was installed or run. Primary-source reads are the only executed research checks; all product/runtime tests below are proposals.

## Plan disposition

### P1 — “Batch automatic transcription then publish text and audio.”

**Disposition: correction; ASR is optional and public release requires documented authorization.** Use batch transcription only after rights and processing permission are checked. The workflow is access classification; approved transcription method; draft creation; volunteer/staff correction; narrator review where feasible; privacy, rights, accessibility, and community review; documented release authorization; publication. Never publish raw ASR output automatically. Gate public audio, transcript, translation, excerpt, index entry, and search snippet on the same release decision.

OHA’s 2025 compiled guidance recommends narrator review and approval of the recording and/or transcript before public use where possible, says public release should wait for formal authorization, and recommends a clear release procedure and timeline [S21]. This is professional ethical guidance, not a jurisdiction-wide legal rule or evidence that a product enforces the workflow. Record the authority, scope, date, permitted uses, restrictions, and review/expiry date. Explain the process in the participant’s language and offer a practical route to review and request corrections, restrictions, redactions, delayed release, or non-release. Keep legacy recordings with missing or ambiguous permission restricted until adjudicated. A non-identifying catalog stub is possible only after rights and privacy review.

ASR is a draft aid selected by language and workflow, not a universal multilingual policy. OHA archival guidance calls ASR a rough draft needing human review for names, dates, and accuracy; a topical index is a valid alternative where transcript creation is infeasible [S09]. Whisper’s model card reports uneven quality by language, accent, and dialect; hallucinated or repeated text; and inadequate evaluation of diarization [S10]. In the pinned code, condition_on_previous_text is a boolean defaulting to true; word_timestamps is false by default and experimental; clip and hallucination-silence thresholds use seconds [S11]. A v20230306 timestamp/repetition report, merged corrective PR, and v20230307 release form a concrete issue/fix/release chain, not evidence that all similar errors are gone [S12–S14].

For this collection, compare local ASR with the hosted ASR4Memory option only on consented representative samples. Preserve the original audio unchanged; record model/version, language, settings, and output provenance; keep machine drafts separate from corrected transcript versions. Alternatives include volunteer transcription, bilingual review, thematic indexing, or a catalog-only record when quality, permission, labor, or language coverage is insufficient. Treat machine speaker labels as hypotheses until a human reviewer approves them.

### P2 — “Keep transcript edits as one replacement text file.”

**Disposition: correction; a plain-text file is an optional export, not the authoritative edit history.** Store attributable, versioned transcript segments with language, speaker identity, timing, correction/review state, and a link to the source audio time base. Preserve volunteer contributions and previous approved versions. Store translations as separately authored and reviewed language variants aligned to the recording and source transcript. Keep speaker names and aliases as identity records with controlled access instead of relying on free-text labels.

Provide transcripts, translations, time logs, and proposed index material for participant review where appropriate, and record corrections and restrictions. Privacy review covers names, pseudonyms, speaker/person records, third parties mentioned, locations, translations, indexes, and downloadable exports. OHA guidance supports review, respect for desired privacy, and translation of key documents into the participant’s language [S21]. A public name, pseudonym, or omission is an explicit release decision.

TXT, VTT, SRT, or another agreed interchange file can be generated. Oral-History.Digital documentation describes per-passage editing and multiple transcript formats, but says transcripts are uploaded one media file at a time and mass upload is still in preparation [S06]. Test exports and round trips for text, language, speakers, timing, rights, and restriction scope before relying on an editor.

### P3 — “Search full text with a general database search index.”

**Disposition: correction; a general database index remains viable after access, privacy, and multilingual validation.** Search approved transcript text, interview metadata, and controlled index terms. Preserve language/script information, spelling variants, permitted speaker names, synonyms, and community-approved terms. Return a passage with an authorized time link. Apply the same policy to result text, snippets, audio, transcript, translation, and exports. Do not index raw ASR drafts or private consent records.

Public catalog metadata is not automatically safe. Before release, review whether names, aliases, locations, sensitive terms, translations, time-linked indexes, or snippets could identify a narrator or third party. Keep controlled identity separate from a participant-approved public name or pseudonym. A non-identifying catalog stub or index-only record can support discovery when audio or transcript stays restricted, if rights and privacy review allows it. Filter restricted material before it enters indexes and caches. After a restriction, suppress affected text and known links. Explain before release that downloads, quotations, or copies outside the archive may not be retractable [S21].

A relational database full-text feature may be sufficient at this scale; the evidence does not require a separate search platform. Oral-History.Digital is a useful broader comparator: its search covers transcripts, translations, index terms, and metadata and leads to a passage, but full-text search requires registration and archive access [S07]. Mukurtu offers community protocol mechanisms for different metadata and media permissions, but item and media settings must be kept aligned [S02–S03]. Decide between anonymous public search and account-gated search with community input. Measure multilingual recall, spelling variants, response time, privacy leakage, and revocation. No archive test can promise removal of third-party copies or external search caches.

### P4 — “One consent flag hides the public recording.”

**Disposition: correction plus community/operator decisions; one interview-level flag cannot represent the brief’s restrictions.** Store permission and access decisions at interview, media-asset, and time-bounded segment scope, with allowed viewers/actions, effective dates, review dates, and authority. Separate metadata visibility from audio, transcript, translation, excerpt, index, and internal consent documents. Apply and recheck restrictions across player, direct media URLs, range requests, downloads, search snippets, embeds, exports, and caches.

Mukurtu distinguishes open and strict protocols. For multiple protocols, All requires membership in every assigned protocol and is the default; Any requires membership in at least one. Item and media settings can diverge, so a metadata setting does not necessarily protect the media [S01–S03]. The pinned Mukurtu v4 README specifies PHP 8.4 support, MySQL/MariaDB, and private files outside the public web root for access control; it does not establish release maturity [S04]. Oral-History.Digital describes interview/media access states and archive grants [S05–S07]. OHMS’s XML-cache blocking change works only if the operator installs the matching Apache or Nginx rule; it blocks direct HTTP access to that cache, not external audio or every derivative [S16–S17]. None of this proves safe enforcement of an arbitrary time range within a one-hour media file.

Keep segment scope as a policy model but mark enforcement **unverified** for every considered product. Test direct byte/range requests, downloads, player seeking, old links, transcript and translation exports, indexes, and caches. If the chosen path cannot protect a restricted range, do not rely on a hidden player cue. Use a separately redacted or split public derivative with matching redacted text/index, or keep the material nonpublic until an approved safe path exists. Keep the authoritative master under its agreement. Choosing among split derivatives, authenticated streaming, or no public access is a community and technical decision for a sandbox demonstration.

Define withdrawal before release in two parts: the archive-controlled response (for example, stop new public access, remove known public derivatives/text/snippets, revoke known links, and report progress within a chosen target time) and limits beyond the archive (prior downloads, quotations, and third-party copies may remain). Provide a request route and explain this boundary to narrators before publication [S21]. A reversible public takedown can be the first operational state while an authorized curator checks the agreement. Whether masters and restricted copies are retained or destroyed depends on agreement, law, and adopted policy; do not silently decide it or promise total erasure. Record actor, reason, scope, effective time, and propagation status.

### P5 — “Keep WAV originals and MP3 listening copies.”

**Disposition: separate-original/listening-copy intent is already covered; correct fixed WAV/MP3 assumptions and add preservation controls.** Keep the received source file as the unchanged authoritative master, whether it arrived as WAV or another supported format. Create an MP3 or other streaming derivative only when measured browser, bandwidth, quality, and cost needs justify it; record lineage, encoding parameters, and rights. Never overwrite a master during cleanup, transcription, redaction, or migration. If adopting a normalized preservation format such as WAV/BWF, retain the received original as policy requires and record a migration/derivative event.

BagIt 1.0 packages payload and documentation with SHA-256/SHA-512 manifest checks; SHA-512 is recommended as the new-bag default. BagIt does not supply replicas, access control, or backups [S18]. PREMIS models digest identity, fixity-check events/outcomes, and time-bounded restrictions [S19]. Require separate verified replicas and a tested restore path. Export, migration, and restore must preserve internal restriction scope and omit restricted material from public derivatives, transcripts, indexes, and snippets. Confirm incoming formats, sizes, storage/egress costs, and permitted derivatives; none were observed.

### P6 — “Validate word accuracy on a few English clips.”

**Disposition: correction; a few English clips are a smoke test, not an acceptance basis.** Before choosing ASR, define a consented human-reviewed sample and language-specific thresholds with staff and community reviewers. Stratify by collection language, dialect, code-switching, recording era/quality, overlap, speaker count, and long silences. Report word/character error by language and relevant speaker group, speaker-attribution errors separately, timestamp alignment error, repeated/hallucinated text, correction effort, and permission/privacy handling. Keep uncertainty and failure cases visible. Failure for one language/class must leave a human-assisted or index-only route open.

Test the actual candidate version/configuration on community-relevant consented recordings after each material model or setting change [S10–S14]. A human reviewer, not a model score, approves publication. Thresholds and reviewer capacity are user/community decisions that must be set before a pilot.

## Independent critique adjudication

I accept the four material findings with these scope qualifications; the changes are integrated above and in validation below.

- **M1, narrator review and authorization — accepted.** OHA recommends review/approval where possible and formal authorization before public release. Record scope and decision details and gate every public derivative. Frame this as an archive ethical release policy informed by guidance, not a universal legal rule. Keep unclear legacy records restricted pending review.
- **M2, withdrawal limits — accepted.** Commit to a bounded archive response, known-link revocation, request route, and target chosen by the community. Do not promise removal of earlier downloads, quotations, or copies beyond archive control. Decide preservation-copy retention/destruction from the agreement and applicable policy.
- **M3, privacy and discoverability — accepted.** Review the catalog stub, names/aliases, locations, third-party references, translations, index terms, snippets, and exports. Public metadata is allowed only field-by-field after review; keep controlled identity separate from participant-approved names/pseudonyms. Include language-aware participant/community input where feasible.
- **M4, segment enforcement — accepted as uncertainty, not product correction.** A segment rule is useful in the policy model; evidence does not prove secure time-range enforcement across a one-hour recording. Test requests, seeking, old links, downloads, exports, and caches. If a path fails, use a redacted/split derivative with matching text/index or withhold public access. A player cue is not a security boundary.
- **Minor finding 1, low-cost criteria — accepted.** Set a spending ceiling/comparison window, count staff, review, translation, and maintenance hours, assign restore responsibility, and set a minimum public-access service level before choosing.
- **Minor finding 2, product ranking — accepted.** Mukurtu is only a conditional pilot preference if community protocols and self-hosting are priorities. Evidence does not rank total cost, accessibility, eligibility, maintainability, or segment control; no winner is selected.
- **Minor finding 3, restriction-preserving round trips — accepted.** Export/import, migration, and restore tests check internal restrictions and the absence of restricted content in public derivatives.
- **Minor finding 4, version maturity — retained as uncertainty.** Mukurtu v4 code requirements do not establish release maturity; the manual’s development status describes the manual. OHD page/repository labels do not identify the hosted deployment. OHMS’s historical PHP minimum does not establish current support. Verify actual deployment versions and operations in a pilot.

## Retained alternatives and conditions

- **Mukurtu CMS:** conditional pilot preference only where community-defined protocols, roles, and self-hosting matter. Its protocol model is relevant, but reviewed evidence does not show segment restrictions or overall superiority. Confirm supported release, private-file configuration, security/update operations, accessibility, staffing, backups/restores, and total cost. The Mukurtu 4 manual says the manual is in development; do not infer code maturity from that.
- **Oral-History.Digital:** hosted portal option with transcript editing, timecodes, translations, multilingual search, and archive access controls. Proceed only if eligibility, registration versus anonymous-public needs, bulk ingest, contracts, export/exit, data location, and segment withdrawal fit. A displayed software-page version 1.4.1 conflicts with repository release v2.11.0 at commit dc81cfa5ffab8819d2f168b5f7fcfe9ab876ede3; hosted deployment mapping remains unknown. Ask for a segment-withdrawal demonstration.
- **OHMS Viewer plus separate archive:** useful where media storage already exists and synchronized transcript/index viewing is wanted. It is a separate PHP deployment using exported XML and configured media hosting, not a preservation master store. Its README’s PHP 5.3+ floor is not evidence of current runtime support. The v3.10.15 cache rule must be correctly installed and protects only the XML cache path.
- **Human-first transcription/indexing:** supports volunteer agency and quality. Use a style guide, bilingual review, speaker correction, and explicit uncertainty. Use thematic indexing or metadata-only access where quality, consent, language coverage, or labor makes a full transcript unsuitable.
- **Whisper or another ASR:** optional local draft generation after permission and language-specific evaluation. Compare hosted processing only after data-handling, retention, and cost terms are reviewed. Do not assume a recognizer works equally across languages/speakers.

No option is selected from evidence alone. The decision depends on archive control, staff/hosting capacity, anonymous-public versus account-based search, language coverage, narrator/community authorization, withdrawal meaning and limits, agreement terms, and actual costs. Set cost ceiling, comparison period, staff/operator-hours budget, restore owner, and minimum access service level first.

## Cross-cutting records and workflow

Represent an interview separately from files and timed content: stable interview identifier; source/master audio; access derivatives; transcript versions by language; translations; speaker/person entries; timed transcript/index spans; rights/restriction records; editorial revisions; preservation events. Use a declared media time base and numeric start/end values with documented units. The brief does not decide word-level versus sentence/subject-level timing; sentence/segment timing may be a practical searchable-catalog start, with finer timing where volunteers and use cases justify it.

Use role-based editorial review. Volunteers suggest corrections and speaker labels; language-aware reviewers, narrators where feasible, and authorized curators review content, privacy, rights, and release. Include an explicit awaiting narrator/community authorization state. Keep drafts out of public search until approval. Separate unverified, needs-language-review, restricted, approved, and published states. Restrict consent documents and controlled identities. Review every public description, alias, translation, index term, and snippet for privacy and third-party risk. For access changes/withdrawals, log actor, reason, scope, effective time, target response, and propagation state.

Preservation policy defines accepted source formats, checksums, replicas, fixity schedule, restoration owner, derivatives, rights basis, and incident process. Keep public access copies separate from preservation masters. The archive must explain before release what can be withdrawn internally and what may remain outside its control. A low license price does not capture manual restoration, dependency updates, review labor, or the cost of finding leaked withdrawn text.
