## Material findings

**M1 — P3's EXIF correction cites evidence the record does not contain (S18).**
The draft corrects P3 with: publishing derivatives can leak embedded metadata — "Cantaloupe's own release history includes fixes for 'issues related to EXIF metadata parsing' (S18)". The retained S18 evidence (releases v4.1.9–v5.0.7, issue #634, PDFBox CVE entries) contains no such wording and no EXIF fact at all. On the retained record this quote is unverifiable — either the releases page said it and the evidence note dropped it, or the quote did not come from the observed fetch. The EXIF-leak *concern* is independently plausible (GPS/author fields on originals are a real risk for a public pipeline), so the correction's *content* may survive; its *citation* does not. Required in the final: re-derive this correction from a verifiable source (Cantaloupe CHANGES.md, or EXIF-stripping behavior of the chosen pipeline) or present it as reasoned risk rather than sourced fact. Uncertainty stated: without a fresh fetch the critic cannot tell which failure mode occurred.

**M2 — P4's "changing rights" mechanism presents a design inference as an S15 fact.**
Draft: "RightsStatements URIs versioned in-path, `/1.0/`; a change = new dated assertion (S15)". Verified against the retained S15 evidence: each of the 12 statement URIs embeds `/1.0/` — that is the *vocabulary's own version*, one value per statement, frozen since the vocabulary's 1.0 release. It does not version per-object assertions. The "dated assertion" change-record pattern appears in the evidence note's applicability paragraph as a *recommendation* ("a record's rights row can carry an immutable statement URI plus application date") — a sound design the museum must build, not a property the data model provides. The draft's citation makes it read as evidence-backed. The P4 rejection does not collapse (the nightly-latency argument stands on its own; tiering is supported by S03's 302/401 mechanics, and TK Labels' conditions-imply-tiers by S16), but the final must restate this as: the fixed 12-statement vocabulary + a museum-built assertion table. Uncertainty: none about the evidence content; the finding is about claim-vs-evidence fit.

**M3 — Discovery's SQLite release chain (3.44.x–3.51.x) is not in the retained record.**
The draft's cross-cutting finding "FTS5 is stable but its edge features churn; pin and re-verify" leans on a seven-item release history (3.44.1/3.44.2 same-day fuzzing fix, 3.45.1 integrity_check regression, 3.46.1 secure-delete false positives, 3.47.0 locale tokenizers, 3.50.2/3.50.3 fixes, 3.51.1 fts5vocab bug). None of it appears in the retained S01 evidence file, which retains only the §2.1 version-bound facts (3.42.0 secure-delete, 3.43.0 contentless-delete, the format-error message). This weakens the O3 chain for FTS5 specifically: on the retained record, one release-chain link (Cantaloupe, S18) is fully evidenced and one (OpenSeadragon, S17) is fully evidenced, but the third rests on unretained claims. Required in the final: re-fetch and retain the SQLite release evidence, or mark the FTS5 churn claim as asserted-not-evidenced. Uncertainty: the claims are plausible (they name specific versions and symptoms) but the critic cannot confirm them from the frozen record.

**M4 — The brief's exports obligation is never designed.**
The brief requires curators to need "transparent corrections **and exports**" (O4/O5 obligations echo corrections and exports as paired curator needs). The draft designs corrections thoroughly (P5's assertion-then-apply workflow) but exports appear only incidentally — CollectiveAccess "batch import/export" (S13) in the platform alternative, nowhere for the thin custom stack the draft's retained findings actually lean toward. An exports gap is consequential: the chosen single-file SQLite architecture makes export the museum's own feature (format, scope, rights filtering, scheduled vs on-demand), and the brief's obligation does not discharge itself. Required in the final: an export mechanism consistent with the recommended stack, or an explicit user-decision entry naming the deferral. This is an omission finding under O4, not a disposition error.

**M5 — Deletion lifecycle is unaddressed where it matters most.**
Rights changes and takedowns (P4's whole subject) require *removal* from the public surface. The draft covers FTS5 insert/update trigger sync (P1) and separately notes sqlite-vec's v0.1.9 headline bug was DELETE operations (S11), but never connects them: deletes from catalog → FTS5 external-content index → vector table → any serialized client index are four surfaces needing one coherent removal path, and the one component with a recent delete bug is the vector store. The IIIF-static tier adds a fifth surface (pre-generated files for a delisted object). Minor in a greenfield build; material here because the draft's own recommended architecture (client-side or single-file, tiered per P4) makes the delete path load-bearing. Required in the final: a deletion/removal design note tied to P4's propagation mechanism.

## Minor findings

1. **S16 tally note is arithmetically muddled.** The retained evidence writes "6+9+6 listed names = 21" and separately a fetcher tally "19 in the listing: 6 + 9 + 5" (which sums to 20, not 19), while listing 5 Permission names. The draft's "says 19 while listing 6+9+6" matches the source-map's "20-21" range and the *uncertainty* is honestly carried; only the note's internal arithmetic is sloppy. No design consequence beyond the existing Hub-verification condition.
2. **remove_diacritics nuance (S01/C02).** Live check confirms the diacritics-removed default but adds that at default `remove_diacritics=1` multi-diacritic codepoints are not folded (fixed at `2`). The discovery's "cafe matches café" framing is right for the common case; the V2 tokenizer test should include the edge cases. No disposition changes.
3. **P6 keeps the ten curator queries "as a regression slice" without criteria.** Reasonable (cheap expert-vocabulary probes catch regressions) but the retention is asserted, not argued; state the criteria (e.g., known-item retrieval sanity) rather than leaving it as sentiment.
4. **Partial fetches are honestly recorded but should stay visibly conditional in the final.** S12/S13/S14 are landing-page-only; search depth for Omeka S and Pawtucket2 is unverified; Wax's fit bounds are inferred. The draft carries these as gaps — keep them visibly conditional so the engine/platform user-decision isn't read as adjudicated.
5. **Inference labeling is inconsistent in two places.** "Level0 servable from pre-generated static files" (P3) is an architectural inference from the S02 compliance levels (sound, but stated flatly); OpenSeadragon intermediate-release years are inferred and *are* labeled (good). Match the labeling standard everywhere.
6. **S18 provenance flag needs to travel.** The CVE-2019-0228→4.1.1 and CVE-2023-37460→v5.0.7 attributions come from search-result summaries, not fetched pages (flagged in source-map). V8's dependency scan should re-verify these from CHANGES.md before they inform the image-server decision.

## False corrections/rejections and invalid critic demands (explained, not repaired)

The critic raised three demands during targeting that the record does **not** support; they are withdrawn here rather than imposed on the candidate:

1. **"Make image search a P2 correction" — invalid.** The brief lists images as content to handle, not a query-by-image capability. CLIP (S10) is correctly an optional enhancement, blocked on unverified card facts. Demanding promotion would manufacture a correction the plan's text does not contain.
2. **"Resolve the unobservable data facts before the final" — invalid.** Field inventory, description-length distribution, and restriction taxonomy are museum-data facts no stage of this experiment could observe (no runtime, no dataset). The draft correctly registers all three as uncertain and gates P1/P2/P4 on them; O6 explicitly permits honest unavailability. The final must keep them as stated conditions, not assume values.
3. **"Overturn P4's rejection because legal takedown needs removal" — invalid as stated.** The community/counsel acceptability question is a genuine user decision (the draft assigns it); the critic's role is to demand the rejection be stated conditionally (done above), not to flip the disposition on policy grounds the evidence cannot adjudicate.

## Answers to the draft's four carried questions (for the final to adjudicate)

1. **Tiered access vs legal takedown:** keep as user decision; the design answer is that full removal must exist as a per-class tier outcome (M5's delete path), so counsel can elect removal where required without re-architecture.
2. **CLIP image vectors:** remain blocked until dimension/text-limit/license are verified from config or the paper (S10 records the absence); adopt as optional enhancement only after V-calibrated value is shown.
3. **Ten-query suite:** survives as the known-item expert-probe slice of the mixed suite (with stated criteria — minor 3), not as the validation standard.
4. **Engine ordering under P4:** P4 forces a split, not elimination: a public index (which may be client-side/static) containing only unrestricted records, plus a server-side index with per-request filtering if restricted material must remain discoverable-as-existing at a lower tier. If a class is legally removed outright, the public index simply excludes it — the delete path (M5) serves both cases.
