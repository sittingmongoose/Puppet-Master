# B-07 / I07 — revised complete planning recommendation

**Scope.** This is a complete planning result for the brief's 20-business shared kitchen, three coordinators, six-tenant pilot, and stated $21,000 system budget. It incorporates the full post-reveal research draft, the full independent verification package, and the full critic package, then checks the consequential disputes against public primary sources. Evidence IDs beginning R refer to this reviser's source index; immutable source IDs in predecessor maps remain namespaced by their original map (research:Sxx, verifier:Sxx, critic:Cxx) and are not reassigned.

**Status.** No product or implementation path is selected. Vendor pages and help articles describe their vendors' published behavior, not a live demonstration, contractual promise, or test. No vendor account, qualified sandbox, code/database witness, tenant dataset, tablet, screen reader, budget quote, board decision, or local jurisdiction was available. The recommendations and validations below are proposed; they did not run.

## Recommendation and scope boundary

Run a time-boxed comparison of The Food Corridor, Spacebring, and Skedda, while keeping two alternatives visible: a scheduler paired with a tenant-private signoff and archive service, and a small custom reservation core with a calendar interface. Use paper as the stated transition process and outage fallback. None of the public sources establishes a candidate's fit for atomic room/equipment reservation, tenant-visible fields, tenant-defined cleaning attestations, a complete tenant archive, accessibility, and the $21,000 budget together.

The product must cover the whole period each tenant can access a room or equipment item. It must reduce double-bookings by rejecting conflicting confirmations for exclusive resources, or by applying a documented capacity rule where a resource is shareable. A calendar display, pending approval, or coordinator memory is not a reservation invariant. The system should keep recipes, customer information, and production volumes from other tenants; it may retain tenant-owned information in access-controlled records when there is a defined need. The shared kitchen's record should say what a tenant attested and when, not certify effective cleaning, allergen removal, or food safety.

The budget period and inclusions are not specified. Before a selection, define whether $21,000 covers implementation, a year of service, multiple years, or the whole lifecycle. Obtain comparable written quotes for six pilot tenants and twenty-business capacity, three coordinators, the count of rooms/equipment resources, the chosen record/export path, data retention, onboarding, transaction fees, integrations, hardware, support, accessibility work, and exit/migration. Do not double-count a platform fee that already includes payment processing.

## Material findings and mechanisms

### 1. Reservation correctness and setup/cleanup

A useful model is one room access interval from setup start through cleanup end, plus a separate interval for each equipment item used only part of the room block. These intervals should be associated with stable resource IDs and local time-zone policy. First classify each resource as exclusive, capacity-limited, or divisible; a simple binary overlap rule is correct only for an exclusive resource. If a booking needs both room and equipment, confirmation should reserve both together, or show a non-confirmed partial state that cannot be mistaken for a complete reservation.

The verifier asked how Skedda behaves in requested, approved, and changed states. Its current published approval guide says a request is pending and not shown as a scheduler booking; multiple conflicting requests can coexist. Admin approval creates the confirmed booking, with the approver able to edit request details in the approval form. Only admins edit approved bookings; an Outlook date/time edit after approval deletes the confirmed booking and returns it to pending. The documentation does not establish that every admin edit rechecks buffers/conflicts or how concurrent approvals serialize. System users can book over buffer periods, and approval rules do not apply to custom admins for the spaces they manage. Treat pending as a candidate, approved as the reservation point, and changed as a state requiring revalidation in a demonstration; do not infer conflict safety from a queue. [R01–R03]

Skedda buffers are useful only where equal before/after gaps fit the operation. A one-hour symmetric gap represents 30 minutes before and 30 minutes after, not independent setup and cleanup durations. Existing bookings are not retroactively given the new rule. Coordinators/system users can bypass the buffer, so any exception should be governed and auditable. [R02–R03]

A custom core can use [start,end) as a proposed convention and resource-specific overlap exclusion, but the proposal must address nonempty bounded intervals, start-before-end, time zone and daylight-saving ambiguity, pending/confirmed/cancelled/changed semantics, atomic room-plus-equipment allocation, and elevated-role exceptions. A failed equipment allocation should roll back the room allocation or visibly leave the room reservation incomplete. No database witness ran. [R13, R15–R16]

### 2. Tenant privacy, visibility, and records

The exact P2 boundary names recipes, customer information, and production volumes. A tenant may need its own private record; the privacy test is whether another tenant can see it. Keep sensitive fields out of shared booking titles, public profiles/community messages, notifications, attachments, and broad exports unless there is a defined need and effective tenant-only access.

Food Corridor documentation establishes calendar/daily occupancy visibility and a Community/profile path, but does not say which other tenant booking fields are visible. That makes it an exposure path to test, not evidence of a P2 breach. Skedda's default regular-user display includes date, time, and space; it hides identity unless rules expose it, while system users always see holder/title/custom fields. A detailed occupancy display may help avoid conflicts but could reveal a tenant's schedule; whether to show exact occupancy, anonymous availability, or per-resource availability is a separate product choice, not a requirement already made by P2. [R05, R17]

A portable tenant-owned signoff might record tenant, individual signer, booking/access range, equipment, attestation time, procedure reference if the tenant wants one, and source/entry metadata for paper transcription. Keep an original paper sheet or traceable archive and append corrections. The current Food Corridor guide documents a client PIN and cleanliness/equipment concern notes, not individual cleaning-completion attestation; kitchen staff can add/edit sign-in records per a separate help article. Distinguish identity for the business account from identity for the human signer. Do not make a shared kitchen checklist the silent source of each tenant's cleaning method. [R06–R07]

For an eventual custom service, RLS is defense in depth, not a turn-key tenant boundary. Test reads, insert/update/delete, assigned tenant context, pooled-connection switching, attachments, notification payloads, and exports for tenant A versus B. Test coordinator roles separately. Avoid returning another tenant's private title or data in conflict errors. [R16]

### 3. Annual records and inactivity

The brief requires a way to export each tenant's own records at year-end; it does not expressly require self-service. A kitchen-generated tenant-specific extract with an agreed secure recipient/delivery path may meet the need if the board accepts it. Record the exact fields and prove there are no other tenants' rows.

The Food Corridor account page says tenants can view reports about their bookings, invoices, and sign-in/out; its current reporting article documents kitchen-side CSV reports filterable by client/date. Its All Bookings report is a current-year snapshot across listed booking states, while the report descriptions do not establish tenant-initiated CSV download or an archive of cleaning attestations, equipment-use episodes, or attachments. Its current inactive-account page says a tenant retains access to its account, past invoices, booking history, and reports; it does not state download format, full scope, or retention duration. Therefore neither tenant report viewing, a coordinator CSV, nor inactive-account access alone satisfies the export obligation. Skedda's visible export path is system-user-only and its default booking/visit retention is one year, so test annual export timing and separate external archive storage if that candidate remains in view. [R05, R08–R09, R18, R04]

The archive acceptance dataset should contain booking and equipment-use rows, tenant and signer identities, original attestation and entry times, status/history, correction records, attachments if any, stable identifiers, resource names, and time-zone meaning. Test a year boundary (including Dec 31/Jan 1), an inactive/departed tenant, deletion/retention limits, and delivery to the tenant in a non-proprietary readable format. Test each tenant's archive independently from the administrator report.

### 4. Signoff meaning and food-code applicability

A signoff is an attributable statement that a tenant submitted a record under its own procedure. It does not by itself show that a person cleaned correctly, that an allergen was removed, or that food is safe. Keep statuses such as missing, late, disputed, corrected, and submitted separate from whether an equipment item is ready for use. An account PIN may identify a tenant account; it does not establish individual identity.

The FDA Food Code 2026 is a model offered for adoption by local, state, tribal, territorial, and federal jurisdictions, not automatically applicable federal law. Its Preface says the model is not federal law or regulation and describes adoption through statutes, regulations, or ordinances. The edition adds a “commissary” term and says operations supporting food offered directly to consumers fall under its framework; operations intended to distribute to another business entity align with the food-processing-plant definition. The regulatory authority determines facility suitability and permitting. The brief gives neither kitchen jurisdiction nor tenant sales channels; do not assume that this edition or one common cleaning instruction governs every tenant. Determine the applicable adopted edition and regulator only if the product or procurement decision depends on it. [R11–R12]

### 5. Accessibility and transition

The entrance tablet is a physical-use requirement, not satisfied by a WCAG target size alone. SC 2.5.8 sets a 24×24 CSS-pixel target baseline with exceptions; SC 2.5.7 requires a single-pointer alternative without dragging for covered functionality, unless an exception applies. The WAI explanation distinguishes this from keyboard access. Use them only as an acceptance basis the kitchen chooses; test actual touch behavior with wet hands and actual assistive-technology flows separately. [R13–R14]

Use scripted accessibility scenarios for booking search, status inspection, conflict handling, signoff review, approval, filters, export, form validation, focus movement, and status/error announcements. Name the tested screen-reader/browser/device configuration. For the entrance device, also test prior-session leakage, kiosk reset, identity confirmation, screen readability in kitchen lighting, and a direct create/select flow instead of mandatory dragging. Food Corridor's guide documents both a drag/drop booking route and a create control; neither means the accessible flow has passed. [research:S07]

During paper/digital overlap, choose one operating mode before the pilot: paper as the authoritative signoff with digital shadow/reconciliation; or digital after a stated cutover with paper as outage fallback. Train the coordinators, define a source of truth, keep original sheets for the agreed period, and decide how to record a late transcription, disagreement, duplicate, correction, and outage retry. Internet availability does not prove that a submission was received.

### 6. Issue/fix/release evidence

FullCalendar issue #6393 reported bookings stacked visually in DayGridDay on version 5.8.0 with eventOrderStrict:true. The issue was confirmed, assigned to v5.9.0, and closed with fix commit 6acf6b2; the v5.9.0 release record identifies the rendering fix, and its tag points to commit 620efb5e9c023f229a22af31e10b8b810fa5b2ba. The changed code concerns segment hierarchy and event placement. Current v7.1.1 documentation scopes eventOverlap to drag/resize interactions and defaults it to true. This is a real UI rendering regression/fix chain and a reason to test calendar rendering after upgrades; it does not establish persisted double-bookings or a reservation defect in any kitchen product. [R23–R26]

## Discriminating validation plan

These are proposals, not executed checks.

1. **Reservation invariant:** In a qualified vendor demo or test witness, use six separate tenant accounts and at least two rooms/equipment items. Submit simultaneous conflicting requests for the same exclusive resource and combined room-plus-equipment. Verify exactly one complete conflicting booking can become confirmed; test partial overlap, exact adjacency under any chosen interval convention, asymmetric setup/cleanup, equipment used for only part of room time, resource capacity, pending versus approved labels, edits, retries, cancellations, rule changes, and coordinator/system exceptions. Confirm a failed equipment allocation rolls back or visibly leaves the room reservation incomplete.
2. **Privacy and availability:** Switch between tenant A, tenant B, and coordinator roles. Inspect booking calendar, daily view, title/identity, business profile/community, messages, attachments, notifications, APIs if offered, and exports. Record whether recipes, customer data, production volume, or tenant procedure text cross the boundary. Separately ask users what occupancy, time, resource, or business identity should be visible.
3. **Attestation and handoff:** Use two tenant-authored procedures on shared equipment. Test person identity versus tenant account/PIN, paper source and coordinator transcription, original attestation time versus entry time, missing/late/disputed/corrected status, duplicate submissions, outage/retry, source-sheet retention, and correction history. Confirm a submitted signoff is never represented as proof of safe food or effective allergen removal.
4. **Annual archive and exit:** Export one tenant's full-year booking/equipment-use data and signoffs around Dec 31/Jan 1; repeat after inactivation/departure and near retention/deletion boundaries. Check stable IDs, timestamps/time zones, signer and transcriber, original/corrected history, attachments, status, equipment rows, readable format, and absence of all other tenants' records. Record who creates and securely delivers each tenant's export.
5. **Accessibility and entrance device:** Name actual tablet/browser and coordinator screen-reader/browser pair. Complete the coordinator task flow using screen reader and keyboard, including errors/status/focus announcements and export. Independently test the entrance device with wet hands, representative lighting, target sizes, direct non-drag controls, kiosk reset, session privacy, and tenant identity confirmation. If WCAG 2.2 AA is selected, check both relevant pointer criteria as well.
6. **Paper transition/outage:** Before pilot, document which record is authoritative and who reconciles. Simulate internet interruption during booking and signoff, later re-entry, duplicate submission, disagreement between paper and digital, correction, and recovery. Preserve original authorship and timestamps.
7. **Cost and pilot exit:** Obtain a dated quote and compare SaaS, scheduler-plus-private-signoff, custom, and paper/hybrid on the same budget horizon. Track collisions, setup/cleanup conflicts, privacy defects, missing/disputed signoffs, inaccessible essential tasks, export completeness, coordinator workload, support, fees, retention, and migration. Define exit criteria for the six-tenant pilot before live data is used.
