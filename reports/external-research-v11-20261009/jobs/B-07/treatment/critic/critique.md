# B-07 / I07 — independent critic package

**Stage:** treatment / critic; method M15.  
**Reviewed:** the exact I07 brief; the revealed P1–P6 plan; the complete same-arm research draft; its discovery note, verification questions, source map, and evidence index.  
**Independent evidence:** primary public documents indexed as C01–C15 in [`sources/index.md`](sources/index.md) and [`source-map.json`](source-map.json).  
**Boundary:** I did not open the treatment/verifier source tree, review packages, evaluator materials, campaign/history files, or other arms. No product account, live vendor runtime, qualified sandbox, database witness, tenant interview, screen-reader test, or tablet test was available or run. The linked vendor material is mutable vendor documentation, not observed product behavior.

## Overall assessment

The research draft is unusually thorough for a small brief: it preserves the tenant privacy and food-safety boundaries, compares a kitchen-specific service, a general scheduler, a second vertical product, a custom reservation core, and paper/hybrid operation, and keeps implementation, procurement, and accessibility checks proposed rather than claiming they ran. Its issue-to-fix chain is properly limited to calendar rendering. The final adjudication additions also catch important issues around resource capacity, atomic reservation, signoff identity, inactive-tenant records, and administrator authority.

Two material corrections are needed before treating the draft as settled planning guidance. First, its P1 disposition says the plan already covers the requirement and calls concurrency/resource reservation an acceptance elaboration. The exact P1 clause names setup, production, and cleanup intervals, but it does not state that a conflicting resource booking cannot be confirmed. The brief says the current calendar can collide and asks for double-booking reduction. That invariant is a missing requirement/acceptance condition, not just an implementation detail to assume from the existing clause. Second, the draft's closing additions call Spacebring euro-billed, whereas the current public pricing page rendered USD pricing in my 2026-10-09 fetch. The displayed currency can be regional or page-state dependent, so neither observation is a kitchen quote; the difference must be recorded as mutable/locale uncertainty and resolved with a dated quote.

The Food Corridor and Skedda findings are directionally careful, but person-level signoff identity, tenant-to-tenant account/community exposure, the complete export boundary, and how board decisions affect pilot configuration should be moved into the main operative findings rather than left mainly in a late “adjudicated additions” section. No exact P-clause merits rejection. Several interpretations are rightly rejected; see below.

## Material findings

### M1 — P1 needs a testable booking-conflict requirement

The plan says to schedule blocks with setup, production, and cleanup intervals. It does not explicitly say that overlapping requests on an exclusive room/equipment resource are prevented from becoming confirmed reservations. Because the brief identifies double-bookings as an existing problem, the research draft should classify P1 as **retain with a material clarification/correction**: keep all three intervals, and state that confirmation enforces the full access interval for every exclusive resource, including concurrent writes and role-based overrides. A pending request or visual calendar layout must not count as a reservation lock.

The draft's distinction between room access and equipment-use ranges is useful: equipment may be reserved for only part of a room block. The record model should represent whether each resource is exclusive, divisible, or capacity-limited before choosing a binary overlap rule. For multiple resources, room and equipment allocation should either be confirmed atomically or leave a clearly visible partial state that cannot be mistaken for a confirmed complete booking. A failed equipment leg must not leave an apparently successful room reservation. The proposed `[start,end)` convention is a reasonable implementation choice, not a brief requirement; disclose it as a convention, retain the wall-clock/time-zone and daylight-saving rules, and test its adjacency semantics.

The cited PostgreSQL range/exclusion mechanism is an appropriate *design witness* for one exclusive resource and a well-formed interval, but not a runtime result or a whole application. It does not by itself establish resource capacity, cancellation/pending-state policy, multi-row atomicity, non-null/bounded ranges, recurring changes, cross-system sync, or handling of elevated users. The draft's later qualification about resource model, range checks, transactions, and daylight-saving policy is sound and should be tied directly to the P1 disposition. [C09]

Skedda approvals are not equivalent to that invariant: requests remain pending and off the scheduler until approved; multiple conflicting requests can coexist, admins choose which to approve, approval rules are plan-gated, and custom admins on spaces they manage are exempt. Its buffers create symmetric gaps, only affect bookings made after the rule is added, and system users can book across them. Those are conditions for a demo/migration check, not proof that confirmed bookings cannot race or overlap. [C01–C02]

### M2 — P2 covers named information, while occupancy and social/profile data are separate questions

The plan correctly protects recipes, customer information, and production volumes. The draft is right to test calendar, alert, attachment, export, and tenant-role paths rather than checking only the calendar title. It is also right to leave whether another tenant may infer occupancy timing as a separate policy choice: the brief does not prohibit seeing an occupied slot, and some visibility may help tenants avoid collisions.

Tighten the wording “keep these data fields out of the shared schedule and records”: the requirement is that other tenants do not see those fields, not that an authorized tenant can never keep its own data in its own private records. Minimize data that the kitchen-operated system does not need, and test visibility separately for tenant A, tenant B, and coordinator roles.

The current Food Corridor account guide says business users can see the kitchen's space/equipment schedule, report views, and a Community tab where users can see and message fellow clients; it describes business-profile fields including products, descriptions, category, and public contact details. This does not establish that recipes, customer lists, or production volumes are exposed, so it is not by itself proof of a P2 breach. It does establish a non-calendar visibility path and makes a field-by-field demo important. Keep the prohibited fields out of public profiles and community messages, and test whether those modules can be disabled or constrained for this pilot. [C03, C15]

### M3 — P3 must distinguish a tenant, a person, and a transcribed paper record

The draft correctly preserves tenant authorship and warns that “submitted” is not proof of effective physical cleaning or allergen removal. Its later addition to separate tenant identity, individual signer, and coordinator transcription is material and should be part of the main P3 treatment.

The Food Corridor's published digital sign-in/sign-out mechanism assigns a four-digit PIN to a client account; it describes hour reconciliation and allows kitchen staff to add/edit sign-in data. Its account article says there can be additional email addresses for users but one account password. Those statements concern attendance/account-level events, not a named person's cleaning attestation. They do not show that the application identifies which person made a signoff, that an account PIN is private to one signer, or that later edits retain original authorship/time. [C03–C04]

If a coordinator enters a paper signoff later, record at least the tenant-supplied attestation time separately from the digital entry time and identify the source paper/transcriber. Corrections should preserve who changed what and when rather than silently replacing the assertion. Keep “missing,” “late,” “disputed,” “corrected,” “tenant signoff submitted,” equipment readiness, a coordinator observation, and a food-safety judgment distinct. Procedure-version metadata or a batch reference may help traceability, but is optional and should not collect proprietary recipe/procedure content without an agreed need.

### M4 — P4 is not satisfied by target size or a marketing claim

The draft correctly treats WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8's 24×24 CSS-pixel target threshold (with exceptions) as a measurable baseline, not as proof of wet-hand use. WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.7 separately requires a single-pointer alternative to nonessential dragging where the criterion applies. These are relevant acceptance aids, but the brief does not name a legal conformance target or say that every tenant-facing screen must conform to a particular WCAG level. If the kitchen adopts WCAG 2.2 AA for procurement, state the scope and test both criteria; do not present them as an already selected legal obligation. Neither criterion proves a screen-reader-compatible coordinator workflow. [C08]

The plan/draft should name the actual tablet, browser, screen-reader/browser pair, and essential coordinator tasks for the demo. Test keyboard and screen-reader completion, focus/status/error announcements, navigation and export; independently test wet-hand operation, lighting, session reset, and identity confirmation at the entrance. A direct create/select path is preferable to requiring drag/drop, but it remains a proposal until demonstrated.

### M5 — P4 paper coexistence needs an explicit operating rule, not an invented authority

The brief requires paper signoff sheets to continue during transition but does not say which record is authoritative. The draft's alternatives (paper authoritative with digital shadow/reconciliation, or digital authoritative after cutover with paper as outage fallback) are useful and material. An accountable operations owner must choose and train one before the pilot; do not assume internet availability resolves outages or discrepancies. Define duplicate handling, late entry, correction, outage recovery, retained original sheets, and reconciliation responsibility. Do not attribute that choice to the board unless the board actually owns it.

### M6 — P5 and P6 remain decisions; proposed pilot defaults need explicit acceptance

P5 is correctly retained as a board decision. The draft's non-blocking missing-signoff indicator is a reasonable candidate pilot behavior, but it must not become a configured default while the board's authority decision is unresolved. The draft says both that no rule may be selected/inherited and that the pilot can show missing signoffs without blocking; its later addition says this interim behavior needs board acceptance. Reconcile those statements: present non-blocking display/reporting as an option and require explicit acceptance before use. Separate a tenant booking sanction from marking equipment unavailable, coordinator review, and a cleanup observation; each has different authority and operational effect.

P6 is also correctly left open. The draft should retain its alternatives—tenant document reference, free-text attestation, configurable checklist, equipment-specific form—and the possibility of no common procedure template. A shared record envelope can support identity/resource/booking/time without deciding the procedure representation. Do not silently turn optional metadata into a standard cleaning procedure.

### M7 — Year-end export must cover the required record set and retention boundary

The draft correctly rejects the inference that coordinator CSV reports or a kiosk/tablet workflow automatically satisfy each tenant's year-end export need. A current Food Corridor reporting article documents CSV downloads and client/date filters, but its “All Bookings” report is a current-year view; other report types cover payments, bookings, clients, and sign-in activity. A separate account guide describes tenant reports for bookings, invoices, and sign-in/out, but does not establish a tenant-initiated complete annual download for the required records. Its inactive-account article says past invoices and reports remain accessible when a client is marked inactive, but it is dated 2020 and does not establish the signoff attachments, data scope, download format, or retention promise. These distinctions support a live test rather than a yes/no claim. [C03–C06]

Specify a tenant-scoped archive schema and test boundary: booking and equipment-use rows, original signoffs/corrections, identity and timestamps, attachments if any, status/history, stable IDs, and time zone. Test a full-year interval across Dec 31/Jan 1, tenant departure/inactivation, reactivation, and provider retention/deletion limits. Clarify whether kitchen staff may generate and securely deliver the export; tenant self-service is a possible design, not an explicit brief requirement. If Skedda remains a candidate, its public page says new accounts default to one-year booking/visit retention, plan support affects longer retention, and downgrades do not reset the configured period; include that in the quote/exit plan. [C14]

### M8 — Candidate and budget comparisons are good but not decision-ready

The candidate set is materially diverse and sufficient for this scope: kitchen-native operations software, a second kitchen/community platform, a general scheduler, a custom booking core/calendar, and paper/hybrid operation. The later reference to a scheduler plus a tenant-private signoff form/service is a useful middle option and should remain visible beside custom build. The brief does not establish that the product must combine calendar, reporting, and cleaning attestation in one vendor.

The $21,000 has no stated period or inclusions. The draft properly asks whether it covers implementation, one year, or multiple years and asks for a quote that covers 20 businesses, a six-tenant pilot, and three coordinators. It should make a comparable cost worksheet the next decision gate: product/plan and room/equipment resource count; pilot and 20-tenant price; payment amount and mix; platform/processor fees without double-counting; onboarding/integration/hardware; accessible-device support; support; retention/export; custom maintenance/hosting; and exit/migration. No cost conclusion follows from a vendor headline price.

A source-state conflict needs explicit handling. The predecessor reports that Spacebring is euro-billed. In this critic's open of its live pricing page, the Business price rendered as **$178/month**, with a six-month minimum; it also showed 100 monthly active users included and member-app/API add-ons priced separately. This could reflect region/locale, a page change, or a different captured state. It is not evidence that the predecessor was fabricated or that a U.S.-dollar quote applies to this kitchen. Preserve the two observations as mutable-source drift and require a dated quote in the kitchen's billing currency, including add-ons and contract term. [C13]

### M9 — The custom technical proposal and FullCalendar issue chain are properly bounded

PostgreSQL 18 documents `[)` range constructors and exclusion constraints for overlapping ranges, which support the draft's custom-core illustration. The draft correctly says that it has not installed or run this design and that application states, resource rows, roles, non-null/bounded intervals, transactions, and migrations need testing. Keep this as a conditional mechanism, not a selection, and include least-privileged roles, RLS policies for read/write/delete, attachment/export/notification paths, pooled-connection tenant switching, and sanitized conflict responses if a custom design proceeds. RLS is defense in depth and must be enabled/tested; the source makes clear that table owners and bypass roles are exceptions. [C09]

The FullCalendar #6393 chain is real and appropriately scoped: the report concerns DayGridDay event rendering with `eventOrderStrict`; the fix commit identifies the #6393 issue and changes segment hierarchy/event placement. Current `eventOverlap` docs describe drag/resize interactions and default to `true`. This is relevant to calendar display and UI regression checks, but it does **not** demonstrate persisted booking overlap, atomicity, or a defect in any named vendor. It meets the issue/fix/release obligation only with that narrow transfer limit, which the draft states. [C10–C12]

### M10 — The source-state appendix should be folded into the usable draft

The draft's last section says “full independent verifier, critic, and reviser packages are saved in review/” and labels later additions authoritative. That phrasing is process-specific and those external review packages are outside the planning draft's self-contained evidence trail. The substantive additions themselves are useful, but final product guidance should cite the source IDs and exact evidence directly rather than depend on unprovided review packages. The predecessor source map records S24–S26 with no precise access time. My independent primary-source opens now record the relevant public facts under C03–C05 with an observed access batch timestamp; they do not rely on the verifier package. Retain immutable IDs in each map and do not silently rewrite the original S IDs. [C03–C05]

A later planning decision may choose among compliant products or workflows, but no candidate passes based on these documents alone.

## Minor findings and editorial improvements

1. Keep proposed acceptance tests distinct from executed source inspection. The draft mostly does this correctly; retain the explicit “none run” status in the final planning view.
2. Put the governing P1 correction and the P5 interim-choice condition in the disposition table itself, not only in late additions, so a reader cannot adopt the earlier “already covered” or non-blocking wording alone.
3. Treat schedule occupancy inference as a question for users/board. The brief names the information that must remain private; it does not specify anonymous availability, exact occupancy, or per-resource visibility.
4. For WCAG, say whether the kitchen voluntarily selects 2.2 AA for the coordinator flow. Do not imply SC 2.5.7 or 2.5.8 directly verifies screen-reader support or the tablet's wet-hand use.
5. Do not treat Food Corridor's food-business account reports or inactive-account behavior as proof of export completeness. Reports for bookings/invoices/sign-in are not evidence of equipment cleaning attestations or attachments.
6. Avoid treating plan and price pages as stable. The Spacebring currency observation above is a concrete reason to preserve dated page state and request a quote; other vendor feature/price claims also need demo/contract verification.
7. The Food Code reference is appropriately used as a status/applicability limit. No jurisdiction is provided, so it cannot answer which local requirement applies or assign this kitchen's legal role.

## Discriminating validation priorities

All entries below are proposals; none was executed in this critic stage or in the referenced predecessor's stated runtime scope.

1. **Reservation invariant:** In a vendor demo or qualified witness, submit simultaneous requests against the same exclusive room/resource and room-plus-equipment combination. Exactly one complete conflicting reservation may become confirmed. Test pending vs confirmed, partial overlap, adjacency, coordinator/system overrides, reschedule, cancel/retry, equipment capacity, and rollback if one leg fails.
2. **Privacy:** Use separate synthetic tenant accounts and coordinator/admin roles. Inspect schedule, profile/community, messages, notifications, attachments, API (if offered), export, and account switching. Record exactly whether any recipe, customer data, production quantity, tenant procedure, business identity, or occupancy detail crosses a tenant boundary; distinguish prohibited fields from optional occupancy policy.
3. **Attestation and transition:** Compare tenant-created digital signoff to a paper sample; test individual identity, kiosk session reset, tenant account PIN, coordinator transcription, late entry, correction, duplicate submission, and time/network failure. Verify original attestation time and source remain reconstructable.
4. **Archive/exit:** Generate a full tenant-only export for a year boundary, an inactive/departed tenant, and the retention boundary. Check stable identifiers, equipment-use rows, signoffs, corrections, attachments, timestamps/zones, and no foreign tenant rows. Confirm a non-proprietary readable format and documented delivery path.
5. **Accessibility/device:** With named hardware/browser/screen-reader, complete coordinator booking search, conflict resolution, signoff review, status/error handling, and export by keyboard/screen reader. Independently test all essential drag-dependent operations by a single pointer without dragging if WCAG 2.2 AA is selected, and conduct wet-hand/lighting/session tests on the actual entrance tablet.
6. **Paper/outage:** Run a planned pilot period with an agreed authority rule; disconnect/reconnect during a signoff and booking submission. Measure duplicate/lost records, reconcile disagreement with paper, preserve attribution, and confirm a clear retry state.
7. **Cost gate:** Obtain a written quote for six pilot tenants and 20-business capacity, with three coordinators, the actual resource count, billing/payment mix, full-year retention/export needs, selected hardware, onboarding, support, and total budget period. Compare SaaS, scheduler-plus-private-signoff, and custom lifecycle cost on the same horizon.
