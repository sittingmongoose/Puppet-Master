## Findings at a glance

- **P1/P5:** Skedda's approval request itself does not hold the resource. Approval makes one request a confirmed booking. Its buffer is a symmetric gap around bookings, and privileged users may override venue booking policies. Approval is useful for human selection among competing requests; the feature does not by itself implement “missing cleaning signoff blocks booking.”
- **P2:** Food Corridor's tenant documentation exposes the kitchen calendar, booked-space/equipment daily snapshot, and an active-client directory. Public help pages do not resolve which other-tenant booking fields appear to each tenant. This leaves P2's named privacy boundary unverified.
- **P3:** A Food Corridor food business can see its own space/equipment booking history and reports about bookings, invoices, and sign-in/out. Its PIN workflow records attendance and allows concern notes. Those sources do not establish a tenant-defined cleaning-completion signoff. Tenant-side CSV export is not documented.
- **P6:** The 2026 FDA Food Code is a model offered for jurisdictional adoption. The relevant regulator's adopted edition and modifications govern applicability. The federal model's 2026 addition of “commissary” is potentially relevant to this kitchen, with classification depending on operations and the authority having jurisdiction.

## 1. P1 and P5 — Skedda booking buffers and approval states

**Requested / pending.** A request is pending before a confirmed booking exists. The request is not displayed as a booking on the Skedda scheduler, and two users can submit conflicting requests; neither request reserves the slot. In Microsoft two-way sync, a pending request appears as tentative in Outlook, while approval is managed in Skedda. Consequently the kitchen should treat a request as a candidate, not as a held production block. [Skedda, Booking Requests & Approvals](https://support.skedda.com/en/articles/11774950-booking-requests-approvals)

**Approved.** The approval workflow creates one confirmed booking, which is then marked unavailable. An approver can inspect and alter request details in the approval form before approving. For a venue buffer of duration **b**, the documented buffer is a gap before and after the booking: a booking from 09:00 to 10:00 with b = 30 minutes has unavailable bands from 08:30 to 09:00 and 10:00 to 10:30 for regular users. Buffer duration follows venue time granularity; the smallest supported setting is 15 minutes, and 30 minutes is the documented default granularity. Multiple conflicting buffer rules on one space resolve to the maximum duration. Buffer rules affect bookings made after the rule is implemented. [Skedda, Buffer time](https://support.skedda.com/en/articles/3653032-buffer-time)

The gap is symmetric. A 30-minute Skedda buffer does not encode a separately named 10-minute setup period and a 40-minute cleaning period. It can provide a simple equal gap; explicit setup, production, and cleanup intervals need to be represented in the booking/block model or tested as a separate configuration. The documentation does not establish that a buffer is tenant-owned time or a cleaning attestation.

**Changed.** A pending request may be changed by the approver in the approval form. Once approved, regular users cannot edit it; admins can. For Outlook two-way sync, a post-approval date/time edit deletes the confirmed booking and returns the request to pending. Skedda's public pages do not specify whether every type of admin edit re-evaluates buffers or how a changed interval is checked against existing bookings. The final interval and buffer outcome require a discriminating test in a qualified sandbox. [Skedda, Booking Requests & Approvals](https://support.skedda.com/en/articles/11774950-booking-requests-approvals)

**Roles and exceptions.** System users may book over buffer periods. Skedda identifies Booking Admins, System Admins, and the Owner as System User types; Booking Admins can create bookings for the venue and most configured venue policies do not apply when they create a booking. System Admins inherit those powers, and the Owner has the broadest privileges. Custom admin approval rules do not apply to spaces that the custom admin manages. Approval rules can also be scoped by space, user tag, and day, so users outside a rule's scope book without approval. [Skedda, Buffer time](https://support.skedda.com/en/articles/3653032-buffer-time), [Skedda, User Access Levels / Types](https://support.skedda.com/en/articles/2218760-user-access-levels-types-regular-and-system-users), [Skedda, Booking Requests & Approvals](https://support.skedda.com/en/articles/11774950-booking-requests-approvals)

**P1/P5 interpretation.** Skedda can support a coordinator selecting among competing requests and recording a confirmed resource interval. A buffer can enforce an equal interval around regular-user bookings. The workflow does not establish a causal rule connecting a missing cleaning signoff to future booking denial; that remains the board's explicit P5 decision and would need a separate policy/configuration capability. A discriminating check should submit two overlapping requests, verify that neither blocks the other, approve one with changed start/end times, then attempt adjacent bookings as a regular user, Booking Admin, and custom admin for a managed space. Record the actual booked and buffer intervals and whether each save succeeds.

**Release/evolution evidence.** Skedda announced Booking Approvals on January 22, 2026 and updated its announcement on June 29 with Outlook two-way sync and approval notes marked live; its support behavior article is dated July 7, 2026. This is a documented feature-release/evolution chain, not a defect or regression record. [Skedda launch/update article](https://www.skedda.com/blog/booking-approvals), [Skedda support article](https://support.skedda.com/en/articles/11774950-booking-requests-approvals)

## 2. P2/P3 — Food Corridor tenant visibility, records, exports, and inactivity

**Other tenants' booking visibility.** Food Corridor's food-business guide says a tenant can view its own submitted, approved, declined, and cancelled space and equipment bookings. It also says the tenant can view the kitchen's spaces and a daily snapshot of what space and equipment is booked. The tenant Community tab exposes the other active clients as a list and supports viewing/messaging those clients. The older role article gives the same calendar and active-client-list behavior for a food-business account used as a read-only account. [Food Corridor food-business account guide](https://help.thefoodcorridor.com/en/articles/1812934-what-does-a-food-business-account-look-like), [Food Corridor read-only account guidance](https://help.thefoodcorridor.com/en/articles/1530570-can-a-kitchen-admin-have-view-only-permissions)

This is meaningful schedule and roster visibility. The public help pages do not say whether the calendar reveals another tenant's name on a booking, booking title, production volume, customer details, or only the time/resource occupancy. They also do not establish visibility of another tenant's recipes. Therefore P2 cannot be certified from these pages; the business-name directory is explicit, and booking-field visibility remains an open product check.

**A tenant's records.** The food-business guide describes the tenant's own space and equipment booking statuses, plus Reports-tab information about its bookings, invoices, and sign-in/out data. The current PIN instructions show attendance clock-in/out and optional notes to the kitchen about cleanliness, equipment problems, low supplies, or other concerns. The cited material supports attendance and issue reporting. It does not define a tenant-authored cleaning checklist, a cleaning-completion attestation, or an equipment-specific sanitation signoff. [Food Corridor food-business account guide](https://help.thefoodcorridor.com/en/articles/1812934-what-does-a-food-business-account-look-like), [Food Corridor digital sign-in/out](https://help.thefoodcorridor.com/en/articles/1414030-how-do-i-digitally-sign-in-out-at-my-kitchen)

**CSV/export and year coverage.** The food-business account guide says tenants can view their automatically generated reports but does not specify an export/download action, date range, status coverage, or whether exported tenant reports include equipment bookings. The separate, recently updated Reporting-tab guide describes kitchen/operator reports downloadable as CSV and filterable by client/date range. Its All Bookings report covers past and future bookings in the current year across approved, billed, declined, cancelled, and deleted states; another usage report explicitly combines space and equipment hours. That is evidence for kitchen Reporting-tab exports, not proof of CSV export from a food-business tenant login. [Food Corridor food-business account guide](https://help.thefoodcorridor.com/en/articles/1812934-what-does-a-food-business-account-look-like), [Food Corridor Reporting-tab guide](https://help.thefoodcorridor.com/en/articles/2322739-what-information-is-in-the-reporting-tab)

Thus the independently verified tenant capability is **viewing** its own booking, invoice, and sign-in/out report information. Its ability to export those records to CSV, and the exact annual/export fields for equipment bookings, remains unknown from public documentation. The kitchen operator's CSV report is a documented alternative source for records filtered by client and year.

**Inactive account.** When the kitchen marks a business inactive, Food Corridor says the tenant remains able to access its account, past invoices, booking history, and reports. Fully deleting the account is a separate process and removes tenant access; the article does not promise an export before deletion. Inactive status therefore preserves online history access, but it does not resolve the tenant CSV question. [Food Corridor leaving-a-kitchen guidance](https://help.thefoodcorridor.com/en/articles/14695452-i-m-leaving-my-kitchen-what-happens-to-my-account-and-charges)

**P2/P3 checks.** In a qualified test environment with two synthetic tenants, inspect each tenant's calendar, Daily View, Community profile, and report records. Record whether the other tenant's name, booking title, times, resource, or any additional fields are visible. For one tenant, compare a year-filtered export (if the UI offers one) against its space/equipment bookings in each status and its sign-in/out entries. Repeat access after setting that tenant inactive. Separately test whether any cleaning signoff can record the tenant-defined procedure, equipment, actor, timestamp, and result, and distinguish it from sign-in/out notes. ## 3. P3/P6 — FDA Food Code 2026 status and jurisdictional adoption

FDA identifies the 2026 Food Code as its model for retail and food-service safety provisions and offers it for adoption by local, state, tribal, territorial, and federal jurisdictions. The 2026 Preface says the model is neither federal law nor regulation and is not preemptive. It becomes a federal requirement only when adopted by federal bodies for federal jurisdictions; the agencies responsible for retail oversight have primary regulatory responsibility. The Preface describes state/local adoption through statute, regulation, or ordinance and permits jurisdictional modifications for existing law, procedures, or policy. FDA also recognizes alternatives that provide equivalent public-health protection. [FDA Food Code 2026 landing page](https://www.fda.gov/food/fda-food-code/food-code-2026), [FDA Food Code 2026, Preface §§1 and 7](https://www.fda.gov/media/194741/download)

The 2026 edition adds “commissary” to its food-establishment definition. Its Annex 3 describes commissaries supporting food preparation/storage for food ultimately offered directly to consumers as within the Food Code framework. It distinguishes operations intended to distribute food to another business entity, which align with the Code's food-processing-plant definition. The regulatory authority determines facility suitability and whether additional permitting is required. The brief does not specify every tenant's sales channel or the kitchen's jurisdiction, so it cannot establish the kitchen's legal classification or which edition/local modifications apply. [FDA Food Code 2026, Annex 3, “Food Establishment — commissary”](https://www.fda.gov/media/194741/download)

**P3/P6 interpretation.** The 2026 Code can be consulted as a model and may be directly relevant to a commissary operation, but it is not a universal, automatically adopted cleaning procedure set. The board should keep the product's operational signoff record distinct from a regulatory compliance determination. A suitable discriminating check is to identify the kitchen's state/local/tribal authority and exact adopted code edition, then ask that authority how the facility and each tenant's retail/wholesale activities are classified and which sanitation records it requires. Only then can the common-procedure question be evaluated against governing requirements. No jurisdiction was supplied, so no specific adopted rule is claimed here.
