## Investigator framing

This is a small shared-resource coordination product, not a recipe-management or kitchen food-safety authority. The specified coordination records should belong to each tenant and record what that tenant says it used and signed off on. Do not store recipes, customers, formulas, output quantities, ingredient lists, allergen declarations, or a kitchen-wide risk rating unless the board separately authorizes the data and purpose. The system should explain that a record is a tenant's submitted use/cleanup record, not a kitchen certification of that tenant's safety program.

The core collision is temporal and multi-resource: each production block uses one or more spaces and may use equipment on subranges. It needs a precise booked-access interval, a distinct setup/teardown policy where agreed, and a way to see true equipment availability. A calendar that prevents overlap only on the nominal production period may still admit one tenant while a prior tenant is setting up or cleaning. The board has expressly not chosen whether a missing signoff blocks later bookings or how cleaning tasks vary across equipment; design must keep these policies configurable/explicit rather than silently selecting a food-safety or enforcement rule.

The $21,000 limit has no period, currency denomination, or indication whether it covers software, implementation, hardware, tax, and future operating expense. It is not valid to declare a vendor affordable until those are clarified. Treat pilot size as six tenants, eventual tenancy as twenty, three coordinators, and paper signoff as a transition channel; support a tenant-owned year-end export as a migration/exit requirement.

## O1 — useful unfamiliar options and materially different approaches

### A. Kitchen-specific hosted suite: The Food Corridor

The Food Corridor is a product built around shared commercial kitchens rather than generic office-space booking. Its public product and help materials describe booking space and equipment, renter accounts, approval, billing/onboarding, digital sign-in/out, and CSV reporting. The pricing page at research time displayed an Annual plan at $206/month billed annually for unlimited clients (base arithmetic $2,472/year) with 2% ACH or 4% card platform fees; it displayed a Starter plan at $129/month for up to five clients and 4%. The 20-tenant scale would exceed the displayed Starter cap; a six-tenant pilot alone is also one over that cap. The displayed dollar currency was not changed/verified, and platform fees can dominate subscription cost as booked value grows.

The public instructions show a tenant can reserve a kitchen space and allocate equipment in one or multiple subranges, with availability shown for each range. Its checklist material describes monthly client cleaning checklists with station tasks and deep-clean schedules. This is a useful lead for managed logistics and cleaning tasks, but available evidence does not show that checklists attach to each production block, can be tenant-defined per equipment item, create immutable time-stamped signoff, or export as tenant-owned year-end records. Digital sign-in/out records attendance; it must not be treated as equipment-cleaning signoff without verification.

Privacy requires a deliberate demo. The tenant-facing account documentation says the Daily View displays a snapshot of spaces and equipment booked across a day, and Community permits a food business to view and message fellow clients. The public docs do not establish exactly what other-tenant fields those views expose or whether Community can be disabled. Those surfaces might reveal identities, production timing, or equipment patterns; they must be compared against the brief's confidentiality rule. The documentation does show own booking reports and a manager CSV report filtered by client/date, but does not prove a tenant can export its complete set of all records (especially handoff signoffs) for a year. The workflow is the strongest low-administration domain candidate if privacy, per-block handoff, export, and full price are demonstrated.

### B. General hosted scheduler plus a separate handoff-record workflow: Skedda

Skedda is a customizable space-booking system used beyond offices. Its official docs provide a concrete buffer rule and visibility controls. Private access can require login; booking can be tag-limited; absent visibility rules, regular users see only date, time, and space for other bookings; system users always see holder, title, and custom fields. Booking custom fields can be required, conditioned on space or user tags, and set to admin-only or visible to the holder. System users can filter and export booking data as XLSX/CSV; regular users have no report-filter/export interface in the cited help article, but can see their own bookings plus bookings made visible to them. This allows less disclosure in the shared schedule, but it does not show that a booking field supports a post-use signature or that the holder can export every year-end record. Any recipe/customer/output fields should be absent.

The buffer mechanism has material semantics: it uses configured time granularity (15 minutes is the smallest documented unit), applies only to bookings created after the rule, takes the maximum if conflicting rules apply, and reserves the same period before and after a booking. Regular users cannot book into the buffer; system users can override it. This can protect changeover time when the board agrees on a single symmetric minimum. It can be wasteful or incorrect when cleanup is one-sided or setup/cleaning differs by item. It also means existing pilot bookings need re-entry or manual review when turning the rule on. Plan caps for number of rules vary. A separate, linked handoff form/log may be needed, using tenant access controls and its own retention/export evidence. This path offers configurable scheduling and stronger documented visibility controls, at the cost of composing and validating two systems.

### C. Self-hosted open-source resource scheduler: LibreBooking

LibreBooking offers resource-level scheduling/approval/buffer controls, resource grouping, usage reports, a responsive interface, and a tablet display. It is a materially different ownership model: the operator controls hosting and configuration but owns upgrades, access rules, backups, monitoring, and response to security releases. The 7.0.0 documentation says anonymous schedule viewing is off by default; where enabled, reservation details remain hidden by default. It describes resource buffer time, but the inspected section does not name its units or direction. Min/max duration default to none, approval defaults off, automatic access to resources for new users defaults on, and administrators are exempt from usage constraints. Those defaults are unsuitable as a presumed tenant ACL; configure and probe tenant groups. Public tablet display requires per-resource public access in the documentation and may also expose feeds, so a kiosk view must not accidentally become a public tenant schedule.

The current public release at the research date was v7.0.0, pinned to commit 422b4b0223335d18c7d5f005ffcdd93719575c20 (2026-10-06). The repository's current README announces an upcoming high-impact security release for Oct 13 and says versions before 7.0 had an unauthenticated administrator takeover issue, with security fixes not backported. This is not proof v7.0 has the undisclosed upcoming issue; it is a reason to budget active patch monitoring. Deployment docs require PHP >= 8.2, Apache >= 2.4 (other server software is not currently project-supported/tested), and MySQL >= 8 or MariaDB >= 10.6. No licensing fee does not mean no total cost.

### D. Separate tenant-owned check-in/check-out record with paper overlap

An alternative architecture separates the booking engine from the tenant's handoff record: book space/equipment in one scheduler, then submit a tenant-authored record linked by booking ID, space/equipment, and time, with who submitted, when, changes/corrections, status, and tenant-scoped export. The printed sheet remains usable during cutover and reconciles to the same booking reference. This makes the system less likely to be mistaken for the authority deciding a tenant's food-safety procedure and makes the unresolved block policy visible. It adds duplicate entry, identity/access, integration, and export risks. Neither TFC nor Skedda public evidence reviewed proves the needed append-only per-block log; a demo/prototype must verify it before selecting this pattern.

## O2 — behavior, defaults, units, limits, applicability

1. **Food Corridor equipment availability:** the tenant first creates the space booking, chooses an equipment subrange, and sees availability for that exact range; multiple pieces can use different ranges. The public article gives a workflow, but no public units/granularity, setup buffer behavior, or conflict race semantics. Validate concurrent attempts for one item and boundaries at exact interval start/end. Do not infer equipment cleanup is reserved.
2. **Food Corridor cleaning/check-in:** the public checklist article says monthly cleaning checklists and station-specific tasks; its frequency does not meet a per-block signature obligation by itself. The PIN and sign-in/out reports are for presence/time. Per-block equipment reference, authorship, timestamp, edits, offline/paper reconciliation, and tenant export are unknown.
3. **Food Corridor booking enforcement:** admin approval can be manual or auto-approve per client. That mechanism is distinct from missing cleaning signoff; neither automatic blocking nor automatic approval is selected by the case.
4. **Food Corridor exports:** the manager report page documents CSV with client/date filters. All Bookings covers current-year past and future bookings in several statuses. Tenant account docs say reports are available but do not claim tenant export completeness. Evaluate requested data records and exact year boundary, plus an account for one tenant against another tenant's export.
5. **Skedda buffer:** per-space configurable; units derive from scheduler granularity with 15-minute minimum documented; applies only to new bookings; same interval is applied before/after; max conflicting rule wins; regular-user booking is blocked, system users can override. There is no evidence that an admin override is recorded as a rejected vs bypassed cleaning signoff; test separate roles.
6. **Skedda confidentiality/export:** logged-in private venue can be limited to tagged users. With no rules, regular tenants see date/time/space, no holder or title; system users see all booking fields. Booking details can be custom fields, optional/required, admin-only or holder-visible. System users export XLSX/CSV and filter; ordinary user export/report filtering is absent in the documented interface. This supports an own-data minimum for regular-user bookings, but not an individual archive that includes a later handoff log. Set tenant roles so they are not system users.
7. **LibreBooking resource config:** documentation defaults are no min/max reservation length, no approval, auto-grant resource access to new users, immediate booking up to current time, no max future horizon, and unlimited resource capacity unless set. Admin roles bypass constraints. Public schedule default hides content and requires login. Buffer is said to make reservations a minimum distance apart, but source text inspected gives no explicit units/direction. Do not configure production policy from this incomplete semantics; inspect settings/UI/code and test actual gaps, cancellation/edit, simultaneous requests, DST, and overrides first.
8. **Accessibility:** WCAG 2.2 criterion 2.1.1 keyboard, 2.5.8 pointer minimum target 24x24 CSS pixels (with exceptions), 4.1.2 programmatic name/role/state/value, and 4.1.3 announced status without focus transfer offer normative acceptance anchors for a web-based coordinator view. For wet hands, meet or exceed the WCAG floor with larger, well-separated controls and low-complexity steps; wet-hands usability itself must be evaluated on the selected entrance tablet. An admin screen-reader claim needs real keyboard and screen-reader flow, including labels, selected states, error/status announcements and validation—not vendor assertion alone.
9. **Cost applicability:** Food Corridor's observed annual base quote arithmetic is $2,472/year for displayed unlimited clients; 2% ACH / 4% card platform fees are additional/transaction-dependent. Starter's five-client limit is below both pilot (six) and eventual (twenty). The displayed dollar currency and budget time horizon are unresolved; hardware, implementation, tax, and payment volume are not included in this arithmetic.

## O3 — relevant issue/fix/release chain

LibreBooking provides a traceable buffer-related regression example. Issue #1008 (opened 2026-02-14) reported that BufferItem::Id() called itself recursively and any caller could overflow the stack. PR #1140 merged 2026-03-13; the fix built the buffer item's ID from the wrapped reservation's ID and added a regression assertion for before/after IDs with a 3,600-second interval. The issue was closed on merge; v4.3.0 release notes (2026-04-03) list the fix. The code at v7.0.0 still delegates the buffer ID to the wrapped reservation item, showing it persists in the inspected current release. This chain demonstrates that even non-business-critical buffer rendering can fail, and gives a concrete case for regression testing boundary/representation behavior. It does not establish that current booking-conflict enforcement is defect-free.

## Interpretation guardrails

- Never store or solicit the actual tenant recipes, customers, sales, ingredient ratios, or production quantity to provide a batch handoff.
- Scope tenant-defined checklist fields to only the equipment used, the tenant's own chosen use/cleaning record and signoff. Let tenants author or choose per-equipment steps if the board selects that feature. Do not centrally invent a food-safe cleaning standard.
- Keep policy states explicit: “submitted/awaiting tenant signoff”, “tenant signed”, “missing”, “corrected”, “coordinator follow-up” are product concepts to discuss; missing-signoff booking effects remain a decision.
- Schedule blocks need start/end and chosen setup/cleaning interval, equipment resource/time ranges, tenant/account, status, and linkable record ID. Use half-open interval checks ([start,end)) as a proposed model only; validate against operator expectations.
- Treat the entrance tablet as a glance-and-act surface with no recipe or other-tenant detail. It needs usable large touch targets; wet hands also raise hardware/cleanability and hand-free workflow questions beyond software standards.
- Screen-reader admin view is a required view with testable accessibility, not an optional enhancement.
- Paper signoff continues during transition. Proposed reconciliation must preserve provenance and avoid silently fabricating digital timestamps for paper events.
- Year-end export is an exit and tenant ownership requirement, not just a manager-level CSV. Define included statuses, timestamps, equipment records, attachments, timezone, format, and deletion/retention expectations before claiming done.
- The no-network/native condition from the brief is not a constraint: kitchen has internet. TFC/Skedda are hosted paths; LibreBooking can be self-hosted. Paper transition still matters.
- Cost claims are a point-in-time displayed plan observation, not a quote.

## Proposed validation program (none executed)

### Scheduling and handoff
- Create six pilot accounts, two spaces, and a finite set of shared equipment items; concurrently try overlapping bookings and back-to-back intervals from two tenant accounts. Verify exactly one resource allocation succeeds and the loser sees a useful error.
- Test a booking with distinct space and equipment ranges; validate setup before and cleanup after against real coordinator/tenant practice. Exercise exact adjacency, cancel, shorten, extend, edit, recurring series, timezone change/DST, and admin override.
- Run paired scenarios for each board choice: missing signoff permits future booking vs blocks it. Check that no policy is active before a board decision.
- Submit the same booking during the paper/digital transition. Confirm digital and paper entries remain attributable and no system-generated timestamp is represented as a tenant's actual time.
- Pilot a per-equipment cleaning checklist authored by a tenant. Confirm the UI records only the selected tenant's details and does not imply coordinator certification; inspect evidence for who authored/changed/signed and when.
- Verify record link uses the booking/resource IDs; tenants cannot edit another tenant's records; coordinators can handle disputes/corrections without erasing the original entry.
- Export one tenant's records for a full simulated year, including amended/cancelled bookings and handoff records; inspect CSV/JSON/PDF readability, timestamps/timezone, encoding, attachment handling, and cross-tenant exclusion.

### Privacy and roles
- Use separate test accounts for each tenant and each of three coordinators. Attempt direct URLs/API/report filters, scheduler search, notifications, community, tablet, CSV export, invitation links, and shared-space calendars.
- Verify other tenants never see recipe/customer or production-volume data; expose only the minimum occupancy/resource availability needed to avoid conflicts. Check whether the Food Corridor Community/Daily View can be disabled or sufficiently minimized.
- Verify a kiosk reset/session timeout and test that a tenant's access token/session cannot be reused to inspect another tenant's record.

### Accessibility and environment
- On the actual entrance tablet, perform booking/check-in/hand-off tasks with wet-hand proxy conditions, gloves if relevant, glare, and realistic cleaning/placement constraints; record touch accuracy, errors, task completion and fallback use. Test screen magnification and pointer target size in physical display context.
- On the coordinator admin view, complete tasks with keyboard only and a screen reader: locate a missing handoff, open/correct a record, filter/export one tenant, and respond to a collision. Check programmatic names, roles, states, focus order, keyboard trap-free behavior, error identification, and status announcements.
- Keep offline/network failure behavior separate from core acceptance because internet is available; still verify paper transition and idempotent recovery after a dropped submission.

### Costs/operations
- Obtain written quotes at current pricing, including fee model against expected booking/payment volume, implementation, hardware, support, tax, data export, storage and retention. Compare within a stated budget horizon.
- For self-hosted candidates, verify upgrade/backup/monitoring owner, supported web stack, tenant ACL configuration, recovery time, and current security advisories; do not build on unsupported server configuration without explicit acceptance.

All items above are proposals. No witness, product trial, conflict test, accessibility evaluation, user research, billing check, migration, or data export was executed in this research stage.
