# I07 research draft — plan comparison and recommendations

**Status:** complete research deliverable for the research-stage scope; no implementation or product-selection approval implied.  
**Frozen inputs:** [brief](../../../../cases/I07/brief.md), [revealed plan](revealed-plan.md), [frozen discovery](discovery.md), [source map](source-map.json), [source index](sources/index.md). Discovery was written and passed through the reveal tool before this comparison; discovery.md is not changed here.  
**Evidence and execution boundary:** all vendor behavior is based on public documentation, not a live account. No software, export, accessibility workflow, concurrent booking, install, or witness was executed. Everything labeled “proposed” below remains unexecuted.

## Exact P-clause disposition

| Exact plan clause | Disposition | Finding to retain |
|---|---|---|
| P1: “Schedule tenant production blocks with setup, production, and cleanup intervals.” | **Correction** | Keep the clause and make interval behavior explicit: distinct setup / production / cleanup spans, resources and equipment allocated to their actual subranges, and conflicts evaluated over every period the tenant occupies a resource. Keep resource occupancy distinct from production duration and billable time; neither inclusion is decided by this brief. Do not convert this into an unexplained symmetric buffer or assume one cleanup duration for all equipment. The brief has not supplied durations or a time granularity or who owns/uses the reserved transition interval. |
| P2: “Keep each tenant's recipes, customer information, and production volumes private from other tenants.” | **Already-covered**; **optional enhancement** | The clause explicitly covers the central secrecy requirement. Add verification coverage across calendar summaries, searches, tenant profile/community, emails, exports, kiosk, direct record URLs, and tenant administration. Busy status and resource/time details might themselves reveal patterns; decide the minimum visible conflict information. Do not collect recipe/customer/output data to perform scheduling. |
| P3: “Record equipment-use and cleaning signoffs using tenant-defined procedures.” | **Correction**; **uncertain product fit** | Specify a tenant-authored record attached to a tenant, production block, and individual equipment item, with submitter, timestamp, status, and correction history plus tenant-scoped year-end export. Where it clarifies the handoff, allow a tenant-generated batch reference and tenant-private handoff state/time; do not require recipe, product, customer, sales, ingredient, or quantity content. If batches are meant to move between tenants, clarify that scope against P2 before adding cross-tenant access. Explicitly state that the system stores a tenant's signoff and does not certify the tenant's food-safety program. Public evidence does not establish that any candidate implements this complete per-block workflow. |
| P4: “Support an accessible coordinator view and a transition period that can coexist with paper signoffs.” | **Correction**; **already-covered** | Keep both requirements. Preserve the entrance-tablet/wet-hands need as a separate user environment from the coordinator's screen-reader-compatible administrative view. Define task-based accessibility checks and paper/digital reconciliation with event provenance; do not manufacture digital timestamps for paper events. |
| P5: “Booking restrictions after a missing signoff and the authority to apply them are board decisions.” | **Already-covered**; **user decision** | Preserve as an unresolved board choice. Offer separately testable “notify/follow up only” and “restrict future booking” behavior; define who may impose, clear, or appeal a restriction. Per-client booking approval/auto-approval in a product is not the same policy. |
| P6: “A common procedure model across equipment types has not been selected.” | **Already-covered**; **user decision** | Preserve as open. Alternatives are one tenant-authored base procedure with optional equipment-specific additions/overrides, or independently authored procedures for each equipment type. Do not prescribe a kitchen-wide food-safety standard. |

“Rejected” findings are scoped to rejected approaches, not a rejection of a P clause: reject collecting recipe/customer/output information as a prerequisite, a centrally imposed cleaning protocol, or a silent default that missing signoff blocks access. Those conflict with the brief's data and authority boundaries. If the board separately changes scope, re-open those decisions explicitly.

## Research findings retained

### Candidate 1: The Food Corridor — lead product to demonstrate, conditional on the gates below

This is an unfamiliar product specifically positioned for shared-use kitchens. Public materials document space/equipment booking, per-client approval or auto-approval, a client PIN sign-in/out flow, business reports, manager CSV reports, client documents, and monthly cleaning checklists [FC-01–FC-07]. A booking can contain an equipment reservation for one time range, and additional equipment can be added for other ranges. This is promising for the collision and multi-resource parts of P1.

The observed signup plan showed Starter at $129/month for up to five clients and Annual at $206/month billed annually with unlimited clients. The latter computes to $2,472/year for the base plan; a separate 2% ACH or 4% card platform fee is displayed. Six pilot tenants and eventual twenty exceed the Starter cap. Currency was not changed in the selector, and the $21,000 budget's time horizon/components are unknown, so this is a point-in-time screen observation rather than a quote or affordability conclusion [FC-02].

The documented monthly station-cleaning checklist is a useful optional capability, but it does not prove that each equipment use has a linked signoff, that procedures are tenant-defined, or that records are revision-safe and individually exportable [FC-05]. The PIN/check-in reports concern attendance/time, not cleanup signoff [FC-02, FC-04, FC-07]. The food-business view can see Daily View space/equipment bookings and Community supports viewing/messaging fellow clients. Before selection, demonstrate whether those surfaces can be hidden or sufficiently minimized to satisfy P2; current public material does not establish exactly what fields other tenants see or whether Community can be disabled [FC-04]. The manager's CSV can be filtered by client/date, but this does not establish a tenant's own complete yearly export of booking plus equipment-use/signoff history [FC-07]. Treat this product as the lead to evaluate first because it may minimize integration work; do not mark P1–P4 complete until those gaps pass.

### Candidate 2: Skedda plus a tenant-owned handoff log

Skedda provides materially useful, documented general scheduling behavior. Under private access, tenants can log in; booking rights can be tag-limited; with no content visibility rules, ordinary users see other bookings only as date/time/space, while system users always see holders, titles, and custom fields [SK-02]. Custom fields can be required, conditional, and holder-only or admin-only [SK-03]. Ordinary users are not given the export filters described for system users; system users can filter and export booking rows with start/end, duration, space, holder, and custom fields [SK-04]. This can limit casual cross-tenant detail but does not prove row-level record isolation for any additional handoff log.

The buffer rule has a nontrivial model: granularity-derived durations with a documented 15-minute minimum; the maximum wins across conflicting rules; only bookings created after a rule is set gain buffers; equal time is held before and after the booking; ordinary users cannot book through it but system users can [SK-01]. It is a candidate only if the kitchen chooses a symmetric minimum changeover gap and accepts the effects on capacity. It is a poor direct fit if equipment needs distinct one-sided setup and cleanup or different per-equipment intervals. A second record tool must independently prove tenant-authored signoff, edit/correction history, access isolation, and year-end tenant export. This path is an alternative if the kitchen wants to control schedule visibility and accepts added workflow/integration burden.

### Candidate 3: LibreBooking — self-hosted alternative for an operator who can own operations

LibreBooking's resource model has booking, approval, resource grouping, buffer and check-in/out controls. The observed 7.0.0 documentation states that anonymous viewing is off by default and that details remain hidden on a public read-only schedule unless enabled. It also shows defaults that require deliberate setup: no minimum/maximum booking duration, approval off, automatic access to a resource for new users on, and administrator exemption from usage restrictions; buffer units and direction were not clear in the viewed documentation [LB-01]. These defaults are not a tenant privacy design. A public tablet/resource view needs particular review because enabling public resource access can expose external calendar/feed URLs [LB-01].

The product is open source, but the project's own install guide names PHP >= 8.2, Apache >= 2.4 (other servers may work but are not supported/tested by the project), and MySQL >= 8 or MariaDB >= 10.6 [LB-04]. Its current release at research time was v7.0.0, commit 422b4b0223335d18c7d5f005ffcdd93719575c20, dated 2026-10-06 [LB-02]. The mutable repository README announces another high-impact security release planned for Oct 13, 2026, and says the pre-7.0 line had an unauthenticated administrator-takeover vulnerability with fixes not backported [LB-04]. The README does not disclose the upcoming vulnerability and does not establish that v7.0.0 is affected. This makes patch monitoring essential if self-hosting, not an automatic disqualification. With three kitchen coordinators but no stated server operator/developer, infrastructure and support costs remain unknown. Do not choose solely because licensing is free.

### Issue/fix/release chain (O3)

LibreBooking issue #1008 (opened 2026-02-14) reported that BufferItem::Id() recursively called itself and callers could stack overflow. PR #1140 merged on 2026-03-13; the fix uses the wrapped reservation item's ID and adds before/after ID assertions. Fix commit b1394612e9e5311d0b6662cffa8e2e528667d6c3 was merged by 4bc0cf5dd17b5cbacae2fd4540800f04ea371c1e. v4.3.0 release notes dated 2026-04-03 list the fix; the pinned v7.0.0 source still delegates the buffer item ID through the wrapped reservation item [LB-03]. The v7 test fixture passes 3600 to WithBufferTime and checks the generated begin/end IDs [LB-05]. The inspected test excerpt does not state the unit, so this draft does not interpret 3600 as seconds. This is a rendering/list-item regression chain only; it does not test reservation conflict enforcement or cleaning intervals.

### Handoff workflow shape

The clearest product-independent alternative is to keep scheduling and tenant-owned handoff as separate concepts, linked by booking/equipment identifiers. Each tenant could author or select its record steps while the board leaves the common-vs-per-equipment model open. The record needs who submitted, when, what block/equipment it refers to, state, and a correction trail; a tenant-defined batch reference or handoff state may help where a tenant needs it, with no recipe/output details and no cross-tenant visibility unless the board changes P2. Tenant-specific records should be independently retrievable at year end. Paper remains a parallel record through a deliberately managed transition. TFC's monthly checklist or a scheduler booking field might seed a pilot, but neither proves this shape's per-use workflow. Separate tools also add duplicate entry, authentication, integration, data retention, and export risks.

## Decision and open-item register

### Board / user decisions

1. **Missing signoff consequence and authority (P5):** choose no automatic restriction with coordinator follow-up, or a future-booking restriction; name the person/role who can apply and clear one and the tenant correction/appeal path. Keep the chosen rule inactive until recorded.
2. **Procedure model (P6):** choose a common tenant-authored base procedure with equipment-specific additions/overrides, or wholly separate tenant-authored procedures by equipment type. A kitchen-wide standard is not implied.
3. **Changeover model (P1):** decide whether setup and cleanup are fixed for each resource, selected per booking, or derived from policy. If different, define whether the system supports asymmetric before/after intervals instead of forcing a symmetric buffer. Also decide who may occupy each transition interval and whether its time is billed.
4. **Confidentiality boundary (P2):** decide which busy/occupied resource details tenants need to prevent conflicts, and whether tenant identities/community/profile or equipment use is permitted to be visible. The brief does not say whether time/space occupancy alone is acceptable.
5. **Budget basis:** define whether $21,000 is one-time or annual and whether it includes hardware, transaction fees, implementation, support, hosting, taxes and operations.
6. **Export and record control:** specify export contents/timezone/format, who can request an export, retention/deletion, and how paper records join digital records. Tenant-owned records must not depend solely on kitchen-administrator exports.
7. **Electronic acknowledgement and handoff:** decide what constitutes the tenant's signoff and its correction process; do not conflate that acknowledgment with coordinator inspection or an authority on food safety. Clarify whether the batch-handoff goal is within each tenant's own work or crosses tenant boundaries. If within-tenant, an optional tenant-created batch reference/status may be enough; if cross-tenant, reconcile it with P2 before exposing any record.
8. **Product-specific unknowns:** confirm TFC's disable/minimize options for Daily View and Community and exact tenant export/checklist semantics; confirm Skedda's plan limits/quote, true per-tenant export and post-use workflow feasibility; or assign a named operator for self-hosting and security updates.

### Conditional shortlist

Demo The Food Corridor first because its documented features are specific to shared kitchen operations and its observed Annual plan covered twenty clients for the displayed base price. This is a shortlist lead, not a selection: fail the candidate if it exposes prohibited tenant information, cannot create per-block tenant-owned handoff records, cannot give tenants their own complete export, or cannot support the approved P1 intervals and P5/P6 policies. If it fails one of those gates, compare Skedda plus a separately validated log or a self-hosted resource scheduler with explicit lifecycle ownership. A custom build is an optional approach only if a costed estimate fits the budget horizon and someone owns maintenance; no such estimate is available.

## Accessibility, privacy, data, and operations requirements

- Entrance tablet: test the actual model, viewing angle, glare, kiosk reset, session isolation, and task flow with wet-hand proxies. WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8's 24 by 24 CSS pixel minimum is a conformance floor, not proof of wet-hands usability. Prefer larger separated targets, reduced typing, clear correction, and a paper fallback [WCAG-01].
- Coordinator admin view: test the actual screen reader and keyboard flow. Relevant criteria include 2.1.1 keyboard operation, 4.1.2 exposed name/role/state/value and 4.1.3 announced status changes. A rendered accessibility statement or automated scan alone is not proof the admin can find, correct, or export a record [WCAG-01].
- Tenant privacy: segregate accounts and test calendar, direct identifiers, reports, notifications, exports, search, and kiosk independently. Keep recipes/customers/output-volume fields out of the data model and event payloads. Do not assume a hidden UI is authorization enforcement.
- Auditability: tenant signoff, coordinator correction, booking edit, batch-handoff state change, and paper backfill must remain distinguishable events. Preserve original entries when corrected; indicate source/time rather than attributing a paper timestamp to the software.
- Booking semantics: explicit timezone, precision, interval inclusivity, equipment subranges, cancellation/rebooking behavior, and setup/cleanup reservations need definition. A reasonable test model is half-open intervals with collision across each reserved resource; treat this as a proposal until participants validate adjacency.
- Operations: live SaaS candidates still require vendor privacy/export/retention terms, account recovery, support, and fee review. Self-hosting requires patching, backups, monitoring, recovery, access administration, and responsibility for the announced security updates.
- Food-safety boundary: the system captures tenant-defined information and signoff as a handoff record. It must not assert that the kitchen has evaluated, approved, or guaranteed the tenant's complete safety program.

## Discriminating validation proposals

**Executed:** source reading only. No live software, witness, or user validation.  
**Proposed:** do the following with a six-tenant pilot; do not represent these as completed.

1. Try simultaneous bookings of the same space and equipment from two tenant accounts, including distinct equipment subranges, exact back-to-back boundaries, and overlaps introduced by setup/cleanup. Check that one cannot silently double-book a resource and that the user sees why.
2. Test create, edit, cancel, recurring reservation, shortened/extended reservation, rule change after existing reservations, timezone/DST boundary and administrator override. Capture whether buffers are symmetric and when their limits apply.
3. Create a record as tenant A, then attempt read/edit/export it as tenant B through the interface, direct link, reports/API if present, notification links, and kiosk. Repeat for recipe/customer/output data fields and prove they are not exposed because they are not collected.
4. Have a tenant define a per-equipment procedure; sign before/after as requested by the board; amend it and inspect authorship/timestamps/history. Attach a tenant-generated batch reference/handoff status where useful and verify it remains tenant-private. Confirm a product's generic monthly checklist does not masquerade as the requested per-block signoff.
5. Export a full simulated year as tenant A and as an administrator. Compare booking states, equipment, signoffs, amendments, deleted/cancelled events, attachments, timezone, CSV escaping, and whether tenant B's data appears. Verify restore/migration from the exported file.
6. Test both P5 alternatives with a missing signoff. Show the status and any booking effect, then verify only the board-authorized role may restrict/clear and a correction is auditable.
7. Test P6 alternatives using at least two materially different equipment types. Confirm task variation can be tenant-authored and no hidden kitchen-wide steps are imposed.
8. Test entrance tablet tasks with wet-hand proxy, gloves where relevant, glare, and realistic cleaning/placement. Record errors, completion, correction, reset, and fallback; separately check touch size/spacing.
9. On the admin view, complete handoff search, correction, tenant export, and booking-conflict resolution using keyboard only and the coordinator's screen reader. Record names, roles, selected/required states, focus sequence, errors, and status announcements.
10. Keep paper for six tenants during transition. Reconcile a same-day paper and digital record by a shared booking reference; confirm provenance survives late entry and network retry without false timestamps or duplicate records.
11. Obtain vendor quotes and confirm the annual/one-time budget horizon, ACH/card fee math against expected use, privacy/export provisions, onboarding/implementation, data retention, hardware, support, and contingency. For self-hosted trials, verify current security release, supported runtime, named patch/backup owner, restore, and access configuration.
