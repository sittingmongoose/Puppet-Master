## Material finding N1-F1 — CSV restored, rejected-row contents made optional

**Type:** FALSE_CORRECTION_OR_REJECTION. **Severity:** medium, material to O4 and O5/P4. **Affected axis:** 4, with preservation consequences in axis 5 and obligation consequences in axis 1.

Exact candidate file: /home/sittingmongoose/PM-Experiments/er11-20261009-116bb1e4/jobs/A-M10-A/treatment/reviser/final.md.

- [Line 49](/home/sittingmongoose/PM-Experiments/er11-20261009-116bb1e4/jobs/A-M10-A/treatment/reviser/final.md:49): “Make raw offending values or entire failed rows a deliberate privacy choice, with clear export disclosure; the original file remains authoritative for exact bytes.”
- [Line 82](/home/sittingmongoose/PM-Experiments/er11-20261009-116bb1e4/jobs/A-M10-A/treatment/reviser/final.md:82): “Make inclusion of raw offending values/full failed rows a disclosed privacy choice.”
- [Line 106](/home/sittingmongoose/PM-Experiments/er11-20261009-116bb1e4/jobs/A-M10-A/treatment/reviser/final.md:106) leaves whether values/full rows are included as a privacy decision; line 125 proposes testing an error CSV with and without raw values.

The governing original requirement is the exact frozen **P4: “Keep failed rows in an error CSV.”** It does not merely request a diagnostic CSV or source-location index. N1-D's P4 had already made the CSV optional. N1-C 11 rightly challenged removal of the output, but also proposed making offending values optional; N1-F 90 accepts that treatment. The final restores a mandatory CSV while retaining the content substitution. It does not clearly preserve a default failed-row CSV and identify a redacted sharing export as an additional, separately approved alternative.

Primary evidence was checked rather than treating the critic as authoritative. [DuckDB's reject documentation](https://duckdb.org/docs/current/data/csv/reading_faulty_csv_files), Reject Errors/Parameters, includes source-line payload separately from locations and messages (E02, corroborated by the web schema at lines 649–667). Location metadata and retained source bytes are distinct from row contents in an exported report. [OpenRefine's export manual](https://openrefine.org/docs/manual/exporting), Export a project, warns that project archives retain confidential history (E21/J context). That establishes a real sharing concern for that product, but does not authorize removing the specified contents of this assistant's error CSV. RFC 4180 §2 (E25) also provides no limitation that makes row payload incompatible with CSV.

The strongest counterargument is that redaction is useful for nonprofits and the immutable source preserves the originals locally. I accept both points as useful conditions. They do not resolve the exact placement obligation: a recipient inspecting or reprocessing the error CSV may have only diagnostics rather than the failed rows, and the final has reclassified that requirement without an owner decision superseding it. This is why the finding is material while mere correction-label imprecision is not. It does not infer a new privacy default or require unlimited byte-perfect malformed-row reconstruction; known raw row evidence and uncertain framing can be reported honestly under the original scope.
