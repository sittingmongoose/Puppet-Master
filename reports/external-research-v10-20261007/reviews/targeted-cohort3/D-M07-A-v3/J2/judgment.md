## 2. Consequential claims and governing conditions

**MF1 / P1 / C-A1 (F11): supported for the stated topology.** Frozen sqlwal §1 lines 177–180 and §2.2 lines 283–297 explicitly require same-host access and exclude network filesystems. Released sqlwalcode lines 129–142 corroborate the shared-memory requirement and the default Unix/Windows `-shm` mapping. Sqlwal §7 lines 601–629 explains the ordinary mmap implementation and a custom-VFS alternative. The final's default-implementation explanation is sound in context, and it correctly assesses the exceptions separately in MF4. Read-only access does not remove this topology restriction. Simultaneous readers and one writer are supported on a valid WAL deployment (sqlwal lines 164–168, 269–297); rejection of the stated direct-open topology is justified.

**MF2 / P2 / C-A2 (F13): writer-lock and snapshot facts supported; inference defective (D1).** Sqlwalcode `sqlite3WalBeginWriteTransaction`, lines 3680–3736, acquires the exclusive WAL_WRITE_LOCK and rejects a stale read snapshot with SQLITE_BUSY_SNAPSHOT. A busy lock normally yields SQLITE_BUSY; other errors are possible. The rule is one writer per database/WAL, not an independent writer allowance per host. Neither this code nor the same-host requirement proves F13's claim that a second host faces none of the enforcement. `walLockExclusive`, lines 1101–1114, calls the VFS shared-memory lock interface. Independently fetched **os_unix.c, version-3.51.3**, `unixShmSystemLock` lines 4703–4758, applies POSIX advisory locks to the shared-memory file descriptor and reports busy on failure. File-lock operation and coherent mmap visibility are distinct requirements. This is not proof that a particular mount works; it refutes the asserted implication from lack of supported shared memory to absence of any locking enforcement. Calling that implication an inference does not validate it. F19(b)'s application serialization policy may be reasonable, but its stated necessity does not follow once all SQLite connections have been moved to the same host, where SQLite itself already enforces one active writer.

**MF3 / P3 / C-A3 (F15): recovery mechanism supported, deployment certification absent.** Frozen sqlwal §9 lines 700–704 describes first-connection recovery after the last connection crashes and concurrent-query SQLITE_BUSY. Sqlwalcode `walIndexRecover`, lines 1374–1607, establishes recovery locks, reads and validates WAL frames, records commit frames, and rebuilds the transient index; lines 139–142 explain its transience. The routine can also fail for I/O, allocation, or format reasons, so source-described recovery is not an executed reliability result. Recovery depends on keeping the persistent WAL with the database (sqlwal §4 lines 478–492); the index's disposability does not make the WAL disposable. The final does not advise deleting it, but does not explicitly carry this operational condition. The same-host restriction legitimately limits the supported recovery scenario.

**MF4 / P4 / C-A4 (F17): supported conclusion and conditions.** Sqlwal §8 lines 635–668 requires EXCLUSIVE before first attempted WAL access, guarantees only one process accessing the database, and explains why returning to NORMAL is ineffective until WAL is exited when the shared index was omitted. Sqlpragma `locking_mode`, lines 1381–1408 and 1431–1437, corroborates the connection locking behavior. Sqlwal §7 lines 624–629 permits a heap index for threads within one process via a custom VFS. Neither repairs two simultaneous direct-opening hosts. The final gives the documented process condition more precisely than P13/C63's single-connection shorthand. EXCLUSIVE connection locks also prevent competing database access; the conclusion that this cannot repair two-host access is supported, regardless of that shorthand.

**MF5 / P5 / C-A5 / R25–41 (F7, F19, F37): useful deployment choice, overstated source resolution (D2).** Independently fetched lockingv3 §6, raw lines 552–576, matches the candidate's capture hash. It conditions corruption risk on malfunctioning OS locks/syncs, warns about many NFS implementations and some network filesystems, and advises avoiding SQLite network files. It does not establish that every Linux network mount is incapable of correct rollback-mode operation. Thus declining the unverified option is a justified conservative decision, while converting feasibility from unresolved to categorically resolved/rejected and calling this the only supported shape overstates the check. F37 repeats the certainty error. Mount-specific suitability remains unverified. A sound same-host application access path also requires a local database path; merely reducing the number of clients of the original network mount does not discharge MF1's network-filesystem exclusion. Returned-mode verification is supported by sqlwal §3 lines 405–410; persistence across reopen and across connections is supported by §3.3 lines 450–465. These operational additions are worth retaining.

**MF6 / P6 / C-A6 (F21): supported, with correctly bounded provenance.** Frozen sqlwal §11 lines 748–760 gives the likely affected range 3.7.0–3.51.2, fix in 3.51.3 and later, and backports 3.44.6/3.50.7. The dangerous pattern involves multiple connections on the same database in separate threads/processes attempting writes/checkpoints concurrently. Lines 777–807 describe the specific completed-checkpoint, subsequent checkpoint/reset race; concurrency alone is not a guarantee of corruption. Lines 822–835 substantiate the dated 2026-08-24 non-test-hook reproducer update and the telemetry-based low-occurrence/non-emergency wording. Restoring the likely qualifier and retaining the upgrade advice are justified. Raising practical salience is an interpretation of the new reproducer, not a measured new frequency. This is the 2026-10-07 documentation capture's report, not a historical statement that the later update was already in the March release. No reproducer was independently executed here.

**MF7 / P7 / C-A7 (F23): supported conditional advice, some lost recovery nuance.** Frozen sqlpragma `synchronous`, lines 1879–1915 and 1964–1976, says EXTRA equals FULL in WAL, FULL supplies ACID durability, and NORMAL retains consistency while potentially losing commits on power loss **or system crash**. It separately states durability across application crashes. Sqlwal lines 324–346 agrees on per-commit FULL sync and checkpoint sync ordering. The final retains the main tradeoff and does not falsely report a power-loss test. Its NORMAL suggestion is conditional on a rebuildable index; the brief never establishes rebuildability or acceptable job loss, so that condition must remain a product assumption. Omitting the system-crash/application-crash distinction weakens the requested restart assessment, although saying commits might be lost on power loss is itself true. Appropriate storage/VFS sync semantics are assumed by these source guarantees.

**MF8 / P8 / R-L1–L5 (F25): useful optional lead with material missing conditions (D3, D4).** Frozen sqlwal lines 256–263, 355–364, and 526–579 supports normal 1000-page autocheckpoints, starvation, reader gaps, and query/disk costs. Sqlpragma lines 2265–2278 identifies PASSIVE automatic checkpoints and the compile-time default override. The final's threshold is the ordinary default, not a verified property of a deployed build.

- **D3 — retention limit presented as an unqualified cap.** F25 says journal_size_limit caps the WAL left on disk; R61–64 accepts it as a disk-usage-cap companion. Sqlpragma lines 1307–1314 applies the WAL limit when the WAL resets. Released sqlwalcode lines 516, 1741–1745, and 4207–4217 enforce it at the first transaction in a new/reused WAL, and explicitly raise the truncation size to preserve the transaction's required frames when those exceed the configured limit. `walLimitSize`, lines 2391–2406, also treats truncation failures as logged, nonfatal errors. It is useful for retained allocation after recycling, not a hard cap on a live WAL pinned by readers or on a large transaction. This governing condition matters directly to the advertised starvation/growth remedy.
- **D4 — completion conditional on locks/readers and handler result.** Sqlpragma lines 2282–2334 validates the proposed pragma syntax and states TRUNCATE's successful-completion condition. Lines 2342–2356 specify returned busy/completion and frame counts. Sqlwalcode lines 2095–2118 stops retrying when the handler returns zero; lines 2347–2388 may return busy without restart/truncation; lines 4324–4355 can fail to acquire checkpoint locks without invoking the handler or fall back to PASSIVE if the writer lock is unavailable. Therefore R55–56's completion guarantee and F25's unqualified blocking/truncation description need qualifications. Measurement gating of thresholds does not substitute for checking actual checkpoint completion. The simpler guarantee in sqlwal §6 lines 575–579 must be read with the detailed pragma and released-code conditions. Reader-blocking possibility is supported by that WAL overview; the detailed pragma additionally notes writer blocking and readers proceeding during RESTART.
- Busy_timeout is supported contention hygiene, not a promise that all contention retries. Frozen sqlpragma lines 576–588 describes setting/replacing the single connection handler. Reviewer-fetched busy_timeout raw lines 127–140 describes finite waiting, eventual busy, and nonpositive timeout disabling the handler; busy_handler lines 149–171 permits immediate busy to avoid deadlock and replacement of an existing handler. The default-null fact checked by this reviewer does not turn the candidate's proposed default check into an executed candidate check. Sqlwalcode's checkpoint-lock exception above is directly relevant to this maintenance lead.

## 4. Incorrect rejection or correction

The version-range hedge correction and detection of the dated reproducer update are justified. The decision to reject immutable access to an actively changing file is justified. The decision that EXCLUSIVE/custom-VFS one-process exceptions do not rescue the two-host deployment is justified. The final correctly accepts RESTART/TRUNCATE pragma syntax despite the narrower WAL overview list; the pragma explicitly defines the argument forms and their equivalence to the C API.

The material incorrect correction is P5's unresolved rollback suitability being changed to source-resolved categorical exclusion (D2). A conservative refusal to deploy on the unverified mount is not itself erroneous; presenting that refusal as a proven universal source fact is. C45–47 also incorrectly approves the cross-host-enforcement inference as sound, and MF2/MF5 carry it forward (D1). Neither inference label nor a source citation repairs that logical gap. The general WAL growth limit and guaranteed-completion acceptance in reserve L3/L4 are overcorrections of incomplete operational advice, as established in D3/D4.
