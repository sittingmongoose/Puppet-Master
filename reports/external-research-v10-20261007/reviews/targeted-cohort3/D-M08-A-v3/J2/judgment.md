## 2. Consequential claims and governing conditions

This checks every material critique item and the corresponding final content against the primary text, rather than treating the critique or citation count as authority.

1. **Representation identity — supported.** Critique:7, amendment:5, dependency:9, final:5. S1 §3.2, lines 719–752, makes selection request-dependent. §8.8.1, lines 3403–3422, scopes strong-validator uniqueness to a resource; the same value across different resources is not equivalence evidence. Lines 3463–3471 and §8.8.3.3, lines 3718–3724, make coding significant: encoded and unencoded data cannot properly share a strong tag when their representation data differs. §12.5.5, lines 5670–5688, allows selection factors outside request syntax for Vary: *. The final correctly avoids treating a selector snapshot as identity proof. Its changed-selection fallback is conservative, not an RFC prohibition against origin-validated reuse; lines 5701–5707 expressly allow that validation.

2. **If-Range and replacement — supported with the stated completion gate.** Critique:9, amendment:7, dependency:11, final:7. S1 §13.1.5, lines 6136–6147, requires Range alongside If-Range, prohibits weak tags, and allows a date only when no entity tag exists and the date is strong. Lines 6153–6180 require exact date matching or strong tag comparison and cause false If-Range to suppress Range, not itself produce 412. The actual source's client date-strength test in §8.8.2.2, lines 3543–3552, requires a cached response Date at least one second after Last-Modified plus the stated common-clock or sufficient-separation basis; an arbitrary Last-Modified is not enough. Other preconditions or ordinary errors can still yield different statuses (§13.2.1–2, lines 6186–6196 and 6236–6278). Final:7's non-guarantee of 200 is therefore correct. GET 200 content represents the target (§15.3.1, lines 6917–6925). Complete receipt is distinct from seeing status 200 (§6.1, lines 1998–2016); atomic staging is sensible local policy, not an RFC-mandated filesystem operation. Close-delimited completion can remain ambiguous; no universal integrity proof was established.

3. **206 validation and combination — supported except for M1's byte-coordinate gap.** Critique:11, amendment:9, dependency:13, final:9. S1 §15.3.7, lines 7072–7083, permits a subset and requires examining Content-Type/Content-Range. §15.3.7.1, lines 7110–7113, requires Content-Range for a single part. §14.4, lines 6596–6607 and 6617–6638, permits an unknown total, forbids recombination with an unknown unit or invalid interval, and makes a known total no greater than the last position invalid. Inclusive zero-based positions (§14.1.2, lines 6386–6400) justify the body-count equation. Requiring start=N and compatibility with a saved total are conservative local contiguous-file checks. They must operate in the representation's coded coordinate space. §15.3.7.3, lines 7191–7200, permits combining same-target partial GET responses only with a common strong validator. Multipart refusal is a supported-parser policy; §15.3.7.2, lines 7170–7187, also forbids a server's multipart answer to a single-range request and requires per-part inspection when multipart is used.

4. **416 — supported.** Critique:13, amendment:11, dependency:15, final:11. S1 §15.5.17, lines 7757–7769, covers unsatisfiable requests and excessive small/overlapping ranges. The field is SHOULD, not MUST. §14.4, lines 6640–6647, defines bytes */N as current selected length, without establishing saved-prefix identity. §15.5.17, lines 7777–7785, and §14.2, lines 6476–6479, permit ignoring Range and returning a full response. No 416 body is representation range payload. The final does not confuse a matching length with validation.

5. **Encoding and lengths — correct source facts, incomplete operational preservation.** Critique:15,21; amendment:13,19; dependency:17,23; final:13,19. S1 §8.4, lines 3069–3075, defines the representation and its metadata in coded form. §14.1.2, lines 6397–6400, excludes decoded octets from range coordinates. §8.6, lines 3203–3210, and §15.3.7, lines 7091–7095, support message length versus representation total. Accept-Encoding identity denotes no encoding (§12.5.3, lines 5537–5538), but a request header does not establish a particular application's exposed byte stream. The RFCs identify no selected desktop library. That uncertainty is correctly retained. It does not make decoded-prefix counts suitable range offsets; M1 concerns this missing binding.

6. **Metadata — supported at recommendation scope, with precise limits.** Critique:17, amendment:15, dependency:19, final:15. S1 §15.3.7.3, lines 7202–7215, governs matching responses: the newest incomplete 200 supplies its fields; a new 206 combined with stored 200s uses the most recent matching 200 fields; all-206 combination starts from the matching *stored* response with the most recent fields and incorporates newly supplied fields except Content-Range. The final's “most recent response” is compressed wording and must be understood in that stored-response context, not as discarding the previous fields in favor of a sparse new 206. This matters because an If-Range 206 may omit other representation fields (§15.3.7, lines 7097–7102). S2 §3.4, lines 444–451, adds cache updating **under §3.2**; lines 375–410 exclude Content-Length and specify storage/processed-field exceptions. A naive overwrite of every new field would not be supported. A complete union also requires complete-200 processing and full-length Content-Length (S1 lines 7217–7228). The final states a summary and requires an explicit sidecar policy; it does not deliver an executable cache algorithm or claim all fields can be overwritten without exceptions. Those limits prevent treating the summary as a complete cache implementation. Its separation of a non-cache sidecar from HTTP-cache rules is justified.

7. **Checks — suitable proposals, not empirical evidence.** Critique:19, amendment:17, dependency:21, final:17. All original categories in draft:17 remain: unchanged resume, changed-tag/full-200 replacement, shorter/416, partial or malformed 206, body-length mismatch, and gzip/decompression. All requested additions remain: weak tags, strong dates, absent 416 Content-Range, failed-condition/non-200, multipart, unknown units, changed Vary/coding, and metadata refresh. Wrong offsets are also proposed. The recommendation does not supply assertions, fixtures, a selected library, or results; none is inferred from RFC inspection.

8. **Library uncertainty — correctly retained; the predecessor's optional lead needs qualification.** Critique:21, amendment:19, dependency:23, final:19. It is justified to defer exact API/writer byte exposure until the library is selected and tested. It is not justified to treat consistent decoded storage, by itself, as safe for coded byte-range resumption. S1 §14.1.2 supplies the governing restriction regardless of library choice. This is the predecessor issue that should have been separated from the otherwise valid uncertainty.

## 4. Incorrect rejection or correction

The rejection of **unconditional** overwrite is justified; it preserves the supported full-200 replacement behavior rather than rejecting it. Refusing unknown/invalid range recombination is normative; declining unsupported multipart processing is a local scope choice, not a claim that every multipart 206 is invalid. Accepting unknown complete lengths and retaining justified library uncertainty are also correct.

**M1 — incorrect broad acceptance / missing qualification:** critique:21's suggestion of consistent coded **or decoded** storage was accepted without its essential range-coordinate condition. Draft:15 had the narrower, supported raw-coded-or-verified-identity rule. Amendment:13 replaced it with consistency; dependency:17 recited coded offsets but dependency:5 nevertheless declared no amendment needed; final:7,9,13 retained the unqualified saved-count rule. This is not a failure of source identity or of the ETag comparison rule. It is the loss of the condition connecting stored bytes to the wire range coordinate.

For a conceptual, unexecuted illustration, a coded prefix might contain 100 octets while its decoded output contains 1,000. A request constructed from the decoded saved-file count as bytes=1000- addresses coded offset 1,000, not the missing coded offset 100. Consistent decoding, the same target, and a matching strong ETag do not repair that coordinate error. It can yield a spurious unsatisfiable request or a gap; subsequent body checks may reject the response, but are not a substitute for a valid request offset. No particular compression fixture or corruption outcome was run or asserted.

The final's accurate first sentence about coded data and its library uncertainty reduce the breadth of the problem. They do not explicitly restore the original actionable storage/count restriction. I assess this as a material omission with an unsafe interpretation available, not as an explicit statement that all decoded resumes are valid.
