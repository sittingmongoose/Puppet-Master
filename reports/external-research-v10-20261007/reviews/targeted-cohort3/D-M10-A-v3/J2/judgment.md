## 2. Consequential claims and governing conditions

**Finding 1, storage eligibility (F 13; bounded answer F 5): supported amendment.** P 7 correctly lists the normal §3 gates, including final status and status understanding where required. C §3, lines 286–324, enumerates them; understanding means recognizing and implementing specified caching behavior, lines 329–331. C §5.2.2.7, lines 1327–1332, permits unqualified private storage even without ordinary heuristic cacheability, subject to §3. F correctly adds C §5.2.2.3, lines 1248–1256: the cache must implement must-understand and understand/conform to the status-code requirements before the SHOULD-ignore-no-store exception applies. This is not general permission for arbitrary no-store storage. The extension caveat is supported by C §3, lines 326–327, and §5.2.3, lines 1410–1439: recognized behavioral extensions can modify baseline requirements; unrecognized directives must be ignored. No actual extension behavior beyond those supplied sources is asserted or verified.

**Finding 2, Authorization boundary (F 15; F 5): supported, with ordinary §3 gates still applicable.** C §3.5, lines 455–464, names must-revalidate, public and s-maxage as this specification's enabling response directives. Their presence removes the Authorization-specific shared-cache obstacle; it does not waive no-store, unqualified private, other storage gates or reuse conditions. C §5.2.2.9, lines 1371–1376, expressly retains §3 constraints. F's opening general-gates and extension qualification provide that context; I do not interpret its list as a universal exhaustive ban on all future recognized extensions. H §12.5.5, lines 5727–5729, actually contains the stated explanation for omitting Authorization from Vary. That explanation is not an account-partition algorithm or a blanket reversal of explicit shared-cache permission. No source-supported private-cache claim was wrongly rejected.

**Finding 3, language variants (F 17; F 7): supported.** C §4.1, lines 530–557, requires matching selecting fields for reuse without revalidation, permits only syntactically allowed whitespace changes, combining compatible field lines and normalization known to preserve semantics, and makes absence match only absence and Vary '*' always fail matching. F's normalization description must retain these conditions; it does not authorize arbitrary reordering of language preferences. H §12.5.4, lines 5634–5641, confirms ordering can affect equal-quality preferences. H §12.5.5, lines 5701–5707, expressly allows origin-validated reuse as the alternative to matching. C §4.1, lines 576–579, and §4.3.1, lines 814–825, permit forwarding a validating request when a variant cannot be chosen. A mismatching cached body is not automatically approved by sending a request or receiving an unrelated 304. F finding 5's suitability/normal-gates qualification preserves that limit.

**Finding 4, freshness and reuse (F 19; F 7): supported refinement, not evidence that P specified the wrong equality rule.** C §4.2, lines 619–624, defines the strict comparison freshness_lifetime > current_age, so equality is not fresh. E 13 used the RFC's looser prose about age not exceeding lifetime; F correctly makes the formula explicit. P 13 merely referred to age versus lifetime and never supplied an incorrect operator. C §4.2.1, lines 654–671, gives shared-only s-maxage, then max-age, then Expires minus Date, with received-time fallback when Date is absent. C §4.2.2, lines 688–701, permits heuristics only without explicit expiry and for eligible status/explicit cacheability. F's 'permitted heuristics' is conditionally correct; it promises no particular retention algorithm. C §4.2.3, lines 721–782, governs current age, including Age/transit/residence; the final does not claim age is simply time since saving. Qualified response no-cache may permit reuse with the named fields omitted or after successful revalidation, subject to other restrictions (C §5.2.2.4, lines 1273–1281). This real exception is retained in F 19, although its other no-cache summaries are less precise when extracted alone.

**Finding 5, validation (F 21; F 7): partly supported; defect J2-D1 is substantive.** The validator-force amendment is correct: C §4.3.1, lines 846–859, requires relevant supplied entity tags on generated validation requests, recommends Last-Modified for a single stored response on a non-subrange request when present, and separately permits date-based range conditions. H §13.1.2, lines 5880–5883 and 5928–5932, governs weak If-None-Match comparison and GET/HEAD 304 outcomes. The 304 suitability condition is also supported: C §4.3.4, lines 957–984, first limits eligible responses and then filters by strong validators, weak validators/most recent matching response, or the narrow single-response/no-validator case. C lines 986–988 and §3.2, lines 375–410, govern metadata updates with their exceptions. H §15.4.5, lines 7456–7478, confirms relevant 304 metadata and absence of response content. F does not authorize updating any arbitrary body on any 304.

**J2-D1 — omitted 5xx exception to the mandatory full-response claim.** F 21 says 'a full response MUST satisfy the request', while F 7 presents the same behavior without the exception. C §4.3.3, lines 938–942, contains the general rule to use a content-bearing full response and optional storage subject to §3. But the immediately following lines 944–949 expressly allow a cache receiving a 5xx while validating to forward that error **or** act as if the server failed to respond, potentially serving a previous response under §4.2.4 or retrying validation. F's normal-gates qualification attaches to its 304 statement, not this mandatory full-response statement, and no final finding/fixture restores the 5xx alternative.

Concrete source counterexample, not an executed fixture: an eligible same-account, same-URI/language entry with private, max-age=60 and an ETag is stale; it has no no-cache/must-revalidate or other applicable prohibition, and the client explicitly accepts its amount of staleness with max-stale. Validation receives a 503 with explanatory content. C §4.3.3 permits the constrained old-response or retry alternative; F's unqualified mandatory full-response disposition excludes it. H §15.6, lines 7854–7860, confirms that 5xx can contain error representations, so the exception cannot be escaped by treating 5xx as never a full/content-bearing response. H §15.6.4, lines 7888–7897, identifies the temporary-unavailability condition. The fallback is not automatic permission to serve any stale body: C §4.2.4, lines 790–799, and §5.2.1.2, lines 1137–1142, still require permission/disconnection and prohibit conflicting response directives. This is an in-scope error in when validation/stale fallback may occur, not an unrelated implementation demand.

**Finding 6, directives/offline (F 23; F 9): otherwise supported.** C §4.2.4, lines 790–799, binds stale service to disconnection or explicit client/origin permission, including an applicable out-of-band contract, without defeating prohibitions. C §5.2.2.2, lines 1224–1235, bars stale must-revalidate reuse and requires a disconnected error, normally 504 unless another status is more applicable; it does not bar fresh reuse of that response. Unqualified response no-cache requires successful origin validation even for a fresh stored response (C lines 1264–1271). C §5.2.2.8, lines 1357–1362, and §5.2.2.10, lines 1390–1402, limit proxy-revalidate/s-maxage stale restrictions to shared caches. Private caches do not use s-maxage as freshness lifetime.

Request no-cache's advisory character is directly supported by C §5.2.1, lines 1113–1114, and §5.2.1.4, lines 1166–1168. For request no-store, C §5.2.1.5, lines 1172–1178, defines nonvolatile-storage prohibition and best-effort volatile removal; lines 1185–1187 preserve an already-stored response when satisfying such a request. F preserves these actual effects rather than claiming deletion of existing entries. Its general advisory statement is the RFC's own introductory wording, followed here by the specific no-store semantics; I do not invent an extra failure from that source-level juxtaposition. Response no-store also covers the immediate request and prohibits later response reuse (C §5.2.2.5, lines 1297–1305), with the specified must-understand exception (lines 1312–1313). F's summarized response-storage language is not permission to store the request.

**Finding 7, fixtures (F 25; F 33–36): supported proposals with limited coverage.** The inherited 200 GET/private/max-age/language/ETag setup is eligible under C §3 and H §15.3.1, lines 6953–6955; fresh matching hits, nonmatching/absent misses, account isolation, 304/changed-200 handling and stale/directive comparisons target distinct source conditions. Added shared Authorization, must-understand with implemented versus unimplemented status semantics, and existing-entry request no-store cases are scientifically useful under the sections above. Their setup must still obey general storage/reuse gates; neither public nor must-understand is a universal override. They are minimal illustrative checks, not an exhaustive suite. They omit the material 5xx alternative diagnosed above.

**Finding 8, uncertainty/process (F 27; disposition F 29): scientific uncertainty supported, process history not independently established.** No supplied application implementation establishes actual account keying, retention, offline UX or HTTP-cache compliance. C §6 supports the cache-versus-application distinction. F discloses a claimed nonblind reading order; I retain that disclosure as an authored limitation rather than attest to hidden instrumentation.
