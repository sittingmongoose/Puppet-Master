## Material findings

### M1 — The margin contract does not establish that a passenger may alight and board

**Locations:** final lines **39–54** (scheduled calculation and permitted/prohibited transfer rules), **64–66** (realtime margin gates), **94–101** (acceptance fixtures), **112** (P3 comparison), and **146** (remaining decisions). The full final and its explicit withheld cases contain no treatment of Schedule `stop_times.pickup_type` or `drop_off_type`, no admission rule requiring regular passenger pickup/drop-off at the selected stops, and no test for those restrictions. The references to alighting/reboarding at lines 52/134 concern *transfer type 5*, a distinct issue.

**Governing primary evidence:** [GTFS Schedule reference at google/transit commit 3c9e7b904b5035349622f03e11851e25c16d1d99](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google/transit/3c9e7b904b5035349622f03e11851e25c16d1d99/gtfs/spec/en/reference.md), captured as `sources/public/gtfs_schedule_reference.md`, lines **307–308 and 316–317**. Arrival/departure time fields and passenger pickup/drop-off permission are separate. Value 1 denies the corresponding passenger activity; values 2/3 require phone or driver arrangements. These are stable Schedule semantics, not an inference from experimental realtime fields.

**Applicable condition and consequence:** two active, fixed-schedule trips can share a curated transfer location, have ordinary applicable transfer rules, and pass every described date/time/ID/freshness gate, yet the outgoing stop has `pickup_type=1`, or the incoming stop has `drop_off_type=1`. For example, incoming arrival 10:00, outgoing departure 10:10, allowance 120 seconds yields a **+480-second scheduled margin** under the authored formula while boarding the outgoing vehicle is prohibited. A `transfers.txt` type-3 check does not subsume stop-level pickup/drop-off permission. The persistent no-guarantee notice addresses uncertainty about making a connection; it does not explain that this is not a passenger transfer at all.

This is an in-scope necessary interpretation condition of useful transfer advice, not a request for citywide routing or accessibility/occupancy support. Selecting actual feeds later could establish that the condition never occurs at the chosen stops, but that admission check is absent too. It is therefore a material conditional omission in the purported complete revision. The example is a **source-text counterexample to the specified gates and arithmetic**, not an executed feed or application witness. I do not claim the unknown feeds contain such rows or that a nonexistent runtime has produced this display.

### M2 — Replacement provenance does not specify realtime/static version coherence

**Locations:** final lines **19–21** (feed identity and snapshots), **64–66** (join and margin eligibility), **70** (historical source record), **94–96** (replacement and realtime fixtures), **110** (P1 comparison), and **146** (dependencies). The final preserves the identity of schedule A in an old saved screen and schedule B in a new view. It does not specify what happens when a fresh realtime response identifies A while B is currently loaded, or how a delay is bound to its referenced schedule. The explicit realtime gates mention feed, trip, service date, location, sequence, age, and missing timing, but no schedule-version mismatch gate or association policy. A generic future parser/feed compatibility check does not specify this transition behavior.

**Governing primary evidence:** [GTFS Realtime reference at the same immutable commit](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google/transit/3c9e7b904b5035349622f03e11851e25c16d1d99/gtfs-realtime/spec/en/reference.md), `sources/public/gtfs_rt_reference.md`, line **109** (`FeedHeader.feed_version`), **169** (schedule-relative delay), and **174–189** (StopTimeEvent). The [Trip Updates guide](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google/transit/3c9e7b904b5035349622f03e11851e25c16d1d99/gtfs-realtime/spec/en/trip-updates.md), `sources/public/gtfs_rt_trip_updates.md`, line **24**, describes deriving event time from scheduled time plus delay. The governing [protobuf definition](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google/transit/3c9e7b904b5035349622f03e11851e25c16d1d99/gtfs-realtime/proto/gtfs-realtime.proto), `sources/public/gtfs_rt_proto.proto`, lines **76–79**, defines the optional realtime `feed_version` as matching the static feed on which the realtime data is based.

**Applicable condition and consequence:** static A and B reuse the same trip/stop IDs and service date; B changes the incoming scheduled arrival from 10:00 to 10:10. A fresh, otherwise usable TripUpdate declares `FeedHeader.feed_version=A` and an arrival delay of +60 seconds relative to A. Correct timing under A is 10:01; applying that delay to B yields 10:11. With outgoing departure 10:20 and allowance two minutes, the two margins are **17 minutes** versus **7 minutes**. Feed identity, service date, matching IDs and recent entity timestamps do not distinguish them. Storing the hashes of both input objects makes the mixture auditable, not semantically coherent.

The field is optional, so this finding does **not** demand that every producer supply it or assert that its absence makes every feed unusable. The concrete condition is an **explicit mismatch when supplied**, with schedule-relative timing and reused identifiers. Other version-association methods would require their own stated applicability. The proposed feed-replacement test proves old/new historical attribution only; it does not test this condition. This is an unresolved material behavior contract, not proof that the candidate intended or executed the incorrect arithmetic. No application or real-feed experiment was run.
