4. **Importer-policy amendment (applicable to 3.12.3).** Pass explicit filter on every extraction: `tar.extractall(dest, filter='data')` / `tar.extract(m, dest, filter='data')`, or `shutil.unpack_archive(path, dest, filter='data')` for tar archives; alternatively set `TarFile.extraction_filter = tarfile.data_filter` (function, not string) on importer-owned class/instance. Catch `tarfile.FilterError` (incl. `AbsolutePathError`, `OutsideDestinationError`, `LinkOutsideDestinationError`) and fail closed (abort import; no fallback to `fully_trusted`). Do not rely on the 3.12 default: it warns and behaves as `fully_trusted`. Enforce in review/lint (no `extract(all)` without `filter=`), not docs alone.

5. **Residual limitations; no universal safety claim.** `data` reduces "surprising/dangerous" behaviors but does not make extraction universally safe: archive content can still be malicious; custom filters can reintroduce risk; `tar` filter is weaker than `data` by design; three outcomes (fatal vs nonfatal vs skip-`None`) must all be handled and fatal handling depends on `errorlevel`; 3.14-switch text is 3.12.3-docs/PEP context, behavior pinned here is 3.12.3 only. No claim about CVEs beyond this lead, other versions/branches, or backports.

6. **Bounded additional regression (PROPOSED, not executed; stop rule met).** Add one test in importer's suite (not CPython): build minimal in-memory tar with one `../evil.txt` member and one absolute-path member, call importer's extract helper under default policy, assert it raises `OutsideDestinationError`/`AbsolutePathError` (or importer's wrapped error) and writes nothing outside dest; repeat with `filter='fully_trusted'` only to assert fail-open (documents why explicit `data` is required). Stop: decision (explicit `data` + fail-closed) is supported by (1)–(3); no further corpus needed.
