## Recommendation

Keep the uploaded score document as the human reference. Let the librarian create explicit work, edition, and part identities, record why the group may use and distribute each source, and preserve every upload and transformation as a separate version. A page-region mark tied to one identified preview is the dependable first locator. A supplied or generated structured score may offer navigation clues, but a measure number or file-local ID does not establish that two editions or parts correspond.

Accept authorized MusicXML/MXL as an additional input. Keep OMR optional and outside the minimum workflow. If considered later, pin the exact build and settings, retain its source and native project data, label its output unreviewed until a person checks that precise result against the exact source, and record known errors. Audiveris 5.11.0 is useful evidence of both possible value and release-specific limits, not a reliability guarantee or a selected dependency [R01–R11].

For collaboration, start with immutable director-published revisions, member-readable offline packets, private personal marks, and separate suggestions tied to the revision they were based on. Reconnection must surface stale suggestions for a person to resolve. A multi-writer CRDT could be evaluated later if simultaneous shared editing becomes a demonstrated need; its merge behavior would not itself define roles, privacy, rights, deletion, or musical correspondence. Leave the renderer, storage, transport, and packet schema unresolved until a small comparative pilot addresses those gaps.

This proposal applies to one ensemble and a small authorized score set. It excludes a public score marketplace, copyrighted-catalog acquisition, automatic composition, replacement of professional engraving, and live performance assessment. No score collection was supplied or used; the score mentioned in Audiveris issue #971 was not downloaded.

## Discovery and useful alternatives

Discovery began from the brief’s mixed intake, rehearsal-note, weak-connectivity, and handoff needs, before reading PLAN.md. Initial public-primary discovery included Audiveris’s project description, W3C annotation and media-fragment specifications, and the MusicXML 4.0 reference [R01, R12–R15]. The options below address different parts of that user problem; none is a planted defect screen.

| Mechanism | What it gives this group | Cost, failure mode, or boundary | Proposal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Page image/document with a visual sidecar anchor** | Works with scans, photos, PDFs, and notation documents whether or not any score recognition succeeds. A musician can confirm the exact page they were handed. W3C’s annotation model separates a note from its target and has spatial selectors; Media Fragments defines a rectangular xywh convention for a media resource [R14, R15]. | An anchor means “this region of this preview,” not “this musical event.” A changed crop, orientation, render, or page makes it stale. | **Structured MusicXML/MXL input with logical clues** | An existing editable score can expose parts and measures for navigation and may help a librarian relate passages. Preserve the exact source digest; use part/measure labels only as secondary clues, never as automatic cross-edition identity. |
| **Human review of source pages vs. optional OMR** | Manual page review works for every permitted input. OMR may save some entry effort and create a structured navigation derivative. | **Revisioned publication vs. multi-writer shared editing** | A director-owned published snapshot and separately queued suggestions make the offline rehearsal state and who approved it inspectable. Concurrent shared editing can reduce waiting when several people must edit the same note. | A single-publisher flow needs stale-base review and conflict choices. A CRDT option requires an implementation and transport decision and still does not answer visibility, access, deletion, or rights. | Start with published revisions and suggestions; reconsider multi-writer sync only if observed use justifies its added conflict and access model. |

An embedded or flattened PDF mark is another possible presentation route. It may make a packet convenient in common readers, but can mix personal and shared marks or lose editability. A separate sidecar preserves the original and can carry private/shared lanes, but requires a declared interchange schema and compatible reader. Keep those as pilot alternatives; do not claim either one is interoperable until round-trip behavior is tested. Similarly, an offline package is simpler to inspect and hand between volunteers, while a live sync service makes later review easier at the cost of authentication, transport, backup, conflict, and account-operation responsibilities. No commercial account or cloud service is assumed.

## Primary evidence and the supplied critique

### Audiveris 5.11.0: bounded implementation and history review

The official release page identified 5.11.0 as the latest Audiveris release at capture; it names commit 9e1e55cd2746037d059345881c53e6a6754bffbd and was published 2026-07-11 [R07]. The README at that exact commit describes an OMR editor and MusicXML 4.0 subset exporter, and cautions that perfect recognition is out of reach in many cases [R01]. The pinned LICENSE identifies AGPL-3.0 [R02]. These are documented facts about this component/release, not measured performance, a license compatibility finding, or a product choice.

At that exact commit, I inspected the implementation. RunTable.afterUnmarshal calls afterMarshal(null); afterMarshal loops using sequences.length without a null check [R03]. ZipFileSystem.open(Path) passes a jar: URI to FileSystems.newFileSystem and does not itself recover an already-open filesystem [R04]. Issue #971 reports an NPE from the same reload path and then FileSystemAlreadyExistsException from its recovery attempt on build 9e1e55cd…; it names a multi-page reproducer and was open at capture [R11]. The source is consistent with the report. This is implementation corroboration of a user’s report, **not an independent reproduction, proof of how often it occurs, or evidence that every score triggers it**. The reporter’s named IMSLP score was not fetched.

The separate historical path is narrower. Issue #953 reports two null-pointer failures that abort batch MusicXML export on imperfect/skewed scans in Audiveris 5.10.2 [R10]. PR #954 adds a null guard in StaffBarlineInter.getBounds() and a missing-geometry fallback in MeasureStack.getXOffset(); the patch touches those implementation files, and the same changes are present at 5.11.0 [R05, R06, R08, R09]. The 5.11.0 release notes list a fix for imperfect-scan export [R07]. The PR author reports applying the patch over 5.10.2, recompiling the two classes, and running batch export on photographed multi-meter parts that had failed; this is a maintainer-contributed manual check, not an independently repeated run. The patch changes two implementation files and adds no automated regression-test file. Therefore this history supports “these two reported code paths were addressed and shipped,” not “imperfect scans now export reliably.” The separate #971 report remains a concrete reason to keep OMR optional and fail-visible.

### Passage identity and coordinate conventions

MusicXML 4.0 distinguishes measure attributes that the earlier proposal had compressed into “file-local IDs.” The optional id is unique within the document. The required number attribute identifies a measure and is used to group measures in partwise-to-timewise conversion; in partwise files, it should match across parts that share the same left barline. It is often numeric but need not be. The optional id is unique within the document. The optional text can show a display number different from number and is ignored when implicit=yes. implicit=yes marks measures whose number should not appear, such as pickup measures. non-controlling=yes allows a part’s left barline not to coincide with other parts’ barlines [R12]. Thus a logical locator should carry an exact structured-file digest and part identity, preserve number and optional id/text as separate fields, and remain file-scoped. Do not derive cross-part or cross-edition equivalence from matching labels.

W3C Web Annotation is an analogy for separating an annotation body from its target and expressing selectors; it does not define a score-specific anchoring or transport system. Its TextQuoteSelector describes normalized text, so ordinary quoted-text anchoring should not be assumed to identify notation symbols [R14]. Media Fragments’ spatial xywh expresses x, y, width and height with a top-left image origin; pixel is the default unit and percentages are relative to the original media dimensions [R15]. That convention does not choose our preview renderer, PDF page transform, orientation normalization, page-index base, half-open edge rule, or reattachment algorithm.

**Proposed visual record, not a standard claim:** source-asset ID; original-source digest; preview digest; zero-based page index (with the human-visible page label kept separately when available); preview width and height in pixels; positive x/y/width/height in that preview’s orientation-normalized raster frame; and renderer/version plus crop/rotation transform. If the app chooses half-open region bounds, state that as its own convention. Store pencil-like strokes as ordered sidecar points in the same declared frame with units/style. A changed source or preview digest makes the target stale: keep the old mark on its old target and ask a human to reattach. Never auto-move a note because a measure number matches.

A shared rehearsal instruction needed on differently paginated parts should be one note with separate, librarian/director-confirmed anchors for each part/version. Preserve a “not matched” state where the person cannot establish a safe relation. Page marks remain part-specific; notation clues can help a person find a passage but must not erase that distinction.

### Provenance, privacy, and export

PROV-O provides useful vocabulary for modeling files/records as entities, transformations as activities, and contributors or tools as agents, with relations such as wasDerivedFrom [R16]. This is a design analogy only. A hash identifies bytes, not authorship, edition identity, permission, or rights. The librarian must record the group’s basis to ingest and distribute a source to named members; software must not infer permission from a successful upload or public URL.

## Integrated minimum workflow proposal

1. **Set boundaries and roles.** One ensemble; one librarian maintains intake, identity, and permission notes; a director selects and publishes shared rehearsal notes; members read assigned published material and maintain their own private marks. The same volunteer may hold more than one role, but a publish/share action remains explicit and attributable.
2. **Import without collapsing identity.** Create local stable IDs for work, edition, part, publication revision, source asset, preview, and annotation. Keep supplied title, composer/arranger, publisher/edition statement, year, instrument name, and handwritten label as editable fields separate from IDs. An upload creates a new immutable asset version with uploader, received time, media type, byte size, digest, source note, correction history, and permission/provenance note. Identical hashes may identify duplicate bytes; they do not merge different work, edition, contributor, or rights records. A replacement is a linked new version.
3. **Preserve source; surface failures.** Keep the uploaded bytes even if parsing or rendering fails. Show which derivative failed and allow a retry without overwriting the source or any previously reviewed output. Record preview/parse/OMR input digest, tool and version, settings, output digest, time, and error state. Accept a small, explicit format set initially; do not imply every notation format imports identically.
4. **Review each interpretation narrowly.** Track unreviewed, reviewed, reviewed with known errors, rejected, and stale for a named source and exact derivative. Record reviewer, time, and notes. Replacement, reprocessing, changed settings, or a different export makes the previous review stale for that new derivative. Avoid a single “score checked” badge that could hide unreviewed pages or parts. OMR is a pilot option and is not required for packet creation.
5. **Annotate separate targets.** Personal marks default private to their author. Director notes become shared only on an explicit publication action. Each annotation stores body, author, create/update times, visibility, target asset/preview version, anchor record, and publication revision if shared. Notes about the same rehearsal passage can be related across part-specific anchors only after a human confirms that relation. Replacement never deletes or silently moves the old mark.
6. **Rehearse offline and review later.** The director publishes an identified immutable revision. A member can download the assigned authorized parts and shared notes, read them without connectivity, and add personal marks locally. A member’s proposed shared change is a distinct pending suggestion carrying the publication ID it used as its base. On return, a newer base triggers a visible stale-review step. If two local edits compete, retain both or ask the member to choose; do not silently overwrite. Retry/import must be idempotent, and last downloaded revision plus pending state must be visible. The initial proposal does not assume a continuous network or service account.
7. **Gate access and state deletion limits.** Check roles and recipient authorization when viewing, caching, publishing, generating a packet, and exporting. Exclude private marks from shared packets by default; add an explicit preview if a member elects to export their own marks. Before real use, choose retention periods for originals, derivatives, shared/private notes, revision history, backups, and local caches. A withdrawal should prevent future publication/distribution and initiate deletion from managed copies; tell members that downloaded or printed packets cannot be recalled and define offline/backup expiry. Do not promise complete deletion until these copies and time windows are specified.
8. **Create a portable handoff.** Export an account-free bundle for a named rehearsal revision. The proposed bundle includes only authorized page/source files needed by recipients, a UTF-8 manifest, the publication’s shared notes and sidecar anchors, and selected optional derivatives. Record IDs and human labels for work/edition/part/version, asset digests, packet revision/time, reviewer state, permission/provenance text, page-index and coordinate conventions, renderer/OMR tool/version/settings, and a list of files. Exclude private marks by default. If shared notes are flattened onto a print view, also include the sidecar and identify the flattened file as a derivative. A clean-reader reopen is a future validation, not an established property of this proposed bundle.
9. **Keep service choices open.** A self-hosted or commercial synchronization service could later support shared review, but no provider, account, authentication, protocol, or license is selected. A file-based suggestion import is a lower-dependency pilot. Compare those paths with volunteers before enabling remote sync; account creation and purchases are out of scope here.

## Observable validation proposal — not executed

Use a small varied set only after its permission for the intended test and distribution is documented: a born-digital PDF, a clean scan, a skewed phone photo, a supplied editable notation file, and two differently paginated parts or editions. Do not use issue #971’s cited score unless its permission is independently confirmed.

- **Identity and provenance:** confirm uploaded bytes and digest; identical bytes do not collapse different metadata or rights records; every preview and interpretation points to a specific source version, tool/settings, and output digest.
- **Failure and recovery:** interrupt an import and force an unsupported/corrupt file; confirm original bytes remain readable and retry does not overwrite reviewed material. Exercise export failure and recovery. A missing derivative is visible.
- **Review state:** verify each source/output pair starts unreviewed; review with known errors remains distinguishable from clean review; reject and stale states are visible; replace/reprocess and confirm the earlier review is not applied to the new output.
- **Optional OMR:** only after license review, use a pinned build with authorized clean, skewed, and multi-page inputs and explicit ground truth. Try the historical #953 path and a separate #971 reproducer without presuming either outcome. Record errors and failures, not an aggregate “accurate” label. A successful pipeline exit is not musical correctness.
- **Visual anchors:** mark page corners and staff regions; reopen at different zoom/pan values; check stored geometry against the same preview raster after orientation/crop normalization. Exercise displayed page labels versus zero-based indices, stroke widths, and changed preview digest. Confirm the app leaves a stale mark on its old source until a person reattaches it.
- **Cross-part relation:** include nonnumeric labels, implicit and non-controlling measures, and different pagination. Confirm matching labels never create an automatic relationship and that a shared note can point to multiple separately confirmed part anchors.
- **Offline and access:** download a named publication; go offline; read it and add private marks and a pending suggestion; publish a newer base elsewhere; reconnect and confirm stale review, visible last-sync state, idempotent retry, and no silent overwrite. Verify private marks do not leak through packet, sync, or shared view, and unauthorized view/export is refused.
- **Deletion and handoff:** test role removal, source/rights withdrawal, personal and shared-note deletion, offline replica, backup expiry, and documented residuals. Export the bundle and reopen it without an account; verify manifest identities/digests, permission text, review state, coordinate/index conventions, authorized contents, and absence of private marks.

Let volunteers set acceptable import, recovery, storage, and retention thresholds using their authorized materials. No threshold or validation result is asserted by this proposal.

## Critique disposition, optional leads, and open questions

- **MusicXML semantics — checked and corrected.** Keep id distinct from number and display text; preserve the file and part boundary. The exact consequences are stated above from the MusicXML 4.0 primary reference [R12].
- **Coordinate claims — checked and narrowed.** W3C xywh describes a spatial rectangle for an original media resource; it does not define the proposed preview transform, page index, half-open convention, or stale-anchor behavior. Those are explicit proposed product conventions [R14, R15].
- **Audiveris incident claims — corroborated but not reproduced.** Pinned implementation matches the #971 trace; the issue report does not establish incidence or prevalence. #953/#954 fixes are correctly bounded to two reported failure paths and 5.11.0; the release note is not a broad accuracy guarantee [R03–R11].
- **Licensing — unresolved.** AGPL-3.0 is recorded from the pinned license. A qualified review is needed before redistribution, linking, embedding, or bundling; no compatibility conclusion is made [R02].
- **Unpinned technology leads — left unselected.** PDF.js, Yjs, and Automerge were raised in the predecessor material, but this revision makes no release/API or integration claim about them. The renderer, native-platform fit, annotation round-trip, sync engine, and transport still need separately pinned code review and pilot evidence.
- **Cross-edition passage equivalence — unresolved.** There is no inspected source that establishes a general solution. Require a human relation or keep anchors unlinked.
- **Access, retention, deletion, packet schema — decisions still needed.** The operational boundaries and tests are proposed, but exact roles, durations, bundle schema, recipient authorization, sync service, and offline-replica expiry need volunteer decisions before use.
- **Optional improvements:** later consider structured event-level anchors, OCR/search, automated suggestions for reattachment, a reader-friendly print view, or CRDT synchronization. None belongs in the minimum scope until the manual workflow shows a need and its evidence, privacy, licensing, and recovery implications have been evaluated.
