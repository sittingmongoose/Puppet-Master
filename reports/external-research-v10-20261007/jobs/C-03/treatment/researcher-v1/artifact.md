## Recommendation

Build the first useful workflow around **immutable source files, librarian-maintained identities and cue links, human-authored annotations, and explicit portable release/return bundles**. Preserve each authorized PDF or page image as the reading authority. Store descriptive metadata and reviewed notes beside it. Let members download and use a rehearsal packet without connectivity, keep their personal marks in their own file/profile, and send a separate contribution bundle back when they choose. The librarian reviews contributions, resolves same-note conflicts visibly, and publishes a new numbered release. A packet should still open as ordinary PDFs and readable JSON/text if the app is unavailable.

Do not require optical music recognition (OMR) for intake, navigation, or annotation in the minimum workflow. Allow a future, optional OMR pass for eligible printed Common Western Music Notation (CWMN), with the untouched source retained and every output marked **unreviewed** until a named person reviews it. Never let a recognition or notation-export result silently replace the page the musician is meant to read. Use a human-maintained ensemble cue as the cross-part meeting point: one cue can link to distinct page/measure locators for violin, cello, and other parts, even when their page turns differ.

This scope addresses the brief's central problem with a smaller failure surface than either “transcribe every score” or “sync every edit live.” Explicit bundles are slower than continuous synchronization, but they fit a small volunteer group, poor connectivity, ordinary file handoff, and the need to prevent personal marks from becoming shared by accident. Revisit hosted sync only if handoff friction is demonstrated to be a larger problem than server and conflict-management work.

## Open discovery and option comparison

Discovery began from the user problem—authorized scans/photos/documents, reliable edition and part identity, useful passage notes across different pagination, offline rehearsal, and later review—not from a defect list in B-P1–B-P7. The first public-primary sweep considered image-to-notation approaches, notation interchange, page and passage annotation models, offline collaboration, archival metadata, and portable packaging. The chosen sources include W3C MusicXML and Web Annotation specifications, implementation candidates' own repositories/releases, PouchDB/Yjs docs, Tropy documentation as an archival-photo analogy, and IETF BagIt. The frozen plan was compared only after that landscape review.

### 1. Preserve-and-annotate pages versus transcribe notation

| Mechanism | Useful when | Costs and failure modes | Proposed disposition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Original PDF/image plus metadata, page anchors, and manual cue map | Volunteers need to identify, read, mark, and distribute the authorized part quickly | Page regions alone do not identify a musical moment in another part; cue mapping takes librarian time | **Minimum path.** Keep the page asset authoritative; use explicit per-part cue links. |
| OMR to structured notation, such as MusicXML | A reviewed symbolic representation would provide navigation or cross-part structure that is worth correcting | Recognition may be wrong or incomplete, conversion may omit information, and correction/reflow can change the printed appearance or pagination | **Optional later capability.** Gate on score type and human review; keep source and OMR run separately. |
| Manual notation entry/encoding | Only a few stable rehearsal landmarks are needed | Re-keying notes for an entire score is costly and becomes a second score that can disagree with the supplied part | Use only for short cue metadata if that proves helpful; not a transcription project. |

MusicXML 4.0 has `score-partwise` and `score-timewise` forms with different nesting of `<part>` and `<measure>`. Its required `<part-list>` has score-part IDs and names; the tutorial says those IDs need to be unique within the document and can be generated by an application. That makes a MusicXML part ID useful for links inside that MusicXML document, not a stable ensemble-wide part identity across independently exported files. Keep a separate local `part_id` and record the document's MusicXML part ID only as a source-specific mapping. MusicXML is an interchange representation, not proof that the output will retain the supplied engraving or be visually equivalent to a scan. [[S01](https://www.w3.org/2021/06/musicxml40/tutorial/structure-of-musicxml-files/)] [[S02](https://www.w3.org/2021/06/musicxml40/musicxml-reference/elements/score-partwise/)] [[S03](https://www.w3.org/2021/06/musicxml40/musicxml-reference/elements/score-timewise/)]

### 2. OMR candidates and applicability

Audiveris 5.11.0 is the more plausible first candidate to evaluate for **printed CWMN** because its release describes an integrated engine and correction editor, multiplatform installers, and a MusicXML 4.0 exporter for a subset of its internal OMR data. Its own 5.11.0 handbook says handwritten scores are unsupported, only common symbols are supported, and recognition is not perfect; it specifically positions the editor for verification and manual correction. These are useful fit boundaries, not measured accuracy for this ensemble. The project is AGPL-3.0 at the captured release. Any plan to bundle, modify, or distribute it needs a deliberate license/distribution review; the proposal does not assume it is a drop-in library. Oemer provides a substantially different, model-led alternative. At pinned repository commit `dbe2a933d630d0f74805d717960eb259473f5978`, its Python entry point accepts an image, runs model inference using ONNX Runtime by default (TensorFlow is an explicit alternative), builds MusicXML, and writes an analyzed-symbol image. ### 3. Passage anchors and coordinates

There are two different things to identify:

1. **A mark on a particular copy/page:** store the target asset-version ID and 1-based displayed page index, plus a region in a canonical page coordinate system. For raster pages, define normalized top-left coordinates `(x, y, width, height)` in `[0,1]` after applying the recorded image orientation. For PDFs, store an explicitly defined normalized crop-box/page viewport system and the page's rotation/crop-box metadata. Do not store screen pixels or a rectangle from a zoomed canvas as if it were stable. PDF.js illustrates why: it renders at a chosen viewport scale/rotation and transforms the usual PDF bottom-left origin to a top-left canvas origin. Any chosen renderer must convert between these systems, and the app must test that conversion; PDF.js is cited here as a concrete coordinate example, not selected as a dependency. [[S05](https://mozilla.github.io/pdf.js/examples/)]
2. **A musical place shared across parts:** create a local stable `cue_id` for a rehearsal point (e.g., “rehearsal mark B, pickup into the section”). The librarian attaches one locator per relevant part: source asset version, page, displayed measure label if present, and a short human-readable landmark. The director's shared note targets the cue; each musician sees that cue resolved to their own part locator. Different pagination and unnumbered/renumbered measures remain explicit mappings. A measure label entered by a person is descriptive evidence, not a key or a guaranteed same-place link across editions.

The W3C Web Annotation model is a useful analogy for a target resource plus a selector (including SVG region and text selectors). It also explicitly warns that position-based text selectors are brittle when their resource changes. A page mark should therefore be bound to an immutable asset revision. If a replacement is uploaded, do not silently move the mark: retain the old target and show it as “on previous part version / re-anchor needed.” A user may create a new re-anchored mark with lineage to the old mark and an explicit reviewer. That preserves both the annotation and the uncertainty. [[S04](https://www.w3.org/TR/annotation-model/)]

### 4. Collaboration and release mechanisms

| Option | Offline and collaboration semantics | Trade-offs for this ensemble |
|---|---|---|
| **Explicit release/return bundles (recommended initially)** | Librarian publishes a numbered package; member copies it locally, marks privately, and optionally exports a separate contribution file. The librarian previews/imports contributions and publishes the next release. | Portable, no permanent account/server, deliberate publication, works with USB/shared folders. Requires a human merge/publish step and requires keeping personal changes outside the common packet. |
| **PouchDB plus self-hosted CouchDB replication** | PouchDB 9.0.0 is the captured package version. Its docs describe bidirectional `db.sync(remoteDB)` and `live`/`retry` handling for offline/online transitions. Eventual conflicts are retained as revision branches, but a deterministic arbitrary revision wins by default; app code must surface `_conflicts` or choose a resolution. | More convenient cross-device sync if the group accepts hosting/admin, user identity and authorization work, server backups, and a visible conflict workflow. Replication tracks changes, not a database's deletion: destroying a replica and starting sync again can re-create transferred data. Not selected for a volunteer-run first stage. The docs are the assessed API evidence; PouchDB implementation code and release-specific regression history were not inspected because it is not a dependency recommendation. [[S06](https://pouchdb.apache.org/guides/conflicts.html)] [[S07](https://pouchdb.apache.org/guides/replication.html)] [[S31](https://registry.npmjs.org/pouchdb/latest)] |
| **Yjs shared text/CRDT, plus a provider and persistence layer** | The docs present shared types attached to a Yjs document and synced through providers. The NPM package snapshot is 13.6.33. | Potentially useful for simultaneous editing of shared text. A CRDT addresses convergence of replicated changes; it does not itself choose the app's access model, bind a note to the right edition/cue, or make a deletion reach already-exported files. More moving parts than needed for an occasional return bundle. Versioned code/history and transport/persistence choice remain unreviewed; defer. [[S08](https://docs.yjs.dev/getting-started/working-with-shared-types)] [[S32](https://registry.npmjs.org/yjs/latest)] |

Use append-only, separately identified contribution records even in the bundle model. Each record carries `annotation_id`, actor alias, scope (`personal`, `submitted`, or `published_shared`), target cue/page, source release ID, source asset-version ID, created/modified time, and optional `supersedes_annotation_id`. When two bundles contain changed versions of the same annotation, preserve both and ask the librarian to accept one, combine them into a new annotation, or leave both; never resolve by timestamp alone. A stale contribution still imports as a proposal on its original edition/release, not as a silently current mark.

Tropy is a useful adjacent-system analogy rather than a score competitor: it organizes photographs into items, lets users attach structured metadata/notes and selection-level information, captures rights-related source context, and can export photos plus metadata/notes as a ZIP. This supports the shape of “assets with contextual metadata and notes travel together,” but does not establish that Tropy's data model, UI, or exports suit music parts. RFC 8493 BagIt is a concrete optional package standard: BagIt 1.0 uses a `data/` payload and checksum manifests, with bag validation rules. Adopt a documented ZIP containing a BagIt 1.0 directory only if maintainers want standard tools to verify transfer; otherwise keep the same explicit manifest and checksums without claiming BagIt conformance. [[S09](https://docs.tropy.org/before-you-begin/metadata)] [[S10](https://docs.tropy.org/other-features/export-with-archive-plugin)] [[S11](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8493.html)]

## Proposed data and workflow changes

### Identity and provenance

Keep the plan's Work → Edition → Part hierarchy, but add immutable file and interpretation records:

- `work_id`: local identity for the abstract work/grouping; titles and composer/arranger are descriptive, editable claims.
- `edition_id`: the particular publication/arrangement/revision used, with transcriber/editor/publisher/edition statement/part-source notes where known. Never merge two files only because the title looks alike.
- `part_id`: local part identity within an edition, with instrument/voice, transposition or part label, and librarian-confirmed role.
- `asset_version_id`: one admitted original PDF/image/notation file. Preserve exact original bytes and SHA-256, byte length, MIME/format, page count/order and any orientation/crop metadata. Hashes establish byte identity, not edition identity or permission.
- `derived_id`: preview/rendition, OMR output, or edited notation. Record input asset hash, producing tool/version/config/model, time, and status. Never overwrite its input.
- `cue_id`: stable rehearsal-list identity with descriptive label and a set of explicit per-part locators.

For every intake, record who submitted it, date received, source/context they supplied, the ensemble's permitted use/distribution status and any limits/expiry/attribution notes. Keep unknown rights as unknown and block distribution until a named librarian confirms authorization. The presence of a file does not establish permission. The brief supplies no score collection, and this work used no score examples or score corpus.

### Annotation lifecycle and scope

Personal annotations default to local/private and are not included in a published packet or return bundle without a specific user action. Shared director notes are separate records, tied to a cue and optionally an individual part locator; the release says which shared-note revision is published. Include author alias and date, but avoid putting an email or account identifier into the portable manifest. Publishing is a separate, explicit action with a preview of exactly which notes/assets will travel.

A page replacement creates a new asset version and an explicit `supersedes`/replacement relation. Old personal/shared marks remain viewable on the previous copy. Present the old region with its old asset hash and offer manual “re-anchor to new version,” recording who did it and when. Never automatically assert equivalence between measure numbers, regions, editions, OMR output, or re-rendered pages. A new release can provide a corrected cue map without changing old releases.

### Access, deletion, and failure recovery

For v1 use three operational roles: librarian (metadata, part mapping, rights, shared-note publication, bundle review), director (rehearsal cues and shared-note draft/publication if delegated), and member (read published packet, create private marks, send opted-in suggestions). A local/offline package cannot enforce access after copying. The UI can prevent accidental publication and label intended audience, but this is not server-grade access control. If the ensemble needs enforceable revocation, per-user accounts, or confidentiality after sharing, that is a product decision to add an authenticated service and threat model, not something a manifest can provide.

Define an understandable retention table before importing the first real score: active current parts, superseded part versions, local private marks, submitted contribution bundles, published release packets, temporary import/render caches, and backups. Let users delete their own local personal marks and let the librarian remove an asset after withdrawal/expiry; purge derived output and caches from active storage with the original. Show when trash/backup expiry occurs. Keep a deletion record without retaining withdrawn score bytes or note text if a minimal audit is still needed. Since downloaded ZIP/PDF copies cannot be remotely recalled, record release ID and intended recipients, send a replacement/withdrawal notice, and be clear that the tool cannot erase other people's copies. Avoid replication in v1 so that there is no hidden offline replica resurrecting deleted data.

Import should be staged and recoverable: validate file type and rights fields; hash/copy to a temporary location; compute preview/page metadata; then atomically admit it as a new asset version. If parsing/rendering fails, preserve the original and report a specific failed stage; never claim a partially processed score is ready. During packet creation, write to a temporary output, verify files against the manifest, then rename to the published release. On corrupt/missing files, show the exact asset and checksum mismatch and offer re-export from the librarian's original. Bundle import is a preview/merge operation: unknown release, wrong hash, duplicate contribution, stale edition, missing page, and malformed records must be reported before anything becomes shared.

### Portable packet and handoff

A released packet should include ordinary PDF parts in a predictable directory, a human-readable `README.txt`, and versioned UTF-8 JSON for the manifest and cue map. The manifest should name package schema version, group-local release ID/sequence, created-by alias/date, included work/edition/part IDs, source asset-version ID/hash for every file, path, byte length, media type, rights/use note, ordered rehearsal cues, and shared annotation IDs/revisions included. Keep private notes out by default. Include an optional `manifest-sha256.txt` and document how to verify it with standard tools. Prefer relative paths and avoid absolute host paths, account secrets, library tokens, or path traversal. Make member contribution bundles separate and make their exact source release explicit. A packet needs to be understandable without fetching a remote catalog or commercial account.

## Observable validation proposal (not executed)

1. **Intake and provenance:** use only a small, explicitly permitted test set selected by the group. Import one scan/photo, one multipage PDF, and one user-supplied notation file. Confirm exact source bytes and SHA-256 survive intake/export, page order/rotation is visible, submitter and rights status are recorded, and an unknown-rights file cannot be published. Confirm similar names do not merge editions; identical bytes alone do not merge works.
2. **Optional digitization gate:** if OMR is evaluated, record OS/runtime, exact release/commit and asset hashes, model/checkpoint hashes, parameters, and per-page outcomes. Include a clean printed score and a skewed phone photo if permission allows. Verify output stays `unreviewed`, compare a human-reviewed result to the supplied page, count corrections/time, inspect a failed page and confirm the original remains usable. Test exported MusicXML in the expected receiving notation tool and reopen the original packet; do not score by note-count or presence of an output file alone. No handwriting accuracy claim is in scope.
3. **Annotation coordinate check:** on a page with known crop and rotation, draw a region at several zoom levels and reopen at different viewport sizes. Confirm the region overlays the same printed passage; capture expected `[0,1]` top-left normalized page coordinates and test PDF bottom-left-to-view coordinates. A crop/rotation change must be represented or flagged, not silently normalized away.
4. **Cross-part cue check:** map one shared cue to two parts with deliberately different page counts/page turns and different visible measure labels. Confirm one shared director note is intelligible and each member is directed to their own page; no algorithm claims the labels are equivalent.
5. **Replacement/re-anchor check:** replace or reorder a page. Confirm old marks still open on the old asset, are labeled old/needs review on the new edition, and no mark disappears or silently changes target. A manually re-anchored copy must retain a link to the old annotation and identify reviewer/time.
6. **Offline/collaboration check:** distribute a published packet, turn off network, create personal marks, close/reopen, then export a contribution bundle. Confirm personal marks are excluded unless opted in. Import the opted-in bundle twice, then import a stale release and two divergent edits to one note. Show duplicate/stale/conflict results and require a librarian choice; confirm the published notes change only in a new release.
7. **Handoff and failure recovery:** unzip on an ordinary desktop, open every PDF, read README/JSON, verify checksums, and detect a changed/missing file. Interrupt a staged import/export and confirm no partial release is published. Test a malformed path/unknown schema and ensure import previews the error without overwriting a library file.
8. **Access/deletion/retention:** test that personal notes never appear in another member's release by default, that publication preview shows every included note, and that a local deletion removes the selected mark/asset and associated active renditions/caches per stated policy. Separately document that already-exported copies are not remotely revocable and check notice/recipient bookkeeping and backup expiry behavior.

**Actually executed:** open-discovery public-source search; direct capture of the sources listed in `source-map.json`; read-only inspection of pinned Audiveris and Oemer source files, release/PR pages, specifications, and candidate docs; SHA-256 and source-map/output-file integrity verification described in `preservation_check.md`. No software was installed or run, no score samples were processed, no product was built, and none of the proposed product validation checks above was executed.
