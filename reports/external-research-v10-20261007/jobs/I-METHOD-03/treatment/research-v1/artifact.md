# I-METHOD-03 — proposed revised MVP: Transit transfer review kiosk

**Research-stage status:** O1–O4 and O6 are addressed below. O5 is explicitly pending the coordinator’s fresh same-family critic; no criticism was supplied to or invented by this researcher. This is a proposed plan, not an implementation or build report. Source IDs and immutable captures are in `source-map.json` and `sources/`.

## Recommendation

Build a small, read-only transfer information service for the 20 selected stops and one configured transfer view across two agencies. Use each agency’s GTFS Schedule snapshot as the schedule baseline and use matched, sufficiently fresh GTFS Realtime **TripUpdates** as estimates when available. Compute only the selected connection’s time arithmetic. Do not deploy a general journey planner or infer arrival predictions from VehiclePositions in this MVP. Keep schedule-only information available when a real-time feed is absent, invalid, stale, mismatched, or unsupported; label that state as schedule-only or unavailable, never as “on time.” No display guarantees a vehicle wait, passenger boarding, transfer success, service accessibility, or dispatch response.

The available brief names no city, agencies, feeds, actual stop pairs, network constraints, or operating timezone. This research therefore establishes a bounded design and test plan, not feed availability, local walking times, real-world data quality, forecast accuracy, or kiosk performance.

## Revised sandbox plan

### P1 — source intake and version binding

An authorized curator selects one public GTFS Schedule feed per agency and, if published, its GTFS Realtime TripUpdates and Service Alerts endpoints. Store the agency/feed identity separately from each publisher’s entity IDs; bind trip identity at least by `(feed_id, trip_id, service_date)` and include `start_time` where frequency-based service requires it. Do not join agencies by coincidentally equal stop or trip IDs. For this fixed cross-agency view, maintain an explicit, curator-reviewed stop-pair mapping with its own version and stated walking-time/buffer source; a geographic proximity match alone is insufficient.

Validate and parse a candidate Schedule archive before making it active. Promote the complete candidate atomically, retain the previous valid snapshot for rollback, and record source URL, fetch time, feed publisher/version metadata where present, archive digest, parser/validator version, and import result. Keep Schedule and Realtime versions distinct. If Realtime `feed_version` is present, compare it with the referenced Schedule version; show a mismatch and suppress affected overlays until the mapping is resolved. Keep an absent version declaration visible as unknown.

Use only GTFS Realtime `FULL_DATASET` semantics in the first release. The pinned proto defaults an absent `FeedHeader.incrementality` to `FULL_DATASET`; accept that default as a complete snapshot. The proto says explicit `DIFFERENTIAL` behavior is unsupported and unspecified; do not merge a differential response into a prior snapshot. Use HTTPS, conditional requests when supported, bounded polling consistent with each publisher’s terms/limits, backoff after errors, and separate fetch time from publisher timestamps. The best-practice 30-second feed refresh and 90-second TripUpdate age are producer recommendations, not evidence that any selected feed meets them. Use 90 seconds as a proposed initial TripUpdate freshness cutoff, subject to checking actual feeds and publisher limits; show the measured age. For Service Alerts, use the published 10-minute freshness guidance as an initial cutoff if alerts are displayed. A missing required `FeedHeader.timestamp`, invalid payload, decreasing timestamp, or future timestamp beyond the accepted clock-skew tolerance invalidates the real-time freshness claim and returns the view to schedule-only.

### P2 — rider view

Show upcoming departures for the 20 selected stops and the one configured transfer. Label each time as **scheduled** or **real-time estimate**, and show the agency/feed and “updated at / age” for each source. A GTFS Realtime trip with no TripUpdate has no prediction information; absence does not mean it is operating on schedule. A missing or stale observation remains unknown. A valid cancellation or a fresh alert affecting the selected trip/stop must be visible; malformed, unmatched, or unsupported updates must not silently alter the schedule.

On a tablet, retain the last valid cached Schedule and rendered view during a connection failure, but label it **cached** with its last successful fetch time and mark Realtime stale/unavailable. Never present cached predictions as current. Use text and icon labels as well as color so schedule, estimate, stale, and unavailable states remain distinguishable. The kiosk interface itself should be keyboard and screen-reader operable; this is separate from, and does not imply, vehicle or stop accessibility data.

### P3 — transfer interpretation

For the configured arriving and departing trips, display the source event times and the arithmetic time available between them. Compare that time with the reviewed walking allowance plus any explicit buffer for the mapped stop pair. If a fresh, matched TripUpdate supplies a future arrival/departure prediction, use it and label it as a publisher estimate; otherwise calculate from Schedule and label it schedule-only. Keep a short explanation with the result (arrival source/time, departure source/time, walk/buffer value and mapping version).

Honor published Schedule transfer rules: `transfer_type=3` forbids the connection; `transfer_type=2` uses its required `min_transfer_time`; `transfer_type=1` may be shown as an agency-declared timed transfer that is expected to wait, but is not a guarantee without dispatch authority. Do not infer an in-seat transfer (`4`/`5`) across agencies or from a shared stop. If a cross-feed connection has no reviewed mapping, show “connection not configured” rather than estimating one. No probability, “safe transfer,” or “likely to make it” label is justified without local outcome data and a validated model. Treat `timepoint=0` Schedule times as approximate when supplied; do not turn the specification’s lack of an explicit `timepoint` into a new confidence score.

Use agency-local service dates/timezones and GTFS service-day times, including times beyond `24:00:00`; do not reduce every service day to a UTC calendar date or assume every day is 24 hours. A repeated stop ID in a loop needs `stop_sequence` to resolve the event. Unknown service date, identifier mismatch, unsupported schedule relationship, or ambiguous match means no Realtime overlay for that trip.

### P4 — volunteer review and history

Provide a separate, read-only volunteer evidence view. For each displayed result, show the exact Schedule and Realtime feed IDs/versions, fetch time, publisher timestamp and its field level (feed or trip), the relevant trip/date/stop sequence, alert if used, stop-pair mapping and walking rule versions, freshness decision, and the arithmetic that produced the rider-facing status. Preserve the source records needed to reproduce that result, not only a screenshot; store the evaluation time and rule/version identity so a later snapshot does not rewrite historical meaning.

Retain seven days as proposed by the frozen plan. Public history should expose a compact result/source-age summary; keep detailed source records in the volunteer view and delete them after seven days. Do not collect rider identity, accounts, precise kiosk interaction histories, or analytics identifiers for this read-only MVP. Retention of vehicle identifiers in source records and the public-versus-volunteer detail split remain policy choices to confirm against the actual feeds and local privacy expectations.

### P5 — components, refresh, and offline behavior

Use a bounded GTFS reader, a small deterministic transfer calculator, and a web view. Keep network polling and archive parsing off the rider request path. The fixed scope does not justify adopting a full routing engine by default. OpenTripPlanner is a credible alternative if the product grows to many origins/destinations, routes, modes, and feed-specific routing semantics; its v2.10.0 docs and public implementation are useful comparison points, not evidence of a performance fit for this kiosk. The OTP VehiclePosition matcher inspected here handles vehicle-to-trip/pattern association and service-date inference; it does not make VehiclePositions equivalent to arrival predictions.

MobilityData’s GTFS Validator is a useful candidate Schedule-feed preflight. The latest-release endpoint observed during this research named v8.0.1 (published 2026-05-12). Its later issue #2174 requests a missing-required-field rule after the Schedule spec made `min_transfer_time` required for `transfer_type=2`. This is a signal not to assume that one validator release enforces the current specification; the application must test this rule itself. The issue was not reproduced here and no validator code was audited, so the proposed plan does not claim a confirmed defect in v8.0.1.

### P6 — proposed acceptance evidence

Before implementation, identify and freeze the actual two Schedule/Realtime feed pairs, stop mapping, agency timezone, device/browser, and publisher limits. Proposed tests (not executed in this research stage):

1. **Archive replacement and rollback:** valid new snapshot activates atomically; malformed, incomplete, wrong-version, or wrongly identified candidate leaves the last valid Schedule active and exposes an operator-readable error.
2. **Identity and matching:** same `trip_id` in two feeds never cross-matches; wrong feed version, unknown trip, repeated stop ID, missing/invalid service date, unsupported relationship, or ambiguous frequency trip suppresses Realtime rather than fabricating a match. Include frequency `start_time` behavior where the actual feeds use it.
3. **Realtime freshness and absence:** fresh future StopTimeUpdate time/delay changes only its matched trip; missing update, missing required header timestamp, stale TripUpdate over the proposed 90-second cutoff, stale alert over the proposed cutoff, malformed protobuf, full-feed entity removal, and `DIFFERENTIAL` each produce the documented stale/unavailable/schedule-only behavior. Verify zero uncertainty is not confused with omitted/unknown uncertainty.
4. **Transfer rules:** exercise types 1, 2, and 3; enforce type 2 `min_transfer_time`, test cross-feed mapping changes, missing mapping, too-short walking allowance, and explicit “expected to wait” copy without a promise. No cross-agency in-seat inference.
5. **Service dates and clocks:** calendar exceptions; `24:xx` and later times; separate agency timezones; DST spring-forward and fall-back; UTC/local clock skew; trip updates crossing midnight. Compare results against hand-calculated fixtures.
6. **Review and retention:** a saved result can be reconstructed from captured inputs and versions; comparison shows what changed; cached view is marked stale after disconnection; seven-day records expire; no rider identifiers appear in stored evidence or logs.
7. **Performance:** measure first render and warm refresh with both agencies’ actual feed snapshots, exactly 20 selected stops, and the one transfer on the named reference tablet/browser. Record p50/p95/p99, payload size, and network state. Proposed product target: warm view update p95 ≤ 1 second after data is already ingested; choose a separate cold-load target only after device/network constraints are known. Neither target is validated by this research.
