# C-04/control/critic-v1 — independent assessment and integrated proposal

## Assessment

**Disposition: accept the researcher’s direction with scope and evidence amendments.** The facsimile-first, versioned library and offline published-packet workflow fit this hypothetical small ensemble and preserve the brief’s constraints. The proposal correctly keeps OMR optional, leaves the source visible, requires scoped human checking, treats page/measure labels as context rather than identity, and avoids automatic cross-part alignment. I found no primary-source basis for claiming that recognition, correspondence, annotation migration, or offline conflict resolution has already been validated.

The researcher’s plan is stronger as a future validation design than as an MVP schema. Keep the safety properties and workflow decisions, but stage the more elaborate event log, semantic event locators, interpretation registry, and backup/tombstone machinery behind a small authorized pilot. The group first needs stable local identities, a source-version anchor, private/shared separation, a published snapshot, explicit returned-note review, and a readable packet.

## Discovery and options

I began with the user’s workflow: authorized scans/photos and digital score files; work, edition, and part identity; checked passage navigation when feasible; private player marks and director notes; different part pagination; poor connectivity; and a portable handoff. Open public-primary discovery then compared mechanisms before reviewing plan defects.

| Mechanism | Benefit for this group | Cost and boundary | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Facsimile first with manual passage labels and page regions** | Makes a packet useful for PDFs, photos, scans, and documents without transcription; preserves what a musician actually sees | A region belongs to one exact asset version and page; crop, replacement, or a new scan can invalidate it; it cannot align parts by itself | MVP foundation. Keep notes readable if a region is stale or absent. |
| **Structured notation plus reviewed links** | Optional measure/event navigation and possible passage correspondence across different pagination | Import, OMR, conversion, and layout can be wrong or lossy; part and measure labels are document-local; identity and correctness still need a person | Later, opt-in aid. Keep the source page beside it and label review scope. |
| **Facsimile-linked encoding such as MEI zones** | Can encode curated links from musical elements to regions in source images | Adds encoding, surface-to-image coordinate transforms, and specialist maintenance | Optional research lead only. The researcher’s detailed MEI claims were not independently recaptured for this critic arm; do not select MEI without a fresh pinned review. |
| **Published packet and returned notes** | Works without a live service; gives the group a clear revision each rehearsal | Updates are asynchronous; librarian reviews competing returns; downloaded copies cannot be recalled | Start here, as the researcher recommends. |
| **Offline local databases with eventual replication (PouchDB/CouchDB is one example)** | Can sync local edits after reconnection and retry after network loss | Adds server/database operation, authentication/access design, conflict handling, and deletion policy; deterministic conflict winners do not themselves resolve rehearsal-note meaning | Keep as a later alternative if packet exchange proves burdensome. W3C Web Annotation is a useful design analogy for separating a note body from its target, selector, and representation state. It explicitly warns that position selectors are brittle when content changes. It is not a score format or a protocol requirement here ([CRIT-WA-01](sources/w3c-web-annotation-model.html), §§3–4).

## Claims checked and corrections

- **Audiveris applicability.** At the pinned 5.11.0 commit `9e1e55cd2746037d059345881c53e6a6754bffbd`, the project handbook limits intended notation to Common Western Music Notation, says handwritten scores are unsupported and only common symbols are supported, and says output needs verification/correction. It describes an interactive editor and AGPLv3 licensing. This supports optional OMR only for suitable printed material; it does not establish phone-photo robustness, score-wide accuracy, or a particular volunteer’s ability to repair results ([CRIT-AUD-01](sources/audiveris-handbook-5.11.0.md), lines 43–69). Treat phone photos as facsimiles first; any use of Audiveris on them needs a separate suitability check.
- **OMR output and identity.** Audiveris’s pinned exporter assigns a MusicXML part ID from its logical-part PID and emits measure numbers from score IDs; repeated measures may receive suffixed labels. These are useful within an export, not stable library-wide or cross-edition identifiers ([CRIT-AUD-02](sources/audiveris-PartwiseBuilder-5.11.0.java), lines 550–552, 628–632, 1981–1986, 2217–2223). The pinned export guide says `.omr` saves reloadable OMR work and distinguishes it from lossy `.mxl`/MusicXML export. Keep `.omr` only when useful and authorized; neither it nor MusicXML replaces the original ([CRIT-AUD-05](sources/audiveris-export-5.11.0.md), lines 9–59).
- **Issue/fix history.** Issue #920 reports an F-clef pitch discrepancy against 5.10.1 compared with 5.9; the 5.10.2 release notes identify a fix for that specific pitch-rounding issue, and the pinned comparison patch changes clef code. The patch has 38 distinct changed paths and none has “test” in its path. That only establishes the scope of this patch; it does not prove no regression test exists elsewhere or that other recognition errors were fixed ([CRIT-AUD-06](sources/audiveris-issue-920.html), [CRIT-AUD-07](sources/audiveris-release-5.10.2.html), [CRIT-AUD-08](sources/audiveris-5.10.1-to-5.10.2.patch)).
- **A current-build failure report.** Issue #971 reports a multi-sheet save/reload/export failure on exact build `9e1e55cd2746037d059345881c53e6a6754bffbd` (Audiveris 5.11.0), including a reproducer and stated environment. It is an issue report, not a reproduced result; this critic did not download its score, run it, or identify a subsequent fix. It supports adding a multi-page recovery/export check before relying on batch OMR, not concluding that all multi-page jobs fail ([CRIT-AUD-03](sources/audiveris-issue-971.html), [CRIT-AUD-04](sources/audiveris-release-5.11.0.html)).
- **MusicXML cross-part numbers.** MusicXML 4.0 says that in partwise files, measures in different parts that share a left barline *should* have the same `number`, and that numbers group measures when converting to timewise. It also defines `non-controlling` measures whose barlines do not coincide, permits nonnumeric labels, distinguishes printed `text`, and warns that duplicate numbers within a part can complicate conversion. Thus the researcher’s “at shared barlines” qualification is important: numbering can propose correspondence but cannot identify a passage across different uploads, editions, or misrecognized exports. `part@id` points back to that document’s own part list. `divisions` expresses duration units per quarter note and can inform exact within-file timing; an event locator still requires parsing and validation ([CRIT-MXL-01](sources/musicxml-4.0-musicxml.xsd), lines 2383–2410 and 2820–2822).
- **PDF coordinates.** PDF.js 6.3.289’s pinned `PageViewport` uses `viewBox`, `userUnit`, scale, rotation, and offsets to transform between PDF and viewport coordinates; `convertToPdfPoint` applies the inverse transform ([CRIT-PDF-01](sources/pdfjs-6.3.289-page_viewport.js), lines 54–110, 185–213; release boundary [CRIT-PDF-02](sources/pdfjs-release-6.3.289.html)). Store durable regions in the PDF page’s source coordinate frame and bind them to the exact asset hash and page. Use the API to convert current pointer positions. Do not make CSS pixels, zoom, window offset, or display rotation part of the durable geometry; preserve page-box/user-unit facts needed to interpret the frame. This is a documented API boundary, not a test result for a proposed app.
- **Rights, hashes, and deletion.** A SHA-256 can identify bytes and detect later change; it cannot establish who created a score, permission to use it, or legal ownership. Keep the librarian’s permission statement as an attributed, dated assertion with scope/expiry if supplied, not a rights verdict. Immutability applies while a source is retained: withdrawal/deletion policy must supersede ordinary version retention, remove active copies/derivatives/caches, expire backups on a stated schedule, and explain that offline packets already downloaded may persist.

## Minimum useful workflow

1. **Intake:** accept only material the ensemble has permission to use. Record work/edition/part labels as supplied, submitter and date, a non-authoritative permission assertion, media type, page inventory, and source checksum. Librarian corrects labels and confirms part-to-edition membership. Similar titles remain unresolved until a person decides.
2. **Prepare:** preserve exact source bytes while retained; produce a page preview with source/version link. On failure or cancellation, keep the original usable and show a retryable failure. Optional OMR uses a pinned build on suitable printed CWMN; store the result as a separate derivative and keep its native project only if the group can support it. Do not label anything checked until a person verifies the relevant passage against the source.
3. **Annotate:** record note body, author, visibility, time, publication/base revision, and one or more source-version targets. A manual label can say “second system, pickup before rehearsal letter B”; it is context, not a key. Personal notes stay in the owner’s local area unless deliberately promoted.
4. **Link parts:** a director or librarian can write one readable shared note and attach separate targets for violin, viola, or other parts only after each target is checked. Different pagination is handled by independent targets, not an assumed universal measure number.
5. **Publish and return:** publish an immutable ordered revision. Players download and open it without connectivity, then can submit an explicitly shared note against that revision. The librarian inspects the base revision and any competing changes before publishing a new one. No account or continuous network is required for the first workflow.
6. **Export and recover:** packet content opens without the application or renderer; include a manifest, labels, checksums, shared notes, and a short README. Restore a backup to a separate location and check hashes, identities, visibility and stale targets before replacing an active library copy.
7. **Withdraw/delete:** stop future distribution; remove active source and derivatives/caches under the agreed policy; expire backups and offer personal-note export/deletion. Explain that previously downloaded files can remain on disconnected devices.

## Optional leads, uncertainty and validation proposal

Optional after this workflow is useful: a locally packaged renderer for reviewed MusicXML; MEI for curated notation-to-facsimile work; or local-first replication for a group that cannot manage returned packets. Require a fresh version/implementation/license review before choosing any of them. Audiveris is AGPLv3 at the pinned handbook; no legal compatibility judgment is made here. Verovio/MEI details in the researcher artifact remain useful leads but were not independently re-captured for this critique.

Material gaps remain: no ensemble score or permission evidence exists; no OMR accuracy, phone-photo suitability, renderer fidelity, anchor transform, cross-part mapping, shared-note conflict, access control, deletion, backup expiry, or old-copy removal was tested. MusicXML labels only assist within the constraints above. PouchDB pages describe v9.0.0 documentation behavior, but source code/release pinning was not part of this critique. These should remain explicit questions, not assumed guarantees.

**Proposed pilot observations (none run here):**

- Ingest a permission-documented sample with a scan/PDF, phone photo, digital score, and one editable notation export. Reopen originals and verify hashes/page inventory. Confirm same-title editions are not auto-merged.
- On rotated/cropped PDFs and oriented photos, place known region marks, close/reopen the packet, and verify coordinate conversion through zoom, rotation, resize and crop. Pass only if the mark returns to its original content region; record frame and transformation.
- Replace a page and a whole part. Old marks must remain attached to their old source and visibly stale; no automatic migration may present the wrong passage as confirmed.
- Give players differently paginated parts and ask each to find the same director note. A target is clickable only when someone verified that exact part/source version; otherwise the note remains understandable as text.
- If OMR is opted into, use only approved printed CWMN pages; check representative notes/rests, rhythm, accidentals, clefs, parts, labels, and review scope. Exercise multi-page save, close/reopen, re-export and cancel/failure recovery on the exact selected release. Compare to the source; successful processing alone is not acceptance.
- Rehearse from a disconnected published packet, add private marks and explicitly submit shared notes from the same base. Simultaneous edits must both remain inspectable and require visible resolution. Reopen the returned and next-published packets; verify no private mark leaked.
- Export, checksum, reopen and restore offline. Exercise withdrawal, active-cache cleanup, backup expiry and restore; verify the packet explains that copies already downloaded may persist.
