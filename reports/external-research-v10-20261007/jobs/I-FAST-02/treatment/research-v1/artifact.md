## Decision summary

Keep the two volunteer locations, one public reference station, one-month window, local read-only notebook, provenance, review alternatives, visible gaps, and no navigation/surge-prediction scope. Tighten the plan so a residual exists only after a reviewable decision about time convention, datum/reference-level relationship, units, station epoch, and matching rule. Preserve each raw series and its interpretation separately. Do not infer that the two volunteer sites share the NOAA reference station's water-level behavior.

Use NOAA CO-OPS predictions as the primary reference series and optionally show NOAA observations from that station as a separately labeled context series. Capture the exact API request, returned bytes, retrieval time, and station metadata. Select pandas merge_asof as the consequential alignment candidate and pin the research evidence to pandas 3.0.6 at commit 2905718b126579d2cf0fe5bae05bd75facb67d77. Its documented matching and tolerance controls fit time-key pairing; it does not interpolate water levels. The project's exact pandas release remains conditional on the selected interpreter and implementation review.

## Proposed replacement plan

### P1 — Inputs and provenance

1. Import volunteer CSV readings for exactly two private local location IDs and one approved public CO-OPS station dataset for the selected month. Keep original CSV and downloaded response bytes unchanged; record paths, byte hashes generated by the host/application, retrieval time, source URL, request parameters, and parser version.
2. For CO-OPS requests preserve station ID, product, begin/end dates, datum, units, time zone, interval, output format, API response/errors, and metadata snapshot. Distinguish predictions, observed water_level, verified hourly_height, and modeled ofs_water_level; only predictions are the primary comparison reference. NOAA says water_level may be preliminary or verified, datum is mandatory for water-level products, and interval/retention limits differ by product [S01].
3. Prefer a UTC request/time basis and canonical internal height unit after explicit validation, while retaining source timestamp text, source time zone, source unit, and source height unchanged. Do not infer missing CSV time zones, units, datum, sign conventions, instrument zero, or sensor-method identity.
4. Import a station metadata snapshot (station details, tide-prediction classification/reference/offsets where applicable, datums and superseded datums, sensors/notices, retrieval date). NOAA's metadata service exposes these as separate resources, including station installation/removal details and sensors/outage messages [S02]. Snapshot the metadata used for this run; do not rewrite old runs when NOAA updates it.
5. Represent each volunteer location's measurement setup as an epoch. A move, gauge-zero/reference change, sensor replacement, recording-method change, unit change, or material timing change starts a new epoch unless reviewers document a justified conversion. Retain source-row IDs so each plotted point and comparison traces to input rows.
6. Keep volunteer labels anonymous in the notebook and exports by default. Do not require publication of coordinates or exact location details. If a private local map is needed, it is opt-in and excluded from ordinary exports.

### P2 — Comparability and interpretation

1. Retain the plan rule: store raw and interpreted values separately, let reviewers choose only supported interpretations, and allow comparison to remain unresolved [frozen plan §P2]. Each interpretation records author, time, evidence, conversion formula/parameters, and competing interpretations.
2. Normalize timestamps only when the source convention is known. Preserve raw time text and declared zone/offset; convert unambiguous time to UTC for ordering. Ambiguous/nonexistent local times at daylight-saving transitions, rounded/date-only times, and unknown zones stay unresolved until a reviewer supplies a documented rule. NOAA accepts GMT, fixed local standard time (no DST), and local time adjusted for DST [S01]; these conventions are not interchangeable.
3. Convert units only when the input unit is stated or otherwise evidenced. A datum is a vertical reference, not a unit conversion. NOAA lists STND as a reference unique to each station, MLLW as U.S. coastal chart datum, and says subordinate-station predictions require MLLW [S01]. Equal datum labels do not prove a volunteer local reference is tied to NOAA's datum.
4. Permit an absolute residual only when both series are demonstrably expressed on a common vertical reference, with mapping evidence and validity interval recorded. If a local gauge has only an arbitrary or changed zero, retain separate raw/relative series and mark the absolute comparison unresolved; do not silently zero-center, calibrate, or apply a station offset.
5. Show public reference-station ID, name, type, geographic context, prediction interval, datum, units, time convention, and relevant update/metadata snapshot next to the series. A single public station does not establish local equivalence at both volunteer locations.

### P3 — Analysis and display

1. Show the selected month for locations A and B separately, with raw observations, interpreted series if resolved, NOAA predictions, visible input gaps, and volunteer setup-epoch boundaries. Keep NOAA observed levels, if imported, visually and semantically distinct from astronomical predictions.
2. Define residual sign before computation; recommended convention is volunteer interpreted level minus NOAA predicted level. For every matched point expose both timestamps, signed time offset, location/epoch, source row, prediction station, and residual. Label results as descriptive differences for this selected series, month, and rule—not prediction accuracy, calibration, navigation advice, or surge guidance.
3. Default to exact timestamp matches after normalization. Offer reviewer-selected nearest matching only as an explicit alternative, with positive tolerance chosen from volunteer time precision and requested prediction interval. Record direction, tolerance, and tie rule; display time offset and leave unmatched rows missing. Do not use unconstrained nearest matches, silent forward/backward filling, or interpolation. If a reviewer needs interpolation, treat it as separate optional analysis with formula, bracketing-data requirement, provenance, and tests; it is not an effect of merge_asof.
4. NOAA supports several prediction intervals for harmonic stations but only high/low predictions for subordinate stations [S01, S03]. Use time-series residuals only where the chosen station/product yields a suitable interval. For subordinate/high-low-only output, offer a clearly labeled event-level high/low comparison or mark a dense residual unavailable; do not synthesize a continuous series.
5. Retain simple descriptive summaries only for a declared comparison and show sample count, unmatched/gap count, time-offset range, and selected coverage alongside any residual summary. Median, mean/bias, MAE or RMS may be offered as descriptive statistics, never a universal accuracy score. NOAA says prediction error varies by station and weather/runoff; its published example uses station-specific, one-year prediction-minus-observation RMS, so it is not a threshold for this two-site one-month volunteer sample [S04, S05].
6. Candidate event annotations are optional, manually reviewed context only. A NOAA citizen-science water-level application described in 2018 used photos and GPS-tagged reports of elevated water/impacts for communication; it is an event-reporting analogue, not evidence that photo/GPS reports are standardized continuous gauge values or that the app remains available today [S06].

### P4 — Provenance and review

1. A run records original files, hashes, retrieval timestamp, exact public URLs/parameters, station and metadata snapshots, parser/software versions, selected month, units, time-zone interpretation, datum/mapping evidence, setup epochs, match direction/tolerance/tie rule, residual sign, and summary options.
2. Preserve reviewer comments and competing interpretations with their evidence. A resolved choice must not erase prior alternatives. If reviewers cannot resolve conversion or station relationship, retain the two views and omit the absolute residual.
3. Every exported plot/table carries a compact context block: station ID/type, product, month, datum/unit, UTC/local convention, location/epoch aliases, comparison status, match rule, selected coverage/count, residual sign, retrieval date, and explicit education-only/no-navigation notice. Exports omit exact volunteer coordinates by default.
4. NOAA updates predictions quarterly and may change station type, subordinate adjustments, or reference station; preserve the particular metadata/retrieval snapshot for each run [S03]. Do not present today's metadata as necessarily the state used for an older input.

### P5 — Components and operating boundary

1. Recommend a local notebook/dataframe workflow using pandas for CSV handling and time-key alignment. In reviewed pandas 3.0.6 source, public merge_asof requires ascending time-key order, supports grouping, exact-match control, direction, and tolerance; _AsOfMerge validates keys/tolerance and dispatches to compiled join logic that returns row indexers. Nearest matching selects the closer timestamp and current implementation chooses the backward row on an exact-distance tie [S09–S11]. This joins rows; it does not estimate a water level between predictions.
2. Pin implementation pandas release and Python interpreter together before build. The source evidence is pinned to pandas 3.0.6, released September 17, 2026; upstream says pandas 3.0 supports Python 3.11 and higher [S15]. If local runtime is older or uses another pandas line, recheck contract, regression test, and implementation at that release. This is not a claim the application was built.
3. Put NOAA acquisition behind a small import step that saves response bytes and metadata, then let the notebook run locally/offline. NOAA API supports CSV/JSON/XML; Tide Predictions also exposes downloadable data and request URLs for independent inspection [S01, S03]. A selected month at 6-minute resolution is at the API's stated one-month retrieval limit; handle exact date range, response limits and any needed nonoverlapping chunks explicitly [S01].
4. Keep plotting library undecided until offline export and interaction needs are demonstrated. Static and interactive plots are alternatives; neither was selected or code-audited here. The notebook remains local and does not require uploading volunteer data or publishing location details [frozen plan §P5].

### P6 — Proposed acceptance and validation

All checks below are proposals; none was run against an application in this researcher stage.

- **Input integrity:** byte-identical snapshots reproduce the same parsed rows; parser errors and unsupported/missing columns are visible, never silently dropped.
- **Time:** test UTC offsets, LST/LDT around a DST fold/gap, date-only and rounded readings, out-of-order rows, duplicate timestamps, incompatible dtypes, and unknown conventions. Raw timestamps remain unchanged; unresolved ones do not enter residual calculations.
- **Vertical/unit handling:** test stated metres/feet conversions, missing/unknown units, unknown datum, station-unique STND, subordinate MLLW constraint, absent local benchmark mapping, conflicting mappings, and mapping validity boundaries. Unresolved cases yield no absolute residual.
- **Station and epoch:** test a volunteer move/zero change and NOAA metadata update (station type/reference, offsets, datums, notices/sensor messages). Changes appear as dated snapshots/boundaries and do not rewrite old results.
- **Matching:** exact; nearest before/after; match exactly at tolerance; beyond tolerance; midpoint tie; duplicates; unsorted keys; missing key; grouping A/B and epochs so there is no cross-location/epoch match. Verify right-side timestamp and delta-time are shown, unmatched rows remain gaps, and no value is interpolated or filled.
- **API and month:** distinguish prediction, observed level, verified hourly height, and OFS products; validate required datum/time/unit parameters, harmonic/subordinate interval availability, request/error handling, one-month 6-minute observation limit, date boundaries, and duplicate-free chunking.
- **Analysis and exports:** verify residual sign with hand-computed fixtures; summary count and coverage reconcile to matched rows; no summary when datum/time matching is unresolved; exports reproduce from saved source/choices and include context, privacy defaults, and no-navigation wording.
- **Review:** two conflicting interpretations remain viewable, comments retain attribution/time, and choosing one does not erase the alternative.

### Rejected or limited leads

- Do not use NOAA operational forecast-system data as the comparison reference: it is model nowcast/forecast guidance and expands scope toward operational forecasting [S01, S08].
- Do not treat NOAA's annual accuracy table as a universal threshold or calibrate two volunteer sites from it. It reports station-specific comparisons and one-year RMS; NOAA explains weather/runoff effects vary by site [S04, S05].
- Do not infer a continuous subordinate-station series from high/low-only output; NOAA describes reference-station offsets and the high/low limitation [S01, S03].
- Do not treat the citizen-science photo/GPS application as a standardized gauge protocol. It is useful only as an optional event/context annotation analogue [S06].
- Do not replace an unresolved absolute comparison with silent zero-centering, interpolation or a nearest match of unlimited age; those transformations answer different questions.
