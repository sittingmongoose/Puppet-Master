# Draft criticism dispositions — B08-P01 control

The original investigator draft is reviewed as a whole. “Accept” retains a sound claim, “keep” retains an alternative or limitation with its stated scope, “amend” means the idea is useful but needs a required correction or qualification, “reject” means the claim is contradicted by primary evidence, and “uncertain” records a question the permitted evidence cannot settle.

| ID | Draft claim or omission | Decision | Primary evidence and disposition |
|---|---|---|---|
| C01 | Use asyncio.Queue(maxsize=20) and four workers. | accept | This is the stated target. CPython documents positive maxsize as the number of waiting entries before put blocks; the four-worker count is the user requirement. Keep both values. [S1, lines 24–40, 80–85] |
| C02 | Queue plus four workers can own at most 24 jobs. | amend | The arithmetic is correct only for accepted queued-or-running jobs, with one active job per worker. It excludes producer-side unaccepted inputs, staging buffers, terminal history, and outputs. Label the quantity an accepted nonterminal bound, not a total memory bound. [S1, lines 24–40, 56–63] |
| C03 | Queue methods are intended for the event loop. | keep | The draft correctly says asyncio queues are not for arbitrary threads. Keep the constraint and specify an explicit thread-safe handoff for cross-thread submitters. [S1, lines 12–14, 40; S5, lines 59–89] |
| C04 | Record a per-job outcome and call task_done once in a finally path for each retrieved item. | amend | Preserve exactly-one acknowledgment for every successful get, including failure/cancellation terminal outcomes. Record the outcome and complete required cleanup before acknowledgment. A job that was never retrieved has no worker task_done; an immediate-shutdown-drained item must not receive another task_done. Extra calls raise ValueError and immediate shutdown already reduces drained-item accounting. [S1, lines 126–138; S2, lines 204–220, 239–269; S3, lines 617–655] |
| C05 | Conversion success requires a validated output and recorded successful outcome. | amend | The brief requires failure and cancellation not to become successful conversions; S1 defines task_done as queue processing accounting, not output validation. Keep the success gate and require publication to the component-owned destination before recording succeeded. Filesystem publication behavior remains a design obligation. [Brief, whole file; S1, lines 126–138] |
| C06 | Use temporary outputs and retain successful results after stopping. | amend | The brief assigns output paths to this component and the draft explicitly proposes temporary names and retaining completed results. Preserve those constraints; define destination ownership, conflict/overwrite policy, publish behavior, and cleanup-failure reporting. The admitted queue primary sources do not establish filesystem properties, so atomicity remains uncertain and must be checked on supported filesystems. [Brief and draft, whole files; source-index primary scope S1–S3] |
| C07 | Graceful stop uses shutdown(immediate=False), then join and worker termination. | accept | This matches CPython's documented drain behavior when admission has first closed: waiting putters are rejected, queued entries can be retrieved, and gets raise QueueShutDown once the queue is empty. Retain the sequence and make admission closure precede shutdown. [S1, lines 97–112; S3, lines 531–582] |
| C08 | Under force-stop, queue.join proves all accepted conversions succeeded. | reject | Immediate shutdown drains waiting entries and subtracts their count from unfinished tasks; when that reaches zero, join unblocks although drained work was never processed. The documentation warns directly against treating immediate-shutdown join as the usual completion invariant. Regression tests demonstrate this exact case. [S1, lines 113–124; S2, lines 250–269; S3, lines 583–655] |
| C09 | A force-stop with no remaining queue entries may be summarized as Completed. | reject | qsize zero says only that no waiting entries remain. Jobs can be abandoned from the queue or still active in workers. Report per-ID succeeded, failed, cancelled, abandoned, and not-accepted states; never derive all-success from an empty queue. [S1, lines 70–78, 93–124; S3, lines 583–655] |
| C10 | Immediate shutdown abandons waiting work. | amend | Keep the intended behavior, but mirror accepted queued IDs in application state and mark each drained accepted ID abandoned. Distinguish these from submitters rejected before acceptance. Do not invoke task_done for drained IDs because shutdown already updated queue accounting. [S2, lines 239–278; S3, lines 583–655] |
| C11 | Cancel active workers after force-shutdown and await termination. | amend | Retain cancellation as the force-stop request, but call it cooperative, run cleanup in finally, record a truthful outcome, and propagate CancelledError after cleanup. A job racing with cancellation is successful only if validation and publication committed first. asyncio cancellation is delivered at the next opportunity and can be suppressed. [S4, lines 276–294] |
| C12 | qsize can represent accepted/running progress. | amend | The draft correctly warns against using queue length alone, but the final must operationalize that warning. qsize counts waiting queue entries; maintain separate accepted, queued, running, and terminal counts in the ID ledger. [S1, lines 70–95; S2, lines 93–115] |
| C13 | Twelve equal two-second jobs on four workers take six seconds. | keep | Correct under the draft's exact assumptions: three waves, no overhead, no retries, and work-conserving equal jobs. Preserve the conditional calculation. |
| C14 | Ideal capacity is two jobs per second. | amend | Correct only as 4 workers divided by exactly 2-second service time under saturation with no overhead. Retain as a model result, not measured performance or a general workload rate. |
| C15 | Consider a process-based execution layer for CPU-bound conversion. | keep | Keep conditionally: asyncio concurrency does not remove event-loop blocking from CPU-bound work, and CPython documents executor options. The actual converter and external child-process behavior remain outside this queue component and must be measured separately. [S5, lines 109–120] |
| C16 | A sentinel is an alternative for runtimes older than 3.13. | keep | Queue.shutdown was added in 3.13. Keep a sentinel protocol as a compatibility option only if older runtimes enter scope; require admission closure, one termination token per worker, ordering, and explicit acknowledgment accounting. Those protocol details are design inference, not a Python-prescribed API. [S1, lines 97–124] |
| C17 | “Durable-in-session ledger” has a defined persistence guarantee. | uncertain | The draft does not say whether this means in-memory lifetime of one session or survival across process crash. Final scope treats it as retained for the live converter session only. Crash persistence and recovery need a product decision and storage design. |
| C18 | No conversion pipeline or throughput benchmark ran. | accept | Preserve this evidence status. The available local python3 is 3.14.4 and python3.13 was not found on PATH; the public v3.13.9 primary source was inspected, but neither it nor a converter pipeline was executed locally. |
| C19 | Propose lifecycle and workload tests but separate evidence from plans. | amend | Keep the draft's proposed tests and expand them to assert the acceptance boundary, active versus abandoned outcomes, output cleanup/publish failures, cancellation, thread boundary, queue accounting, UI responsiveness, and measured workload behavior. Mark all as planned, not passed. |

## Corrections applied to the final

The final keeps the selected queue and worker count, bounded waiting queue, outcome ledger, one task_done per retrieved item, graceful shutdown, sentinel compatibility option, output-retention intent, and valid ideal calculations. It replaces the force-stop “Completed” interpretation with per-job states and a separate queue accounting interpretation. It explicitly states the acceptance boundary and the limitations of qsize, cooperative cancellation, memory bounds, local runtime, and filesystem evidence.

## Unresolved questions

- Does “durable-in-session” require persistence across application restart or only through one live session?
- Which filesystem(s) must support output publication, and what overwrite/conflict policy is expected?
- What user wording should force-stop use when waiting jobs are abandoned while active work is cancelling?
- What real conversion workload, file-size distribution, memory budget, and UI responsiveness threshold should determine queue capacity and worker count?
