# D-P04 — Software planning brief

A shared floor-layout planning tool needs a comment-anchoring and reconciliation component. Teams sketch movable shapes, connect them, and attach review threads to a shape, connector, or selected region. Several people may work while disconnected. Research how comment references should behave when another user moves, groups, replaces, or deletes the referenced object, and how reconnection should expose conflicts without silently losing a review thread.

The deliverable is a source-backed component proposal with alternative identity and merge approaches, not an implemented collaborative editor. A comment's author should be able to understand its original target and why it now appears elsewhere or is unresolved. Geometry can change without the target's meaning changing, while a replacement shape can look identical but represent a different object. Discover maintained collaboration mechanisms, repository callers/tests, and useful public regression history; do not assume a particular merge library or hosted service is the answer.

Investigate ordering, durable identities, tombstones or other deletion representations, and the boundary between merging layout edits and resolving comment intent. Include storage growth and privacy implications of retaining enough history. Review threads need exportable context, including unresolved anchors, for a read-only snapshot sent to someone outside the editing group.

Do not resolve ambiguous intent solely by nearest coordinates, resurrect a deliberately deleted object without notice, or discard comments because an object is not currently visible. No real shared document or account should be changed. Compare what can be automatic with what requires a user decision. Propose tests with synthetic edit histories, distinguishing any actually executed merge experiments from proposed future editor integration, permission checks, and usability studies.

Complete all substantive research and planning obligations within a maximum of 60 elapsed minutes. Finish earlier when the complete output is ready. There is no minimum research duration, no requirement to consume the allowance, and no credit for waiting. The deadline includes arm-specific setup, provider queueing after admission, tools, native retries, handoffs, revision and delivery. Output quality is assessed independently of elapsed time.
