# D-P02 — Software planning brief

A cataloging application needs an image-derivative component for photographs submitted by many contributors. Research a design for inspecting an input, generating predictable thumbnails and review copies, and exporting selected images without altering the original. Inputs can carry orientation metadata, wide-gamut color profiles, transparency, unusually large dimensions, or broken metadata. The result should be a research-backed component plan, not a deployed image service.

Reviewers compare images side by side, so surprising rotation or color changes affect their decisions. The component must explain its output policy and retain enough provenance to regenerate a derivative after settings or decoder versions change. Some exports must omit identifying metadata while preserving the visual result; other internal review copies need selected technical metadata. Discover maintained implementations, governing format and color-management documentation, and useful issue-to-regression history without choosing a winning image library in advance.

Compare decoding and color-transformation approaches, including the interaction of scaling, orientation, alpha, and output profiles. Examine resource limits and predictable failure when an input would consume excessive memory. A batch may contain a mix of supported and unsupported images, and cancellation must leave understandable output state.

Do not overwrite originals, upload contributor files, equate deleting one metadata field with removing all identifying information, or claim visual fidelity merely from equal dimensions. Do not silently substitute a plausible-looking image for a failed decode. Explain how users learn about unsupported inputs or policy exceptions. Any executed checks may use generated images or public fixtures; distinguish their actual observations from proposed perceptual review, malicious-input testing, and production integration.

Complete all substantive research and planning obligations within a maximum of 60 elapsed minutes. Finish earlier when the complete output is ready. There is no minimum research duration, no requirement to consume the allowance, and no credit for waiting. The deadline includes arm-specific setup, provider queueing after admission, tools, native retries, handoffs, revision and delivery. Output quality is assessed independently of elapsed time.
