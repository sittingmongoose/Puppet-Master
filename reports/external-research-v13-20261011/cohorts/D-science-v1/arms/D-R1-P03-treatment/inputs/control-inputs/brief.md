# D-P03 — Software planning brief

A desktop collection manager needs a portable backup and restore component for a local workspace containing structured records, attachments, and user preferences. Users may interrupt backup, copy an archive through removable storage, or restore onto a machine with a newer application schema. Research how to produce a recoverable archive and a restore preview that can explain conflicts before changing the live workspace. No backup product, storage engine, or encryption package is selected.

A backup should identify what it includes and what was excluded, detect damaged or missing archive members, and avoid presenting an incomplete copy as current protection. Restore should support a new empty workspace as well as a deliberate merge into an existing one. Attachments can share names while containing different bytes, and records can refer to attachments that are unavailable. Investigate identity, referential integrity, schema migration, and atomicity boundaries using primary documentation, relevant code/tests, and public repair history.

Some users want a passphrase-protected archive. Explain the choices and operational limitations without inventing a recovery channel or making assurances based only on an encryption label. Compare packaging and restore strategies, including staging versus direct application and whole-workspace versus incremental backup.

Do not delete the live workspace before a restore is verified, silently overwrite a same-named attachment, upload archives, or promise recovery after a forgotten passphrase. Preserve a clear distinction between integrity checks, successful extraction, and a usable restored workspace. The deliverable is a proposal and validation plan. Executed synthetic archive or storage checks are welcome if feasible, but mark their actual scope and keep future application migration, fault-injection, and user-data testing explicitly proposed.

Complete all substantive research and planning obligations within a maximum of 60 elapsed minutes. Finish earlier when the complete output is ready. There is no minimum research duration, no requirement to consume the allowance, and no credit for waiting. The deadline includes arm-specific setup, provider queueing after admission, tools, native retries, handoffs, revision and delivery. Output quality is assessed independently of elapsed time.
