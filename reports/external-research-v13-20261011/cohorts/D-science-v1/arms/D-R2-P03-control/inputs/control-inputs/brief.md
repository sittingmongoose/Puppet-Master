# D-P07 — Software planning brief

A volunteer support center wants a planning workspace for recurring duty rosters. Coordinators draft assignments, workers request swaps, and a supervisor publishes the schedule people should follow. Some users edit while disconnected, and an assignment can depend on a role qualification or on another shift. Research connected components for offline edits, conflict resolution, approval, and published snapshots. The deliverable is a source-backed software plan with alternatives and tests, not a live scheduling deployment.

A published roster must remain distinguishable from a working draft. Two coordinators may independently assign the same person to overlapping duties or accept different swaps involving one shift. Reconnection must preserve intent and identify decisions that need human resolution. Workers should be able to see which schedule version their request concerned, and a coordinator needs a concise explanation of what changed before republishing.

Discover maintained synchronization and transactional mechanisms, relevant repository callers/tests, and useful repair history rather than preselecting a collaboration service. Compare data representations and merge strategies for ordinary field edits versus constrained assignment decisions. Investigate authorization and approval boundaries, local storage recovery, and how notification or export consumers reference a published version.

Do not treat a fieldwise merge as proof that the roster is valid, silently publish offline edits, overwrite a supervisor's approval, or promise that a delivered notification can be recalled. Unknown center rules can remain assumptions, but the proposal should specify where such rules would be checked. No real roster, account, or notification system should be touched. Executed synthetic traces must be distinguished from proposed future rule validation, multi-client fault tests, and organizational acceptance.

Complete all substantive research and planning obligations within a maximum of 60 elapsed minutes. Finish earlier when the complete output is ready. There is no minimum research duration, no requirement to consume the allowance, and no credit for waiting. The deadline includes arm-specific setup, provider queueing after admission, tools, native retries, handoffs, revision and delivery. Output quality is assessed independently of elapsed time.
