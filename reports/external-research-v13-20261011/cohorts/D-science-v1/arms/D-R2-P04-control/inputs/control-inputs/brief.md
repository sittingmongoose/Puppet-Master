# D-P08 — Software planning brief

A research notebook needs an incremental attachment-search component. Notes can contain multilingual text, scanned PDFs, slide exports, and short audio clips with optional transcripts. Users want to find a passage and jump to its location, while recognizing whether a result came from original text, optical recognition, or a supplied transcript. Research a practical indexing and provenance design with implementation alternatives and a validation plan.

Attachments may be renamed, moved, replaced, or partially unreadable. Extraction can take longer than editing a note, and a cancelled or failed extraction must not make older content appear current. A result should identify the attachment version and page, region, or time span it refers to when such location information is available. Unknown or approximate locations must be shown honestly. The proposal should discover applicable primary format/parser documentation, maintained repositories, governing code/tests, and useful issue-to-regression history; no search or extraction product is preselected.

Compare lexical and other relevant retrieval approaches, language handling, and strategies for incrementally replacing extracted content. Explain bounded background work, error reporting, and how original evidence can be opened without requiring a user to trust an extracted snippet. A modest initial scope is acceptable if unsupported types and limitations are explicit and the requested provenance and freshness obligations remain covered.

Do not upload private attachments, treat recognition text as a faithful original, invent precise locations for unsupported extractors, or leave deleted content searchable without explanation. Do not add a requirement to transcribe every clip or prove accuracy on our unavailable corpus. Synthetic and public fixtures can support executed checks; separate those from proposed multilingual relevance, recognition-quality, and future notebook integration testing.

Complete all substantive research and planning obligations within a maximum of 60 elapsed minutes. Finish earlier when the complete output is ready. There is no minimum research duration, no requirement to consume the allowance, and no credit for waiting. The deadline includes arm-specific setup, provider queueing after admission, tools, native retries, handoffs, revision and delivery. Output quality is assessed independently of elapsed time.
