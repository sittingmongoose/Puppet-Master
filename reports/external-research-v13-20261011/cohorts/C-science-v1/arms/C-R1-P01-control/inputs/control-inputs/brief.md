# C-P01 — Software planning brief

We maintain a desktop intake console for community-service applications. Its reusable results grid can show thousands of submitted records, including long names, missing fields, duplicate-looking labels, and several editable columns. Research a component design that makes browsing, editing, selection, and validation usable with keyboard navigation and assistive technology while keeping large result sets responsive. The deliverable is an evidence-backed proposal with implementation boundaries, alternatives, and a validation plan, rather than an implemented application.

Rows can disappear after a filter changes or after another view updates a record. Sorting may move the row currently being edited. Users need to know which record an error belongs to, return to it, and distinguish selection from keyboard focus. Investigate how rendering strategy, stable record identity, focus ownership, and exposed accessibility information interact. Discover relevant primary specifications, maintained implementations, and useful failure or regression history; do not assume a particular toolkit or grid library is already suitable.

Preserve unsaved edits and explain recovery when the edited record leaves the visible result set. A pointer must not be required to complete ordinary editing or recover from a validation error. Do not solve responsiveness by removing accessible navigation, cap the product to a tiny dataset, or announce every background row update. Include plausible alternatives to one rendering architecture and identify where platform support needs measurement. Separate tests actually performed on public examples or small synthetic fixtures from tests proposed for our future console. Unknown application data and unavailable assistive devices may remain explicit limitations, but available mechanism research should still proceed.

Complete all substantive research and planning obligations within a maximum of 60 elapsed minutes. Finish earlier when the complete output is ready. There is no minimum research duration, no requirement to consume the allowance, and no credit for waiting. The deadline includes arm-specific setup, provider queueing after admission, tools, native retries, handoffs, revision and delivery. Output quality is assessed independently of elapsed time.
