# C-P02 — Software planning brief

A small documentary team wants an offline preparation workspace for interviews recorded on separate devices. Research how to design linked components for media inspection, timeline alignment, transcript attachment, and portable review export. Their recordings can have different start times, variable frame rates, multiple audio tracks, or uncertain clock metadata. We need a source-backed planning proposal that identifies sound architecture choices and unresolved assumptions; we are not asking you to build an editor or process private recordings.

The workspace should preserve each imported original and allow reviewers to create named excerpts without rewriting it. A transcript correction or a change to an alignment offset must have understandable effects on existing excerpts and comments. Reviewers also need a portable package whose references can be resolved after files move to another machine. Discover relevant maintained tools, format specifications, repository mechanisms, and issue-to-fix history rather than assuming one existing media suite wins.

Compare approaches to time representation, inspection versus decoding, and derived preview generation. Explain what must be stored to keep alignment and excerpt boundaries reproducible, and how uncertainty becomes visible instead of being silently rounded away. Some users have modest storage and cannot duplicate every original for each review.

Do not upload recordings, overwrite source files, assume all devices share a reliable wall clock, or treat transcript text as a timing oracle. Preserve a path for manually correcting alignment and reporting unsupported inputs. A useful answer may recommend several interoperating components or a smaller initial scope, but it must account for all requested behaviors. Propose validation with synthetic media and public fixtures, distinguishing any checks executed during research from future integration work.

Complete all substantive research and planning obligations within a maximum of 60 elapsed minutes. Finish earlier when the complete output is ready. There is no minimum research duration, no requirement to consume the allowance, and no credit for waiting. The deadline includes arm-specific setup, provider queueing after admission, tools, native retries, handoffs, revision and delivery. Output quality is assessed independently of elapsed time.
