# Complementary truth critique — C-P04 export-worker architecture

**Stage:** critic-truth, run C-R2-P02-treatment. **Scope:** independently reviewed the complete released investigator package (`discovery.md`, `draft.md`, `source-map.json`, `revealed-plan.md`, `plan-reveal.json`, and the bounded-source index) against the brief. **Disposition:** retain the recommendation and clause dispositions, with one material correction required before treating descendant cancellation as guaranteed. No executable witness or application integration was run.

## Material finding: a Linux process group does not guarantee descendant teardown

The draft says the coordinator must terminate and reap the “whole job, including children,” and describes a per-export “process group / OS job / sandbox” as a possible lifecycle boundary. On Linux, a process-group signal alone does not establish that guarantee. The governing `setsid(2)` interface permits a process that is not already a process-group leader to create a new session and process group; a forked child that is still in the worker’s original group can therefore leave that group. Signalling only the original group may miss that child and its descendants. See S22, `setsid(2)` DESCRIPTION and NOTES. This matters directly to the brief’s untrusted converters, cancellation, and host-platform enforcement requirements.

Tighten the wording and policy as follows:

- Treat a process group as a convenient signal target, not as Linux process-tree containment. For Linux’s stronger claim, attach the worker before untrusted code starts to a dedicated, non-root cgroup and keep descendants in that cgroup; use `cgroup.kill` only when the documented cgroup conditions are met. The investigator’s S02 already records that `cgroup.kill` covers descendants and concurrent forks, but only for a non-root, non-threaded cgroup with the needed host support/delegation.
- Keep Windows Job Objects conditional on verified job membership and disabled/unavailable breakaway paths; S03 records the documented exceptions. Separately specify and verify the macOS descendant-termination mechanism. The reviewed Apple sandbox material establishes file-access conditions, not a general process-tree kill guarantee.
- Amend the proposed cancellation and platform witnesses to spawn a child that calls `setsid()`, forks again, ignores cooperative cancellation, and attempts a late staging write. Require that teardown leaves no job process able to modify outputs before verification begins. A passing normal-child test does not establish this case.
- If a host cannot enforce and verify the required process-tree boundary, report that control as unavailable and apply the owner-selected degraded-mode policy; do not promise that forced cancellation has reaped the entire job.

## Other consequential claims checked

- **Memory and CPU policy:** The package correctly separates policy from enforcement. Investigator S02 says cgroup `memory.max` defaults to `max`, can invoke OOM handling, and usage may temporarily exceed it; CPU weights share eligible active work rather than reserve UI capacity or define queue order; `io.max` is device-specific. S04–S05 correctly qualify Windows job limits by the required flags and distinguish committed-memory limits from RSS and CPU scheduling behavior. Keep these host conditions attached to any user-visible guarantee.
- **WASI and macOS:** The WASI/Wasm discussion correctly identifies opt-in fuel and per-linear-memory limits as guest-level controls, not process RSS/CPU isolation (S06–S08). The macOS wording is strongest where it says the reviewed sources establish no general per-converter quota; preserve that evidence-bounded phrasing instead of asserting that no macOS mechanism could ever provide a quota. The inherited selected-file-access caveat is explicitly attributed to archived Apple guidance (S10), with revalidation called out.
- **Capabilities and saved presets:** LSP is explicitly an analogy rather than a converter standard (S01), and the draft’s preflight rejection contract is presented as a proposal. The Pandoc 3.8.3 regression examples are specific evidence that output semantics can change across an update (S12–S14); they support review/pinning but do not by themselves prove that every compatible version range is unsafe. The recommendation leaves compatibility policy with the owner, which is appropriately scoped. LibreOffice’s separate extension/filter/parameter fields also support the warning against treating a filename extension as a stable capability identity (S15).
- **Artifact success and transfer:** The proposed coordinator-owned verification/transfer gate addresses the plan’s unsafe “promote on exit” assumption. Keep converter-supplied size/digest values as assertions: independently enumerate the closed job output tree, read and hash actual bytes, compare them with the negotiated output contract, then transfer/commit and verify at the destination as its adapter permits. Linux rename and Windows `ReplaceFileW` are correctly limited by filesystem/volume and error-state conditions; neither source justifies universal bundle atomicity or durability (S16–S18). No destination witness was run.
- **Fairness and progress:** Bounded/coalesced advisory progress and queue ordering are reasonable design proposals, not guarantees from LSP or kernel weights. The draft correctly says numeric finite wait requires bounded service and capacity, and separates queue delay from execution. Preserve that limitation; no scheduler witness was executed.
