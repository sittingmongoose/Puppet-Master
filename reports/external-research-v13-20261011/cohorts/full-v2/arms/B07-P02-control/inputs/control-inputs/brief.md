# Accessible recording review annotations

A desktop interview editor needs a review component in which staff listen to recordings, mark a time interval and attach a textual comment. The recordings may be long, and reviewers may work entirely from a keyboard or use a screen reader. Several comments can overlap. A transcript, when available, should provide a second route to the relevant passage, but the review workflow must also work when no transcript exists.

Research time representation, seeking and interval editing, annotation identity, transcript association and accessible navigation through comments. A later replacement recording can have a different lead-in or sample rate; existing annotations must not silently point at the wrong passage. Users need to export comments in a human-readable form with useful timing context. Assume local files and occasional stereo recordings; the playback engine is not fixed. Do not require mouse dragging, encode interval distinctions by color alone, invent transcript timings, alter original recordings, or transmit audio to a remote service. A missing transcript must not block a keyboard review. Compare suitable annotation and interaction approaches, state media and timing limits, and propose integration plus validation of both accessible interaction and annotation fidelity. The deliverable does not require recruiting real interview subjects, deploying a player or annotating private recordings.

Produce a research-backed software proposal, not an implementation. Explore relevant primary documentation, repository code, callers/tests, and issue-to-fix or release history where consequential. Compare feasible alternatives without assuming a winning product. Distinguish proposed validation from work actually executed. The exact draft plan is withheld during brief-only discovery and released once at the declared comparison point.
