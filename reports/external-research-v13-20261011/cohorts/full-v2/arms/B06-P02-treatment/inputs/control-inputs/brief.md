# Volunteer shift handover across unreliable connections

A volunteer-run food distribution center wants a shared handover system. Teams record tasks, location notes and unresolved equipment problems between shifts. The desk computer normally has a connection, but volunteers often use phones in a loading area with poor reception. A shift leader needs an intelligible summary of what changed and which issues still need attention; a task can be claimed by two volunteers during an outage.

Research end-to-end workflows for reading recent handovers, drafting offline changes, reconciling concurrent updates and searching older issues. Separate a volunteer's acknowledgment that they read a note from a resolution of the underlying issue. Photos are optional and may contain sensitive information; the design should state how attachment upload and deletion work without assuming unlimited mobile data. About thirty volunteers rotate through weekly shifts. Do not turn reading into a performance score, let old offline writes silently reopen resolved issues, expose private contact details in search results, or require a new commercial account for each volunteer. Unknown organizational retention and role policies should remain explicit decisions. Compare synchronization and shared-access alternatives, propose a small adoption sequence and failure-oriented validation. No real volunteer invitations, account creation, deployment or use of live center records is requested.

Produce a research-backed software proposal, not an implementation. Explore relevant primary documentation, repository code, callers/tests, and issue-to-fix or release history where consequential. Compare feasible alternatives without assuming a winning product. Distinguish proposed validation from work actually executed. The exact draft plan is withheld during brief-only discovery and released once at the declared comparison point.

Complete all substantive research and planning obligations within a maximum of 60 elapsed minutes. Finish earlier when the complete output is ready. There is no minimum research duration, no requirement to consume the allowance, and no credit for waiting. The deadline includes arm-specific setup, provider queueing after admission, tools, native retries, handoffs, revision and delivery. Output quality is assessed independently of elapsed time.
