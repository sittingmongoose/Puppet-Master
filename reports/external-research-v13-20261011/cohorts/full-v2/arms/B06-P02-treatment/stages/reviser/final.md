# Research-backed proposal — Volunteer shift handover across unreliable connections

**Run/stage:** B06-P02-treatment / reviser  
**Status:** Complete proposal responding to the brief and every clause of the released provisional plan. This is a research deliverable, not an implementation, vendor selection, deployment, or approval to use live center data.  
**Evidence:** Source IDs are stable within this run. The navigable register is [sources/README.md](sources/README.md); full source identity and access records are in [source-map.json](source-map.json). S, C, and T records are carried from the predecessor registers without rebinding; R records are this stage’s direct primary-source checks.

## Proposal in brief

Design a text-first shared handover around four distinct concepts: shift handovers, assignable tasks, contextual location notes, and equipment issues with an explicit state history. A shift leader sees accepted changes since the prior handover, work still open, unresolved equipment issues, late-arriving offline events, and the last confirmed sync time. Volunteers can draft and acknowledge notes while disconnected, with each item clearly labeled local, queued, accepted, rejected, or awaiting review. The service owns canonical state and applies current access rules when queued operations arrive.

Treat an offline task claim as a request, never as a shared lock. Preserve both attempts when two phones claim during an outage; apply a documented server rule transactionally or send the collision to a leader. A read receipt records only that a person read a note. It never changes a task or issue and never becomes a volunteer score. An old offline issue state cannot overwrite a later resolution; reopening requires a separate, current, explicit action.

Keep older-issue search access-filtered and exclude private contact details from the index, snippets, suggestions, result counts, and offline projection. Photos remain optional and separate from text delivery. Ask before upload, show bytes and progress, use a conservative data default, and represent local deletion, remote deletion, other phones, and backups as distinct states.

Compare viable sync and shared-access choices against the same synthetic failure cases. Relational storage with local SQLite and an explicit upload contract is a useful first spike, but this research does not select it or any provider. The center must decide who operates the service and who may read, claim, resolve, search, and delete; it must also decide identity, retention, offline cache expiry, photo consent, and backup deletion. The proposal recommends a synthetic tabletop and bounded technical comparison before any real pilot. No account, invitation, deployment, or live record was created or accessed.

## End-to-end workflow and domain rules

### Start and hand over a shift

A handover groups the outgoing shift’s accepted changes and unresolved work. The leader’s summary should have separate, scannable sections:

1. **Changed since the prior handover:** accepted task updates, new location notes, new equipment reports, comments, acknowledgements where the viewer is allowed to see them, and explicit issue transitions.
2. **Tasks still needing attention:** task status, any confirmed assignee, provisional or disputed claim requests, next action, and the source shift.
3. **Unresolved equipment issues:** approved location and equipment label, current status, last accepted transition, responsible role or next action, and when the latest update was authored and accepted.
4. **Resolved or carried-forward context:** recently resolved issues and notes that help the incoming team understand what changed, without presenting them as open.
5. **Late arrivals and review:** changes authored offline in an earlier shift but accepted later, rejected operations, and conflicts requiring a person’s decision.

Make the shift boundary explicit in the product, such as an outgoing leader confirming a handover identifier and the prior handover it follows. Use the service’s accepted event sequence or an explicit handover boundary to decide what changed; use a device clock only as a displayed, potentially inaccurate authored time. A late offline event must remain visible and traceable to its originating shift instead of silently disappearing or being sorted as if its handset time were authoritative. Local-only drafts belong in the author’s draft view and must not be described as accepted center-wide facts.

A task is an assignable unit of work that can be claimed or completed. An equipment issue describes a continuing problem with explicit open, resolved, and deliberately reopened transitions. A location note adds place context and may be linked to a task or issue; by itself it does not imply a claim, resolution, or completed work. Keep these distinctions visible in the model and summary.

### Read recent notes and acknowledge

Show the latest handover, the age of the last confirmed sync, and a clear stale/offline label. Let a volunteer mark an individual note or handover item as read. Store that receipt separately from task and issue state. It can help the next shift understand what was seen, but it does not prove work was done or a problem was fixed. Do not add read-speed metrics, streaks, rankings, leaderboards, or individual productivity exports. Whether a receipt is visible to the note author or leader, and how long it is retained, are owner decisions.

### Draft and synchronize offline changes

Allow text drafts for tasks, location notes, issues, comments, and acknowledgements to be saved locally without a network. Show the status on every pending item: local-only, queued, uploading, accepted, rejected, or needs review. Keep the text usable if an attachment cannot upload. Sync on foreground/open and provide a clear retry action; background sync may help, but neither a mobile OS nor browser should be presented as guaranteeing prompt delivery.

Use a stable client-generated entity/event identifier plus a separate operation/idempotency identifier. Store the actor and device/session that authored the operation, its target, creation time for display, and the base issue/task generation for state-changing requests. The backend should treat an exact replay as already applied, reject or quarantine the same operation ID with a different payload, apply current authorization, validate the target’s current state, and return an explicit outcome. These are proposed application rules. PowerSync’s documentation describes a per-client ordered upload queue and places conflict policy in the app backend; its simple example is per-field last-write-wins and it requires idempotent operation handling. That documentation does not give disconnected clients a common ordering or decide this center’s claim policy. [R01, T01, S01]

A queued operation keeps its original author and content, but not a permanent right to apply. The service checks current identity, membership, role, target visibility, and allowed action on receipt. If a volunteer has been removed, preserve the authored draft and return a rejection or review outcome with a useful explanation. A disconnected device cannot learn of revocation, erase its local cache, or stop showing already cached information until it reconnects. Cache expiry, credential expiry, and recovery or re-enrollment after restoring a device backup require an explicit policy. [R03, T03, S02, S07, S17]

### Reconcile claims and issue state

An offline claim is labeled “requested on this device,” not “assigned.” Two volunteers can both make a valid provisional request while disconnected. On reconnection, the service should use a transaction and a schema-defined uniqueness rule to ensure at most one canonical active claim for the same task, or return the collision for a leader to adjudicate. Retain both claim attempts and tell each affected volunteer what happened. A selection rule such as first accepted by the service must be documented; device wall-clock time cannot choose the winner. Reassignment and fairness are owner/workflow decisions. If physical work cannot wait, tell volunteers to use the existing verbal or paper handover fallback until a claim is confirmed.

Resolution is an explicit, authorized event against the current issue generation with an actor and concise reason. If the request is based on a stale generation, preserve it for review; do not apply an old “open” snapshot over a newer resolution. A later comment appends information but does not alter status. A comment that suggests a recurrence can create a visible new concern for follow-up. Reopening requires a distinct, authorized action after the current resolved state is shown, with a reason. A claim against a resolved issue pauses for review rather than implicitly reopening it.

A unique database index and transaction can protect a chosen canonical active-claim representation, but only if the schema, constraint predicate, and application operation express that invariant. PostgreSQL documents atomic ON CONFLICT behavior when its arbiter index and statement conditions apply; that is a persistence safeguard, not offline arbitration, fairness, or human review. [R05, R06, T05, S12]

### Search older issues privately

Provide a bounded searchable history beyond the active shift. Search only approved issue text, equipment category, approved location, status, and date range. Keep contact records out of the issue/search schema and out of the phone’s synchronized search projection. Free text can still contain an accidentally typed phone number or email; give input guidance and test a server-side masking, redaction, or review rule before indexing. Apply authorization before producing result rows, snippets, autocomplete/suggestions, counts, pagination metadata, or offline matches. Do not expose a protected value through an excerpt or an aggregate count. Author names and historical locations remain role-policy decisions.

Keep the local history subset, age, and expiry bounded by an owner decision and test storage limits on supported devices. If a role is revoked while a device is offline, downloaded data may remain visible until that device reconnects or its local policy expires; do not promise instant remote revocation of offline copies. CouchDB’s documented database membership is broad database-level access, not a ready-made volunteer row/field policy. [R03, T03, S07, S17]

### Optional photos and constrained mobile data

Text is saved first and remains usable without a photo. Do not automatically capture, upload, or download photos. After a volunteer selects an image, show its size and a proposed reduced copy, preview it, and ask for explicit confirmation. A proposed Wi-Fi-only default, explicit one-time mobile-data override, configurable size cap, local resize, and location/EXIF stripping should be verified against the chosen device, storage service, consent policy, and accessibility needs; these are design proposals, not guarantees established by the sync sources. Show queued, uploading, progress, retry, cancel, and failure states. Photo failure must not block the handover text.

Use a separate attachment identifier and object lifecycle rather than making the text event depend on a large upload. For deletion, record an authorized tombstone, stop pending retries, remove the local copy promptly, queue a retryable service-side object deletion, revoke serving access, and propagate the tombstone to other devices at their next sync. Show “deletion pending” until remote deletion is confirmed. Confirmation of the remote operation does not mean that an offline phone or retained backup has been erased. The owner must decide retention period, backup expiry, cache expiry, deletion evidence, and any legal or organizational hold. Do not promise global erasure before those decisions and device behavior are defined. [S03, S04, S14, S18]

## Alternatives to compare; no winner selected

### Synchronization and data model

| Option | Why it is plausible here | Costs, caveats, and decision gate |
|---|---|---|
| Relational source of truth with local SQLite and a PowerSync-style queued upload path | Local queries and a documented client upload queue; backend can implement current authorization, idempotency, explicit transitions, and claim outcomes. A useful first technical comparison if an operator and supported client platform are available. [R01, S01, S02, S15] | The queue is ordered per client, not globally across volunteers. The app backend supplies conflict behavior; simple per-field LWW is not safe for domain state. Service/auth/database boundary, owner, operating burden, hosting and fees, backup/restore, and attachment integration remain to be selected and tested. |
| PouchDB clients with Apache CouchDB replication | Direct local/remote bidirectional replication can fit disconnected peers and can be self-hosted. Replicated branches can coexist. [R02, S05, S06] | A deterministic winner is returned by ordinary reads/views; the other revision may be hidden until conflict metadata is requested and an application resolves it. A stale revision write on one node is rejected with 409 rather than silently merged. Database member permissions are broad, so separate volunteer scoping must be designed. PouchDB 9.0’s release notes reported IndexedDB stability work and a default find result limit of 25; this is a release claim, not proof of this app’s behavior. The investigator’s issue/PR observations are reports at access, not verified general defects or shipped fixes. Test paging, concurrent writes, recovery, and deletion on pinned versions. [R02, R03, S07, S08, S09] |
| Automerge for collaborative note content | A CRDT can merge concurrent note/list edits without a central write round trip; same-property alternatives can be inspected with its conflict API. [R04, T04, S10] | Reading a property returns a deterministic winner; alternatives are queryable through getConflicts, but the next assignment to that property resolves and removes that conflict view. Keep any required durable review/audit history in the application’s own event log. A CRDT does not supply issue transitions, exclusive claims, authorization, or leader review. Keep this as a bounded note prototype unless the app supplies those rules. [R04, T04, S10] |
| Custom responsive client with local database/outbox, API, and relational database | Direct control over authorization, stale-state checks, claim outcomes, search filters, and attachment deletion. PostgreSQL can enforce schema-defined uniqueness under concurrency. [R05, R06, S12] | The team owns durable local storage, retries, idempotency, filtered downloads, migrations, conflict UI, backups, and support. Browser storage is best-effort by default and can be evicted; a persistence request is not a universal guarantee. Compare browser and native SQLite clients, and require foreground sync/status even if background work is added. [R07, S13, S14] |

Treat PouchDB/Powersync issue or PR history as bounded issue-to-fix evidence only. The investigator register records a PowerSync upload-queue report, an older closed transaction-grouping report without a visible closure explanation, and a PouchDB IndexedDB issue/proposed PR with reviewer concerns. Those records do not establish a present general defect or a released fix; preserve package version, environment, state at the source access, and missing reproduction/resolution as recorded. Recheck exact versions and issue status before making a procurement or release decision. [S08, S09, S16, S19, T07]

Compare the same event contract, data, and failure scenarios across finalists. Score offline behavior, recoverability, identity/access fit, operator burden, supportability on low-end phones, search/privacy controls, attachment lifecycle, backup duties, and projected service/hosting charges. About thirty rotating volunteers is a small-team adoption context, not a capacity result; load and usability still need synthetic measurement.

### Shared access and volunteer identity

“No commercial account for each volunteer” does not remove the need to know who authored a change or whose read receipt is stored. Prefer a center-owned workspace and a managed volunteer identity lifecycle, or deliberately accept less attribution with a pseudonymous alternative. Do not assume that one shared code answers identity, permissions, or accountability. Compare these options with the center:

| Access model | Attribution and receipts | Offboarding, offline copies, workload, and fee questions |
|---|---|---|
| Center-managed identity per volunteer in one center-owned workspace | Best individual attribution if credentials map to internal volunteer IDs; can distinguish author and receipt without requiring each person to buy a separate vendor subscription. | Define issuance, recovery, role mapping, and revocation. Recheck queued events when they arrive. Offline devices may retain data pending reconnect/expiry. Confirm whether the selected service charges per seat, active user, usage, or workspace; no fee model is assumed here. |
| Shift-scoped pseudonymous aliases | Can preserve a consistent shift label while avoiding a named identity in routine screens; receipts are attributable only to the alias for that shift unless the center keeps a separate protected mapping. | Easier rotation may reduce credential work, but weakens person-level continuity and audit. Decide whether alias reuse, re-enrollment, role checks, and receipt retention are acceptable. Device copies still need expiry and purge rules. |
| Shared station/session access | Lowest setup burden for a desk station; suitable only where the center knowingly accepts weak individual attribution or separately records who is on duty. | Poor fit for individual acknowledgements and concurrent mobile edits; a left-open session can expose more than intended. Require session timeout, sign-out, limited roles, and a separate mobile identity choice. It does not resolve per-volunteer fees or offline-device revocation. |

These are options, not recommendations to use named accounts, aliases, or shared access. Decide which actions require individual attribution and which roles may see names, notes, historical locations, or read receipts. A center-controlled app identity is distinct from purchasing a personal commercial subscription; actual service pricing and terms must be checked for any finalist.

## Adoption sequence

1. **Resolve ownership and policy questions.** Name a service operator and volunteer-facing support contact. Decide roles, attribution, identity/offboarding, offline cache and credential expiry, retention, backup deletion, photo consent/data limits, historical search access, and the fallback for delayed sync.
2. **Run a synthetic tabletop.** Use a desk and two phones with invented data, one shift boundary, two competing claims, a location note, one resolved and one unresolved equipment issue, receipts, a revoked actor, and an optional image. Disconnect at different steps. Keep the paper/physical handover.
3. **Compare two technical slices.** Use the same event and authorization contract with relational/local SQLite plus queued writes and a custom outbox/API. Add PouchDB/CouchDB if self-hosting is a deciding constraint; test Automerge only for a bounded note-edit case. Record setup, recovery, access, search, support burden, mobile data behavior, and actual provider/hosting fees.
4. **Close policy before a real pilot.** If the center approves, use a small, time-boxed group and low-sensitivity data, a named leader, explicit rollback and deletion procedure, and the physical fallback. Review every rejected write, duplicate claim, and deletion failure. Do not score volunteers.
5. **Expand, revise, or stop after review.** Check outcomes on supported low-end devices, support workload, late sync, search access, retention/deletion, and whether any required action unexpectedly depends on a volunteer buying a personal vendor account.
