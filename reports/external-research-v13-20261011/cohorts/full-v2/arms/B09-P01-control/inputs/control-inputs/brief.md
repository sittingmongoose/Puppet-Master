# Neighborhood lending inventory and returns

A small neighborhood tool library needs software for inventory, lending and returns. Volunteers currently maintain separate lists of items and loans, and duplicate item names make it hard to tell which physical drill or ladder was lent. Research a system that gives each item a durable identity, shows current availability and preserves an explanation when a loan or return is corrected.

Two volunteers may work from separate laptops during a busy session. Internet access is unreliable, but they can normally reach a local network. Some items are lent as kits with removable parts, and a damaged return should create a maintenance hold rather than immediately mark the item available. Search needs to find aliases and distinguish similar items. Contact details should be limited to what is necessary for a loan reminder; the policy for sending reminders is undecided. Do not require paid cloud accounts, infer a return from the passage of a due date, erase loan history when an item is retired, or automatically send borrower messages. About eight hundred items and five years of records are expected. Compare identity, concurrency and deployment approaches, identify organizational policy gaps and propose staged adoption with recovery validation. No deployment, live borrower processing or outbound communication is requested.

Produce a research-backed software proposal, not an implementation. Explore relevant primary documentation, repository code, callers/tests, and issue-to-fix or release history where consequential. Compare feasible alternatives without assuming a winning product. Distinguish proposed validation from work actually executed. The exact draft plan is withheld during brief-only discovery and released once at the declared comparison point.

Complete all substantive research and planning obligations within a maximum of 60 elapsed minutes. Finish earlier when the complete output is ready. There is no minimum research duration, no requirement to consume the allowance, and no credit for waiting. The deadline includes arm-specific setup, provider queueing after admission, tools, native retries, handoffs, revision and delivery. Output quality is assessed independently of elapsed time.
