# Independent critique — disconnected field-survey edit journal

## Scope and result

I reviewed the full brief, the investigator’s discovery, draft, source map, released plan and plan-reveal metadata, plus the carried source index. The discovery and plan byte counts and SHA-256 hashes match plan-reveal.json. I did not read parent history, other candidates, or paths outside the listed inputs and the carried source roots.

The proposal covers the substantive brief and gives a disposition for every released-plan clause P1–P9. I found no material wrong correction and no material brief obligation that the proposal silently drops. The most consequential issue is a conditional version-and-validation gap for SQLite WAL. The existing desktop application’s repository and integration constraints remain honestly unresolved because the brief identifies no application or repository. A source locator/version detail also needs tightening for reproducibility.

## Brief coverage and scope

- Three disconnected laptops and a week offline: durable operation exchange, bounded/idempotent batches, retry, receipts, missing-parent handling, and three-device validation are covered.
- Observation fields, up to 50,000 records, and unknown note/photo sizes or connection speeds: fields are accounted for; performance remains a measured question, with no invented throughput or storage promise.
- Competing same-field edits and nonoverlapping edits: alternatives are retained for review; automatic merge is limited to proven independent field operations.
- Deletion overlapping an edit or photo addition: tombstones control ordinary visibility; neither an incoming stale edit nor photo metadata is a restore; explicit restore is a new operation.
- Separate photo content and references, no automatic upload, and unavailable local photos: the draft gives stable attachment identity, explicit/on-demand transfer, digest verification and visible missing/pending states.
- Power loss during a local save or reconnect: the draft proposes transaction boundaries, recovery state, idempotent retry and failure-oriented validation. Actual durability remains unproven until the selected storage/VFS/filesystem and protocol are tested.
- Human-readable recovery: the sample review message explains what changed, preserves alternatives, offers explicit keep-deleted/restore/compare choices and allows deferral.
- Manual transfer without network, no hosted account and no wall-clock winner: all are retained.
- Feasible alternatives: SQLite plus app-owned operations, CouchDB revision-tree replication and Automerge are compared with constraints and remaining application responsibilities; no benchmark-supported winner is claimed.

## Evidence and validation status

My independent source checks were read-only. They confirmed: SQLite WAL’s host, writer, sidecar and synchronous conditions; SQLite’s current WAL-reset bug advisory and fixed/backported releases; Session Extension schema/primary-key/conflict-callback constraints; CouchDB’s conflict-leaf/deterministic-winner behavior; CouchDB issue #6126’s 3.5.2 attachment scenario, PR #6129’s revision-position fix and the 3.5.3 release note; and Automerge’s concurrent map delete/set rule, deterministic default conflict value, conflict inspection API, and pinned v3.4.1 implementation/test behavior. Exact URLs, versions, access window, observed operations, conditions and limits are in this stage’s source-map.json; sources/index.md is the navigation page.

The investigator reports primary-document/repository-source review and plan reveal, but no application prototype, benchmark, crash harness, integration test, suite run or user study. I found no wording that upgrades any of those proposed validations into completed work. This critique is not a final proposal and does not repair or replace the investigator’s draft.
