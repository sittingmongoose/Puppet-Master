# Brief-only discovery — shared workshop booking and equipment readiness

**Run/stage:** B02-P02-treatment / investigator  
**Evidence cutoff:** 2026-10-12 UTC, before plan release.  
**Scope:** Research-backed proposal for roughly 100 workshop members, member equipment bookings, staff maintenance holds, a daily readiness view, shared extraction equipment, and jobs requiring a trained supervisor. Compare local and shared deployment choices, preserve understandable conflicts/reasons, cover recurring maintenance, cancellation/interruption, simultaneous submissions, and Internet outages. No real installation or member-data processing is authorized.

## Initial finding

No off-the-shelf candidate is yet a complete fit. LibreBooking is the strongest feature match to test because its model supports a single reservation with several resources and accessories, recurrent blackouts, approvals, and role-based administration. MRBS is a simpler intranet booking system with repeating entries and conflict rejection; its schema is room-centered and stores one room/resource ID per booking entry/series, so compound tool + supervisor + extractor booking needs a linked workflow that has not been shown to be atomic. A small purpose-built local system offers the clearest way to make fault holds and dispute explanations first-class, but introduces build, support, backup, and security obligations. A wall calendar/spreadsheet or a general calendar may remain a read-only display or temporary fallback; it must not be treated as capacity or concurrency enforcement.

This is a shortlist for evaluation, not a selection. Product pages and repository tests are evidence about documented and coded behavior, not proof of safe runtime behavior in this workshop.

## Scheduling model and safeguards to carry into the proposal

1. **Represent the thing that is scarce.** Model each physical tool as its own reservable resource. Model an extractor as a shared capacity-one resource or a quantity-one accessory, with a constraint tying it to eligible tools. A required supervisor must consume capacity too: use a named qualified staff resource if a person is assigned at booking time, or a staffed-role capacity pool with a later explicit named assignment and qualified shift coverage. The role model, skill validation, and staff override rules need an owner decision. Do not treat a text label saying “supervisor required” as consuming a scarce supervisor slot.
2. **Use one compound reservation and atomic claims.** Each reservation has one interval and one owner, with allocation rows for every selected tool/person/unit. Use local-time display plus unambiguous stored instants and a consistent half-open interval convention so adjacent slots can touch. An availability preview is advisory; the authoritative write rechecks and commits all claims together. PostgreSQL range exclusion constraints can enforce non-overlap per resource; separate allocation rows can be inserted in one transaction so a failed claim leaves no partial booking. If instead using SQLite on the application host, `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` can serialize short writers and surface busy conflicts; SQLite permits many readers but only one writer. Never share the SQLite database file directly over SMB/NFS. At this membership size SQLite may be sufficient when all SQL runs locally in one application process/server, but peak simultaneous writers and recovery need measurement. For more writers or independent app connections, prefer a client/server database such as PostgreSQL over increasing SQLite complexity.
3. **Keep maintenance independent from member edits.** A maintenance hold belongs to a staff role, requires a reason and scope, and remains active even if a member edits/cancels their booking. It blocks new requests. A post-acceptance fault produces an explicit impact list: existing reservations remain visible as affected/unsafe-to-run until a human resolves each one. Show the member-facing conflict in plain language, the unavailable resource, interval, and safe next step. Preserve before/after values, actor, UTC event time, and required reason for any move/cancel/override. Avoid silent automatic reassignment or deletion until displacement, priority, notification, and consent policy are settled. Operational status and reason need a retention period and least-access view; log scheduling changes only, not unnecessary browsing/session/activity data.
4. **Make cancellation and interruption distinct.** Use a lifecycle such as requested/confirmed/in-use/completed/canceled/interrupted/needs-reschedule, with a cancellation or interruption reason and actor. Cancellation releases allocations while keeping a bounded explanation. An interruption records actual end and safety reason; it must not silently rewrite the original accepted interval. Policy must decide whether a waitlist is fair and whether a canceled spot is offered automatically.
5. **Bound recurring work.** Require an end date for recurring maintenance and recurring reservations, display generated occurrences, and support a one-occurrence exception without changing the whole series. Store local zone and test daylight-saving transitions. A maintenance exception should not remove unrelated future holds. LibreBooking's current source rejects a recurring blackout without a termination date; its v4.3.0 changelog records this as a fix. Repeated-series editing has had regressions in both candidates, so instance-only cancel/edit and series edits belong in validation.
6. **Offer an understandable readiness view.** A staff view should show today/next shift, tool status (ready / held / out of service / affected booking), required supervisor assignment/coverage, extractor availability, cleaning/inspection buffers, and an explicit hold reason. Member views should disclose available/unavailable times and the member's own reservation details; they should not reveal another member's job notes or broader usage history. The scheduled daily view is an operations view, not a new identity-surveillance log.

## Deployment and offline options

- **One local workstation:** run the application and database locally, with staff using its browser. This has the least infrastructure and keeps booking available during an Internet outage. Members can book only while able to reach that machine, unless it also serves the local Wi-Fi/LAN. The workstation being off or its disk failing is an outage; keep tested backups and a paper/manual contingency.
- **One on-prem LAN host:** place the web application and database on a small managed local host/mini-PC, use the workshop router/Wi-Fi for member phones and a staff workstation, and keep DNS/auth/assets/email dependencies local or optional. If Internet is down but the LAN and host remain up, the service can continue. If LAN or host is down, stop accepting authoritative bookings; write provisional requests in a clearly marked paper queue, then staff reconcile serially against the canonical schedule when service returns. Do not let offline clients each confirm the same slot.
- **Hosted/shared cloud service:** easiest remote access, but Internet dependency conflicts with outage operation and may impose a hosted account/data relationship. It is not the initial fit without an explicit owner decision and verified offline/sync behavior.

For the tailored local path, SQLite on the same host minimizes administration at low write concurrency. PostgreSQL gives stronger concurrent service handling and declarative overlap enforcement, at higher setup and maintenance cost. SQLite’s official guidance favors client/server databases when many machines directly share the database file; a browser may connect to an application server on the LAN while that server alone opens the local SQLite file, which is a different topology. No direct file sharing should be proposed.

## Issues, fixes, versions, and applicability

- **LibreBooking version/security:** official release v7.0.0 (2026-10-06, commit `422b4b0`) is the first version the live README names as beyond the disclosed critical pre-7.0.0 administrator takeover. The README observed during this research also announced an additional high-impact security release for 2026-10-13. That announcement is not itself a published vulnerability detail or a judgment that 7.0.0 is compromised; it is a reason to recheck before any later adoption. This request does not install anything.
- **LibreBooking compound booking and conflicts:** at `422b4b0`, `ReservationRepository` writes primary/additional resources and accessory quantities under a reservation series. `ResourceAvailabilityRule` reads conflicting bookings/blackouts, offers a `skipconflicts` retry for a recurring series, and logs which occurrences it skips. Its tests cover multiple-resource conflict identification, buffers, blackout retrieval, and recurrence dates. `ReservationHandler` validates then calls persistence; repository insertion writes several rows. The inspected unit tests are not an interleaving/concurrent-transaction witness. Require a true simultaneous-submission test and inspect persistence transaction/DB guarantees before relying on it.
- **LibreBooking fault/blackout semantics:** `ManageBlackoutsService` takes a blackout title, resource IDs, repeat options, and a conflict-resolution strategy; it rejects a recurring blackout with no end date. At the same tag, `ReservationConflictResolutionTest` confirms two distinct choices: delete a conflicting reservation instance (and notify), or split a blackout around an existing reservation; a fully covered occurrence can be omitted. That confirms flexibility but exposes the unresolved workshop policy: “work around the booking” leaves a fault-period hole, while delete can erase the user-visible reservation. The reviewed API/service/test gives no proof that a fault reason is retained on a changed booking, so require a reasoned impact ledger or custom integration and verify retention.
- **LibreBooking recurrence history:** the open issue #1535 describes a shared recurrence-widget state bug when add and edit blackout dialogs coexist on `develop` commit `add7ebfbe`; it links a recent browser freeze fix (#1533) and a separate DST/termination-date issue (#1536). This is a development-branch report, not proof that the v7.0.0 release has those exact defects. It makes recurrent-blackout add/edit and DST-boundary tests important. A historical #1416 report against v3.0.3 documented failure to delete one recurring instance; it is not treated as a current-release defect.
- **MRBS version/fixes:** latest official release observed was 1.12.2 (tag/commit `38d51e0`, 2026-05-26). The release notes list two security fixes (unauthenticated open redirect in kiosk mode, SSRF in import) and a fix for editing a repeated series with the simultaneous-booking prevention setting enabled (#3965). MRBS says conflicting entries cannot be entered and supports repeating bookings, but this evidence is scoped to its one-room resource model; it does not establish atomic compound allocation or fault audit semantics.

## Decisions that remain open

- What interval granularity and minimum/buffer rules fit each tool, and whether setup/cleanup or inspection consumes additional time.
- Which tasks require a supervisor, what evidence makes a member/staff member qualified, whether bookings reserve a specific person or a role pool, and how shift coverage works.
- Whether any tool jobs require the extraction unit, whether tools are interchangeable, and whether an alternative is ever a valid substitute.
- Whether a new maintenance hold may overlap accepted reservations; who can declare an urgent fault; which member bookings can be displaced, by what priority/notice/appeal; whether staff can override and what reason is mandatory. Do not choose a priority rule for the workshop.
- Member self-registration vs staff-created local accounts, cancellation window, no-show/check-in policy, recurrence limits, waitlist and notification methods when email/Internet is unavailable.
- Which reservation fields are visible to members/staff, how long dispute reasons/history must be kept, and who can export it.
- Who owns the local host, backups/restores, patching and update checks; whether one workstation or LAN access is needed; and recovery steps if the Internet works but LAN/host does not.

## Validation proposal versus checks performed

**Proposed before operational adoption:** (a) with synthetic people/resources only, submit simultaneous bookings for the same tool, same supervisor, shared extractor, and overlapping multi-resource reservations, including both application-admin and member roles; prove exactly one valid winner per capacity unit and no partial allocations. (b) Race a member reservation against a staff hold and race two hold writes; verify hold authorization, blocking, readable conflict, and reason persistence. (c) Repeat hold/bookings across finite recurrence end dates, per-instance cancel/edit, timezone/DST transitions, all-conflict and partial-conflict conditions. (d) Test cancellation vs interruption vs displacement with full prior/after/actor/reason history, rebuild/readiness rendering, and least-privilege views. (e) Disconnect WAN but retain LAN; then separately stop host/LAN; confirm the service’s advertised behavior and paper queue/reconciliation without duplicate confirmed reservations. (f) backup, restore and update the exact pinned release in a synthetic environment; audit outbound Internet dependencies and access roles.

**Actually executed for this discovery:** brief-only requirements reading; public documentation/repository/release/issue source review; static reading of selected tagged PHP/source/test files. No application was installed, no downloaded source was executed, no live or synthetic booking was submitted, and no concurrency, outage, backup/restore, or browser test was run. All validations above are proposals.
