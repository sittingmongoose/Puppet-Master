# Shared workshop booking and equipment readiness

A neighborhood workshop currently uses a wall calendar and several spreadsheets to reserve equipment. Research a small software system for member bookings, staff maintenance holds and the daily readiness view. Members reserve time on a tool, while some jobs also require a trained supervisor and a shared extraction unit. Staff may discover a fault after bookings have already been accepted. The system should show an understandable conflict and preserve the reason when a booking changes.

Cover resource modeling, simultaneous booking attempts, recurring maintenance, canceled or interrupted reservations and practical operation when the workshop's internet connection is down. Staff can use one local workstation; member access may use a browser if that is justified. The membership count is about one hundred, and policy for urgent displacement is not yet settled. Do not assume calendar entries enforce capacity, allow a maintenance hold to disappear through member edits, charge payments, or collect identity documents. Avoid mandatory hosted accounts. Booking history needs enough explanation for staff to resolve disputes without becoming surveillance of workshop activity. Compare simple local and shared deployment approaches, identify unresolved policy decisions, and propose staged adoption plus concurrency and failure validation. No installation in the real workshop or live member data processing is requested.

Produce a research-backed software proposal, not an implementation. Explore relevant primary documentation, repository code, callers/tests, and issue-to-fix or release history where consequential. Compare feasible alternatives without assuming a winning product. Distinguish proposed validation from work actually executed. The exact draft plan is withheld during brief-only discovery and released once at the declared comparison point.
