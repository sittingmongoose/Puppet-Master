## Decision summary

Keep the local service and staff-first adoption direction. Make the service—not a wall calendar, spreadsheet, cached form, or ICS feed—the only authority that confirms availability. A booking should reserve a required set of resources atomically. Keep recurrence finite and visible, protect staff maintenance holds from member edits, preserve cancellation and fault decisions as minimal audit events, and fail closed when the current authoritative availability cannot be reached.

No product can be selected from desk research alone. Compare:

- A small local-first service with SQLite on one host for low setup and ISP-outage operation. SQLite allows many readers but one writer; a write transaction should be acquired before rechecking availability. WAL works only when its processes are on the same host, not over a network filesystem; if WAL is selected, require a SQLite runtime with the documented WAL-reset fix [S01–S02].
- A self-hosted LAN web service with PostgreSQL if concurrent browser submissions and multi-client access are required and someone can own database backups, upgrades, and restore. PostgreSQL exclusion constraints can enforce nonoverlapping time ranges per exclusive resource, using `btree_gist` for a resource key plus a range [S03].
- MRBS and LibreBooking as self-hosted configure-and-fit candidates. MRBS is more room/area oriented, supports configurable booking rules and custom/private fields under particular settings, and has a simultaneous-booking guard that is off by default [S17–S21]. LibreBooking has multi-resource reservations, separate accessory-quantity checks, recurring blackouts, and conflict-resolution choices, but it brings a PHP/Apache/MySQL-or-MariaDB stack [S05–S13]. The current develop snapshot advertises a high-impact security release planned for October 13, 2026; its recurrence date code also matches an open west-of-UTC defect report. Recheck its supported release and fixes after that security release and run a recurrence/time-zone pilot before considering it [S14–S18].
- Calendars may export/read availability, but cannot be the write authority: an event feed does not make all allocations atomic or protect a maintenance hold.

A browser route is justified only when it reaches the on-premises service on the workshop LAN and the workshop has agreed who can use it. ISP loss alone need not stop a local workstation/LAN service. A lost workstation, local network, or power still requires paper fallback. A hosted service or offline browser queue cannot promise a current confirmation during that failure.

## Coverage of the original brief

1. **Member booking, staff holds, daily readiness:** one booking/reservation model; staff-owned hold/fault records; distinct current readiness view.
2. **Tool + conditional supervisor + shared extractor:** resource allocations saved all-or-none; job-specific qualifications and extractor contention checked in the same transaction.
3. **Fault after acceptance, understandable conflict, reason preserved:** hold immediately, mark affected bookings for staff review, present resource/window/reason, record old/new state and notifications; no automatic delete.
4. **Resource modeling and simultaneous requests:** per-resource capacity and qualification; database/transaction invariant; stale read is not confirmation.
5. **Recurring maintenance:** finite local-time series, horizon aligned to booking lead time, exceptions/overrides, boundary and DST tests.
6. **Canceled or interrupted reservations:** status history, effective release of future allocations after cancellation/early checkout, reason; interrupted equipment remains unavailable until staff clear the fault. Exact cancellation/no-show rule is open.
7. **Internet outage, one workstation, possible browser, ~100 members:** one local host serves local work without ISP; optional LAN browser; no delayed offline writes; host/LAN/power outage uses dated paper log and printed readiness.
8. **Urgent displacement unsettled:** kept open; options below. No automated displacement is assumed.
9. **No calendar capacity enforcement, hold loss through member edits, payment, identity documents, mandatory hosted accounts:** service is authority; role-protected holds; explicit exclusions; on-prem/local accounts or minimal lookup should be assessed, not a hosted provider requirement.
10. **Dispute history without surveillance:** limited staff-only event history and purpose-limited export; retention/access decisions remain open.
11. **Local/shared deployment comparison:** SQLite local host, PostgreSQL LAN service, and self-hosted MRBS/LibreBooking compared; Internet-hosted option only as a conditional alternative.
12. **Staged adoption and validation:** staff shadow → small staff pilot → gated member LAN route; full proposed conflict, recurrence, outage, security, backup and restore checks below.

## Proposed domain and operating rules

- **Resource catalog:** tool or work area, extraction unit, schedulable supervisor/person, equipment state, quantity/capacity, operating hours, and allowed job classes. Qualification records should state who maintains them and when they expire. Use allocation rows attached to a single booking so the UI and storage share one bundle.
- **Booking:** reference, minimal member identity, created-by actor, interval, job class, state, required allocations, optional public-safe display label, and event history. Treat user cancellation as a status transition, not row deletion. “Interrupted” closes the remaining time only after staff records actual stop time; record the interruption reason category without creating a detailed activity dossier.
- **Maintenance/fault:** staff-only record with affected tool/resource, start/end, fault category, recurrence rule or one-time interval, protected status and creator. Member permission checks operate on a separate surface from holds. If a hold overlaps an accepted booking, preserve both records and require staff disposition.
- **Availability:** calculate current conflicts at commit time. Use half-open intervals; define setup/cleanup buffers explicitly and apply them consistently to tools, staff and extractor. In a multi-resource request, reserve all or none. A UI may preview, but only the final committed response is confirmation.
- **Conflict handling:** return a clear message such as “Extractor is reserved 14:00–15:00; no part of this booking was saved.” For a staff fault, list affected bookings and let authorized staff select move/cancel after contact. Append the explanation and result. Preserve historical values so later edits do not rewrite the dispute record.
- **Offline:** distinguish ISP-only loss from workshop LAN/host/power loss. During ISP loss, local LAN works if on-prem networking is healthy. During authority loss, show no new confirmation; use a numbered, timestamped paper log and latest printed readiness view. After recovery, enter items through the same conflict checks, mark them as offline-originated, retain their original time/reason, and resolve duplicate/stale requests manually.
- **Privacy/security:** no payments or identity documents; no usage score or member surveillance. Keep booking names visible only to authorized staff by default. Limit members to their own booking and non-identifying availability. Pick a minimal local lookup token and set a retention schedule only after owner input. Use named staff roles with least privilege, especially for fault holds, exports, and administrator configuration.
- **Operational ownership:** a named person owns updates, daily backup status, recovery instructions, and access removal. A second protected backup and a restore rehearsal are go-live criteria. Calendar exports remain optional read-only convenience.

## Decisions that need workshop owners

- Does a safety fault make equipment unavailable immediately even if that displaces an accepted reservation? Who can authorize a move/cancellation, and what member notification is required?
- What is the safe response if a fault is discovered during an in-use reservation?
- Which job classes require which currently certified supervisor? Can one person supervise more than one concurrent job?
- Which tools or extractor configurations permit paired/shared use, and what capacities apply?
- How early may members book? Set recurring-hold horizon at least as far as that limit plus maintenance lead time.
- What are cancellation deadlines, no-show behavior, interruption/check-out semantics, and member appeal process?
- What minimum local member identifier is acceptable? Which booking fields are visible to members versus staff?
- How long should event history be retained, who may export it, and who approves exceptional disclosure?
- Does outage planning cover only ISP loss or also local network, host, and power failure? Who owns paper-log reconciliation and restore?
- Will members have locally managed accounts, staff-entered reservations, or a shared LAN form? Do not require a hosted account provider without a separate decision.

For urgent displacement, two policy shapes can be presented for decision: (A) “hold the affected equipment and keep accepted booking records unchanged while staff contact each member and arrange a move/cancel”; (B) “a designated safety lead can override an accepted allocation after documenting the fault, affected bookings, member contact, and reason.” Neither policy is selected here.

## Staged adoption and proposed validation

1. **Process and policy map:** inventory tools, extractor, supervisors and qualifications; define local timezone/hours, buffers, booking lead-time, roles, cancellation, urgency, privacy, and outage scope. Use synthetic names/IDs only.
2. **Fit comparison:** configure a disposable local MRBS candidate, evaluate LibreBooking only after its announced security release and recurrence status are rechecked, and compare each against a minimal local-first design. Verify resource bundles, protected holds, explanations, role controls, recurrence and support/update effort. Do not decide by feature checklist alone.
3. **Staff shadow:** no member writes; compare generated availability/readiness to the existing calendar and maintenance process. Resolve mismatches while the old process remains authoritative.
4. **Small pilot:** synthetic or staff-controlled test records, then a limited set of tools after owner approval. Add maintenance recurrence, interruption and fault workflow. Demonstrate backup restore and paper reconciliation.
5. **Member route gate:** enable LAN member booking only after concurrency, role, privacy, outage, restore, and issue-triage checks pass. Keep the staff view and printed fallback.

**Proposed checks — not run:**

- Use two independent clients to request the same exclusive tool, supervisor, or extractor window at once. Repeat with overlapping different tools sharing one extractor and with a multi-resource bundle. Assert at most one valid allocation, complete rollback for rejected bundles, clear conflicting-resource output, and no double entry after client retry.
- Check adjacent end/start boundaries and configured setup/cleanup buffers. Try to submit from a stale page after a competing hold or booking has committed.
- Confirm a member can cancel only eligible own future bookings, cannot modify another member’s reservation, and cannot create/edit/delete/evade a maintenance hold by changing a resource or recurrence. Confirm staff edits preserve explicit occurrence scope.
- Exercise recurring maintenance at the exact horizon rollover, with an excluded date, a one-instance edit, future-series edit, series edit, and a fall/spring DST transition in the workshop timezone. Also test browsers west of UTC; the current LibreBooking branch needs this check [S17–S18].
- Accept a booking, record a new equipment fault, and verify immediate readiness change, visible affected list, no automatic deletion, preserved reason/old state, and correct member notification; test each authorized staff disposition.
- Cancel, finish early, mark interrupted, and record no-show cases. Check which future allocations are released and whether active fault/hold state remains.
- Remove ISP while local service is up; verify the local route continues and remote route (if investigated) is unavailable. Then remove LAN/host or simulate power loss; confirm the interface blocks confirmation, a local draft is labeled unsubmitted, paper procedure works, and re-entry after recovery flags conflicts instead of silently overwriting.
- Simulate process restart, database unavailable/locked, disk-full/write failure, duplicate submit, backup restore, second-copy loss, and recovery of a booked/held resource. Verify last-refresh labeling and staff escalation.
- Review every form, log and export with synthetic records for unnecessary identity, history exposure, payment or document collection; test staff-only name visibility, purpose-limited export, retention, and deletion.
