# Oral-history intake and transcript handoff

A small museum has an existing collection catalog and wants an intake component for donated oral-history recordings. Volunteers receive audio files, a transcript when one exists, and a spreadsheet of recording identifiers and permissions. File extensions are inconsistent, some recordings have two channels with different speakers, and a corrected transcript can arrive after initial intake. Research an intake design that preserves original media and gives catalog staff a previewable, accessible record of what was accepted or rejected.

Cover format identification, metadata and duration extraction, channel handling, transcript-to-record association, safe batch restart, and portable handoff to the catalog. A warning must distinguish unreadable media from unsupported preview, and an absent transcript must not look like failed audio. Staff should be able to operate the review flow by keyboard and obtain meaningful textual status. A batch is usually forty recordings but can reach five hundred; no particular codec or catalog API is fixed. Do not alter original bytes, guess missing usage permissions, send recordings to cloud transcription, or require transcoding every item before it can be cataloged. Compare feasible approaches and explain where format support ends. The deliverable is a research proposal with staged integration and validation, not a migration of the museum's actual collection.

Produce a research-backed software proposal, not an implementation. Explore relevant primary documentation, repository code, callers/tests, and issue-to-fix or release history where consequential. Compare feasible alternatives without assuming a winning product. Distinguish proposed validation from work actually executed. The exact draft plan is withheld during brief-only discovery and released once at the declared comparison point.

Complete all substantive research and planning obligations within a maximum of 60 elapsed minutes. Finish earlier when the complete output is ready. There is no minimum research duration, no requirement to consume the allowance, and no credit for waiting.
