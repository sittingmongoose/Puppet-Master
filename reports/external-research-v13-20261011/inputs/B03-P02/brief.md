# Portable project archive export

An existing desktop project tracker needs an export component for people who leave a team or keep long-term records. A project contains tasks, comments, dates, custom fields and attached files. Some tasks link to other projects that the exporter cannot access. Users want a portable archive they can inspect without the original application and a structured representation a future importer could use.

Research snapshot consistency, stable identifiers, attachment packaging, text and date representation, schema evolution and safe verification of a completed export. The export should explain omissions rather than implying every link or permission has been preserved. Users may export fifty tasks or fifty thousand; attachments can exceed available temporary disk space. A canceled export must leave the source intact and make incomplete output distinguishable from a finished archive. Do not mutate source records, include inaccessible project content, invent missing attachment bytes, or require internet access to read the human-readable archive. Do not claim round-trip fidelity without defining what is retained. Compare package and streaming approaches and describe reasonable boundaries for a future importer. The requested deliverable is a research-backed design and validation plan; it does not authorize exporting any actual team's records.

Produce a research-backed software proposal, not an implementation. Explore relevant primary documentation, repository code, callers/tests, and issue-to-fix or release history where consequential. Compare feasible alternatives without assuming a winning product. Distinguish proposed validation from work actually executed. The exact draft plan is withheld during brief-only discovery and released once at the declared comparison point.

Complete all substantive research and planning obligations within a maximum of 60 elapsed minutes. Finish earlier when the complete output is ready. There is no minimum research duration, no requirement to consume the allowance, and no credit for waiting. The deadline includes arm-specific setup, provider queueing after admission, tools, native retries, handoffs, revision and delivery. Output quality is assessed independently of elapsed time.
