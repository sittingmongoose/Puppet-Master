## Finding F-1 — pixel-coordinate export is not RFC 7946 GeoJSON

**Affected claims:** revision §4.3, especially “GeoJSON may be offered as a secondary 2D interchange export using `[x,y]` pixel coordinates” and the proposed property saying those are image pixels, not geographic coordinates. The caveat that downstream consumers may differ does not make those coordinates conform to GeoJSON’s coordinate semantics.

**Evidence:** RFC 7946 §3.1.1 defines a position’s first two values as longitude/latitude (or easting/northing); §4 fixes GeoJSON coordinates to WGS 84 / CRS84 with decimal-degree units and explains that alternative CRS support was removed for interoperability. §§6.1, 7.1, and 8 say foreign members have no normative processing model, cannot change GeoJSON member semantics, and a format that changes those semantics must not be identified as GeoJSON. Primary source: [RFC 7946 text](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7946.txt), August 2016; complete public response captured 2026-10-06, SHA-256 `cb52f07b9cc6c841750d75850988fe8f32c7b7f0e1f7420f2069d5b5ded124f8`, capture `f940cb33d11144b3a17ea652f7554a9d`. QuPath’s own export guide is an instructive limit, not a GeoJSON exception: it explicitly warns that its pixel-unit/top-left-origin shape coordinates may differ from what other software expects (QuPath 0.7.0 capture `0030.body`, SHA-256 `df12ec58ce64a72dea1596677e444ade4684b12328555ed36fda1175c341f420`, §§125–140).

**Correction:** retain CSV as the canonical pixel-coordinate table. Either export a clearly named project-specific JSON/pixel-geometry format, or explicitly label the output “GeoJSON-shaped, non-RFC-7946” and do not promise ordinary GeoJSON interoperability. Call it RFC 7946 GeoJSON only when coordinates are transformed to the required geographic CRS with a defensible georeferencing transform; an image-pixel property alone cannot supply that transform. Put the format-conformance status and coordinate semantics in the manifest and UI. This changes the §4.3 export choice and any format claims in the manifest, but not CSV or raster overlay plans.

**Proposed check — UNEXECUTED:** submit a pixel-coordinate example and a properly georeferenced example to a standards-aware GeoJSON validator/consumer; verify that the former is rejected or treated as geographic coordinates, and that the latter carries the stated CRS84 coordinate values. This checks interop labeling, not the application exporter.

## Finding F-2 — constrain the new affine contract across mixed axes

**Affected claims:** revision §§4.1–4.2, 4.3 and §7 validation. The revision defines `M_array_to_world` over “array_axis_indices” for the represented axes and permits cross-axis terms, while annotation geometry is separately ordered `[x,y]`/`[x,y,z]` and time/channel are selectors or channel scope. For an array such as `t,c,z,y,x`, the text does not yet state whether the affine includes categorical channel indices, which axes may be mixed, or how fixed selectors are assembled with XY/XYZ coordinates before applying the matrix. A matrix can encode numbers here, but that alone does not make mixing channel, time, and spatial units meaningful. This is a contract ambiguity inferred from the revision and the brief, not a claim about NGFF or a cited application.

**Correction:** define the affine domain explicitly. A small, testable choice is to apply the spatial affine only to named spatial axes in declared order; keep channel/custom axes as discrete selectors; represent time separately with its own unit-aware mapping if required. Specify the adapter that inserts annotation XY/XYZ and fixed z/t/channel selectors into stored array order, and reject cross-terms between unlike axis roles. Preserve the useful one-matrix authority for each valid spatial frame, the sample-center convention, and separate view transforms.

**Proposed check — UNEXECUTED:** use a `t,c,z,y,x` fixture with non-square spatial spacing, nonzero spatial origin, multiple channels/timepoints, and a rotated spatial mapping. Round-trip a plane annotation through array-order mapping, physical coordinates, and CSV; assert that changing only c/t selectors cannot move the spatial ROI. This is needed before the revision’s W-R01 arithmetic example can support the mixed-axis application contract.
