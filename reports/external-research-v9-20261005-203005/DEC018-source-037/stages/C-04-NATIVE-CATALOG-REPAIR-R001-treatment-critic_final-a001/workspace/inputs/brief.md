# Confirmation brief H2 — offline field observations and map exchange

Design a local-first fieldwork application for a small environmental survey group recording observations while disconnected. The group works on laptops and tablets and exchanges project bundles after returning from a survey. They need a trustworthy way to place observations on a map, preserve edits and source context, and reconcile two copies without silently losing work. This is a research field tool, not emergency navigation or a cloud account service. Deliver an implementable proposal, not a complete mapping product.

A project imports a bounded map area and a local observation table, then records points, short tracks, notes and optional photographs. A typical session loads the project, inspects coordinate/reference metadata, searches and previews existing records, adds or edits an observation offline, saves, reopens on another machine, combines a colleague’s changed project copy, and exports a table plus a map overlay. Originals remain intact. Coordinate systems, axis order, angular/linear units, altitude and time provenance can differ among imports. Unknown or ambiguous metadata must remain visible and require a bounded correction/import choice rather than an undocumented guess.

Define the relationships among stored observation coordinates, displayed map coordinates, geometry/edit identity and exported coordinates. Explain the actual support boundary for reference systems and geometry types, and how transformations are versioned and validated. A displayed pin is not proof that exported data retained its correct location or attribution. Consider observations close to a coordinate/domain boundary, tracks spanning a discontinuity, incomplete coordinates and conflicting identifiers as cases to investigate; do not invent universal behavior for them. Previewing a sample of records cannot establish that an entire import is valid.

Offline editing is a minimum requirement. Explain project/data identity, record revisions, append/edit/delete semantics, duplicate detection, conflict presentation and merge/recovery behavior when two project copies diverge. A practical explicit conflict choice is acceptable; an unsupported promise of automatic perfect merge is not. After a interrupted save, import or merge, the product should reopen into a recoverable state and distinguish incomplete work from committed records. Re-importing the same source and relocating project media need defined behavior. Do not hide failed records or discard provenance to make a merge appear successful.

The first version runs on a 4-core machine with 8 GB RAM and should inspect a project with 250,000 observations through bounded spatial/table access. This is an engineering target to research, not a measured benchmark. Plan indexing/cache invalidation, map/photograph storage, viewport access, cancellation, large export handling and what can remain usable without a network. Bundled map/source-data permissions and licenses must be investigated for the actual selected source; do not assume offline availability follows from a web viewer. No proprietary licensed data, private accounts or paid service purchase is provided.

Users need legible coordinate/provenance and error feedback, accessible record editing, keyboard navigation where appropriate, an obvious offline state, undo or reversible edits, and inspectable conflicts. Real-time collaborative synchronization, route guidance, a hosted service, broad map-source support and automated classification can be useful later opportunities. Keep optional adoption distinct from minimum requirements, and preserve worthwhile leads without pretending all must ship.

Start from this brief alone and discover public primary sources yourself. Investigate at least two independently useful implementation precedents with source pins and concrete lessons. Follow at least one real public issue/failure through associated fix and regression test and assess release/branch applicability. Components for spatial storage, projection, local revision/merge or map rendering can be precedents if their mechanism actually fits; no repository is prescribed. An issue title or a passing isolated test does not prove whole-product correctness.

Deliver one standalone current proposal with the workflow, data/coordinate/revision contract, coherent architecture or bounded alternatives, critical dependencies, support/resource/recovery limits and a justified validation plan. Include at least one useful opportunity and one plausible alternative. Separate external facts, engineering inferences, product choices and uncertainty; use exact pins/locators. If tiny synthetic transform/index/merge examples are executed, author them on the affordable candidate model inside the admitted isolation and state their scope. Otherwise label checks proposed/UNEXECUTED. Do not contact maintainers, write to external projects or inspect evaluator material.
