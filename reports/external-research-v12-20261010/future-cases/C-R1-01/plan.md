## Project situation

Rooms were rearranged last winter; nobody kept a reliable before-and-after log. We can borrow a recorder and a basic sound meter for a later pilot, and we can temporarily move freestanding panels. The building manager wants a defensible investigation before spending money on more treatment. These are hypothetical project facts, not measurements.

## Meaningful useful alternatives

Investigate quieter source practice or time coordination, moving equipment away from possible coupling points, reversible room-layout changes, and targeted isolation or absorption concepts. Each has a useful role only under particular conditions. A documented complaint-and-session log may itself be the appropriate first deliverable if events cannot be attributed. ## Mechanism opportunity

Seek a causal account connecting excitation, transmission, receiver location, frequency, and perception. Differentiate reducing room reverberation from changing energy transmitted to another space. Possible structural coupling and airborne leakage are research hypotheses, not established properties of this building. Ask which measurements or interventions would discriminate between them and where a simple model would cease to be credible.
