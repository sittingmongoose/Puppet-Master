# Uncertain provenance after an oral-history archive migration

Case ID: ER12-D-R1-03-FRESH
Slot ID: D-R1-03
Domain: digital preservation

## Project situation

A local oral-history archive moved audio files and transcripts from an older shared drive to a new storage arrangement. Staff notice that some transcripts refer to file names that no longer exist, and some recordings have two apparently different copies. A volunteer remembers that files were renamed in batches, but no full migration log has been found. The archive wants a research proposal for a bounded provenance investigation before any cleanup. The archive scenario is synthetic; no actual files or personal records are supplied.

## Request

Use a 60-minute research session to develop a proposal for studying file continuity, recording identity, and transcript relationships across the migration. Compare useful strategies, explain what each mechanism establishes and leaves unresolved, and investigate implementation and preservation history. Give a reversible pilot plan with clear evidence limits, not an archive-management tool or a complete migration repair.

## Requirements to preserve

- Separate bit-level continuity, identity of the recorded interview, completeness, and the correctness of transcript-to-recording links.
- Compare manifest or fixity approaches with metadata and content-based relationship investigation and a useful manual sampling alternative.
- Address renamed files, transcoded versions, partial recordings, and ambiguous duplicates without assuming equal names or unequal hashes settle identity.
- Include migration and comparable implementation history, specifying which missing records would change the evidential interpretation.
- Propose a reversible pilot, independent checks of sampled relationships, provenance recording, and handling of unresolved items; distinguish proposed validation from any checks actually executed.

## Boundaries

- Do not delete, merge, rename, transcode, publish, or upload archive material.
- Do not infer consent, authenticity, or completeness from a checksum match or a plausible transcript.
- Do not access personal interviews, contact interviewees, create accounts, or invent recovered migration records.

## Still unresolved

The old-drive availability, formats, naming rules, transcript origin, consent restrictions, file counts, baseline checksums, and migration tools are unknown. We have not said whether both apparent copies are preservation originals, access derivatives, or incomplete exports. Keep these questions unresolved until evidence supports a conclusion.

## Research question and incompleteness

How can a bounded and non-destructive provenance study determine which continuity and relationship claims are supportable when the original migration record is incomplete?

Availability of pre-migration evidence, scope of allowable content access, the meaning of a complete interview, and staffing for manual adjudication remain open. The proposal should offer a useful evidence inventory and next decision even if final relationship repair is not yet justified.

## Meaningful useful alternatives

Consider establishing present-state manifests, comparing available prior inventories, tracing identifiers and metadata, investigating derivative relationships, and carefully scoped manual sample review. Quarantining unresolved relationships in an inventory without modifying files is a useful outcome. Restoring an older naming convention may be unnecessary or harmful; retaining current names with a documented mapping is another possibility to investigate. No recovery or matching route is designated correct.

## Mechanism opportunity

Explain what hashes can demonstrate when an appropriate prior baseline exists, what they cannot establish when it does not, and how transcodes or partial exports complicate comparisons. Study the relationship between file identity, recorded event identity, transcript coverage, and provenance. Content similarity can suggest relationships but needs validation and permissions; it is not automatic evidence of completeness or authenticity.

## Implementation and history opportunity

Investigate migration procedures, tool versions, batch rename rules, access-derivative practices, and public preservation implementation reports. A history that documents checks before and after a move offers different evidence from a retrospectively generated inventory. Recovering staff notes or prior manifests is a later opportunity; none is supplied as an answer. Compare failure handling, rollback, and unresolved-item reporting in relevant implementations.

## Proposed versus executed validation

A future pilot could operate on an authorized read-only copy or permitted metadata inventory, preserve original references, and test proposed relationships on a sample with independent adjudication. Record uncertainty and contradictory evidence, including how missing baselines restrict conclusions. Synthetic manifests or toy hash examples actually produced in the research hour must be labeled synthetic; they do not validate archive continuity, interview identity, or transcript correctness. Any later content access requires the archive's permissions.
