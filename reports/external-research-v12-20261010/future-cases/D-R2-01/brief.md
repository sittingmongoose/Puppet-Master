# Uneven leaf symptoms in a teaching greenhouse

Case ID: ER12-D-R2-01-FRESH
Slot ID: D-R2-01
Domain: controlled-environment horticulture

## Project situation

A teaching greenhouse has a bench of potted herbs with pale lower leaves and occasional visible droplets on some mornings. The irrigation timer and the circulation-fan schedule were both changed before the symptoms were noticed. Plants closest to the door seem less affected, but the observations were informal. The instructor wants a research proposal before changing the teaching crop protocol. This is a hypothetical setting with no diagnostic samples, environmental logs, or treatment results.

## Request

Use a full 60-minute research window to propose an investigation of the competing environmental and crop-management explanations. Explain plausible mechanisms, compare useful non-chemical alternatives, and investigate operating or implementation history that could affect transfer of evidence. Design a later observational or reversible pilot and identify when specialist diagnosis would be needed. We need a research proposal, not instructions to treat the plants today.

## Requirements to preserve

- Separate visible leaf symptoms, plant growth, water use, and the possibility of a condition requiring diagnosis; symptom reduction alone does not identify a cause.
- Compare irrigation or substrate-management inquiry with ventilation or microclimate inquiry and a useful observation-first alternative.
- Account for plant position, watering distribution, morning conditions, crop age, and the simultaneous schedule changes without assuming droplets prove one explanation.
- Include greenhouse operating history and comparable implementation evidence, stating differences in crop, substrate, climate, and controls.
- Propose an interpretable baseline, observation plan, limited comparison, and escalation criteria; distinguish proposed crop validation from any literature or desk checks actually executed.

## Boundaries

- Do not diagnose a pathogen or deficiency, prescribe pesticides or fertilizer doses, or conduct a crop intervention in this assignment.
- Do not claim food safety, disease prevention, or yield improvement from the anecdotal pattern.
- Do not invent humidity, moisture, temperature, symptom prevalence, or measured treatment response.
