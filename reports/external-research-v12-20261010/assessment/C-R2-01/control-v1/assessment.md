## Remaining material finding

### M1 — Required reversibility comparison is asserted covered but absent from the final decision study

The exact original [plan](ER12_RUNTIME/frozen-inputs/C-R2-01/plan.md:59) says: **“Compare reversibility and annotation preservation as well as apparent adhesion.”** The final [plan register](ER12_RUNTIME/runs/C-R2-01/control/stages/reviser/final.md:107) says this is covered. The final names replaceability and whether mechanical retention is workable as owner choices, and cites NARA’s permanent/difficult-to-remove adhesive. Its prospective measurements/gates nevertheless contain only dimensions, bond failure, readability, identity and contact risk. It supplies neither a comparison of reversibility nor an explicit prospective reversibility question/assessment for the candidate and retain-current/nonadhesive options. Reversibility appears as a claimed covered gate only in the clause register. A bond peel measurement and spontaneous lift/ooze recording do not themselves compare whether a label/retention method can be undone while preserving the sleeve and original information.

This is an explicit original plan criterion for choosing among attachment approaches, not an invented requirement to use a reversible adhesive or remove collection labels. NARA’s specification values permanent attachment whereas the NMS guidance distinguishes secure attachment from reversibility. An option can meet the final’s stated bond/legibility gates while leaving that requested tradeoff unassessed. The decision study therefore cannot support its claim that all exact plan clauses are covered. The independently retrieved [NARA laser specification](https://www.archives.gov/files/preservation/storage/pdf/label-stock-laser-printer.pdf) and [thermal-transfer specification](https://www.archives.gov/files/preservation/storage/pdf/label-stock-thermal-transfer.pdf) explicitly seek permanent, difficult-to-remove adhesion, while [National Museums Scotland’s own guidance](https://www.nms.ac.uk/our-impact/national-work/training-and-guidance-for-museums/collections-care/object-labelling) treats security, reversibility, safety and visibility as distinct considerations. These sources demonstrate the tradeoff; they are not imposed as universal sleeve requirements. Local evidence locators: E03/E04 p.1 and E09.txt lines 13–23, 42–45.

The owner may still decide whether replacement/removability is required, and no universal reversibility threshold or performance test is required now. The missing element is the requested research comparison and how that unresolved requirement bears on choosing an approach. Merely leaving replaceability open does not perform that comparison.

The investigator discovered removable loop/tag/insert/secondary-enclosure options and a separate mock-up with visibility, retrieval, snagging, handling and misassociation checks. The final retains an environmental alternative well, but compresses mechanical retention to a tag citation and claimed mock-up in the register; it does not preserve the substantive alternative evaluation. The earlier draft itself only weakly operationalized reversibility, so this is both an unresolved plan omission and a loss of supported alternative substance. The [draft](ER12_RUNTIME/runs/C-R2-01/control/stages/investigator/draft.md:37) gives a concrete nonadhesive comparison and the [coupon study](ER12_RUNTIME/runs/C-R2-01/control/stages/investigator/draft.md:72) retains a separate usability mock-up. The final has a source citation and register assertion, with no corresponding substantive final comparison. The retain-current monitoring alternative is substantive and does satisfy the brief’s at-least-one-alternative minimum; this finding does not falsely mark Q2 wholly absent.

The materiality conclusion is an independent reviewer inference from the exact plan, the complete final and the primary-source distinctions; no actual sleeve or product failure is claimed.
