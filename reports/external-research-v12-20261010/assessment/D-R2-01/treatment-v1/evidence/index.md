## Independently retrieved governing primary sources

### E-S01: Guttation: A Pressure Relief for Plants

[2009-06-01 publication; 2015-09-30 revision shown in footer](https://ipm.missouri.edu/meg/2009/6/Guttation-A-Pressure-Relief-for-Plants/index.cfm) — Official extension full text.

[Preserved independent source text](primary/S01.txt) (first governing locator L27; SHA-256 `68e2a00d1e28849ffee58dd4c51bf2d68319540f981c11129ec39a768369405a`). Hydathodes at margins/tips can exude water under root pressure, especially when transpiration is low. This mechanism differs from dew but appearance alone is inconclusive. Unspecified herbs and no leaf/air measurements: a physiological hypothesis, never a case diagnosis. Rare disease associations in the article are not inferred for this bench.

### E-S02: Controlling the Greenhouse Environment

[HGA-00336; retrieved PDF filename 2024-11-27](https://www.uaf.edu/ces/publications/database/gardening/files/pdfs/HGA-00336-Controlling%20GreenHouse%2011-27-24.pdf) — Official extension full PDF.

[Preserved independent source text](primary/S02.txt) (first governing locator L391; SHA-256 `8b41f731786d931ac76c10a66637b0597d6cbb69dd6d33ab5426e4c971e88c53`). Internal horizontal airflow mixes air and canopy conditions; ventilation exchanges greenhouse air. Condensation and humidity depend on the operating environment. Alaska greenhouse guidance and numerical equipment advice are not herb settings. The changed fan may be circulation, exhaust or cooling; its actual role must be established.

### E-S03: Greenhouse Ventilation

[AE-10/AE030; DOI edition 10.32473/edis-ae030-2019](https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/AE030/pdf) — Official extension full PDF.

[Preserved independent source text](primary/S03.txt) (first governing locator L17; SHA-256 `075b2c0062dfd130f793c72caa4fb70bffb1fd189ed382a4c97fda480d130606`). Condensation is consistent with a surface below air dew point. Air exchange can remove moisture while increasing winter heating demand. Weather, greenhouse, heat and crop assumptions govern rates. No fan setting or promised humidity response is imported into the final.

### E-S04: Guidelines for measuring and reporting environmental parameters for experiments in greenhouses

[Plant Methods 11:43 (2015); DOI 10.1186/s13007-015-0083-5](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13007-015-0083-5) — Full primary article XML via Europe PMC.

[Preserved independent source text](primary/S04-epmc.txt) (first governing locator L19; SHA-256 `b4236914fad4f65367794710e2e8064072885b00fbcacc17ad0ee3d2bba0128d`). Reproducible greenhouse measurements require sensor principle, placement, calibration and spatial/temporal context. Canopy measurements independent of control sensors and reported water-delivery variation support the proposed baseline. Research-reporting guidance, with effort matched to the investigation; it does not prescribe crop operating standards or identify this symptom cause.

### E-S05: Measuring the Sprinkler Irrigation Requirement of Container-Grown Nursery Plants

[ENH1197/EP458; exact revision date not displayed in retrieved body](https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/EP458) — Official extension full web text.

[Preserved independent source text](primary/S05.txt) (first governing locator L20; SHA-256 `b3784d4031b9e83f405166cb30091c780ec1dc462d8f661a9db373fcf806f77f`). Container water requirement depends on evapotranspiration, canopy capture and distribution uniformity. Repeat weighing can estimate water loss under the guide's stated drainage and timing conditions. Sprinkler nursery context; water delivered is distinct from water retained or consumed. The final uses representative pot mass as a conditional measurement option, not a diagnostic threshold.

### E-S06: Field Evaluation of Container Nursery Irrigation Systems: Measuring Uniformity of Water Application of Sprinkler Systems

[FS98-2/AE194; exact revision date not displayed in retrieved body](https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/AE194) — Official extension full web text.

[Preserved independent source text](primary/S06.txt) (first governing locator L1; SHA-256 `25e9d5d3c85b2f16d1f9de1683e667730624f11f869947a39fe957542badb02b`). Catch-can distribution uniformity evaluates spatial sprinkler application; pressure, nozzle, overlap, clogging and wind affect it. An overhead-sprinkler audit is conditional on delivery method. Numerical nursery acceptability thresholds are not adopted as this herb bench's mandatory standards.

### E-S07: Irrigation System Check-ups

[University of Maryland; updated 2021-03-30](https://www.extension.umd.edu/resource/irrigation-system-check-ups) — Official extension full web text.

[Preserved independent source text](primary/S07.txt) (first governing locator L54; SHA-256 `b6f1ca874376a75fc1257e8a7cb94f0de7418419438856202348486d75c2899f`). Inspect operating pressure and emitter consistency; apparently thorough watering can leave dry root balls. Map collected output during an irrigation audit. The final preserves inquiry into actual delivery and wetting. It does not implement the page's repair, cyclic-watering or fertilizer recommendations today.

### E-S08: The Use of Soil Moisture Probes for Improved Uniformity and Irrigation Control in Greenhouses

[Acta Horticulturae 893 (2011), article 119; DOI 10.17660/ActaHortic.2011.893.119](https://doi.org/10.17660/ActaHortic.2011.893.119) — Official ISHS primary abstract.

[Preserved independent source text](primary/S08.txt) (first governing locator L20; SHA-256 `1b5e9fcbc8929da8d14e46580ff3b9094927211b32c910caa9ebe4451409c5d7`). Soil/substrate moisture sensing for irrigation depends on water distribution, root uptake, substrate properties and placement; a sensor may not represent a larger heterogeneous root zone. Abstract supports an optional sensing approach, not a universal threshold, number of probes, automatic saving or validation on unspecified herbs.

### E-S09: Implementation of Sensor-based Automated Irrigation in Commercial Floriculture Production: A Case Study

[HortTechnology 28(6), 2018; DOI 10.21273/HORTTECH04114-18](https://doi.org/10.21273/HORTTECH04114-18) — Full primary published article reprint.

[Preserved independent source text](primary/S09.txt) (first governing locator L107; SHA-256 `16c553cd5cf5443a93ffac0dcdd6b41be427f2c6039b1b41027926d35953417b`). In 2014–2015 Georgia greenhouse geranium/poinsettia trials, grower-controlled sensor irrigation did not reduce water use against historical timer practices. Staff changed thresholds; labor reallocation and support mattered. Peat-perlite, ornamental crops, seasonal trials and grower control differ from this case. Water comparisons were not uniformly replicated. The final reports study history, not a general causal saving estimate.

### E-S10: Implementation of Soil Moisture Sensor Based Automated Irrigation in Woody Ornamental Production

[Journal of Environmental Horticulture 38(1):1–7, 2020; DOI 10.24266/0738-2898-38.1.1](https://doi.org/10.24266/0738-2898-38.1.1) — Full official primary published PDF.

[Preserved independent source text](primary/S10.txt) (first governing locator L7; SHA-256 `6ecf879b045bab0a92421187fc28865ce9e32b3e2d31b1236c489b29603fa3d5`). The woody-nursery trial reported approximately half the irrigation volume and severe 2015 Rhododendron loss. Authors emphasized crop-specific drought/capture and averaged thresholds. Reported EC did not exceed 2.0 mS/cm, which they considered generally insufficient for damage. Four woody taxa in composted pine bark with overhead irrigation, shade and Georgia weather. Salt discussion does not establish a cause of losses. Final qualification is independently supported by the full PDF, despite the reviser's fresh-access failure.

### E-S11: Subirrigation for Greenhouse Crops

[UMass subirrigation factsheet; publication/update date not shown, footer copyright is not an edition date](https://www.umass.edu/agriculture-food-environment/greenhouse-floriculture/fact-sheets/subirrigation-for-greenhouse-crops) — Official extension full web text.

### E-CR02: How to Avoid Common Problems with Leaf Wetness Sensor Installation and Maintenance

[IEEE Sensors Journal 24(5):7009–7017 (2024); DOI 10.1109/JSEN.2023.3343574; online 2024-01-04, issue 2024-03-01](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10381613) — Primary IEEE publisher abstract via independent web retrieval; Crossref identity metadata also retained.

### E-CR04: Partial saturation under ebb and flow irrigation suppresses Pythium root rot of ornamentals

[Crop Protection 33:29–33 (March 2012); DOI 10.1016/j.cropro.2011.10.010](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0261219411003371) — Primary ScienceDirect publisher abstract and introductory context.

### E-CR05: Phytophthora nicotianae transmission and growth of potted kalanchoe in two recirculating subirrigation systems

[Scientia Horticulturae 119(1):75–78 (2008-12-10); DOI 10.1016/j.scienta.2008.07.026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304423808002793) — Primary ScienceDirect publisher abstract and record.

Nutrient-flow wick versus ebb-and-flow, named host/pathogen and inoculum route. Supports conditional contamination risk and exceptions, not universal spread or a herb diagnosis.
