## Material finding F1 — signed claims versus job-resistant provenance accuracy

Candidate exact locator: `discovery.md:9`, corroborating tradeoff row `:19` and prospective tests `:35–36`.

> Inference: this directly addresses a job claiming the right commit while using another builder or changing build parameters.

The surrounding entry describes artifact digest binding, signature/predicate validation and source/builder/buildType/externalParameters equality. It gives dependency incompleteness and compromise of a trusted platform as failure boundaries. It does **not** expose the separate boundary between a signed expected claim and provenance that a compromised user-controlled build job cannot forge or alter, nor the trusted builder's maximum Build-level mapping.

[SLSA v1.2 verification](https://slsa.dev/spec/v1.2/verifying-artifacts) makes Step 1 a Build-level/root-of-trust check; its mitigation discussion assigns protection against compromise of build execution/provenance generation to L3 while excluding compromise of the trusted platform itself ([S1 numbered evidence](primary-evidence/S1.txt), lines 118–136). [Build Track Basics](https://slsa.dev/spec/v1.2/build-track-basics) distinguishes L2 post-build tampering protection from L3 protection during builds ([P9](primary-evidence/P9.txt), lines 106–165). [Producing-artifact requirements](https://slsa.dev/spec/v1.2/build-requirements) distinguishes authenticity, accuracy and completeness: L2 control-plane provenance can include tenant-generated optional dependency fields and has no minimum anti-tamper strength; L3 adds strong resistance to tenant forgery and keeps signing secrets outside user build steps, with explicit exceptions and best-effort dependency completeness ([P10](primary-evidence/P10.txt), lines 179–238).

This omission matters specifically because the brief posits a job lying about its build. An honest accepted platform with weaker job controls, or tenant-controlled provenance fields, is a different case from compromise of the platform itself. A matching signature/digest and apparently correct recorded fields do not alone establish that the job used the claimed inputs. The candidate's visible-mismatch tests discriminate source/builder/parameter expectations, not the origin and accuracy of apparently compliant claims. Its statement that signatures do not establish product-policy compliance is useful, but semantic equality against fabricated claims does not close this accuracy gap.

This is a supported missing failure boundary and overbroad applicability inference, not a demand for a deployment, a certification program, or adoption of one mandatory SLSA level. No finding claims L3 guarantees complete dependencies, hermetic builds, safe producer behavior or external runner integrity. The accurate digest/signature/predicate/expectation checks, dependency caveat, trusted-platform exclusion and complementary rebuild option remain preserved. No candidate wording was repaired and no feedback was sent.
