# Discovery record — ER12-C-R2-02-FRESH

## Purpose and scope
Propose a reproducible pilot for improving discovery of local observational studies and municipal reports on urban-heat interventions while preserving each record, report, and underlying study’s identity. This is a discovery-method study, not a systematic review, not a judgment on whether interventions work, and not an estimate of recall against a complete corpus.

## Main finding: keep three units and three outcomes separate
A retrieved record is a database or portal representation; a report is a document/version; a study is the underlying investigation. A study may have a journal paper and several municipal reports, while a municipal report can describe multiple studies. Preserve each retrieved row under an immutable record ID and each document under a report ID. Assign a separate study-cluster ID only after comparing evidence. Report (1) discovery coverage as distinct eligible study clusters and report types, (2) screening workload as records/decisions/time, and (3) identity quality as adjudicated pairwise match/false-merge/uncertain decisions. Record counts alone can rise because one route returns more versions or duplicate records.

## Candidate retrieval routes
1. **Lexical/database baseline and revision.** Freeze a broad baseline around urban heat and named intervention concepts, then run a distinct expansion arm adding known variants (urban heat island, heatwave/heat wave, extreme heat, heat exposure, overheating, thermal comfort, cooling/greening) and local language spellings after language scope is chosen. Search intervention and exposure concepts without forcing outcome/effectiveness terms. Compare fielded title/abstract searching to broader search as separately named arms. OpenAlex supports scoped lexical queries and explicit rerank/semantic options; its default ranking and changing data require saving the exact query URL, query mode, date, parameters, result order, and raw export (S06–S07). If an accessible subject database is selected, translate and peer-review the query per source (S02, S14).
2. **Citation and repository discovery.** From screened-in eligible seed reports/studies, perform backward reference-list checking and forward citation search, preferably in two indexes when both are available; log seed, direction, index, date, round, and all candidate IDs. Repeat for a preregistered number of rounds or until no new eligible study is found in a round, and report the stopping condition. TARCiS treats citation search as supplementary rather than a stand-alone completeness method; its reporting fields are useful, with an erratum affecting Recommendation 5 to check first (S03–S04). In parallel, search selected public municipal planning, environment, public-health, resilience, and open-document portals using city and local-area aliases, agency names, report-series terms, and heat/intervention variants. Search engine/site search and direct portal browsing are separate operations; keep failed, inaccessible, and zero-hit searches in the log. Crossref/OpenAlex relation metadata may identify references or version links but is deposited metadata, not a guarantee of complete local-report coverage (S07–S09).
3. **Manual or expert-curated alternative.** Before retrieval results are reviewed, have a local-information specialist or project collaborator compile a dated seed list from existing knowledge, public bibliographies, and municipal report catalogues. Include the collaborator’s remembered items as leads only if titles/citations are actually supplied and provenance is recorded; none were supplied in this task. Keep this arm separate from the other routes. It is useful for surfacing local naming and repository blind spots but is not an independent gold standard and can preferentially find familiar cities or known organizations.

## Geographic terminology and report/study identity
Before searching, choose eligible jurisdictions, administrative levels, language set, historical period, and urban boundary. Build a logged alias table from official locality names, municipality/metro names, neighborhood or district names, local-language forms, transliterations, abbreviations, and historical names. Apply aliases across title, abstract, report metadata, and portal queries; do not require geography to appear only in the title. Preserve the exact string and alias that retrieved each row.

Use conservative links. Exact DOI, official report number, or explicit issuing-agency version relation can create a high-confidence candidate, but a DOI may identify one report version and not the study. Compare authors/agency, year, site, population, methods, sample, and stated report relationship. Preserve preprint/published, translated, updated, abridged, and local-report/journal manifestations as linked reports unless the evidence establishes the same study; never merge them merely for a similar title, city, or year. Crossref has typed version/preprint/translation relations but metadata may be absent and some identifiers are unverified (S09). Zotero’s documented title/identifier/year/author matching is a candidate mechanism with false-positive limits, not a study-cluster rule (S10). ASReview v3 can hide duplicate title/text rows from screening, so its full input and export must remain available (S11–S13).

## Pilot protocol to preregister
**Decisions required before a comparative claim:** intervention and study definitions; eligible observation designs; whether reports with no extractable study methods qualify as leads or eligible evidence; urban/geographic boundary; jurisdictions and administrative levels; languages and translation policy; dates; public-access constraints; source platforms and accounts already available; screening time cap; and independent screening/adjudication staffing. These choices are unresolved and must be signed off before the primary comparison. A provisional discovery run may test query syntax and alias usefulness, but it cannot claim comparable coverage until the scope is frozen.

1. Record protocol version, scope decisions, responsible searcher, UTC start/end, and an empty route/source ledger. Define route IDs before searching: A baseline lexical, B expanded lexical/geographic terms, C citation plus public repositories, D curated/manual leads.
2. Use a fixed retrieval window (for example, one 60-minute discovery pass after scope freeze); allocate and record minutes per route, and preserve every exact query, platform, fields, filters, dates, page limits, hit count, export format, API snapshot/version, and access failure. The allocation is a protocol parameter to set before the run, not a post hoc optimization.
3. Import all results additively. Preserve source row, raw citation, raw file/URL, retrieval time, stable source ID, and file SHA-256 where a file is lawfully available. Do not delete candidate duplicates. Normalize a copy for matching; retain original values.
4. Screen with written eligibility rules. Use two independent reviewers on a preselected random sample stratified by route, report type, jurisdiction, language, and match-risk; dual-screen all uncertain identity pairs and all candidate inclusions. Resolve disagreements by recorded adjudication. Keep screening decisions separate from identity decisions. If the dataset exceeds the time cap, draw and document a probability sample rather than screening whichever records are easiest; preserve strata and inclusion probabilities.
5. Create a blinded adjudication set for record/report/study linking, oversampling near-title pairs, reports from the same agency/series, and known version relations. Report pair-level match, distinct-study, and uncertain labels; verify sampled negative pairs so missed links are visible. The sample estimates only the sampled frame and does not imply global recall.
6. Compare arms by unique eligible study clusters, unique report manifestations, records per study, fraction of clusters found by one route only, screening minutes per unique eligible study, full-text retrieval success, duplicate-candidate precision, false merges, unresolved-pair rate, and source/jurisdiction/language mix. Use a route-by-route union and marginal yield table; stratify uncertainty by report type and geography. Do not call any value “recall” unless a defensible bounded reference set is explicitly defined.
7. Publish exact strategies, alias table, source/date ledger, unedited exports where sharing permits, deduplication/link rules, screening form, version/commit, record-to-report and report-to-study crosswalk, adjudication log, exclusions and reasons, sampled frame/probabilities, per-arm time/counts, failed queries, and limitations. PRISMA-S and TARCiS offer reporting fields; neither proves complete coverage (S01–S04).

## Implementation-history implications
OpenAlex’s 2022 launch replaced a former data flow and acknowledged inherited coverage/structure problems; current synchronization documentation describes quarterly public snapshots, entity merges/deletions, and changed merge-history support after its 2025 cutover (S05, S07). Record the index snapshot/date and preserve raw IDs/results. Crossref’s relationship model is member-deposited metadata, and relation coverage is not assured for local reports (S08–S09). Zotero documents a heuristic within-library detector and no false-positive opt-out; ASReview’s versioned changes include duplicate hiding and migration fixes, while a 2024 user report shows spelling variants can reappear (S10–S13). These histories support version pinning, additive preservation, and human study-level adjudication.

## Work actually performed and not performed
Executed: web discovery searches and inspection of primary method/tool documentation listed in sources/index.md. This was research about search/reporting systems, not retrieval of urban-heat studies.

Not executed: no intervention-topic search, database/API request, municipal portal search, citation search on a case seed, screening, duplicate adjudication, or outcome/effectiveness analysis. All route performance results and validations above are proposed, not observed. No intervention records were available, and no complete reference set exists; no recall estimate is justified.
