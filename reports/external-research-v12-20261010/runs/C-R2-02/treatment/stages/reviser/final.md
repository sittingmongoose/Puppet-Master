# Reviser final — C-R2-02-treatment

## Decision and scope

This is a reproducible proposal for a bounded discovery pilot about local observational studies and municipal reports on urban-heat interventions. It preserves the released ER12-C-R2-02-FRESH brief and every released-plan obligation. It does not conclude whether an intervention works, claim a comprehensive review, assert completeness, or estimate corpus recall. The source universe is unknown; the remembered municipal reports are a reason to investigate, not a reference list.

The proposal keeps three outcomes separate: eligible evidence observed through each route; the human time and screening load each route requires; and the accuracy of record-to-document and document-to-study links. Higher hit counts alone establish none of them. The plan is to settle eligibility and geographic scope first, compare distinct retrieval routes under that common scope, retain every source record and version, and report only conditional observed yields and audited identity measures.

## Original brief, reproduced verbatim

# Missing local studies in a small evidence collection

Case ID: ER12-C-R2-02-FRESH  
Slot ID: C-R2-02  
Domain: scholarly information retrieval

### Project situation

A university group is preparing an evidence collection about urban heat interventions. Their first search returned many general reviews and few local observational studies, although a collaborator remembers several municipal reports. Titles in the working list sometimes differ between repositories, and the team is unsure which items are duplicates. There is no supplied search export or known complete reference set. This is a synthetic planning situation, not a request to conclude whether an intervention works.

### Request

Use the 60-minute research window to propose an evidence-discovery study that can tell us how to improve coverage without losing the identity and provenance of individual studies. Explain retrieval and screening mechanisms, compare useful routes, and investigate the implementation history of related collection efforts. Provide a reproducible pilot protocol rather than an application or a finished review.

### Requirements to preserve

- Distinguish discovery coverage, screening workload, and correct study identity; increased record counts alone do not establish improvement.
- Compare terminology or database search changes with citation or repository-based discovery and a useful manual or expert-curated alternative.
- Address reports, multiple versions, ambiguous titles, and geographic terminology without treating every similar title as a duplicate.
- Explain what a prior search strategy or collection implementation would need to disclose for its claimed coverage to be informative here.
- Propose an auditable sampling and comparison design, provenance recording, and uncertainty treatment; label searches or desk checks actually executed separately from future validation.

### Boundaries

- Do not claim a comprehensive systematic review, calculate recall against an invented complete corpus, or infer intervention effectiveness from search yield.
- Do not scrape restricted services, use private accounts, contact authors, or create a search product.
- Do not delete ambiguous records merely to make the collection look cleaner.

### Still unresolved

The intervention scope, languages, jurisdictions, date limits, review inclusion rules, access routes, and time available for later screening are undecided. We want the proposal to identify which decisions must precede a meaningful coverage comparison and which can be tested provisionally.

### Record identity and non-destructive handling

Maintain linked but separate entities:

1. **Source record:** the unmodified row returned by a source, with source-local ID, route/query, retrieval UTC, raw citation/title, and available fields.
2. **Document/version:** one article, report, preprint, thesis, or other artifact, with raw title, issuing body/authors, original place names, language, dates, edition, DOI/report number/ISBN/repository accession/stable URL if present, and explicit links to other versions.
3. **Underlying study:** the investigation or data collection, with its own local ID, place, population/data, observation period, design, intervention, and related documents.

Store raw and normalized titles separately. Normalization only generates candidate links. A shared title, city, author, organization, or year is not proof of identity; no DOI is not proof of distinctness. Use explicit states: repeated source record; same document with metadata variants; distinct versions of one study; distinct studies with similar titles; or unresolved. Record the evidence source and confidence for each changed field. Keep ambiguous pairs linked and visible; never delete them to improve counts.

Software flags create candidate edges only. Zotero’s current documentation says its detector works within one library; it compares title/DOI/ISBN and, under stated conditions, year and creator fields, and says false positives cannot currently be marked as non-duplicates. Separate group libraries do not cross-match. Preserve the raw exports and perform identity adjudication outside destructive merge decisions [S008; R005].

Two reviewers should independently assess candidate links; a third resolves disagreements. Review DOI/report number, authors or issuer, dates/edition, geography, observation period/design, and content together. Keep same-document duplicates separate from different documents reporting one study. If any sampled relationship remains unresolved, retain that state and report its evidence gap.

For an auditable identity estimate, define the finite pair universe before sampling: every unordered pair of distinct source records in the frozen retrieved union, with separate adjudication outcomes for (a) same document, (b) different versions of one study, (c) distinct studies, and (d) unresolved. If the union is small, enumerate it; otherwise draw pair IDs reproducibly from the full combinatorial frame without materializing every pair. Independently of the deduplication matcher, partition pairs by frozen blocking rules: shared stable identifier; shared normalized-title key; a predeclared title-similarity threshold plus a shared author/issuer/place/date cue; other matcher blocks; and outside all blocks. Assign each pair to one stratum by a published priority order. Cross these strata with flagged/unflagged status. Before labels are reviewed, set the sample size n_h for each cell; draw a simple random sample from its N_h pairs, with known inclusion probability n_h/N_h, or census small cells, and publish seed and counts. This includes random pairs outside the matcher blocks, so a missed-link rate does not silently mean only “missed within the matcher’s own blocks.” Estimate precision among flagged pairs and the weighted number/proportion of related links missed in the unflagged frame separately; state that both are conditional on the retrieved record-pair universe, not absent studies.

### Sampling, screening, and outcomes

1. **Freeze scope and protocol.** Publish the eligibility matrix, query/source frame, citation seed rule, identity states, reviewer instructions, sampling frame, stop rules, and data dictionary. Keep any known-item set independently selected and use it only as a retrieval check, never a recall denominator [S002].
2. **Run separate routes.** Run baseline and changed-term/database arms over the same eligible scope. Change one main factor per arm. Run citation and finite repository/manual routes independently. Preserve original results without overwriting them. If a service caps results or changes during the run, capture that boundary and any remaining queue.
3. **Sample and screen.** Screen all records if feasible. Otherwise draw reproducible random samples within route × source × document type × language × year strata; disclose seed and inclusion probability. Oversample route-unique records to make incremental yield visible, then use weights for overall sample estimates. Mask route from screeners where practical; double-screen a preregistered subset, all uncertain records, and likely eligible reports. Draw an additional probability sample of records initially screened ineligible from each route/source/document-type/language/year stratum and independently re-screen them (including full text where accessible) to audit screening misses; record the seed and inclusion probabilities. At full text, log access failures and exclusion reasons. Mark unscreened as unassessed, not irrelevant.
4. **Define the route-conditional miss proxy.** The alternate-route audit set is every eligible record found through the declared citation and/or repository/manual routes, including eligible records also retrieved by baseline. This inclusion rule is necessary to count overlap. For each audit-set item, distinguish: baseline retrieved and judged eligible; baseline retrieved but missed during screening; baseline did not retrieve it. Report the categories by alternate route and union. This is a conditional diagnostic of this route combination, scope, source frame, and screening process. Routes are dependent, the alternate set is incomplete, and no population recall or completeness follows.
5. **Compare three axes separately.**
   - **Discovery:** raw source records; distinct documents/versions; adjudicated studies; eligible items observed; route-unique eligible additions; overlap; and sampled screening yield with uncertainty.
   - **Screening workload:** records at title/abstract and full-text stages, reviewer-minutes by stage, agreement/disagreements, retrieval failures, adjudications, and time per eligible study. Compare at equal screened records and equal screening/adjudication hours.
   - **Identity quality:** candidate-link precision; related links found in the probability-sampled unflagged pairs; false merges; distinct-study false links; multi-version groups; unresolved identities; and field-level provenance completeness. Show denominators and sample design.
6. **Account for both cost scopes.** “Reviewer-hours” is otherwise ambiguous. Report screening/adjudication hours separately from end-to-end route effort, which includes strategy development/translation, source curation, query execution, portal browsing, retrieval, screening, and adjudication. Show eligible yield per screening hour and per total pilot hour, plus equal-record comparisons. Keep setup time and repeatable run time as separate ledger fields. Do not choose a route on the screening-only denominator if it used materially more search/curation time.
7. **Stop and update transparently.** Predeclare a boundary for each route: closed site/jurisdiction list, page/record cap, or carefully bounded consecutive-run rule. Record final page/record, unreviewed queue, and stop reason. Cochrane notes that stop-rule approaches have little formal evaluation and calls for caution with sparse evidence; a quiet run is not assurance [S002]. Preserve each update as a new dated event with metadata deltas.
8. **Report for replay.** Adapt PRISMA-S as a disclosure checklist, not as an algorithm or completeness standard. Report exact per-source strategies, interfaces and dates, database/platform limits, citation seeds/direction, source-by-source counts, web/portal search extent, reuse of prior strategies, and deduplication process [S001]. Provide open navigable source and record indexes; retain raw and normalized fields, hashes, versions, reviewers, decisions, and uncertainty tags.

## Ordinary critique findings and other corrections

1. **Unflagged identity-audit frame — accept.** The prior draft did not define “unflagged pairs.” The full pair universe and stratified probability sampling rule above make clear which pair relations are estimable, include a random sample outside similarity blocks, and prevent estimates from being presented as identity truth for missing documents.
2. **End-to-end route cost — accept.** Keep screening workload distinct, while also measuring query preparation, translation, curation, portal work, and retrieval effort. Report both yield per screening hour and yield per total pilot hour.
3. **Search implementation over time — accept and extend.** The 2021 report and ASReview release notes alone did not compare the INSPQ 2009 search method. The recovered 2009 primary report adds its dates, databases, supplementary routes, terms appendix, and criteria. Documented differences can be described; causal coverage change cannot be inferred because source frames, criteria, years, and detail differ.
4. **Audit-set ambiguity — accepted as SCW-1 above.** This is the same issue, not a second finding.
5. **OpenAlex ranking — accept correction.** Remove “may favor highly cited general reviews.” The current documentation supports only that citation counts contribute, with a cap, to default relevance ranking. Report the actual sort/ranking and do not treat a truncated result list as exhaustive.
6. **Crossref citation span — accept correction.** The earlier S005–S007 span incorrectly included OpenAlex for the Crossref clause. Cite S006–S007 and reviser checks R003–R004.
7. **Predecessor availability — amend.** S018 records the original URL’s 404 accurately and is not rebound. R007 is the alternate official PDF; R008–R009 document conflicting English/French catalogue dates and correction notes. The search-history question is materially better answered, while the precise file/translation chronology remains uncertain.
