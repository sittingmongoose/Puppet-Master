# Final revised proposal: a repeating brightness pattern in a teaching telescope archive

**Case:** ER12-D-R2-03-FRESH · **Slot:** D-R2-03 · **Run:** D-R2-03-treatment  
**Status:** Evidence-informed investigation plan for a synthetic case. No cause is selected. This document distinguishes general instrument guidance from facts actually known about the archive.

## Decision summary

Treat the pattern first as a measurement-chain question. Preserve and inventory the authorized original images and headers, recover the change chronology, and reconstruct the documented reduction before exploring alternatives. In parallel, define target, comparison, and check-star measurements and their detector positions, backgrounds, times, and observing conditions. Then run a small, pre-specified sensitivity matrix that changes one relevant choice at a time. Use existing independent observations only if the field, epoch, cadence, bandpass, angular resolution, and data lineage make them useful. Do not announce a variable object or schedule observations on the present evidence.

This order is provisional, not a winner among methods. The instructor's choice between pipeline reliability and astrophysical classification remains an owner decision. If required raw frames, calibration provenance, or timestamps cannot be recovered, report which inference is blocked and ask whether a separately authorized controlled pilot or reference/calibration acquisition is warranted.

The project description supplies no images, instrument identity, dates, field, filters, software, calibration files, comparison-star identities, or weather log. Thus every case-specific mechanism below is a testable branch, not a finding.

### SCW-01 — independent-data lineage

- Exact draft claim: “Use existing independent observations only if the field, epoch, cadence, bandpass, angular resolution, and data lineage make them useful.”
- Intended C: independent observations mean separately acquired measurements from another source or instrument.
- Diagnostic C*: independently processed measurements that reuse the teaching-archive exposures. The changed selector is acquisition versus processing lineage.
- Evidence and relation: the official ZTF page describes its own public survey and releases; NASA/HEASARC describes spacecraft images, selected target pixels, and SPOC light curves [RV05](sources/RV05.md) [RV06](sources/RV06.md). - Disposition: ACCEPT WITH QUALIFICATION. Keep the independent-data route conditional. Call evidence independent only when acquisition and product lineage are documented; a second reduction of the same teaching exposures is a robustness analysis, not independent confirmation. Case coverage and lineage remain unknown. C* is UNDETERMINED for this case.

### SCW-02 — cloud cancellation condition

- Exact draft claim: “A uniform cloud may partly cancel in differential photometry only if stars share sufficiently similar conditions; that assumption is often imperfect.”
- Intended C: cancellation requires sufficiently similar effects on target and comparison measurements.
- Diagnostic C*: cloud spatial uniformity alone is sufficient, even when the measured effect on stars differs.
- Evidence and relation: AAVSO says the rare cancellation case occurs when a thin, uniform cloud affects the target and comparisons to the same degree [RV01](sources/RV01.md), p. 69, lines 2049–2054. This supports C in substance but gives a more precise condition. It neither establishes C* nor proves that every spatially uniform cloud must produce different attenuation.
- Disposition: AMEND to “A thin, uniform cloud may cancel only when it affects target and comparison stars to the same degree; the guide says this is rare.” Spatial uniformity alone is not enough to establish cancellation. C is supported; C* is UNDETERMINED, not disproved as a universal possibility. No cloud is established in the case.

## Claim ladder: three different questions

1. **Repeatable measured changes:** Does the same documented measurement procedure produce a stable pattern from the preserved data, with uncertainty and repeatability evaluated across nights? Repeating a reduction or plotting the same measurements again is not independent replication.
2. **Calibrated photometric performance:** Does the full acquisition and reduction chain recover known flux ratios or standard-field magnitudes within an independently established error budget under matching detector, filter, temperature, illumination, and observing conditions? Repeatability alone does not demonstrate accuracy. A check star tests consistency within a field; a suitable standard field and traceable calibration are needed for an accuracy claim. [S01](sources/S01.md) [S02](sources/S02.md)
3. **Intrinsic source variability:** Does target-specific variation remain after documented instrumental, background, atmospheric, comparison-star, timing, and selection effects are addressed, and does it recur in data independent of the original measurement chain? A significant-looking curve or period is not sufficient.

Do not promote evidence at one level into a claim at the next without the additional controls.

## Proposed staged work and decision gates

1. **Confirm scope and access.** Ask the instructor to choose the primary endpoint: (A) reliability of the measurement pipeline or (B) astrophysical classification. Record the secondary objective and acceptable error/replication criteria. Confirm authorized read-only access and custodians before touching data.
2. **Inventory and freeze inputs.** Copy or mount read-only the raw science images, calibration frames, headers, masks, observing logs, reduction scripts/configuration, software/version information, catalog cross-identifications if any, and output tables. Record file names, hashes, timestamps, provenance, missingness, and any gaps. Preserve originals; never let a reducer overwrite source images. The AstroImageJ warning and IRAF output behavior illustrate why this matters. [S04](sources/S04.md) [S06](sources/S06.md)
3. **Reconstruct chronology and geometry.** Make an event table for camera rotation/pointing changes, filter/exposure changes, calibration acquisition, reducer revisions, observing routine changes, and weather/environment notes. For every source and exposure, derive detector x/y and orientation only from validated headers/WCS; separately preserve sky coordinates and the method used to transform them. Compare the timing of apparent changes with these event boundaries without treating coincidence as cause.
4. **Reproduce before changing.** Recover the actual recipe and settings, run only on copies, and save recipe/version, inputs, hashes, output hashes, logs, masks, flags, and exceptions. First establish whether the reported measurements can be reproduced. If not, identify which missing or ambiguous input prevents reproduction; do not silently substitute modern defaults.
5. **Pre-register a compact sensitivity matrix.** Before examining which result favors a candidate, state target and comparison selection, excluded frames, background/aperture/flat alternatives, timing treatment, candidate-frequency range rationale, and the limited comparisons to report. Change one factor at a time: (i) original versus justified calibration branch; (ii) background/aperture choice; (iii) ensemble composition with held-out check; (iv) position/rotation epoch; (v) night/condition subsets. Preserve all attempted variants, including null or inconvenient outcomes. Avoid an unconstrained pipeline multiverse.
6. **Separate precision from accuracy.** Estimate repeatability from appropriately grouped repeated measurements, accounting for within-night correlation. Assess calibrated accuracy only against suitable standards/check observations and the required calibration lineage. If such standards or references do not exist, say calibrated accuracy remains unmeasured; do not substitute low scatter for accuracy.
7. **Assess source variability.** Inspect target and comparison residuals against position, sky, seeing, airmass, exposure, rotation, and pipeline events. Report cadence and window structure. Only after the above, test predeclared periodicity hypotheses and compare candidate peaks with aliases. Hold out whole nights or an independent dataset for confirmation where possible; splitting adjacent exposures randomly would leak shared conditions.
8. **Independent comparison or escalation.** With coordinates and date range, assess ZTF/TESS or another suitable independent product before any new observing. Verify coverage, cadence, bandpass, resolution, flags, and extraction lineage; seek raw/single-exposure/pixel products if catalog processing could hide a relevant artifact. If this route fails and missing archive evidence blocks the selected endpoint, return to the instructor with the specific gap and an option for a controlled, authorized pilot or reference calibration acquisition.
9. **Report and decide.** Deliver a provenance table, change chronology, input and output hashes, measurement definition, uncertainty limitations, sensitivity results, residual checks, null results, excluded data rationale, and explicit distinction between executed checks and proposed follow-up. The endpoint decision should state whether the evidence supports repeatable measurements, calibrated performance, or only a future test of intrinsic variability.

### Timing, dependence, selection, and periodicity

Preserve raw time tags, exposure durations, start/end/midpoint meaning, clock source and synchronization, site, filter, and original time scale. Do not relabel a value as UTC/BJD without its source and conversion metadata. If event timing precision requires barycentric correction, use verified source coordinates and observatory location, document scale and correction, and retain the original values. [S01](sources/S01.md) [S08](sources/S08.md) [S09](sources/S09.md)

Measurements from a single night share atmosphere, tracking, detector, and processing conditions; adjacent points are therefore not automatically independent. Many candidate stars, comparison sets, filters, nights, frequency ranges, and reduction variants create selection and multiplicity effects. The Lomb–Scargle literature explains that window structure creates aliases and that white-noise/single-frequency assumptions do not answer a search across many trial frequencies. The paper also cautions that an ordinary value-resampling bootstrap does not handle correlated noise and that a false-alarm probability is not the probability that these data are periodic. A later analysis should predeclare the search space, examine the sampling window, calibrate its maximum-peak statistic over the full search using a null procedure appropriate to observed dependence and sampling, and confirm on held-out nights or independent observations. A night/block-based resampling or correlated-noise model may be considered after diagnostics; no one resampling scheme is justified by this brief alone. These are safeguards for a future study, not results. [S13](sources/S13.md)
