## Decision frame

Keep three claims separate.

1. **Repeatable measured brightness change** means a relative flux or magnitude pattern survives a reproducible measurement of the supplied images under declared settings. Repeating the same reduction on the same exposures tests reproducibility; it does not create independent observations.
2. **Calibrated photometric performance** means the instrument/reduction's precision and accuracy have been estimated against controls with known or independently measured behavior, such as a check star, a standard field, uncertainty and linearity checks. Small scatter by itself is precision, not accuracy. The AAVSO guide distinguishes them and recommends both repeated measurements and external standards [S01].
3. **Evidence for intrinsic source variability** requires the candidate's behavior to remain after reasonable instrument, processing and environment explanations have been challenged, while check/comparison behavior is consistent, and ideally to appear in independently acquired data or a later controlled pilot. It is not established by an attractive repeating plot or by rerunning the same software [S01, S02].

The brief therefore supports an investigation proposal, not a variable-object announcement, period, amplitude, significance, calibrated accuracy claim or astrophysical classification.

## Competing explanations and discriminating observations

| Explanation family | Mechanism that could produce apparent changes | Useful discriminators and limits |
|---|---|---|
| Detector position, camera rotation, optical illumination | Pixel response is not perfectly uniform; vignetting, edge behavior, dust/flat residuals and subpixel PSF/aperture changes can depend on where a star falls. A camera rotation can change the mapping between sky stars and detector positions if it was a physical sensor/rotator change. AAVSO describes measurable flux-ratio changes from small image shifts and edge location [S01]; a precision-observing guide recommends holding a target on the same sensor pixels [S03]. | Recover plate solutions and raw detector centroids for every exposure, rotator angle/orientation, flips, aperture footprints and filter-specific flat IDs. Plot every star's residual against x/y, distance to edge, centroid/subpixel phase, FWHM and orientation epoch. Re-run with archived flats and plausible matched flat/illumination alternatives. A signature that follows detector coordinates or a rotation boundary supports an instrumental contribution; it does not alone prove the entire signal is instrumental. |
| Calibration/reduction revision | Bias, dark, flat, illumination, trim/mask, background estimate, aperture/annulus, centroiding, ensemble, normalization or detrending changes can change measurements across all stars. Software defaults and in-place behavior vary: IRAF 2.18 ccdproc documents default calibration switches and an empty output name that replaces input images; it selects flats by subset and leaves illumination correction off by default [S04]. Lamp-flat illumination mismatch can leave a large-scale pattern, for which IRAF documents separate blank-sky or illumination-flat corrections with different conditions [S05]. AstroImageJ has a distinct Data Processor and Multi-Aperture workflow [S06] and a release history with fixes/features tied to specific versions [S07]. | Obtain exact app/library release or commit, configuration, master-frame hashes, input/output hashes, operation order, comparison list, aperture/annulus, exclusion masks and logs for each term. Preserve raw inputs and outputs; reproduce the old and revised recipes in separate products. Compare star-by-star curves, check stars, and leave-one-comparison-out ensembles. A version changelog is a lead for what to inspect, not evidence that the case used that version or hit that defect. |
| Background and nearby artificial lighting | Skyglow/light pollution contributes background counts and photon noise; estimating and subtracting a local sky annulus removes an estimated level but does not remove the noise or a spatial/time-varying gradient. The AAVSO guide describes annulus subtraction and the uncertainty caused by background [S01]. A nearby sports field could be a source to investigate, but the brief supplies no measurements linking it to the images. | Recover contemporaneous staff lighting schedule/orientation, filter, moon, weather and background maps. Measure annulus/blank-sky levels and gradients per frame and per detector region; compare target and all comparison/check curves with background and orientation. Do not infer causation from the note alone. |
| Atmosphere, airmass, seeing and scintillation | Transparency/clouds may affect target and comparisons together only when conditions are sufficiently uniform across the field. Color/airmass differences can leave differential extinction; seeing/PSF changes alter aperture losses. Scintillation is a separate atmospheric intensity fluctuation from image blur and depends on site/airmass/exposure conditions [S01, S02, S10]. | Inspect contemporaneous weather/seeing/transparency notes, airmass, FWHM/ellipticity and per-star residuals. Keep color-similar, nearby comparison stars where possible; plot check stars and use standard fields across airmass if observations are authorized later. Uniform-cloud cancellation is a conditional possibility, not a default assumption [S01]. No site-specific scintillation number is justified here. |
| Exposure, detector linearity and timing | Changes in exposure length, gain/readout, filter, saturation or cadence can produce response shifts or sampling artifacts. The AAVSO guide says to assess linearity and avoid saturated target/comparison stars; precision depends on exposure, filter, background and settings [S01]. Header timestamps may label start, midpoint, or another event and can use different time standards. | Inventory exposure duration, filter, gain/offset/binning/readout/temperature, maximum pixel counts, cadence gaps, FITS time keywords and clock synchronization. Preserve original time tags; confirm whether they mark exposure start/midpoint and which scale is used. If precise event comparison is scientifically needed, report site-arrival UTC and a correctly computed BJD_TDB using source coordinates and observatory location [S08, S09]. No time conversion or period analysis was performed. |
| Comparison-star selection or behavior | A comparison star may itself be variable, blended, saturated, near an edge, color-mismatched, or subject to a different detector/background/airmass effect. An ensemble may reduce reference noise but can also hide a member's problem or propagate a selection error. | Plot each comparison and check star independently. Keep at least one check star out of the ensemble; use leave-one-out and one-comparison/ensemble sensitivity checks, inspect companions and catalog uncertainties if suitable coordinates and records become available. The AAVSO guide gives these check/ensemble tradeoffs and favors nearby, color-similar, unsaturated stars [S01]. The present brief does not identify suitable stars. |
| Intrinsic astronomical variation | A true source may vary, but its class and timescale are unknown. | Require robustness to declared reductions, comparison choices, detector-position terms, background/weather terms and timestamp correction; then seek an independent instrument/data source with compatible epoch, passband, cadence and crowding. Only after these gates should a science owner decide what astrophysical follow-up is justified. |

These causes can coexist. No one is selected as the cause in this discovery.

## Alternatives and initial recommendation

**First use the existing archive without new observations.** This is the lowest-intervention route and best separates pipeline reliability from astrophysical classification. Freeze copies/checksums of original FITS files, headers, calibration frames and contemporaneous logs. Reconstruct the exact old and revised recipes, then measure both versions from the same untouched inputs in separate output trees. Keep all intermediate calibrated images, tables, settings and exclusions. Compare epochs on both sides of camera rotation and each processing change; do not mix stages silently. Compute no period until timestamps, cadence, passbands, quality flags and sampling window are understood.

**Reduction alternatives:** (a) reproduce the actual historical recipe and current recipe exactly; (b) a transparent fixed-aperture/local-sky and ensemble differential reduction with a held-out check star; and, if crowding/PSF changes justify it, (c) an independently configured PSF/model-based extraction as a sensitivity analysis. Use the same raw exposures and report where methods agree/disagree. Alternative reductions are robustness checks, not independent observations. Compare filter-specific bias/dark/flat/illumination choices rather than applying one generic master indiscriminately [S01, S04, S05].

**Observation strategy alternative:** if archive recovery cannot answer the question, propose a small authorized pilot only after the instructor chooses the first objective. For the baseline block, hold camera orientation, focus, filter, gain/readout and target placement as stable as practicable; observe target and nearby color/magnitude-matched comparisons in the same field, include a separate check star, avoid detector nonlinearity/saturation, and record exposure midpoint, synchronized clock, site, WCS, weather and background. Add standard-field measurements spanning useful airmass for accuracy checks [S01, S02]. A separate, clearly labeled diagnostic block may deliberately move/rotate the field while keeping all settings/calibrations recorded, to test whether residuals follow detector coordinates. Do not combine deliberate geometry changes with the baseline as if they were interchangeable. A wider field can provide more comparison candidates, but increases scope for spatial/color extinction differences; prefer local, color-similar candidates unless field coverage forces a documented tradeoff [S01].

**Independent/no-new-observation alternative:** no new telescope time may be needed if the raw archive, calibrations, and a truly independent public time series overlap. After coordinates and epochs are released, check ZTF public DR24 coverage (g/r, single-exposure images and light curves) and/or TESS sector products. ZTF's current release schedule specifies field/date-dependent release timing; its images and derived light curves are distinct products [S11]. TESS SPOC light curves are SAP flux plus cotrending/systematics removal, with separate files possible across sectors; target pixels allow a different extraction path [S12]. Compare bands and cadence carefully; different filters and pipelines can make shape/amplitude comparisons nontrivial. Retrieve source-level images/pixels and quality metadata where possible. A public pipeline curve is independent of the teaching-camera pipeline, but not a fresh measurement if it reuses the same teaching-archive exposures. No coordinate/catalog query or archive download has been done.

## Provenance and proposed decision gates

1. **Recover and preserve inputs:** raw images and checksums; FITS headers; bias/dark/flat/illumination masters and their constituent frames; filter IDs; camera make/model/serial/firmware and sensor settings; rotator/mount/meridian-flip history; exposure start/end and timestamp convention; processing source/version/commit/dependencies/settings; output hashes; comparison/check definitions; weather, transparency, seeing, sky background and sports-field lighting notes. Unknown stays explicitly unknown.
2. **Characterize the alleged signal before model selection:** construct per-star, per-frame calibrated and differential measurements with uncertainties; separately record raw/relative flux, calibrated magnitude where supported, detector position/orientation, exposure/time, filter, background, FWHM, airmass and quality flags. Display each comparison and check star. Leave-one-out ensemble and aperture/annulus variants are diagnostic analyses. Preserve results before/after each change point rather than visually choosing only a favorable segment.
3. **Assess repeatability:** ask whether the change recurs from original inputs under the same frozen recipe and whether it survives reasonable reduction choices. This is not independent confirmation.
4. **Assess photometric performance:** use held-out check stars, standard fields if available, empirical uncertainty and linearity/saturation checks; report repeatability separately from accuracy. Define thresholds from the chosen science goal and actual detector behavior rather than inventing them.
5. **Assess source variability:** only if the earlier gates pass, check independent data with adequate sky position, overlapping dates, passband/cadence and quality. Keep cross-pipeline processing lineage visible. A visible pattern, a catalog label, a single preferred reduction or repeated output is insufficient.
6. **Decision:** begin with archive/pipeline reliability. Authorize the controlled acquisition pilot only if raw provenance is irrecoverable or ambiguity remains after analysis and the selected science goal requires it. If reliability is established but an intrinsic-source claim remains, require independent data or an approved targeted pilot before any classification or announcement.

## Transferability and unresolved owner choices

The useful prior implementations establish concrete operations, not case facts. IRAF ccdproc is a historically mature CCD calibration workflow with explicit task defaults and flat/illumination alternatives [S04, S05]. AstroImageJ is a time-series differential-photometry implementation with calibration, multi-aperture, header/time conversion tools and an evolving release history [S06, S07]. Their evidence transfers at the level of candidate operations, assumptions and audit questions; it transfers to this archive only after instrument format, calibration regime, algorithm and exact software version are matched. The AAVSO guide is broad observing practice, not a camera specification [S01].

Still unknown: field/coordinates, filters, camera make/model and orientation meaning, detector positions, exposure/cadence and timestamp convention, archive/calibration availability, weather/sky/background records, pipeline and revisions, comparison selections, source color/crowding, public-survey overlap, and whether the first objective is pipeline reliability or astrophysical classification. Preserve all of these as open questions; do not infer the unreleased plan or choose a cause.
