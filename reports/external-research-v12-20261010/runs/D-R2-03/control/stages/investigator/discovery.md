## Independent candidate explanations and discriminators

- **Detector response, orientation, and position.** A star's sky position is transformed to different detector pixels after a camera rotation; sensitivity, vignetting, illumination, flat residuals, aperture losses, and nearby contaminants can vary with detector coordinates or angle. The transferable diagnostic is to plot per-star residuals against detector x/y, rotator angle, centroid shift, and time, then ask whether changes follow detector coordinates or the star. A FORS2 study documented orientation-dependent illumination terms and standard-star improvements, but its correction coefficients and effect size are specific to that instrument/filter. Use it only to motivate a test, not to assert the teaching camera has the same defect (FORS2-ROTATING-ILLUMINATION; DOME-FLAT-STANDARDS).

- **Exposure, calibration, and processing.** Recover exact exposure, gain, readout, saturation/linearity range, calibration-frame associations, filter, reduction version, parameters, masks, and date-effective revisions. Compare saved outputs against reproducible reductions of the same immutable raw frames, changing one factor at a time: calibration frames and order, background/annulus treatment, aperture/centroid method, and ensemble. Track unsaturated counts and per-star residuals as well as the target ratio. Explicitly record dark scaling; in ccdproc 2.5.1 it is not applied by default, whereas the flat is mean-scaled before division (CCDPROC-2.5.1). This is a candidate implementation, not evidence the archive used it. AIJ provides an integrated calibration and time-series workflow, but its measurement-error model requires correct detector gain, dark current, and read noise (AIJ-PAPER-2017). Its release notes show behavior changes over time, including a TESS-specific BJD fix and later FITS / plot fixes (AIJ-RELEASE-5.4.0; AIJ-RELEASE-6.0.11). First identify actual historical versions/settings; do not rerun an untracked "current default."

- **Background, field lighting, and atmosphere.** Measure sky counts and spatial gradients locally in every frame, separately from stellar aperture flux; compare background with time, direction on detector, moon/twilight, and any recoverable sports-field lighting schedule. Changing background increases shot noise and can bias aperture/annulus subtraction if it is spatially nonuniform or contaminated. This is a physical hypothesis, not a finding that the field is responsible. Record airmass, seeing/FWHM, transparency/clouds, sky level, and target/comparison colors. Differential photometry suppresses shared correlated changes but does not remove target/reference color-dependent extinction, non-common scintillation, detector effects, or noise introduced by comparison measurements. Nearby, color-similar stars and independent check stars help; first- and second-order extinction remain possible (DIFF-PHOTOMETRY-2023). Do not use the optional temporal binning of comparison-star measurements unless cadence/noise regime is suitable: the study assumes good photometric conditions and low-frequency systematic noise, and warns that high-frequency clouds or blending violate its conditions.

- **Timing and sampling.** Recover original FITS time fields, clock source, shutter start/end or exposure midpoint convention, exposure length, gaps, cadence, observatory coordinates, and changes in computer or camera timing. Report native site-arrival time and time scale before any barycentric conversion; JD/BJD/HJD labels alone are incomplete, and BJD_TDB needs target coordinates and site metadata (EASTMAN-BJD-2010). Plot raw cadence and the spectral window before exploring a period. Uneven nighttime sampling can produce aliases; Astropy false-alarm values are approximate under a non-varying Gaussian-noise null and do not absorb correlated systematics (VANDERPLAS-LS-2018; ASTROPY-LS-8). Repeating a plot or rerunning the same pipeline is not independent confirmation.

- **Intrinsic source behavior.** A source-specific pattern becomes a plausible astrophysical candidate only if it survives validated reductions and reasonable aperture/calibration/ensemble variants; does not recur in check stars or track detector coordinates, sky background, airmass, exposure, or pipeline version; and is independently reproduced with suitable time coverage and source separation. A periodogram is a later characterization, not a classification step. There is no real target identity or supplied catalog record to check.

## Useful alternatives and staged pilot

1. **Archive-first calibration/reduction comparison.** Work only on copies of the original files. Hash and inventory inputs; preserve the original processing route; record every version, header interpretation, parameter, calibration source, mask, and exclusion. Reprocess the same sequence with the historical route if recoverable, then a transparent alternative (for example, explicit ccdproc reduction plus a separately documented aperture/ensemble analysis). Compare fixed and FWHM-varying apertures, local-sky choices, and multiple ensembles; leave one comparison star out at a time and retain independent check-star curves. Inspect each star's raw aperture flux, local background, FWHM, centroid, detector position, and differential flux. Keep a copy of every intermediate table and the result-to-input hashes. This is the lowest-risk first gate because rotation and software changes are already reported.

2. **Controlled acquisition pilot after pipeline reliability.** If archive provenance/calibration is not recoverable or residuals remain detector-linked, obtain a limited, pre-approved pilot rather than declaring a variable. Hold filter, exposure, cadence, processing version, and comparison set fixed; record shutter timing, temperature, focus, gain, sky and weather. Include a stable-orientation sequence and a deliberate camera/rotator angle crossover, with validated calibration flats at the corresponding orientations or a measured orientation correction. Keep the target and nearby color-similar comparison/check stars in the field, select stars spanning detector positions, and inspect field gradients. Where safe and feasible, time-tag field-light state or obtain paired background records; do not assume it is the cause. This design predicts different behavior for detector-fixed artifacts versus sky-source behavior. Avoid changing orientation, exposure, and software together.

3. **No-new-observation independent data.** Once coordinates and source separation are established, query suitable public time-series archives. MAST lists TESS calibrated FFIs, target-pixel files, and light curves; coverage depends on sky location/sector. TESS's 21-arcsecond pixels and distinct passband/cadence can blend neighbors or miss a short/local signal, so inspect pixel-level data and cadence/quality flags rather than treating a catalog light curve as an automatic verdict (MAST-TESS-PRODUCTS; MAST-TESS-MISSION; TESS-SDPD-REV-F). Lightkurve can read mission pixel and light-curve products, but quality masks and selected flux columns matter; its release notes document product-reader and time-format fixes, so freeze version, file, flux column, and mask (LIGHTKURVE-API; LIGHTKURVE-2.5.0). This route may be unavailable if no coordinate coverage or useful cadence exists. A genuinely independent instrument is stronger than another run of the same local pipeline; a different passband or epoch also changes what a non-detection means.

**Decision proposal:** default to an archive reliability gate first, because two procedural changes co-occur and comparison stars drift. Proceed to a small acquisition pilot only if the archive cannot separate the principal systematics.
