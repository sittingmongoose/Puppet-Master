## Proposal and provisional decision

Treat the reported repeating pattern as a measurement question first. The case provides no image, calibration, telescope log, reduction code, field coordinates, or object identity. Therefore, no pattern has been independently measured here. Keep three questions separate:

1. Does an unchanged, documented measurement procedure reproduce a brightness pattern in the archived frames?
2. Do control stars and calibrated standards show that the procedure has acceptable precision and accuracy under these conditions?
3. After instrumental and environmental tests, is there source-centered evidence for intrinsic variability?

A positive answer to one question does not answer the next. Repeated output from one pipeline shows that the pipeline can repeat its output, not that the source varies. This proposal recommends an archive-reliability gate first because camera orientation and processing both changed while some reference stars drifted. That is a provisional order of evidence collection, not a finding that either change caused the pattern. The instructor remains the owner of the unresolved priority between pipeline reliability and astrophysical classification.

The case is synthetic. The observations below are reported context, not verified facts about a real instrument or star. The illuminated sports field is a lead for background measurements and operating-history review, not an established cause.

## Mechanisms and discriminating observations

| Candidate mechanism | How it could enter the measurement | Discriminating evidence to seek |
|---|---|---|
| Camera rotation, detector position, and optics | Stars can fall on different pixels or optical-field locations after rotation. Pixel response, residual flat-field structure, vignetting, coma, focus, centroiding, aperture losses, and blends can vary over position or orientation. A FORS2 study measured static and rotation-dependent illumination terms, but its correction coefficients are specific to FORS2 and cannot be transferred to this telescope ([FORS2-ROTATING-ILLUMINATION](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.6467)). | Retain per-frame WCS and detector x/y, camera/rotator angle, centroids, FWHM, raw and corrected fluxes. Plot each star’s residuals against detector coordinates, angle, and time. A signal that follows detector position or orientation across different stars implicates an instrument path; one that follows one identified source across positions is more consistent with source-linked behavior, but still needs calibrated and independent confirmation. Test known standard stars across the field; agreement between two visually smooth flats is not enough ([DOME-FLAT-STANDARDS](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0510233); [AAVSO-CCD-CMOS-GUIDE-2022](https://aavso.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/CCD-CMOS-English.pdf)). |
| Calibration and camera response | Bias/readout structure, dark current, bad pixels, flat-field illumination, changing filter/dust geometry, temperature/readout settings, saturation and nonlinearity can affect raw aperture sums and their errors. A flat removes a modeled signal pattern but its noise also contributes; subtraction cannot remove the shot noise of unwanted background light. | Retrieve calibration frame identities, dates, filters, camera temperature, gain/offset/readout/binning, dark method, flat orientation and combination history. Compare standards or stable field stars across detector position. Check target and references against the measured linear range; inspect profiles for saturation even without visible blooming. Keep scaled-dark and unscaled-dark routes distinct. The AAVSO guide requires compatible calibration settings and warns against mixing dark methods in a session; adapt only after the actual camera is identified. |
| Exposure and cadence | Exposure changes alter photon/background noise and can interact with saturation or nonlinearity. Shutter timing, dropped frames, cadence gaps, exposure start versus midpoint, and nightly scheduling affect the apparent phase and available period search. | Preserve original timing headers and exposure lengths; reconstruct the actual shutter convention and clock. Compare repeated exposures without mixing saturated and unsaturated stars. Show time gaps and the sampling window before period searches. Record observing schedule and any batch/firmware changes. |
| Sports-field lighting and sky background | Skyglow or scattered light can raise background counts, alter the local background estimate, increase shot noise, and create a spatial gradient. Light pollution is a known background contribution in teaching-scale photometry, but the field note alone does not show that this field contaminated these images ([AAVSO-CCD-CMOS-GUIDE-2022](https://aavso.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/CCD-CMOS-English.pdf)). | Measure local sky/background and its spatial gradient on each image outside stellar apertures; compare the location and time of any gradient with the field-light operating record, moon/twilight, and residuals of stars at different positions. Record weather and illumination conditions. Do not subtract a trend solely because its timing resembles a light schedule. |
| Atmosphere and comparison-field strategy | Airmass, color-dependent extinction, cloud, transparency, scintillation, seeing, and target/reference separation may leave common or non-common residuals. Differential photometry can remove shared variation, while comparison-star noise enters the resulting ratio and first- or second-order extinction can remain. | Prefer nearby, color-similar comparisons within a well-behaved detector region; use more than one comparison and an independent check star. Plot each comparison against the remaining ensemble, then perform leave-one-out reductions. Log color, airmass, seeing/FWHM, sky level, and cloud notes. The 2023 temporal-binning method is optional. The paper assumes good photometric conditions, low-frequency systematics, and no blending. Its procedure says to leave high-frequency-trend intervals unbinned; its discussion allows binning during otherwise suitable intervals around occasional rapid trends. With cadence and residual structure unknown here, keep this as a separately tested variant, not a baseline ([DIFF-PHOTOMETRY-2023](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/526/3/3482/7285831)). |
| Reduction revision or implementation behavior | A pipeline revision can change calibration order, scaling, masks, background, apertures, errors, timestamps, or default comparison selection. If release date tracks camera rotation date, the two changes can be confounded. | Recover code commit/release, full settings, saved aperture/ensemble files, master calibrations, and dated change log. Reprocess copies of the same raw frames with the historic route, then test one documented change at a time. Compare outputs by frame and star. Do not substitute a modern default and call it an independent check. |
| Intrinsic source variability | A real source may vary, but a visually repeated curve may also be caused by shared systematics, a variable comparison, or sampling aliases. | Require a star-specific result that survives validated calibration/reduction and reasonable fixed aperture, background, and ensemble choices; is absent from check stars and is not tied to detector position, sky level, airmass, exposure, or software version; then seek suitable independent observations. There is no target identity to cross-match now. |

### Relevant implementations and what transfers

- **AstroImageJ (AIJ)** is a plausible integrated workflow for calibration, multi-aperture time-series photometry, ensemble selection, plotting, and timing conversion. Its paper says error calculations depend on entering detector gain, dark current, and read noise. Its legacy guide describes FWHM-based variable apertures, which depend on an initial fixed aperture large enough to contain the largest FWHM; its historical numerical recommendations are not universal ([AIJ-PAPER-2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.04817); [AIJ-GUIDE-LEGACY](https://astroimagej.com/guides/legacy/)). The release history is consequential: 5.4.0.00 lists a BJD timestamp fix for TESS TICA FFI images; 6.0.10.00 adds latitude/longitude header options for BJD and airmass conversion; 6.0.11.00 lists later FITS and comparison-star display fixes. These do not show that the local archive used AIJ or suffered these bugs ([AIJ-RELEASE-5.4.0](https://astroimagej.com/releases/54000/); [AIJ-RELEASE-6.0.10](https://astroimagej.com/releases/601000/); [AIJ-RELEASE-6.0.11](https://astroimagej.com/releases/601100/)). Recover the actual release and preferences first.
- **ccdproc 2.5.1** offers a transparent staged CCD reduction alternative. Its documented dark-subtraction API does not scale darks by default; the user must request scale=True and supply appropriate exposure metadata. Its flat correction normalizes the master flat by its mean. The combined ccd_process operation performs overscan, trim, gain, mask, uncertainty, bias, and flat steps. These are implementation-specific defaults, not evidence about the archive’s pipeline; do not install or run it for this stage ([CCDPROC-2.5.1](https://ccdproc.readthedocs.io/en/2.5.1/reduction_toolbox.html)).
- **AAVSO’s Guide to CCD/CMOS Photometry with Monochrome Cameras, Version 1.0 (July 2022)** is unusually relevant to a small teaching observatory: it treats repeatability, background/skyglow, frame position, color, calibration, saturation, comparison/check stars, standard fields, and root-cause review after system changes. The authors describe it as a developing guide and distinguish scaled from unscaled calibration methods. Transfer its controls and diagnostic questions, not its camera-specific numeric settings or calibration counts ([AAVSO-CCD-CMOS-GUIDE-2022](https://aavso.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/CCD-CMOS-English.pdf)).
- **Lightkurve plus MAST/TESS** is a conditional no-new-observation alternative after coordinates are known. MAST lists calibrated FFIs, target-pixel files, and light curves. Its mission page lists 21 arcsecond pixels, so crowding, aperture contamination, cadence, passband, and sector coverage must be checked. Lightkurve’s reader exposes quality-bitmask and flux-column choices; the live docs say the default mask drops severe flags, hard can remove good cadences, hardest is not recommended, and the default light-curve column is pdcsap_flux. Version 2.5.0 release history includes reader/cadence/time-format fixes. Freeze the exact product, code version, column, and mask before comparing. Mission data are independent of the teaching camera but not automatically independent of a mission pipeline, and an unmatched cadence/passband or blended source makes a non-detection weak ([MAST-TESS-PRODUCTS](https://archive.stsci.edu/missions-and-data/tess/data-products); [MAST-TESS-MISSION](https://archive.stsci.edu/missions-and-data/tess); [TESS-SDPD-REV-F](https://archive.stsci.edu/missions/tess/doc/EXP-TESS-ARC-ICD-TM-0014.pdf); [LIGHTKURVE-API](https://lightkurve.github.io/lightkurve/reference/api/lightkurve.io.read.html); [LIGHTKURVE-2.5.0](https://github.com/lightkurve/lightkurve/releases/tag/v2.5.0)).

## Proposed authorized reanalysis

1. **Authorize and inventory.** Obtain read-only access to copies, not the archive itself. Make a manifest and SHA-256 for each raw image, calibration, and previously produced table. Record original file names, headers, timestamps, filter, exposure, gain/offset/readout/binning, temperature, camera/rotator angle, firmware/software release or commit, calibration-master IDs, and any edits. Keep original outputs and files unchanged.
2. **Reconstruct the timeline.** Establish dates for camera rotation, processing revision, calibration acquisition, exposure changes, observing routine, and any known lighting/weather changes. Verify actual FITS time keyword, clock time standard, site, target coordinates, and whether the timestamp is shutter start, end, or exposure midpoint. Do not convert to a barycentric time until inputs are known. JD, HJD, and BJD must identify the time standard; Eastman et al. recommend reporting site-arrival time with BJD_TDB for precise event timing ([EASTMAN-BJD-2010](https://arxiv.org/abs/1005.4415)).
3. **Recreate a baseline before optimizing.** If history is recoverable, reproduce the original reduction from the same frames and calibration masters. Record executable version, config, calibration order, masks, gain, dark scale, flat treatment, background estimator, aperture/annulus, centroid, ensemble, and exclusions. Save output checksums and a per-frame/per-star table. If history cannot be recovered, state that explicitly and establish a new documented baseline on copies; do not call it a reproduction.
4. **Test only a small, predeclared set of alternatives.** Compare historical versus documented calibration; fixed versus FWHM-scaled apertures; local background choices; original versus alternate ensemble; and leave-one-comparison-out runs. Keep all other parameters fixed within each pair. Compare raw flux, corrected flux, differential flux, uncertainty, local background, FWHM, centroids, detector coordinates, saturation and quality flags. Test standard stars and check stars separately from the suspected target. Use the detector-position, field-uniformity, and known-magnitude tests in the AAVSO guide to identify a measured performance problem before introducing a correction.
5. **Separate nuisance trends from source behavior.** Plot target and every comparison/check star by time, detector x/y, camera angle, exposure, airmass, FWHM, sky level, and reduction version. Check whether changes co-vary among stars or follow detector coordinates. Compare target-to-ensemble and each comparison-to-the-rest. Examine nightly offsets and background gradients before any fitted detrend. Do not select a new ensemble based on which one makes a visually preferred candidate curve unless the selection procedure is recorded and validated on a held-out star or night.
6. **Handle time dependence and selection.** Measurements adjacent in time are not automatically independent. Pont, Zucker, and Queloz show that correlated photometric noise can make uncertainty decrease more slowly than the white-noise n^-1/2 rule ([PONT-RED-NOISE-2006](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0608597)). Plot RMS versus bin size and inspect residual autocorrelation/cross-correlation with controls; use a block/covariance-aware simulation if significance is later needed. A Lomb–Scargle peak can be a sampling alias, and current Astropy false-alarm methods are approximations under a non-variable Gaussian-noise null ([VANDERPLAS-LS-2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.09824); [ASTROPY-LS-8](https://docs.astropy.org/en/stable/api/astropy.timeseries.LombScargle.html); [BALUEV-FAP-2008](https://arxiv.org/abs/0711.0330)). If students first select the most periodic-looking star from several stars or try multiple apertures, ensembles, detrends, or period ranges, a per-star/per-period false-alarm probability does not account for the full search. Predeclare a candidate and primary reduction, reserve nights or an independent data set for confirmation, or simulate the entire selection/search process with the observed sampling and correlated-noise structure. Do not treat repeated plotting or processing of the same images as independent evidence.
7. **Check independent data conditionally.** Only after astrometric identity is known, check MAST/TESS products and other suitable public time series. For TESS, establish sector overlap, cadence, pixel-level contamination, quality flags, and appropriate flux column; compare time coverage and passband to the proposed signal. No archive availability search has been run because the needed coordinates are absent.

## Proposed later observation pilot

A pilot is justified only if the existing frames lack recoverable calibrations/metadata or the archive diagnostics leave detector/environment effects unresolved. It is not authorized or scheduled by this proposal.

- Use a preselected target/check/comparison field, preferably with nearby color-similar stars and comparison stars spanning detector positions. Include a standard field with known magnitudes to check repeatability and residual field response.
- Hold filter, exposure, cadence, gain/readout, calibration recipe, and processing version fixed. Record shutter start/end or validated midpoint, camera temperature, focus, airmass, weather, sky/background level, camera angle, and local field-light state when available.
- Collect a stable-orientation baseline, then a planned camera/rotator-angle crossover with matched stars still in the field. Take or validate appropriate flats for the orientations; do not assume a flat from one camera angle corrects all orientations. If field lamps are a plausible contaminant, measure background and compare time-tagged light state; do not manipulate the field or infer causation from coincidence.
- Keep all relevant stars below the camera’s verified linearity/saturation limit and log every frame exclusion before interpreting the candidate. Preserve raw files and compare each source independently before differential ratios.
- Prediction: an instrumental effect should correlate with detector location, orientation, flat residual, or optical-field position across stars; a sky-level effect should co-vary with background/illumination measurements and affect an appropriate set of stars; an atmosphere/reference effect should covary with airmass, color, seeing, clouds, or the comparison ensemble; an intrinsic source signal should remain localized to the same identified star across the controlled changes and independent data. These are predictions, not current findings.

## Decision rule

- If the pattern disappears or changes materially under historically faithful versus controlled calibration/reduction, first resolve the processing path and do not classify the source.
- If standards/check stars show detector-, background-, exposure-, or atmosphere-linked residuals, quantify and document those performance limits; decide whether additional calibration or a pilot can resolve them.
- If calibrated measurements repeat across justified variants, standards support performance, controls do not share the source-specific pattern, timestamps and selection procedure are sound, and suitable independent observations agree, then the instructor may authorize a separate astrophysical classification stage.
- If metadata, timing, calibrations, coordinates, or independent coverage remain inadequate, report the ambiguity and stop short of classification.

A real photometric-accuracy statement requires measured standards/repeatability and reported uncertainty. A source-variability statement requires source-centered independent evidence. Neither can be supplied from this synthetic description.
