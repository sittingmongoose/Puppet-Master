# Independent critic review — D-R1-04-control

## Finding

The investigator package is a careful, usable research proposal for discussion with a qualified site partner. It keeps the hypothetical facts hypothetical, separates entry, storage, drawdown/infiltration, and downstream routing, compares several useful paths, and labels future field work as proposed. The central scientific reasoning is supported by the primary guidance and study records I checked. I found one material scope gap: the draft marks the explicit 60-minute research-window requirement fulfilled even while reporting that the investigator had only about 29m40s. The disclosure is good; it does not make the duration requirement fulfilled.

This review covers the full brief and exact revealed plan, the complete investigator discovery, draft, source map and source index listed by input-map.json. My independent source checks and their limits are recorded in [source-map.json](source-map.json) and [sources/index.md](sources/index.md). I did not inspect site records, conduct field work, execute a model or test, or draft/repair a final proposal.

## Independent primary-source checks

- **EPA Bioretention Design Handbook (C-01):** The 2023 handbook supports the draft’s attention to contributing-area delineation and grade, inlet sizing/placement and flow path, pretreatment, construction, operation, and maintenance. It also makes clear that design selection depends on site conditions and local requirements. The draft uses the handbook to frame questions and alternatives, not to transfer dimensions.
- **MPCA O&M and bioretention guidance (C-02, C-03):** The O&M table lists debris/leaves as possible flow-path blockage, mulch as a possible inlet/outlet/bypass clogging source, and sediment/pretreatment/loading as possible explanations for prolonged standing water. The 48-hour entry is presented in the source as guidance in a particular maintenance context, not a universal threshold. The draft explicitly leaves governing criteria to the local design/partner. The overview distinguishes bypass after a facility reaches ponding capacity from other routing and warns about erosion/energy dissipation; the draft does not assume bypass is necessarily a failure.
- **MPCA compaction guidance (C-04):** Construction traffic, equipment, storage and excavation can compact soil; wet/clay soils are more susceptible. Ponding may be an indicator, while measurements and methods have limitations and restoration is site-specific. The draft’s “plausible lead, not proof” treatment is appropriately cautious.
- **Queens study record (C-05):** The peer-reviewed article abstract reports variable capture, inlet bypass despite high infiltration at that particular sandy-soil, low-loading-ratio facility, and associations with duration, total precipitation and peak-hour intensity. It labels maintenance frequency as anecdotal/future work. The draft does not transfer the facility’s percentages or attribute this case’s behavior to the same cause.
- **USGS records (C-06):** The official Omaha report record describes two monitored gardens, event-dependent volume reduction at one, and no recorded overflow at the other; it is useful as an example of event variation, not a site prediction. I could not independently re-open the official report text through the browser, so I did not use its detailed event examples as an independently checked finding. The Madison official report result supports considering intensity and antecedent dry days at those purpose-built roof-fed gardens (C-07); those conditions do not establish this site’s soil or capacity.

These checks support the draft’s central distinctions, maintenance-history inquiry, and transfer caveats. They do not validate a local cause, geometry, performance, threshold, or repair.
