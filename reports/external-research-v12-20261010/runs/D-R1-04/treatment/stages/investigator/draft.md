# Investigator draft — uneven runoff entry into a demonstration rain garden

## Decision summary

The available description does not distinguish entry failure from a full garden, designed high-flow bypass, slow drainage, or changing storm conditions. The first useful proposal is an owner-approved records review followed by a short event-observation pilot that records entry, storage, drainage, and downstream effects separately. The proposal supports discussion with a qualified city/site partner. It does not select a repair, dimension a facility, or claim the garden is malfunctioning. If records confirm that some-event bypass is intended and the partner values safe routing over full capture for every storm, limited capture could be an acceptable observed baseline; that remains an owner decision.

Scenario details are hypothetical. “Appears to receive,” the volunteer mulch/leaf work, and possible delivery compaction remain unverified. This stage executed desk research only: no installation or maintenance records were obtained, no site was visited, and no field observations, infiltration or capacity tests, numerical water balance, or model run were performed. ## Mechanisms, evidence, and alternatives

### 1. Entry capture and surface routing

Record separately whether runoff reaches the curb opening, enters the garden, and bypasses it. Leaves, sediment, mulch, an obstructed gutter/edge, opening damage, local slope or grade can affect entry. A curb-cut practice may also be designed to accept ordinary flows and pass high flows down the gutter once ponding capacity is reached. Therefore, visible bypass is not by itself evidence of failure; compare it with as-built elevations, approach flow, event size, and whether the garden is already full. EPA guidance discusses opening placement/grading and site-dependent runnel or low-profile berm alternatives; these are topics for a qualified partner, not recommendations to alter this curb [S02, S03].

### 2. Temporary storage

For each event, note whether water arrives, the maximum observed ponding extent/level at a stable reference, whether it reaches an overflow, and the time it recedes. Without surveyed levels and a known stage-storage relationship, a photograph cannot show how much storage was available or whether the design volume was exceeded. An untimed photograph without rainfall/input context also cannot distinguish entry from later recession. Verify intended storage and overflow from records [S01, S02].

### 3. Infiltration and drainage

After entry, water may pass through engineered media to underlying soil, leave by an underdrain, remain temporarily in surface/media storage, or overflow. The underdrain, media, subgrade, and water-table conditions are unknown. Repeated slow recession could reflect wet antecedent conditions, surface sediment/clogging, limited storage, media or native-soil behavior, an outlet/drain issue, or more than one factor. A delivery-related compaction hypothesis needs history and qualified evidence. Minnesota and EPA O&M guidance uses ponding/drawdown observations as a reason to investigate, and calls attention to maintenance and sediment; it does not identify the cause at this site [S04, S05].

### 4. Downstream effects

Document observed bypass/overflow route and any erosion or local ponding from a safe, approved location. The drainage boundary and receiving conveyance are unknown. Do not infer flood protection or downstream benefit from a rain garden photo or a study at another site. Water quality sampling is outside the current question and has not been done.

### Conditional alternatives for partner discussion

- **Maintenance / soil-management inquiry:** obtain dates and descriptions of leaf clearing, mulch addition, sediment removal, street sweeping, foot traffic, planting, delivery route/equipment, and any work within the garden edge. Compare photographs or logs from before and after those activities. Routine debris removal and prevention of unnecessary traffic are potentially relevant, but no action should be taken by volunteers to manipulate the study. A surface observation cannot establish subsurface compaction.
- **Inlet / surface-routing inquiry:** first compare approach flow, opening position and slope, surveyed local grade, design bypass elevation, and whether storage was already full. Depending on those facts, an owner might consider routine cleaning, improved maintenance access, or having an engineer review routing/pretreatment alternatives. EPA guidance describes curb-cut grading, a runnel/berm for some steep-flow conditions, screens/grates, trench drains, and overflow/bypass features; local geometry, safety, maintenance burden, and approval govern suitability [S03].
- **Pretreatment inquiry:** select by observed flow regime, leaf/sediment type, space, and maintenance capacity. Minnesota guidance associates vegetated filter strips with sheet flow/small gardens, forebays with channelized flow, and screens with large debris [S07]. UMN's field-tested options included grass- and rock-lined inlets, Rain Guardian Bunker/Turret devices, and a shallow sump; reported capture varied by test intensity and overflow condition, and the summary found differing maintenance demands [S08]. Those results are comparison evidence, not a preferred product or a performance promise here.
- **Observation-first alternative:** preserve existing conditions except for the site's normal safety/maintenance duties, log those duties, and collect comparable event observations before proposing physical or soil intervention. This is the lowest-commitment way to learn whether the limiting process appears to be approach/entry, storage/overflow, or recession.

### Conditional water-balance use

Two linked balances can clarify the future measurement need:

- At the approach boundary: runoff arriving at the opening = volume entering the garden + volume bypassing the opening.
- At the garden boundary: volume entering + direct rainfall ≈ change in surface/media storage + overflow + underdrain flow + exfiltration + evapotranspiration + measurement residual.

A simple event water balance could be informative if the partner can define the boundary and obtain credible runoff/inflow, rainfall, storage geometry/levels, bypass/overflow, and underdrain data. It could show whether measured inputs/outputs close within uncertainty. It would not isolate infiltration if overflow, underdrain flow, storage change, or evapotranspiration are missing. With today's unknown catchment, geometry, rainfall, layers, and drain path, the terms cannot be populated; no site water balance or diagram was executed.

## Installation and maintenance history to investigate

Ask the city/site owner for original plans and as-builts, curb/inlet and overflow detail, intended contributing area, surveyed elevations, soil/media and underdrain specifications, construction/acceptance/commissioning inspection, planting and mulch records, and any original drawdown/infiltration checks or performance documentation. Ask who has maintenance responsibility, what access/observation permissions apply, and which local drawdown or bypass criterion governs.

Build a dated chronology from work orders, inspection reports, photos, volunteer notes, sweeping/cleaning, leaf and sediment removal, mulch type/quantity/date, plant replacement, and delivery/construction access. Verify whether the delivery entered the footprint or edge, its route and timing, and whether maintenance traffic or stockpiling occurred. Note evidence such as ruts, localized surface change, debris accumulation, erosion or trampling only if directly observed by the partner. If records are missing, record the gap and ask the partner to arrange a qualified survey/inspection; do not fill it with a literature value.

Evidence from other gardens transfers only as a hypothesis about mechanisms or useful measurements. Useful implementation reports should identify catchment, geometry, event conditions, operation, and measured outcomes; otherwise transfer is especially weak. The Minnesota materials are jurisdiction-specific guidance; the EPA handbooks are design/maintenance references. The UMN pretreatment trials tested five devices rather than this system's catchment/storage. The 2026 Beijing study monitored two roof-fed systems with different media and drainage boundaries; it supports recording storm and antecedent-storage conditions but not importing its performance numbers. Local geology, climate, storm distribution, hydraulic loading, installation age, maintenance, design intent, and measurement methods can change results [S01–S10].

## Proposed later field program

**Preparation.** Confirm owner priority (entry capture, drawdown, or both), permission, safe accessible vantage points, responsible observer, local applicable criteria, and emergency contact. Review records before deciding what event measurements are feasible. If public-access permission or safe vantage is absent, stop at records review and seek city-led observation.

**Pilot.** After approval, observe approximately four to six naturally occurring events to identify patterns, not to certify performance or estimate a long-term failure rate. Spread observations across the locally observed range of event depths/intensities and antecedent conditions; classify events from data collected, not assumed design storms. For each event record:

- date/time and rainfall source/resolution, event depth, duration and available short-interval intensity;
- preceding dry interval and available pre-event wetness indicator (such as a consistent safe visual soil-moisture class or permitted sensor);
- leaf/sediment/mulch condition, recent maintenance, season and relevant site activity;
- whether approach flow reaches, enters, or passes the opening, and whether the garden was already ponded;
- peak ponding relative to a stable reference installed by the responsible partner if permitted, visible overflow, and downstream bypass route;
- post-event visible water level/ponding at consistent safe times (for example soon after, then around 6, 24 and 48 hours), erosion, and any concurrent rainfall.

Use a nearby city or official rainfall gauge if a site gauge is not available, and explicitly flag distance/resolution mismatch. Do not send volunteers into a roadway or moving water during storms. Use fixed photo points only with permission and without recording members of the public. Do not install a gauge/logger without site approval.

**Comparisons.** Separate the entry, storage, recession, and downstream records. Compare events with similar rainfall and antecedent conditions; also compare clean versus debris-affected observations when they arise naturally. Record normal cleaning before/after an event. Do not leave a blockage in place, add/remove mulch for an experiment, or treat uncontrolled before/after storms as causal proof.

**Escalation and stop conditions.** Pause field observation if access is unapproved or unsafe, traffic/lightning/high flow prevents a safe vantage, or the partner identifies a public/surface hazard; refer the condition to the responsible city contact. If modest events bypass despite a clear approach/opening and available capacity, request qualified review of the as-built flow path and survey before any proposed routing change. If entry occurs but water repeatedly remains beyond the applicable local drawdown criterion, request qualified review of records, storage, underdrain, surface/media and native-soil conditions. Some guidance uses standing water at about 48 hours as a further-evaluation trigger; absent a local criterion, that may be a provisional discussion trigger only, not a compliance test or a diagnosis [S04, S05]. Any capacity/infiltration test or invasive inspection would require a separately authorized qualified partner; none is performed or required as the first step.

## Corrections, optional improvements, and owner decisions

**Corrections to the brief:** None. Its unknowns and hypothetical framing are retained. The possible compaction is not upgraded to a fact; bypass is not labeled a failure without design/event context.

**Optional improvements to the study brief:** Use a standard event sheet and fixed photo/reference points; collect rainfall at short intervals if available; log recent maintenance and antecedent wetness; use a staged records → visual/event observation → qualified capacity test sequence; define the system boundary before a water balance. The 4–6-event pilot is an illustrative scoping suggestion, not a statistical sufficiency claim.

**Owner/partner decisions required before field work:** Which outcome is primary; what level of bypass is acceptable for the site's actual purpose; which local design/drawdown criterion applies; who owns and maintains the feature; permissions and safe observation locations; whether rainfall/level equipment is allowed; which staff can supply plans and work history; what budget and evidence threshold would justify a survey or later test; and whether the partner wants any model after real inputs are recovered.

## Executed work versus proposed validation

**Executed in this reference stage:** desk research of the cited primary guidance, official project summary, software manual/release history, and one recent study's publisher-indexed abstract/methods; a conceptual mechanism and measurement proposal. The discovery was frozen by the assigned release helper before the exact plan was read.
