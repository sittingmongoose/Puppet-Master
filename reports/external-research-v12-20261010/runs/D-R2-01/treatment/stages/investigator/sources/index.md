## S01 — Guttation: A Pressure Relief for Plants

- Source/version: University of Missouri Integrated Pest Management extension article; Published 2009-06-01; no later revision shown.
- URL: <https://ipm.missouri.edu/meg/2009/6/Guttation-A-Pressure-Relief-for-Plants/index.cfm>
- Locator: Night-condition/hydathode discussion; rendered lines 35–52.
- Observed operation: Describes guttation as liquid water exuded from hydathodes at leaf margins/tips when transpiration is low and root pressure moves water under high humidity, cool air, and warm soil. Notes that droplets can resemble dew.
- Conditions/exceptions: A margin/tip pattern is consistent with guttation but not conclusive. The article notes rare bacterial entry via droplets and possible edema association under prolonged humidity/excess moisture; neither is evidenced here.
- Applicability: Mechanism only. Species is unknown; morphology and frequency cannot identify the droplets. Compare air/leaf dew-point conditions, irrigation timing/splash, and plant records.

## S02 — Controlling the Greenhouse Environment

- Source/version: University of Alaska Fairbanks Cooperative Extension publication HGA-00336; PDF filename carries 2024-11-27; document HGA-00336.
- URL: <https://www.uaf.edu/ces/publications/database/gardening/files/pdfs/HGA-00336-Controlling%20GreenHouse%2011-27-24.pdf>
- Locator: Printed pp. 9–11, Air Circulation, Humidity Control, and Ventilation and Airflow; rendered lines 601–697.
- Observed operation: Explains mixing, dew-point condensation, and canopy variation; distinguishes internal horizontal airflow (HAF) from air exchange. HAF mixes local air; ventilation removes moist air.
- Conditions/exceptions: HAF is not a substitute for air exchange; airflow must cross/penetrate canopy and fan operation should coordinate with exhaust. Numerical sizing and Alaska season context are not universal setpoints.
- Applicability: Identify the changed fan's actual function and map microclimate. Sizing advice is not directly transferable without greenhouse, climate, crop, and system details.

## S03 — Greenhouse Ventilation

- Source/version: UF/IFAS Extension engineering publication AE-10; DOI 10.32473/edis-ae030-2019; edition AE-10 (2019 DOI edition).
- URL: <https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/AE030/pdf>
- Locator: PDF pp. 1–3, winter ventilation, condensation, and summer ventilation; rendered lines 0–108.
- Observed operation: Defines ventilation as removing indoor air and replacing it with outside air. Explains condensation when a surface is below air dew point and that ventilation may reduce humidity but raise winter heat demand.

- Applicability: Separates circulation from exhaust and cautions against prescribing a fan schedule without weather, system, crop, and heating context.

## S09 — Implementation of Sensor-based Automated Irrigation in Commercial Floriculture Production: A Case Study

- Source/version: Peer-reviewed HortTechnology commercial greenhouse case study by Wheeler et al.; HortTechnology 28(6), pp. 719–727, December 2018; DOI 10.21273/HORTTECH04114-18.
- URL: <https://doi.org/10.21273/HORTTECH04114-18>
- Locator: Abstract and water-use/setpoint/staff-operation discussion; PDF pp. 1 and 4–6.
- Observed operation: Two years of side-by-side poinsettia/geranium trials found no irrigation-water reduction in this grower-controlled sensor setting. The discussion links this partly to repeated upward setpoint changes reflecting historical timer-based habits despite training.
- Conditions/exceptions: Commercial poinsettia and geranium, not culinary herbs. System, substrate, season, staff and crop targets differ; labor changed and crop-specific growth differences were also reported.
- Applicability: Operator behavior can alter results. Recover who changed the timer/fan, why, exact settings and access/override history; a new system alone does not guarantee intended response.

## S10 — Implementation of Soil Moisture Sensor Based Automated Irrigation in Woody Ornamental Production

- Source/version: Peer-reviewed Journal of Environmental Horticulture commercial nursery case study by Wheeler et al.; Journal of Environmental Horticulture 38(1), pp. 1–7, March 2020; DOI 10.24266/0738-2898-38.1.1.
- URL: <https://doi.org/10.24266/0738-2898-38.1.1>
- Locator: Abstract and discussion of crop grouping, overhead capture, salt buildup and staff access; PDF pp. 1 and 4–6.
- Observed operation: A 2014–2015 trial reported about 50% less irrigation overall; several woody crops had similar growth/losses, while >50% Rhododendron loss occurred in 2015. - Conditions/exceptions: Woody plants, bark media, overhead capture, and nursery production differ materially from herbs. Proposed causal explanations are not universal thresholds.
- Applicability: Do not pool crop ages/species/substrates or transfer a water-saving result. Record crop identity, canopy, pot, delivery, season, water and operator history.
