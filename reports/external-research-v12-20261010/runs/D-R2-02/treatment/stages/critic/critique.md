## SCW-1 — Codex logger location

**Exact draft claim and context:** Under “Comparable implementation history and transfer limits,” S1 describes the Codex transport code and then states: “Its illustrative small air-recorder location is in a likely warm zone near the top, side wall and rear door, away from discharge air.”

**Intended reading C:** The code’s general instruction is to place an available air recorder between packages in the warmest area; recorder-company guidance then gives a top/side-wall/rear-area example away from discharge air.

**Diagnostic C*; one selector delta:** Change only the load-position relation, from *between packages in the warmest area* to *loose on top of the load*. Do not infer that the two mountings are equivalent.

**Evidence identity and scope:** S1, CAC/RCP 44-1995, Amendment 1-2004, official FAO-hosted PDF, §2.19, p. 155; https://www.fao.org/4/a1389e/a1389e00.pdf; accessed 2026-10-10T07:19:19Z. Scope-bearing excerpt: “between packages in the area where the warmest temperatures occur”; “one-third of the way in from the rear doors.” The first is the code’s placement criterion; the second is part of the recorder-company example.

**C relation — ENTAILS:** The source supports an air recorder at a likely warm location and supplies the stated top/side/rear coordinates. It does not make that air reading a product measurement.

**C* relation — UNDETERMINED:** The source’s company example says “on top of the load,” but neither that phrase nor the code specifies that the logger should be loose or unsecured. The source does not settle attachment state.

**Disposition:** Qualify the attribution and locator. Supported replacement: “Codex §2.19 places an available air recorder between packages in the warmest area; recorder-company guidance gives a top, side-wall, one-third-in-from-rear example away from direct discharge air.” Then keep mounting/attachment as a case-specific placement detail.  
**Affected obligation:** Useful measurement-placement alternative; avoid treating the original loose logger as product temperature.

## SCW-2 — 2022 route-study sensor subsystem

**Exact draft claim and context:** Under S4, the draft says the study “contextualizes readings by crate and route,” and explicitly limits them to near-crate air rather than product-core temperature or delivered quality.

**Intended reading C:** The study used distributed temperature/relative-humidity modules mapped to crate locations during a multi-stop route; those environmental readings contextualize crate exposure, not product core or quality.

**Diagnostic C*; one selector delta:** Change only the measured subsystem from *crate-near air* to *product core*. Keep the route, crate map, and study conditions unchanged.

**Evidence identity and scope:** S4, *Processes* 2022, 10(11), 2286, version of record 4 November 2022; https://doi.org/10.3390/pr10112286; accessed 2026-10-10T07:19:19Z. MDPI’s publisher page identifies “eighteen wireless traceability devices with temperature and relative humidity sensors” monitoring crates over five stops (4 h 30 m). §§2.1–2.4 describe the SHT30 modules and crate-position mapping; the study’s results also discuss stop/opening and refrigeration context. Direct page open returned HTTP 429 in this review; the evidence used is the publisher’s indexed article text and version record.

**C relation — ENTAILS:** The publisher text describes crate-positioned T/RH modules and a route map, supporting the stated implementation comparison.

**C* relation — CONTRADICTS:** The described sensors are environmental T/RH modules assigned crate positions, not probes inserted in produce tissue. The study does not supply product-core or delivered-quality readings.

**Disposition:** Retain the draft’s limitation; no replacement needed.  
**Affected obligation:** Distinguish logger air readings from actual product temperature and quality.

## SCW-3 — 2023 PCM result endpoint

**Exact draft claim and context:** Under S5, the draft says PCM reduced fluctuation in orange crates on one winter route with refrigeration off, and adds that the small air sensors do not demonstrate product-core or quality benefit.

**Intended reading C:** The reported result is lower temperature variation in ventilated sensor modules placed among oranges in PCM crates relative to empty-alveoli controls, in the stated single-route conditions.

**Diagnostic C*; one selector delta:** Change only the measured outcome from *air temperature around oranges inside crates* to *orange core temperature*. Keep the route, PCM, and control comparison unchanged.

**Evidence identity and scope:** S5, *Energies* 2023, 16(13), 5191, version of record 5 July 2023; https://doi.org/10.3390/en16135191; accessed 2026-10-10T07:23:56Z. §§2.1–2.4 and Tables 2–3 describe orange crates, RT5HC PCM versus empty alveoli, SHT30 air sensors in perforated boxes positioned among produce, and a separate cargo-air sensor; §§3–4 report crate-sensor variation and the finite phase-change reserve. The paper reports the temperature variation as “less than 1 °C” for PCM boxes versus “up to 3 °C” for controls. Conditions include one insulated van route, refrigeration off in winter, six unloading stops, and reported 1–10 °C ambient conditions.

**C relation — ENTAILS:** Those measurements support reduced variation in the crate air-sensor readings for that setup. The excerpt explicitly identifies air temperature and sensor interaction with ambient air.

**C* relation — CONTRADICTS:** The methods identify ventilated SHT30 modules that read air, placed among the produce, not probes in orange cores. The study’s result does not measure core temperature or delivered quality.

**Disposition:** Retain the explicit limitation. No supported replacement is needed. The study is a package-air field analogue, not a transferred efficacy estimate.  
**Affected obligation:** Compare passive packaging while keeping air, product, and quality endpoints separate.

## SCW-4 — artificial-fruit performance scope

**Exact draft claim and context:** Under S6, the draft says the prototype was within 5% of ten apples in that test, whereas water-filled simulators differed by up to 16%; it uses this only to motivate evaluating a crop-matched surrogate.

**Intended reading C:** The reported performance applies to the tested artificial apple during cooling against ten real apples, with the water-filled comparison as reported.

**Diagnostic C*; one selector delta:** Change only product scope from *apple* to *all produce types*. Keep the reported performance figures unchanged.

**Evidence identity and scope:** S6, *Journal of Food Engineering* 215 (December 2017), pp. 51–60, publisher abstract/version-of-record record; https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfoodeng.2017.07.012; accessed 2026-10-10T07:19:25Z. The ScienceDirect abstract describes a fruit-of-interest-specific shell and mixture and an artificial apple compared during cooling with ten apples. It reports cooling time “within 5%” and water-filled simulator “differences up to 16%.” The full article endpoint was not accessible here; this verdict uses the publisher abstract only.

**C relation — ENTAILS:** The publisher abstract states the apple comparison and reported figures.

**C* relation — UNDETERMINED:** The source neither tests nor establishes the same figures across all produce types. That missing cross-species result is not evidence that a generic simulator would fail.

**Disposition:** Retain the crop-matched and validation qualifiers; no replacement needed.  
**Affected obligation:** Useful measurement-placement alternative without inventing a generic sensor response or product type.
