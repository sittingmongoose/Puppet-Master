# Temperature excursions during a cooperative produce route

Case ID: ER12-D-R2-02-FRESH

Domain: cold-chain logistics

## Project situation

A produce cooperative uses insulated crates on a morning delivery route. A few logger traces reportedly show brief warm excursions near the middle of the route, but the logger sometimes travels loose on top of the load. Delivery order changed recently, and customers also began receiving smaller batches. The cooperative wants a research proposal before purchasing powered cooling. There are no raw traces, product-temperature readings, route records, or verified quality outcomes. The project setting is synthetic.

## Request

Spend the 60-minute research window developing a proposal to investigate the excursions and compare feasible ways to improve route conditions. Explain the transport and measurement mechanisms, research relevant implementation history, and propose a later pilot with defensible endpoints. Provide a useful alternative if powered cooling is unjustified. Do not build a routing system or issue operating instructions for today's shipments.

## Requirements to preserve

- Distinguish logger air readings, actual product temperature, delivered quality, and food safety.
- Compare passive packaging or preconditioning changes with route or handling changes and a useful measurement-placement alternative.
- Consider door or crate opening, load size, initial conditions, route timing, and sensor response rather than treating every short spike as a product excursion.
- Investigate the route and packaging history and the conditions reported by comparable implementations before transferring their claimed benefits.
- Propose a pilot with a baseline, traceable shipments, relevant measurements, practical costs, and a decision criterion; label future validation separately from analysis actually executed.

## Boundaries

- Do not declare safe storage limits, shelf life, compliance, or product suitability from unverified air-temperature traces.
- Do not alter deliveries, enter customer systems, contact customers, purchase cooling equipment, or run a live trial.
- Do not invent route timings, thermal properties, produce types, logger response, or quality results.

## Still unresolved

Product types, initial temperatures, crate construction, logger specifications, packing practice, stop durations, outdoor conditions, and acceptable quality loss are unknown. There is no supplied cost ceiling or agreement that route order may change. Identify which questions must be resolved before alternatives can be fairly compared.

## Research question and incompleteness

Can a bounded thermal and operational study determine whether the reported excursions represent an actionable product problem and which low-complexity intervention is worth testing?

The product-specific endpoint, access to measurements, acceptable operational variation, and cost comparison remain open. The proposal should sequence measurement interpretation and intervention testing so that equipment expenditure does not become the assumed solution.

## Meaningful useful alternatives

Consider improving logger placement and interpretation, crate packing or insulation, preconditioning, managing openings, departure timing, and delivery-order or load-size changes. Powered cooling is one option to investigate against energy, capital, handling, and maintenance costs. Better measurement with unchanged operations is a useful alternative if the apparent signal is not yet tied to product conditions. No cooling or routing strategy is the expected outcome.

## Mechanism opportunity

Investigate heat transfer, initial thermal state, thermal mass, opening exposure, sensor response and placement, and the relationship between air and product observations. An air spike may reflect a real exposure without establishing the magnitude of product heating. Smaller batches and different route order can change multiple factors simultaneously. Explain discriminating evidence and limits of any simplified thermal model.

## Implementation and history opportunity

Seek implementation reports with actual products, packing, weather, dwell times, measurement placement, route practices, and quality endpoints. Procurement claims about insulation or cooling require operating context. Investigate past route manifests and packing routines as future evidence; recollection of a changed route is not a validated causal comparison. Consider operational failures and ongoing maintenance, not only initial equipment performance.

## Proposed versus executed validation

A later authorized pilot could document shipments, initial conditions, selected air and product measurements where feasible, openings and stops, and quality observations defined by an appropriate partner. Explain repeated routes, comparable loads and weather, sensor checks, and how costs enter the decision. An assumed heat-balance illustration or toy trace analysis actually executed in the research hour cannot establish real product temperatures, food safety, or delivered-quality improvement.
