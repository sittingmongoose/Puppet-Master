### S01 — The Commercial Storage of Fruits, Vegetables, and Florist and Nursery Stocks

**Primary record:** [https://www.ars.usda.gov/is/np/CommercialStorage/CommercialStorage.pdf](https://www.ars.usda.gov/is/np/CommercialStorage/CommercialStorage.pdf)
**Version:** Agriculture Handbook 66, revised February 2016
**Locator:** Chapter 'Precooling and Storage Facilities'; PDF pages 20–24, especially 'Initial Cooling Methods', 'Forced-Air Cooling', and 'Hydrocooling' (PDF p.20 lines 994–1053 and pp.22–23 lines 1060–1173 in web text view).
**Observed report/method:** Separates initial cooling from transport; says special facilities remove produce heat, highway trailers have insufficient airflow for dependable initial cooling, and forced-air performance depends on airflow, produce diameter, vents and internal packaging.
**Conditions and exceptions:** Commodity and package dependent. Forced air is broadly adaptable; hydrocooling needs products that tolerate water and water-resistant packaging; vacuum cooling is for rapid moisture-releasing crops; room cooling has exceptions including certain citrus and CA-stored apples. Avoid transferring numeric storage tables to unspecified produce.
**Use and limits here:** Supports asking about initial product condition and evaluating product-compatible preconditioning, ventilation and packaging before buying transport cooling. General handbook, not cooperative measurements, not a current product-specific specification, and not evidence of safety or a specific temperature limit.

### S02 — Protecting Perishable Foods During Transport by Truck or Rail

**Primary record:** [https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/HS1328](https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/HS1328)
**Version:** Current web publication viewed 2026-10-10; page does not display a publication/revision date. Preface identifies it as a revision of Handbook 669 (last revised 1995), which revised the 1987 edition and superseded Handbook 105 first published in 1956 and revised in 1970.
**Locator:** Preface lines 130–136; section I 'Telematics' and 'Air Temperature vs. Product Temperature' lines 483–499; section II 'Temperature Recording and Tracking' lines 802–823; section III 'Type of Air Delivery System' lines 831–840.
**Observed report/method:** Explains that air-space readings can change faster than product temperatures; recommends date/time plus documented logger location and gives three-position guidance for trailer/container mapping (front, center, rear). Describes air-delivery and package/stack effects.
**Conditions and exceptions:** Temperature and correct placement depend on cargo location, commodity, air-delivery type, package and loading. Three positions are transport-mapping guidance, not a universal minimum or placement prescription for a small insulated-crate route.
**Use and limits here:** Supports paired air/in-crate/product measurements, position labeling, route and load event records, and treating logger channels as different measurements. Extension handbook guidance, not an experiment on this vehicle or produce; it contains commodity guidance not used here to set any threshold. Food-safety sections are not used to infer safety.

### S03 — Identifying cooling heterogeneity during precooling and refrigerated trailer transport for the citrus supply chain by extensive temperature monitoring within full-scale experiments

**Primary record:** [https://research.wur.nl/en/publications/identifying-cooling-heterogeneity-during-precooling-and-refrigera/](https://research.wur.nl/en/publications/identifying-cooling-heterogeneity-during-precooling-and-refrigera/)
**Version:** Version of record published November 2024; volume 165, article 110672; DOI 10.1016/j.foodcont.2024.110672.
**Locator:** Abstract and record metadata lines 25–37; article DOI and CC BY access links lines 34–52.
**Observed report/method:** Measured air and fruit temperatures with 108 sensors in one Greece–Switzerland commercial citrus shipment and added rear-trailer measurements in 30 shipments; reports room-precooled fruit not uniformly at study target after 24 h and some middle-of-trailer reheating associated with insufficient pallet ventilation.
**Conditions and exceptions:** Large refrigerated trailer, citrus, specific precooling and export route; the paper's setpoint is specific to this study. Air and fruit values are spatially variable.
**Use and limits here:** Shows why initial cooling, airflow, pallet/crate configuration and mapped positions should be part of the investigation. Not a short cooperative route, not the unspecified produce or insulated-crate system here, and does not establish a transferable temperature target.

### S04 — Thermal Management of Short-Range Distribution of Perishable Food Products Using Phase Change Materials in Packaging: Real-Time Field Data Acquisition

**Primary record:** [https://ubibliorum.ubi.pt/server/api/core/bitstreams/c3835e14-2d0e-4a27-9c1e-fd2bd93ff81b/content](https://ubibliorum.ubi.pt/server/api/core/bitstreams/c3835e14-2d0e-4a27-9c1e-fd2bd93ff81b/content)
**Version:** Version of record, Energies 2023, 16, 5191; DOI 10.3390/en16135191; open CC BY.
**Locator:** Methods pp.3–8 (crate/PCM, orange loading, van and sensors); results pp.9–11; conclusions p.11. Relevant web text lines 147–153, 219–256, 263–315, 383–386, 461–480, 683–705, 713–733.
**Observed report/method:** Compared reusable PCM alveoli with empty alveoli in orange crates on a short delivery route; temperature/humidity sensors were in and near produce, sampled/transmitted every five minutes, with ambient and rear/front crate positions. The abstract reports less temperature variation with PCM; unload spikes were observed.
**Conditions and exceptions:** Orange crates stored around 5 °C overnight, bespoke 600×400×90 mm PP crate, about 48 oranges per crate, PCM transition around 5–6 °C, insulated IVECO Daily van, winter run with refrigeration off, crates near right wall and rear door, mixed customer-order cargo. The abstract describes 8 h while methods list 08:00–14:05. Sensors measured surrounding air, not pulp; paper identifies PCM quantity/autonomy, route/crop/climate fit and reuse as open issues.
**Use and limits here:** Strong comparator for short-route PCM inserts, logging positions, stop/open events and passive package buffering. Single route/context with crate-air readings and no measured delivered-quality endpoint; no direct evidence for this crop, crate, weather, route, reefer status or economics.

### S05 — Artificial fruit for monitoring the thermal history of horticultural produce in the cold chain

**Primary record:** [https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0260877417303035](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0260877417303035)
**Version:** Published December 2017; volume 215, pp.51–60; DOI 10.1016/j.jfoodeng.2017.07.012.
**Locator:** Abstract and introduction in the article record; verification comparison is between one artificial apple and ten real apples during cooling.
**Observed report/method:** A fruit-shaped, biomimetic thermal simulator contained its own logger; the prototype's apple cooling time was within 5% of ten real apples and was more representative than water-filled simulators, whose response differed by up to 16%. It can be packed at multiple cargo positions.
**Conditions and exceptions:** Validation was for an apple-like prototype during cooling, with a target-specific shell/filling and thermal response. A simulator must be matched to the crop and operation.
**Use and limits here:** A plausible measurement-placement alternative for later repeated core-like temperature mapping without relying on a loose top logger. Does not validate a simulator for unknown vegetables or an actual route. It estimates thermal history, not quality or safety.

### S06 — Heat and mass exchanging processes in a refrigerated truck body during multi-drop urban distribution of fresh herbs

**Primary record:** [https://revista.sangregorio.edu.ec/index.php/REVISTASANGREGORIO/article/view/1251](https://revista.sangregorio.edu.ec/index.php/REVISTASANGREGORIO/article/view/1251)
**Version:** Published 2020-04-04; Vol. 1, No. 37, Special Edition March 2020; original research article.
**Locator:** Publisher abstract lines 31–46; publication date and issue metadata lines 97–110.
**Observed report/method:** Two-stage study in an 11 m³ refrigerated truck body with dill and parsley; measured air temperature, air velocity and humidity under different door-open durations and assessed herbs after transit/storage. Its abstract reports rapid air/moisture entry after opening.
**Conditions and exceptions:** Specific herb, vehicle and experimental sequence; a heat calculation used an empty body; the paper's microbiological results are not treated as safety evidence.
**Use and limits here:** Supports logging door/opening duration and route-stop events as covariates, not assuming a short spike means core temperature changed. A single specific truck/herb setup; not applicable for numeric thermal/quality/safety predictions for this case.

### S08 — Cooling efficiency and effectiveness of single-crate evaporative cooler for selected fresh produce

**Primary record:** [https://www.ukdr.uplb.edu.ph/journal-articles/5138/](https://www.ukdr.uplb.edu.ph/journal-articles/5138/)
**Version:** August 2018; volume 43, issue 2, pp.47–55; repository record 5138.
**Locator:** Abstract and issue metadata lines 26–39 and 40–58.
**Observed report/method:** Tested a portable evaporative crate made by wrapping a plastic crate with duck cloth for selected lettuce, rambutan, bitter gourd and eggplant; reports cooling/quality effects in those tests.
**Conditions and exceptions:** Evaporative cooling depends on crop and ambient conditions, including evaporative potential; the available abstract does not provide enough local RH/route/start-temperature detail to transfer benefits.
**Use and limits here:** Screening lead for a non-powered option only if crop, climate, water access and handling conditions match. Full methods unavailable in the accessed repository record; not recommended or treated as a proven alternative for this cooperative.

### S09 — Evaporative cooling with a wet fabric blanket for non-refrigerated horticultural produce transport: An experimental study

**Primary record:** [https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666154324003764](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666154324003764)
**Version:** Version of record, volume 18 (December 2024), article 101339; DOI 10.1016/j.jafr.2024.101339; CC BY.
**Locator:** Abstract and methods summary: full-scale Thai cargo chamber; 29–30 °C and 70–73% RH; airflow 0.8–3.6 m/s; three-hour test; every-minute thermocouple/hygrometer readings; lettuce mass-loss comparison.
**Observed report/method:** Evaluated a wet fabric blanket as a passive evaporative transport medium. The thermal surrogate load used hollow plastic balls; the abstract also reports lettuce mass-loss observations. Air temperature fell by about 3–4 °C under the studied condition.
**Conditions and exceptions:** Tropical fixed climate and simulated transport airflow; exact result depends on external humidity, airflow, water management, package/load and crop. Thermal surrogate is not actual produce core.
**Use and limits here:** A non-powered alternative to screen if local product, climate, airflow, water availability and handling match; include as a controlled pilot option only after baseline. One chamber experiment and lettuce mass-loss endpoint; no proof for cooperative route or unspecified crop; no safety inference.

### S10 — Full-scale experiments in forced-air precoolers for citrus fruit: Impact of packaging design and fruit size on cooling rate and heterogeneity

**Primary record:** [https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1537511017306797](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1537511017306797)
**Version:** Version of record published May 2018; volume 169, pp.115–125; DOI 10.1016/j.biosystemseng.2018.02.003.
**Locator:** Search-result record, abstract and conclusion; study ran commercial forced-air precooling in a 40-pallet facility using Navel oranges, Nova mandarins and Eureka lemons, package/wrap conditions and high-resolution pallet sensors.
**Observed report/method:** Full-scale commercial forced-air experiments found cooling heterogeneity mainly along airflow direction; wrapping slowed cooling and increased heterogeneity. Package/fruit-size comparisons showed cooling-rate differences up to 42% for particular citrus/package comparisons.
**Conditions and exceptions:** Specific commercial facility, citrus types, forced-air cooling and carton designs; differences depend on fruit size, airflow direction, package vents, wrapping and pallet stacking. This is pre-cooling evidence, not a short insulated-crate route test.
**Use and limits here:** Supports inspecting crate vents, liners/wrap, packing and air paths, and testing package changes under matched loads before claiming that insulation or ventilation helps. Citrus and a commercial forced-air precooler; search abstract only was read. Do not transfer its cooling rates to this route or crop.

**Independent reviser check (2026-10-10):** The ScienceDirect publisher search result exposed the abstract stating the 42% comparison. Direct opens returned 403; no full text was accessed. The compared observations use different citrus fruit and cartons, so the 42% contrast is not treated as a package-only effect.

### S11 — A phase change material (PCM) based passively cooled container for integrated road-rail cold chain transportation – An experimental study

**Primary record:** [https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1359431121006438](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1359431121006438)
**Version:** Version of record, August 2021, volume 195, article 117204; DOI 10.1016/j.applthermaleng.2021.117204.
**Locator:** Publisher abstract and highlights: integrated road–rail refrigerated container with PCM cold-energy storage plates, separate charging facility, four types of fresh produce, monitored charging/discharge and product quality; article reports discharge duration up to 94.6 h.
**Observed report/method:** Built and tested a PCM-based container with storage plates and a separate charging facility that charges the plates. Compared operation with diesel-powered refrigerated container and assessed four fresh fruit/vegetable types.
**Conditions and exceptions:** Vehicle-scale road–rail use and separately charged plates; four crops and long-distance conditions are mentioned but not specified in the accessible abstract. Claimed duration/cost/energy results belong to that setup only.
**Use and limits here:** Shows that passive thermal storage still depends on an upstream charging/conditioning operation and sufficient thermal capacity; useful background for recording conditioning labor/energy and autonomy. Abstract-only source access; vehicle-scale long road–rail implementation is not a small-route crate insert and no result is transferred to this case.
