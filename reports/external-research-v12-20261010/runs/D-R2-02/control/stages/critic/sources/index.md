## S01 — USDA-ARS Agriculture Handbook 66

[Open source](https://www.ars.usda.gov/is/np/CommercialStorage/CommercialStorage.pdf). The “Initial Cooling Methods” passage says produce is cooled in facilities designed to remove heat and that highway trailers have insufficient airflow for dependable initial cooling. The handbook makes method selection commodity- and package-dependent. It does not supply a universal temperature target for this unknown produce.

## S02 — UF/IFAS HS1328

[Open source](https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/HS1328). The page states that air-space readings change faster than product temperatures and explains cargo microenvironments. It offers three-position recorder guidance for trailer/container loads and product-reading recommendations. These are contextual guidance, not a mandatory layout for one small insulated-crate route.

## S03 — Full-scale citrus shipment monitoring

[Open source](https://research.wur.nl/en/publications/identifying-cooling-heterogeneity-during-precooling-and-refrigera/). The abstract identifies 108 sensors in one Greece–Switzerland shipment and rear measurements across 30 additional shipments. It reports that some fruit had not reached the study’s 4–6 °C target after 24 h of room precooling and that some fruit in the trailer middle reheated above 10 °C with insufficient pallet ventilation. These are reported conditions/findings from that specific citrus supply chain, not this route.

## S04 — Short-range PCM crate field study

[Open publisher article](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/16/13/5191). The paper’s abstract reports an 8-hour field test; its methods describe loading from 08:00 to 14:05. Its orange-crate comparison used an insulated van with the reefer off during the winter field test; crates were held near 5 °C overnight. Sensors were in/near the crates, so readings are not pulp temperatures or delivered-quality measures. Unloading spikes and lower variation with PCM are reported for this setup. Reuse, autonomy, quantity, and route/climate/product matching remain limitations.

## S05 — Artificial fruit simulator

[Open publisher record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0260877417303035). The abstract reports an artificial apple’s cooling time within 5% of ten real apples and up to 16% differences for water-filled simulators. This is prototype validation for apple-like cooling, not the unspecified produce or heating response in the cooperative route.

## S06 — Multi-drop herb-truck study

[Open publisher page](https://revista.sangregorio.edu.ec/index.php/REVISTASANGREGORIO/article/view/1251). The study describes an 11 m³ refrigerated body carrying dill and parsley and measures air temperature, velocity, and humidity with different door-open durations. Its empty-body heat calculation and specific herb/truck conditions are not a product-response estimate here.

## S07 — Reusable plastic-crate guide

[Open FAO record](https://www.fao.org/4/i0930e/i0930e00.htm). The official abstract identifies good handling and storage practices, crate cleaning/sanitization protocols, and a returnable-crate management model. It is a history checklist source, not a thermal test or proof of the cooperative’s crate type.

## S08 — Single-crate evaporative cooling study

[Open university repository record](https://www.ukdr.uplb.edu.ph/journal-articles/5138/). The abstract reports a duck-cloth-wrapped plastic crate tested with lettuce, rambutan, bitter gourd, and eggplant. The repository marks the full document unavailable, so its detailed conditions cannot be checked there. The candidate correctly treats it as a screening lead.

## S09 — Wet-fabric evaporative transport study

[Open publisher page](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666154324003764). The abstract describes a real-scale Thai cargo chamber at 29–30 °C and 70–73% RH, airflow of 0.8–3.6 m/s, a three-hour test with hollow plastic balls as the main load, and a separate lettuce mass-loss comparison. The reported air-temperature reduction and lettuce result do not establish produce-core outcomes for another climate/route.

## S10 — Forced-air citrus packaging study

[Open publisher record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1537511017306797) · [accepted manuscript metadata](https://www.empa.ch/documents/33059/0/Wu-2018-Full-scale_experiments_in_forced-air_precoolers-%28accepted_version%29.pdf/d4064f9d-f84f-4158-a12d-55819ff6f6ef). The record describes a 40-pallet commercial forced-air precooler, three citrus types, package/wrapping/fruit-size comparisons, and heterogeneity along airflow. The 42% contrast reported in the abstract compares Nova mandarins in Opentop cartons with Eureka lemons in Supervent cartons, so it does not isolate a package-only effect. Full-text access was unavailable during this review.

## S11 — Road–rail PCM container

[Open publisher record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1359431121006438). The abstract describes cold-energy storage plates charged by a separate facility in a vehicle-scale road–rail container. The reported runtime and operating comparisons belong to that equipment and route; they do not predict performance of an insulated-crate insert.
