# Independent discovery — ER12-D-R1-02-FRESH

## Basis and scope
This discovery is based only on the released case brief at `control/inputs/brief.md`. The exact plan has not been released or consulted. The scenario supplies no raw traces, sensor/logger model or configuration, track survey, fault inspection, wheel condition, speeds, loads, or alert logic. The recent bracket move is a temporal association, not a causal finding. No cart, track, live railway, logger, or physical installation was accessed; no data simulation or check was executed.

## Interpret the observation in three layers
1. **Unusual-signal detection:** a logger warning means only that the configured device or rule marked a signal unusual. Until its rule, full scale, sample clock, filter, threshold, and firmware are known, it cannot be compared across days.
2. **Repeatable-event location:** a signal can be tied to the bend only if replicated passes align to the same surveyed route position or independently marked point, with direction, speed, and timing checked. A time peak by itself is not a track coordinate.
3. **Physical diagnosis:** identifying a wheel, bearing, bracket, cart, or track defect needs independent physical evidence and qualified inspection. Vibration alone, including an event that repeats near the bend, is not a diagnosis or safety authorization.

## Mechanisms and competing explanations
The measured acceleration is the motion of the surface carrying the sensor, transformed by wheel/track contact, cart frame or suspension, bracket stiffness and resonance, sensor orientation and coupling, and the logger's range, filtering, sampling, timestamps, and warning logic. The bracket was moved before warnings rose, so changed coupling, orientation, fasteners, or bracket flex is a credible measurement-chain explanation. A moving or compliant mount can change frequency response; the cited ISO and vendor evidence describes that dependency (S01–S03). A rail-bogie bracket field study also found a bracket mode implicated in fatigue under its own high-frequency wheel–rail excitation; this is a mechanism example, not evidence about this cart (S12).

Track input, wheel/caster condition, speed through the bend, path, load mass and distribution, and background motor/drive/brake behavior can interact. Speed can change vehicle response and time-to-distance mapping; a load change can change static loading and system dynamics. A tank-wagon experiment compared empty and loaded running but also used different speeds, so its different readings do not isolate load as the cause (S07). That limitation reinforces controlled comparisons. An in-service rail study used speed compensation because speed materially changed signal energy (S04). Another used same-train measurements because changing vehicle changes readings (S06).

## Useful approaches and alternatives
- **Configuration and mounting audit:** first recover the logger make/model, sensor type/range/calibration, mount drawings and change date, orientation, fastening/torque, firmware, warning rule, sample rate/timebase, filters, and raw files. Compare current and prior configurations. This is low-cost and can expose changed sampling, scale, or coupling; it cannot establish a physical defect.
- **Controlled repeat passes:** hold one mounting and logger configuration fixed; repeat the same direction and surveyed path, with fixed/disclosed speed, secured and measured load placement, and recorded motor/drive/brake state. Replicate a baseline condition before varying speed and then load separately (or in a small predeclared factorial design). Repeatability at a fixed route point supports localization; it does not by itself identify a defect. Rail deployments show repeat passes and speed/location alignment can be useful, but their train dynamics, speed ranges, route geometry, and validated thresholds do not transfer to this cart (S04–S06).
- **Mount/acquisition comparison:** on a qualified bench or fixture, compare the old and new bracket with the same sensor/logger, same axis and excitation, and a characterized reference accelerometer at the bracket base. Check fastening, mount stiffness, bracket resonances, clipping, sample gaps, filtering and clock drift. A controlled change that moves the response points toward the measurement chain; a null result does not rule out a mechanical source.
- **Acquisition alternative:** compare the current logger's raw channels with a calibrated accelerometer and simultaneous multichannel DAQ that records its configuration, common trigger, actual sample intervals, and unfiltered trace. A tank-wagon study reported a 19.2 kS/s amplifier, but its table advances by 0.02 s while its discussion says 50 ms (20 Hz); the paper does not reconcile this effective-cadence discrepancy (S07). That is a reason to inspect actual files and settings, not to copy either rate.
- **Independent observation/reference:** for an approved later study on the disused track, a fixed, calibrated trackside accelerometer or geophone at the bend, triggered from a shared event/position mark and paired with surveyed distance and cart speed, can test whether a local track response coincides with the cart event. A strain or displacement channel is a stronger independent response measure where a qualified engineer can install it. A location-specific reference cannot inspect the whole route or diagnose a defect. A published scaled rail rig synchronized track strain and cart acceleration from a rail-gap trigger while separately measuring speed optically (S08); this is a design pattern only, with limited transfer from its 1:10 rig and rail model.

## Implementation and history findings
A 2019 Irish in-service train study analyzed 60 passes over two months and compared acceleration-derived indicators with a Track Recording Vehicle; its speed correction and pass selection illustrate why speed and repeat passes matter (S04). A later Network Rail demonstrator aligned an in-cab stream with NMT track-geometry data using time/GNSS and checked speed alignment; a repeated run detected the same reported location. The paper also reports reduced correlation at low or changing speed and weaker lateral correspondence (S05). A Japanese regional-rail deployment operated from Oct 2020 to Aug 2022, with little data Jan–Jun 2022 during general inspection/software updates; it kept the same train because responses changed with vehicle, used position correction, and reported that speed was not corrected in the analyzed sections (S06). These provide operational history, interruptions, and known method limits—not evidence that the logger on this cart uses the same architecture or that rail alarm thresholds apply.

For version-specific acquisition risk, ST's LSM6DSM datasheet (DocID028165 Rev 7, Sep 2017) documents a timestamp FIFO element disabled by default; AN4987 Rev 5 documents the filtering chain and configuration-dependent bandwidth/settling. An ST technical-moderator support answer (6 Jul 2026) traced one report of jumping timestamps to a disabled FIFO timestamp data set / stream parsing setup, and suggested configuration and parsing checks. It is a scoped support diagnosis, not a confirmed hardware bug or a formal erratum. It matters here only as an example: obtain the cart's actual model, firmware, filter/ODR/timebase and configuration before interpreting warning timing (S09–S11). No model-specific issue, fix, regression, or release history for the cart logger or its bracket can be identified from the brief.

## Open dependencies and safe escalation
The pilot owner must define whether the future endpoint is repeatability, location accuracy, or diagnostic utility. Also needed are sensor range/bandwidth/calibration and warning logic; mount rigidity/orientation/fasteners; raw data and timestamp quality; route/direction/speed; load mass and position; wheel/caster state; and motor/drive/brake behavior. Proposed checks are not executed checks.

After records are recovered, a later authorized, controlled study should use replicates and a reference channel. Escalate to qualified mechanical/track inspection if an event remains repeatable at the same mapped point under controlled speed/load and stable mount/acquisition, and independent reference evidence co-locates a response. If only the bracket/logger comparison changes the observation, inspect the measurement chain first; if the event varies with operating state or load, design the next comparison to separate those factors. Any result informs whether inspection is warranted; it does not diagnose safety, authorize use, set a field warning threshold, or replace inspection.
