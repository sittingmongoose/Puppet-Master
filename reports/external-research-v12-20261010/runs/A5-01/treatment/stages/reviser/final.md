# Final research proposal — volunteer oral-history deposit package

**Run/stage:** A5-01-treatment / reviser  
**Scope:** Complete original brief and the exact revealed plan at `stages/investigator/revealed-plan.md`. This is a planning proposal at pilot scale, not an implementation.  
**Evidence and limits:** Prior-stage public-source identities and observations are preserved in [`source-map.json`](source-map.json), with a navigable [`sources/index.md`](sources/index.md). I independently reopened the governing EBU, BagIt, and BWF MetaEdit primary pages for the disputed claims. No local media, application, package, account, or accessibility workflow was run. Research evidence and proposed tests below are not product validation.

## Recommendation

Use a sidecar-first deposit with received originals preserved unchanged. Give each interview/session and each recording, photograph, and note a stable identifier independent of its filename. Record receipt context, original name or physical location, size where applicable, full-file SHA-256 for digital objects, observed embedded metadata, descriptive fields, and relationships in an external UTF-8 record. Keep the archivist-designated catalogue/sidecar as the current descriptive authority. Package the original digital files, relevant records, and approved derivatives in BagIt 1.0 when a checksum-verified transfer package is useful.

Treat interviewer notes as part of the received collection, not as an unaccounted input or a transcript. Inventory each note, link it to the interview and related recording, preserve digital note files as received, and retain physical notes in their designated physical storage. A scan of a physical note, if later wanted, is a separate derivative and must not replace the received item. Access to notes, photographs, audio, and derivatives remains subject to an external consent and restriction decision; receipt or possession does not establish permission to publish them.

Offer BWF embedding only as an optional second workflow on a verified copy when staff and recipients have a demonstrated need for metadata that travels inside a BWF-aware application. Keep the external record authoritative, select a small field subset with the collections archivist, and validate the exact file, tool release, and target readers before use. Neither workflow requires volunteers to use a particular recorder application.

Retain the brief-authorized human-reviewed plain-text transcript and accessible listening-copy path as an optional capability. When the interviewer approves transcript revisions and the external access review permits them, create separate linked derivatives from the preserved source; do not make them mandatory or imply that their interoperability or accessibility has been tested.

## Pilot object and identity model

Create an interview/session ID and a different object ID for every received recording, photograph, and note. Keep the supplied filenames as provenance and convenient package paths, not as identifiers. Record an original filename, byte size, full-file checksum, receipt date/source, and observed metadata for each digital object. For physical materials, record item identity and location; digitization is a separate action and derivative. Link photos and notes explicitly to the session and, when known, the relevant recording. This prevents a renamed or duplicated filename from breaking relationships.

Preserve received audio bytes and embedded metadata as received. Capture any fields already present as observations, without treating them as authoritative merely because they are embedded. The recommended local policy is for the external catalogue/sidecar to carry current descriptive values and provenance; the collections archivist must decide whether that is the society's authority model. An optional BWF copy may repeat only a selected subset and should point back to a stable external record where a short pointer is useful. MediaArea's LIST-INFO help warns that embedded values can become outdated and recommends following identifiers for later or better data [LISTINFO](sources/index.md#LISTINFO).

For every generated file, record a separate object ID, checksum, creation event/date/operator, source object ID and checksum, tool and released version, settings, and access/restriction status. The preservation original, any metadata-bearing copy, transcript revision, and listening copy are distinct objects. A transcript and listening copy are sibling derivatives of the recording unless an actual workflow shows otherwise; do not imply that one was generated from the other. PREMIS 3.0 offers a model for structural and derivation relationships, identified related objects, and optional event links, but the pilot may keep these in a simpler table [PREMIS](sources/index.md#PREMIS).

For transcripts, keep UTF-8 plain-text versions separate rather than correcting one file in place. Give each revision a stable ID, date/status, checksum, link to the source recording and prior transcript revision, and interviewer approval attribution. Preserve a supplied transcript separately if one exists. The interviewer approves corrections and may identify uncertain speech; neither automatic speech recognition nor an embedded transcript is required.

Workflow A is the starting recommendation because compatibility with varied volunteer recorders and preservation of received bytes are central to this brief. Workflow B is a conditional convenience path, not the selected universal default. A simple directory plus a society-defined manifest is a lower-overhead alternative to BagIt, but the society would then need to define and verify its own manifest rules. A larger repository or full PREMIS implementation may be useful later; neither is required for this pilot.

## Embedded metadata: scope, constraints, and rewrite uncertainty

A `.wav` suffix alone does not identify the actual codec, BWF status, chunk set, encoding, or rewrite behavior. BWF extends RIFF/WAVE with a `bext` chunk; optional chunks are not supported by all applications. EBU Tech 3285 v2 says applications need not interpret or use unspecified private chunks and does not guarantee their data integrity, while BWF-compliant applications shall pass such chunks. Generic RIFF/WAVE readers are expected to ignore unknown chunks. This distinguishes ignoring meaning from a conformance pass-through expectation; neither statement guarantees that an arbitrary editor will preserve the chunk intact [EBU](sources/index.md#EBU).

Older BWF readers may ignore UMID or loudness metadata added in later versions. For example, EBU describes BWF v2 as backward compatible while warning that v0 readers ignore UMID/loudness and v1 readers ignore loudness; early BWF-aware devices may not recognize later-defined chunks [EBU](sources/index.md#EBU). FADGI v3 applies to Broadcast WAVE embedding and marks elements as strongly recommended, recommended, or optional; it is not a rule that every volunteer WAV tool supports the same fields [FADGI](sources/index.md#FADGI).

BEXT has bounded ASCII fields, including a maximum 256-character Description and 32-character OriginatorReference. FADGI describes OriginatorReference as a useful file-level identifier and Description as a possible short pointer, while cautioning against sensitive paths. FADGI's BEXT origination date/time has local archival-entity semantics and is not an unambiguous UTC timestamp [FADGI](sources/index.md#FADGI), [BEXT](sources/index.md#BEXT). CORE exchange also carries field and filepath conditions; a filepath is not durable identity across rename, move, or duplication [CORE](sources/index.md#CORE). Keep names, longer descriptions, multilingual text, correction history, rights/consent decisions, and flexible provenance in UTF-8 sidecars rather than forcing them into constrained fields.

FADGI documents an `ltxt` purpose value `tran` for transcription, but `ltxt` text is ASCII and associated with a cue range; RIFF null-termination and padding requirements apply. This is a possible future path for short timed segments only after a specific reader/writer round-trip test. It does not establish a broadly portable whole-transcript field [FADGI](sources/index.md#FADGI). MediaArea's current XML-chunk help also says its aXML/iXML/XMP editing does not validate XML or schema instances and has no undo; this is a tool-specific caution, not a universal statement about all WAV editors [XML](sources/index.md#XML).

If the archivist chooses an embedded subset, first identify the file's real structure and supported target readers, then edit only a verified duplicate. Compare before/after full-file hashes, audio-data hashes, metadata extraction, and chunk inventory, including unknown chunks. Never promise a lossless write based only on an extension or on a tool's stated intent.

## Round-trip/encoding history and collection impact

A bounded release-history example shows why metadata exchange must be version- and field-specific. The investigator's source review of MediaArea's GUI history reports: version 0.2.2 (2009-12-22) fixes for XML carriage-return encoding, special-character escaping, and a platform-specific complete-file-write issue; version 21.07 (2021-07-09) a fix for importing XML fields with `UNICODE=1`; version 23.04 (2023-04-10) read/write support for CP437, CP850, CP858, Windows-1252, ISO-8859-1/2 fields and CSET encoding; and version 25.04 (2025-04-30) an ISO-8859-1 fallback when reading a CORE file [HISTORY](sources/index.md#HISTORY). The reviewed history was on mutable `master`; an immutable commit was not exposed. These entries document named historical changes, not current behavior of every release or application. The official product page listed version 26.08.1 at the time of the investigator's access, but no binary was installed or run [METAEDIT](sources/index.md#METAEDIT).

For this collection, CSV/XML exchange containing accented names, non-ASCII text, quotes, CR/LF, or long values can depend on the tool version and code path. BEXT ASCII constraints are separate from BagIt's strong recommendation to use UTF-8 for tag files; one policy does not change the other's field encoding [FADGI](sources/index.md#FADGI), [EBU](sources/index.md#EBU), [BAGIT](sources/index.md#BAGIT). Preserve rich text in sidecars. Before any embedded workflow, test representative values with the selected released version and target readers; compare extracted fields, line endings, truncation, CSET/code page, full-file hash, audio-data hash, and unknown chunks. This test is proposed, not run.

## Optional transcript and accessible listening-copy path

Retain this as an authorized optional pilot path. “Supported” here means that the brief permits consideration; it does not mean that any implementation or target device has been proven compatible. If consent/restrictions permit and staff can maintain it, create a human-reviewed UTF-8 transcript with speech, speaker labels where known, and relevant non-speech audio information. The interviewer approves every correction. Create a separately identified listening derivative from a verified copy and link it to the original with source/output hashes, format, tool/version, and settings. Present the transcript as directly readable text alongside an accessible player. W3C WAI guidance recommends transcripts and accessible media players; its planning guidance lists a separate transcript for prerecorded audio-only media at WCAG Level A. This is guidance for the proposed experience, not proof that a player or transcript here passes a conformance test [W3C](sources/index.md#W3C), [W3C-PLAN](sources/index.md#W3C-PLAN).

Consent/access review, staffing, target codec and delivery platform determine whether to create or publish a derivative. If review restricts public access, record that owner decision and apply the society's approved controls; do not let a software default publish it. If staff or platform testing is unavailable, defer creation and keep the path optional and marked NOT_CREATED/NOT_RUN rather than claiming technical incompatibility. No ASR is required. Do not embed a full transcript in WAV by default; `ltxt` remains a constrained optional research lead.

## Owner decisions and safeguards

- **Collections archivist:** choose the descriptive field model, identity scheme, authority between catalogue and sidecars, and whether any approved BWF subset is copied. This proposal recommends external authority but does not claim the archivist has decided.
- **Interviewer:** set correction and uncertainty conventions and approve transcript corrections/revisions. That approval was not obtained during research.
- **Consent/restriction reviewer:** decide preservation, staff access, public access, redaction, and reuse for audio, notes, photographs, transcripts, and derivatives. This is an external owner input. Possession of a recording is not consent, and software cannot supply this decision.
- **Society/pilot owner:** choose resourcing, transfer/storage practice, whether to produce a listening copy for a particular deposit, and the target accessible player and delivery format.

The research did not alter received originals; no sample media was supplied. The proposal does not promise lossless edits from `.wav`, require ASR, or infer consent from possession. No downloaded code was run, no application installed, and no account, purchase, production access, or live write was used.
