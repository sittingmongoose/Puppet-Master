## Feed semantics grouped by source

### Static identity, service dates and clock

GTFS static data relates route_id → trips.trip_id/service_id → stop_times.trip_id and stop_id/stop_sequence; calendar and calendar_dates determine which service_id runs on a date. GTFS uses route_type 4 for ferry. IDs are references, not rider copy. Schedule time is in agency.agency_timezone, not stop_timezone. A service day can cross midnight and its time can exceed 24:00:00; e.g. 25:35:00 is 01:35 on the next civil day within the prior service day. Time is measured from noon-minus-12, effectively midnight except on daylight-saving change days [S01]. Therefore show the actual local sailing time using the agency zone, preserve the service date through overnight/DST handling, and test calendar exceptions rather than treating an after-midnight trip as next-day service by default.

### Realtime identity and meaning

GTFS-RT is defined relative to a particular static feed; entity IDs resolve against it. The current specification version is 2.0. Header timestamp is when feed content was created; optional FeedHeader.feed_version matches feed_info.feed_version in the static feed and indicates which schedule the real-time data is based on [S03]. For a typical scheduled trip, trip_id identifies it; frequency-based trips require the triple trip_id, start_date and start_time. start_date is service date, not merely the civil date of a displayed clock time; start_time keeps GTFS service-time semantics. If trip_id is omitted, route_id, direction_id, start_time and start_date must jointly identify the scheduled instance. Ambiguous/unresolvable descriptors may be discarded [S03]. Stop updates refer to stop_id or stop_sequence; sequence is needed if a stop is visited repeatedly [S04,S05].

TripUpdates carry measured/predicted stop event times or delays, and can represent cancellation, added or replacement trips. A missing TripUpdate means no real-time information for that scheduled trip; the consumer must not infer on-time status. NO_DATA denotes unknown information for portions of a trip, not a prediction. Service Alerts describe broader route/stop/network effects; individual-trip delays/cancellations ordinarily use TripUpdates. In a single informed_entity, route+stop fields join with AND; separate informed_entity selectors each apply independently. Thus a route-and-terminal alert is not route-wide. GTFS IDs and active periods govern alert applicability; with no alert time range, the documented display behavior is to leave it present while it remains in the feed [S04,S05]. Keep these semantics separate in the board model and UI.

Freshness has two layers. Header timestamp identifies when a feed snapshot was created, while the embedded event/update can itself be old. GTFS Realtime best practices recommend a refresh at least every 30 seconds and content age no more than 90 seconds for TripUpdates/VehiclePositions or 10 minutes for Alerts [S04]. These are recommendations, not ferry SLA or guaranteed consumer behavior. Agree a partner polling interval and stale threshold; expose last-update time; after the chosen threshold, withdraw RT estimates and show the published schedule plus an explicit stale status. A feed absent or missing a trip update is not evidence of on-time running [S04,S05].

### Feed-version failure/change chain

GTFS Realtime PR #434 arose from issue #362, titled “Static file version information is missing in the real-time data feed.” Its proposal initially included both a version and a schedule URL. The discussion documents why URL alone was ambiguous: a stable URL can point at a candidate/upcoming file, and static file publication and RT activation can happen at different times. The URL was removed; PR #434 added optional FeedHeader.feed_version to match GTFS feed_info.feed_version. The maintainers' revision history records this in Dec 2024. The PR reports the Swiss Open Data platform producing the field and TransSee consuming it to apply a GTFS update after the version changes; it merged 2024-12-05 [S06,S07]. This is a released feed-to-consumer change chain, not proof that this ferry partner implements it. It governs safe synchronization of an RT producer's active schedule revision with a consumer's static timetable, rather than identifying each trip. Recommend comparing those version values and withholding incompatible RT until the publisher-approved version is loaded; because field publication is optional and schedule/RT cutovers can differ, the partner and schedule publisher must agree the release/activation procedure. If no version is supplied, mismatch state is unknown; do not silently bind IDs to the nearest-looking schedule.

## Owner and safety constraints recorded for later proposal

- Schedule publisher owns static timetable versions and approves when a new revision becomes authoritative.
- Duty manager owns a manual advisory and wording shown when feeds are stale. Treat an editorial banner as an authorized optional path, not a feed capability: label it “Staff advisory,” retain author, issue/update time and expiry, keep it visually distinct from live feed status, and require a manager-controlled expiry/review.
- Regional partner must state whether it publishes TripUpdates, Service Alerts, VehiclePositions, feed_version, or any SIRI service, and define identity mapping, timestamps, polling/age threshold and cutover behavior.
- Never dispatch vessels, synthesize an ETA from missing data, conflate a Service Alert with a TripUpdate, or label a manual advisory as a realtime observation.
