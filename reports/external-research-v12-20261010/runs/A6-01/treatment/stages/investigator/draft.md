## Recommendation

For one seasonal route and two terminals, start with a small public board that reads the schedule publisher's GTFS Schedule dataset and can overlay partner GTFS Realtime data when the partner confirms its feed contract. Keep the static timetable usable when realtime is absent. Add realtime only after explicit identity, age and timetable-version checks. A direct, narrowly scoped board requires less system surface than deploying a general trip planner for two fixed terminal views; this is a product-fit inference, not an implementation result.

Treat OpenTripPlanner (OTP) as the strongest reusable backend candidate if the operator already operates it or expects broader trip-planning use. The reviewed current release is OTP 2.10.0 (2026-09-09, commit 521cd5d). It can load GTFS-RT updates and alerts, and has a GTFS API, but its documented purpose is journey planning and it still needs a separate board display/editorial path [S13–S15, S20]. Do not adopt its feedId as the timetable version: feedId is a namespace that joins an updater to a dataset; feed_version identifies a particular data revision [S03, S15].

A useful additional product opportunity is OneBusAway. Its documented sign-mode is designed for large displays, and its server consumes GTFS with separately configured GTFS-RT endpoints [S09]. It may provide a more direct display starting point than OTP, though deployment, version, ferry behavior, revision mismatch and stale wording all require a fit check. Its reviewed project docs are moving main; the latest listed release at research time is v2.7.1/095bf1a [S09,S10].

An analogous schedule-plus-operational-update pattern is TransXChange planned timetable data paired with SIRI-VM vehicle updates; the UK Department for Transport describes subscriptions, timestamps and shared timetable/realtime journey identifiers. SIRI also separates estimated timetable (ET) and situation exchange (SX) services. OTP 2.10.0 documents ET and SX request/response updaters for Nordic/EPIP profiles [S11,S12,S19]. This is an alternative only if the partner already supports a compatible SIRI profile. The BODS guidance is a bus implementation, not ferry evidence.

## Proposed board behavior and evidence

### Static schedule and trip/service identity

**Observed standard behavior:** A GTFS Schedule dataset connects route_id to trips.trip_id and service_id, then to stop_times by trip_id, stop_id and stop_sequence. Calendar/calendar_dates determine active service dates. A service day can differ from a civil date and run past midnight; times above 24:00 belong to that service day. Stop times use agency.agency_timezone, not stop_timezone, and GTFS time has a daylight-saving transition caveat. Schedule datasets are versions; the reference recommends stable publication and persistent IDs across revisions [S01].

**Board choice:** Use the schedule publisher's currently approved dataset as the source of scheduled departures at the two named terminal stop IDs. Label them “Scheduled.” Preserve the service date when sorting/rendering overnight trips; don't reassign a 25:xx trip to the next service date merely because its local clock reads after midnight. Read agency timezone, calendar and date exceptions from the feed. Keep trip_id/stop_id/stop_sequence as data identifiers, never rider-facing names. The schedule publisher controls when a timetable revision becomes authoritative.

**Observed realtime identity behavior:** GTFS Realtime is defined in relation to a static GTFS feed and resolves its identifiers against that feed. For a non-frequency trip, trip_id commonly identifies the scheduled trip; frequency-based trips require trip_id, start_date and start_time. start_date is the GTFS service date, and date/time may need to disambiguate overlapping instances. If trip_id is absent, route_id, direction_id, start_time and start_date are required together; an unresolved descriptor can be discarded. A repeated stop_id requires stop_sequence to identify which call is updated [S03–S05].

**Correction to the released draft plan:** “Use trip IDs alone to join every realtime message” is rejected. Even when a trip_id alone is sufficient for a particular non-frequency scheduled update, it is not universal: IDs are scoped to the correct static feed; frequency instances need the service-date/time triple; missing trip IDs and ambiguous service instances require other fields; multiple routes/datasets need feed scoping. OTP likewise joins real-time updaters to static datasets through a feedId, which should be explicitly configured rather than left to a changeable numeric fallback [S03,S15].

### Realtime messages, freshness and schedule mismatch

GTFS Realtime distinguishes TripUpdates (stop event times/delay and trip changes), Service Alerts (broader notices scoped to agency/route/trip/stop), VehiclePositions and TripModifications. A scheduled trip with no TripUpdate has no realtime data; the consumer is explicitly told not to assume that it is on time. NO_DATA means a portion of a trip has unknown realtime status. A delay is meaningful only when applied to its matching scheduled time [S02–S05].

A Service Alert is not a TripUpdate. The spec guidance says individual-trip delay/cancellation should usually be represented by TripUpdates; an alert is a textual notice with a distinct affected-entity scope. Within one informed_entity, multiple identifiers are joined with AND: route plus terminal means that terminal on that route. Multiple informed_entity selectors describe separate scopes. If no alert TimeRange is supplied, documented display behavior is to keep showing the alert while it remains in the feed [S06]. Do not turn an alert's text into a predicted departure.

The Realtime header timestamp identifies when the feed content was created, but embedded records may themselves be old. GTFS Realtime best practices recommend refreshing at least every 30 seconds and keeping embedded TripUpdates/VehiclePositions under 90 seconds old and Alerts under 10 minutes old [S04]. These are recommendations, not a ferry-partner service guarantee. Ask the partner what it publishes and how often; the duty manager owns the local stale wording and cutoff. Show the last successful update time and a clear freshness state. Use the best-practice ages as initial acceptance thresholds only if the partner can meet them; otherwise set an explicit threshold with the partner and duty manager, based on expected cadence, and label old data visibly.

**Local fallback and mismatch choices:**

| State | Passenger-facing proposal | What must not happen |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Realtime absent or empty | Show publisher-authorized scheduled departures and “Live updates unavailable” or another duty-manager-approved neutral phrase. | Do not say “on time” or infer an ETA. |
| No TripUpdate for this trip | Keep the scheduled departure; mark that no prediction exists only if useful and understandable. | Do not treat omission as a zero delay. |
| TripUpdate is NO_DATA or required identity is ambiguous | Do not display an estimate for that portion; keep the published schedule and explain that live detail is unavailable if needed. | Do not fill gaps using a made-up time. |
| Realtime is older than the agreed threshold | Withdraw the estimate, retain scheduled time, show “Live updates stale” with the last update time. | Do not leave an old estimate displayed as if current. |
| Incoming GTFS-RT feed_version does not match active static feed_info.feed_version | Retain the schedule publisher's currently approved timetable, suppress incompatible realtime overlays, show a clear mismatch/stale status, and raise an operational issue for publisher/partner resolution. | Do not fuzzy-match an old schedule revision or silently apply its trip IDs to a new timetable. |
| Version field is absent | Mark revision relation UNKNOWN; apply no uncertain cross-version trip prediction until an owner-approved fallback contract exists. | Do not claim a verified match from feedId or a stable URL alone. |
| A route/terminal Alert applies | Show it as an alert with the scope and active period, independently from any predicted trip departure. | Do not convert the alert into a delay/ETA. |

The exact GTFS-RT FeedHeader.feed_version field is optional and matches GTFS feed_info.feed_version. It lets consumers identify which static feed the realtime feed is based on, or when another feed version is available [S03]. This is the useful version link; OTP feedId and the static feed version are different facts [S15]. OTP's v2.10.0 GraphQL feedVersion addition lets clients read the static data version loaded in an OTP instance, but the PR describes version exposure/refresh avoidance, not automatic comparison with a realtime FeedHeader [S17].

### Identifiers, dates, time zone and alerts: one source neighborhood, distinct rules

These questions were grouped by shared governing neighborhoods to avoid repeating navigation: Schedule identity/calendar/time fields [S01], Realtime FeedHeader/TripDescriptor/StopTimeUpdate [S03–S05], and Alert scope/TimeRange [S06]. Each rule was then resolved separately. This reuses source bytes, not conclusions. A competent ordinary sequential review would handle obligations in numbered order; the grouped method reduces duplicated reads, while the per-clause table below retains the distinct conditions and exception cases. No benchmark was available to claim a measured time or quality improvement over that reference method.

- **Static service_id:** a calendar pattern activated/deactivated by calendar/calendar_dates for a service date, not an event ID or the visible time.
- **TripDescriptor.start_date:** service date in YYYYMMDD, subject to the spec's collision/frequency conditions; not automatically the date after midnight shown on the display.
- **TripDescriptor.start_time:** service-day clock semantics; required with trip_id/date for a frequency-based trip instance.
- **Agency timezone:** schedule stop times use agency.agency_timezone even if a stop_timezone is present; daylight-saving dates need explicit sample cases.
- **Stop-time identity:** stop_id normally points to a stop; stop_sequence distinguishes repeated calls to the same stop.
- **Alert applicability:** one selector's multiple IDs are conjunctive; a distinct selector is a separate affected scope. Use EntitySelector.route_id for route-wide scope, and an appropriate stop selector for one terminal. Never place a TripDescriptor.route_id in a way that is mistaken for a route-wide alert selector [S03,S06].

Useful consumer evidence reinforces the service-day concern: OTP issue #4058 records VehiclePositions being discarded when start_date was omitted. Maintainers discussed the conditional requirement and the need to associate positions to internal trip instances for the correct service day; the issue was closed in linked PR #4066, which proposed inferring a service day. The first tagged release containing that fix was not established in the inspected changelog, so this is a historical consumer case, not evidence of OTP 2.10 behavior [S18]. Test the actual partner's messages rather than assuming either the bug or its resolution applies.

### Relevant released history and operational scope

1. **Static revision identification in GTFS-RT.** Issue #362 reported that the static schedule version was missing from realtime. PR #434 initially considered both a version and a GTFS URL. Discussion documented that “current” and “upcoming” schedule URLs can be ambiguous because static publication and realtime activation may happen at different times; the URL was removed and optional FeedHeader.feed_version retained. The PR merged 2024-12-05, and the official GTFS Realtime revision history records the field in December 2024. The discussion reports a Swiss Open Data producer and TransSee consumer using the field to apply a new GTFS feed after the version changes [S06–S08]. **It governs the relation between an active realtime feed and the static timetable revision it references**, not the departure time of one trip. It is optional; require the regional partner to confirm whether it publishes it. The proposed guard—compare the RT header value to the publisher's loaded feed_info value before overlaying estimates—is a local product choice, not a mandated automatic cutover algorithm.
2. **Stale vehicle-position snapshot correction in OTP.** OTP PR #6523 reports that the service did not correctly remove data from the previous vehicle-position download. The merged change replaces records using copy-on-write semantics, adds tests and returns the last position-update timestamp in ISO form. The OTP changelog lists it in v2.8.0 (released 2025-09-10), so it is present before the reviewed v2.10.0 candidate [S13,S16]. **It governs replacement of successive VehiclePositions snapshots and their observable last-update time.** It does not establish a feed-age cutoff for TripUpdates or Alerts, nor does it prove timetable-version mismatch protection. Retain explicit freshness logic and verify it with the selected release.
3. **OTP static feed-version visibility.** PR #7445 merged 2026-03-30 and is listed in v2.10.0. It exposes the loaded GTFS feed version to GraphQL clients so they can identify the data version in an OTP instance [S13,S17]. **It governs client visibility of the static dataset deployed in that router**, not automatic reconciliation of the static revision with realtime.

### Optional staff advisory and authority

The brief expressly authorizes a staff-authored advisory banner when machine-readable disruption data is unavailable. Keep it as an authorized option; do not promote it to mandatory scope or claim a standard or candidate product supplies its editorial function. It is a straightforward local board/editorial-path proposal, but the owner must confirm operating workflow.

Recommended conditions: a duty manager may publish when the partner feed is absent, stale, mismatched or does not encode a useful service notice; the banner has a clear “Staff advisory” source label, wording approved by that manager, issue/update time and a review/expiry rule. Keep the regular timetable visible. If usable machine data and a manual message coexist, keep their provenance separate and require manager review for conflicting text rather than silently merging them. The duty manager owns the advisory and stale-data wording; the schedule publisher owns versions; the regional partner confirms its actual realtime types and publishing behavior [brief; S03–S06].

Do not dispatch vessels, derive an ETA from missing data, treat Service Alerts as TripUpdates, or present an editorial advisory as a realtime-feed observation. Those are binding product constraints.
