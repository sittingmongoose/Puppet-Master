## Proposal summary

Before anyone changes the notice, establish the actual fee and enrollment process, recover the current notice and distribution paths, and separate the effects of content, layout/channel, and staff assistance. A later formative comparison should test the same owner-approved policy facts in three conditions: the verified current notice, a content/information-structure alternative, and a presentation/channel alternative using the current words. A staff-supported route can follow an unassisted first attempt. The comparison should measure fee comprehension, reservation-status comprehension, task completion, and confidence as separate outcomes.

The case does not establish that wording, translation, mobile display, or staff explanation caused the questions. The simultaneous shortening and translation revision confound possible causes. A staff question could reflect misunderstanding, a cautious request for confirmation, or a real inconsistency in the service process. The study should identify which explanation fits its observations.

No priority is set between fewer staff questions and better reader understanding. The center’s owner must set the decision priority after the policy and audience are understood. The proposal does not change fee or reservation rules.

## Research question

For people who may use these courses, what combinations of clear policy facts, information order, bilingual presentation, mobile delivery, and staff support help them identify the actual materials cost, understand the form-submission status, and complete the correct next step?

First determine whether readers are interpreting the notice correctly against the true policy. If the policy itself is absent or contradictory, route the issue to the owner before evaluating wording. If it is settled, compare notice conditions without varying policy facts.

The reported question types are known; their frequency and context are not. No archived baseline or response evidence was supplied. This cannot support a before/after effect estimate.

## Corrections, optional improvements, and owner decisions

**Required research corrections and safeguards**

- Establish the existing policy before proposing text. Keep a policy-fact matrix signed off by the person accountable for fees and enrollment.
- Keep the four outcomes separate. In particular, a person can find the amount but misunderstand inclusion, understand the fee but assume a place is reserved, finish the task with staff help, or answer correctly with low confidence.
- Compare one factor at a time where feasible: current words/current route, content/order change in the same medium, and the same words in an alternate presentation/channel. Do not use one combined “improved” option that confounds translation, wording, layout, and distribution.
- Ask about language choice, reading comfort, device/accessibility needs and prior enrollment experience within each group. Do not infer needs from nationality or presume identical literacy/proficiency.
- Treat staff questions as signals to investigate. Their cause may be uncertainty, cautious confirmation, inaccessible detail, or policy inconsistency.

**Optional alternatives to test after factual review**

1. **Information structure:** place the approved total fee, separate materials cost/inclusion, and payment timing together under direct labels; place the status immediately after the form-submission step with the actual next action and any condition.
2. **Policy explanation:** if staff or the notice cannot state the exact existing policy, ask the owner to clarify the policy. If policy is settled, test an explicit explanation of that same policy. This step does not change the policy.
3. **Bilingual presentation:** compare a paper arrangement only if the current physical format is available; compare a selected-language digital page versus another language navigation pattern only if that is a realistic delivery option. An option such as separate language pages may help assistive technology in some web systems, but it must be tested in the actual implementation and is not a general rule for all bilingual print (E04).
4. **Mobile presentation/channel:** compare actual SMS preview and linked destination as a journey; test a text-based page that reflows at narrow widths, supports zoom, and identifies the language of page/parts. These are proposed web desk checks from WCAG 2.2 (E05), not evidence that the center’s current page meets or fails any criterion.
5. **Staff support:** keep the current notice and offer a consistent, owner-approved explanation only after the independent first answer. Record requested help and whether each answer changes. The assistance route should remain available in the eventual service even if the notice improves.

## Proposed comparison study

### Design and sequence

**Phase 0 — Resolve policy and materials.** Obtain the records listed above. Create a status diagram from form submission through receipt, accepted/declined status, reservation, payment, and confirmation only if those states actually exist. Resolve missing facts with the owner.

**Phase 1 — Prepare comparable stimuli.** Freeze all actual facts and versions. For each language, prepare: A current authorized notice; B an owner-approved wording/information-order alternative in the same medium; C the exact A words presented through the proposed alternate layout/channel. If a language version is not available or cannot be reviewed, do not silently translate it; mark the comparison unavailable. For D, use only a consistent explanation approved by the owner and delivered by trained staff or a qualified interpreter as appropriate.

**Phase 2 — Formative sessions.** Use moderated, one-to-one, task-based sessions, in person or remotely depending on access and privacy. Use a neutral script and realistic but fictional course scenarios based on verified facts; do not submit a real form or collect a participant’s account, financial information, or registration details. Give each participant one primary first-exposure condition to reduce learning effects. Balance condition order across people within each language/access profile where recruitment permits. After capturing independent answers, offer D as support and record what changes. A small first round is for issue discovery, not estimating population performance.

### Participants and sampling

After the languages and service-area evidence are known, recruit adults who are actual or plausible course users through voluntary, center-approved outreach and relevant community partners. Include variation within every written language in self-described reading/speaking comfort, literacy/reading confidence, prior class-registration familiarity, mobile access, and use of zoom/screen readers or other access features where relevant. Avoid treating either language group as homogeneous. Ask which language and mode the person wants for the session; do not assume a “native” language or use ethnicity as a proxy. If youth are actual service users, add the required safeguarding and consent design before including them.

Provisional formative scale: 12–18 people per materially used written language across two waves, aiming for 4–6 independent first exposures to A, B, and C in each language before making a comparative judgment. These numbers support exploratory issue-finding only; they are not statistical power or a claim of representativeness. The actual language count, access needs, recruitment route, and budget may require a larger plan or a narrower, explicitly incomplete comparison. Use service-area evidence to prioritize when funds cannot cover all cells.

### Recruitment, consent, privacy

Provide a readable information sheet, invitation, consent record, and task introduction in each selected language and accessible format. Offer oral explanation and language support; confirm the participant’s understanding of what will happen and the ability to stop. Explain the purpose, data captured, observers, recording (if any), storage/retention, intended reporting, and contact/complaint route. Keep participation voluntary and separate from course eligibility or access. Prefer coded notes and no audio/video unless separately justified and consented. Allow a non-recorded route if the team can take structured notes. Collect only characteristics needed to interpret language/device/access outcomes. Do not ask about financial hardship; do not report identifiable quotations or participant-level results. The center’s privacy/ethics lead must approve the protocol and any retention period before recruitment. Translation, consent adaptation, interpreter availability and extra session time must be budgeted before scheduling.

### Session tasks and outcomes

Use a prompt that does not name the answer or suggest a path. For example, ask a participant to imagine choosing a short course and submitting the listed form, then explain what they would expect and what they would do next. Record their unprompted answer before a follow-up question such as “What in the notice led you to that?”

Score each outcome separately against the approved fact matrix:

1. **Fee comprehension:** What is the listed course charge? Are materials included or separately charged? If separately charged, what amount and timing are stated? Allow “not stated/not sure.”
2. **Reservation-status comprehension:** What is the reader’s status after submission? Is a place held or confirmed at that step? What action, response, or time limit follows? Score against actual policy, never against the moderator’s expectation.
3. **Task completion:** Can the participant locate and report the policy facts and carry out the stated next step without assistance? Record completion, time, lookup location, errors, and help requested separately from comprehension answers.
4. **Reader confidence:** After each answer, request a separate confidence rating and an optional explanation. Keep confidence distinct from correctness; a confident wrong answer merits investigation. Do not treat high confidence or fewer staff questions as successful comprehension.

Also log study condition, language/version, device/viewport, relevant reading/access preferences, and assistance given. Predefine error categories: missed/separate materials fee, wrong amount/timing, form submission mistaken for reservation/confirmation, condition/exception missed, message preview/link not followed, and answer not stated by the policy. Analyse errors and outcomes within each language and condition; report the actual denominator and missing values.

## Revision decision

Before recruitment, the owner and research lead should agree on the minimum policy facts that must be understood and what counts as a critical error. After each wave, compare every outcome by condition, language, and relevant access profile. Prefer a condition only when it preserves the approved policy and improves independent comprehension and task completion without creating a critical error or masking an access barrier. Use confidence to identify mismatches between certainty and correctness. Retain the supported route if it helps people and the service can sustain it.

A lower count of staff questions is a separate service outcome. Track it later only if a lawful aggregate measure is available and the owner chooses it as a priority. Do not interpret fewer questions as comprehension by itself; a reader may stop asking while still misunderstanding. If participants cannot answer because the policy is missing or contradictory, return an owner decision and pause the notice comparison. Do not change the fee or reservation policy to make a version win. Any selected version still needs independent language review, a fresh participant check after material revisions, accessibility review appropriate to its media, and separate publication/deployment approval.
