# Investigator discovery — ER12 D-R2-04-control

**Stage:** investigator; brief-led independent discovery.  
**Case:** ER12-D-R2-04-FRESH, “Confusion over a bilingual community-course notice.”  
**Status:** discovery completed from the supplied brief and primary desk sources before plan release. No draft copy, translation, notice, form, participant evidence, or center-specific policy has been created or inferred.

## Established scope and limits

The setting is fictional. Staff report repeated questions about whether materials fees are included and whether submitting a form reserves a place. The notice was shortened while its translation was revised, and some distribution moved to mobile messages. The packet supplies no response log, interviews, stable older notices, current notice, exact language pair, fee or reservation policy, audience distribution, assistive-technology use, reading context, or recruitment budget. The report of questions is a signal to investigate, not a measured rate or evidence of which wording caused them.

The request is to investigate before any rewrite or deployment. No recruiting, contacting readers or the center, collecting personal data, publishing or deploying a notice/form, or changing fees/reservation policy is in scope. No score, preference, translation equivalence, or representative demographic profile may be invented. Reducing staff questions is not an agreed outcome priority; it must not replace reader understanding. A readability score cannot establish comprehension or accessibility/legal compliance.

## Initial causal frame and unresolved service facts

Plausible mechanisms, to test rather than assert:

1. **Fee meaning or prominence:** “materials fee” could be missed or interpreted as included, extra, optional, due later, per course, or subject to conditions. The actual amount, timing, inclusions, exceptions, and exact policy are unknown.
2. **Reservation state:** readers may map “submit form” to “seat reserved” even when the service means only received, pending review, wait-listed, or awaiting confirmation. None of these states can be assumed until the center’s actual process is documented.
3. **Content structure:** shortening may have removed context or made the fee, action, and next step less distinguishable. A one-idea/clear order may help scanning, but more or fewer words are not evidence by themselves.
4. **Language and translation:** revision may have changed terms, information order, register, or detail across language versions. Since the source and target languages and translation history are missing, equivalence or the direction of any mismatch is unknown.
5. **Presentation and channel:** print hierarchy, narrow phone view, preview/truncation, message length, and context of receiving/reading the notice may affect whether details are noticed. These are hypotheses, not claims about actual devices or readers.
6. **Service operation and explanation:** the actual path from inquiry to form receipt, capacity check, confirmation, payment, cancellation/wait-list, and staff explanation may be unclear or inconsistent. A clear message cannot compensate for an unverified or inconsistent process.

The simultaneous shortening and translation revision, together with a channel change, confound attribution. A useful local timeline must recover available notice/version dates and approvals; source/target copy and translator/editor decisions; print, phone-message and other distribution forms; policy changes; the actual form-to-seat workflow and exception paths; and how staff currently explain the two points. The packet contains none of that history. Staff accounts can describe operations and perceived question patterns, but are not reader-comprehension evidence. Any records review or staff interviews belong to a later authorized study, not this stage.

## Independent approaches and transfer limits

- **Content and structure:** GOV.UK interface-writing guidance favors user language, short direct sentences and putting important words early, while cautioning against adding explanations before research shows they are needed [S01]. This suggests testing a reorganized, complete fact sequence, not silently deleting required terms or writing a replacement now.
- **Language-specific testing:** the ONS Census 2021 development report describes Welsh focus groups and cognitive interviews across dialects and proficiency, with specialist translation and language/policy assurance [S09]. It is evidence for a method—test language-specific terms in context with varied readers and qualified reviewers—not a transferable Welsh finding. A community course with an unspecified language pair, smaller resources and different stakes needs its own evidence.
- **Process states:** the GOV.UK confirmation pattern calls for a reference when one exists, next steps and timing, contact details and a saved record; it openly notes a research gap for a confirmation embedded in a wider task [S06]. Its 2018 backlog item records the pattern’s publication history, not efficacy for a course notice [S07]. The MOJ appointments pattern maps pending confirmation, payment, assisted routes and failure cases, but labels itself a proof of concept [S08]. Together they make useful questions, not policy answers.
- **Channel:** GOV.UK transactional-message guidance describes a message following a user action and emphasizes channel preference, length, the first sentence, sender, next step and timing [S05]. That example confirms an existing booked transport journey; it does not support texting an unconfirmed course place or prove that mobile distribution is best here.
- **Accessible digital presentation:** WCAG 2.2 specifies applicable web requirements including reflow, language identification and text-spacing tolerance; reading-level support is AAA and conditional [S10]. This is a desk-check framework if digital content is later built, not a paper/SMS comprehension result or universal legal finding.
- **User research and ethics:** GOV.UK methods guidance suggests 4–8 participants as a typical moderated round and asks for actual/likely users and accessible recruitment [S02]. Separate consent guidance covers accessible information, voluntary participation and withdrawal [S03]; moderated-test guidance covers neutral tasks, interpreters/assistants and 30–60 minute sessions [S04]. These are UK public-service practice references, not the center’s legal rulebook. The design-system live-assessment report records resolved accessibility issues and continuing gaps in end-to-end research transparency [S11], reinforcing the value of retaining change history and open questions.

## Meaningful alternatives for later comparison

Compare dimensions separately before any combined candidate:

| Alternative | What changes | What stays fixed / key question |
|---|---|---|
| A. Current/unchanged notice | Nothing, as actually distributed | Recover the exact current artifact first. If unavailable, label the baseline unavailable; do not reconstruct it from memory. Pair it with the current legitimate help route, if one exists. |
| B. Wording/information structure | A policy-verified, professionally translated and reader-tested organization of complete facts (for example, distinct fee facts and distinct submission/reservation/next-step facts) | Keep visual treatment/channel constant so content effects can be examined. No final wording is authorized here. |
| C. Presentation/channel | Render the same verified facts and wording with a different hierarchy/spacing or the existing mobile/print presentation | Keep content constant to isolate visibility, reflow, preview and channel-context questions. Do not invent a new channel or assume SMS capability. |
| D. Assisted/unchanged path | Keep the notice and test an existing, standardized staff/help explanation or assisted enrollment path | Verify the help route actually exists, is available to both language readers, and does not itself imply a seat is held. Staff explanations are not a substitute for reader outcomes. |
| E. Combined candidate (only after A–D) | Combine a supported content change with a supported presentation change | Test end-to-end after policy and language owners approve accuracy; retain a no-change option if evidence does not justify change. |

A small formative comparison should be diagnostic, not statistical. If one participant sees multiple variants, counterbalance order and use matched but non-leading tasks; disclose possible learning/order effects. If the study needs clean causal attribution, prefer staged separate rounds over overloading a small sample with many variants.

## Proposed later investigation and decision

First obtain policy-owner-confirmed facts and a service map: whether the fee is included, its amount/timing/conditions, and the exact state after form submission; confirmation/wait-list/capacity, expected response and fallback; current notice/channel versions and translation history; and the staff route. Reconcile policy records with operational reality before creating scenarios. Preserve unresolved owner decisions as decisions, not copywriting fixes.

Then recruit adults who are actual or likely course readers only after the center defines the eligible audience and language pair. A realistic formative plan is two moderated waves of 4–8 per round (8–16 total if feasible, consistent with [S02–S04]), with purposive coverage across the two supplied language versions, self-described comfort reading each, literacy needs, phone/print contexts and any requested assistive support. Do not equate spoken proficiency with reading proficiency, assign a language from ethnicity, or treat either language group as uniform. If budget/recruitment cannot cover a relevant reader/access context, report the gap and defer confidence; this small sample is for finding failure modes, not estimating prevalence.

Use participant-chosen language for sessions and consent. Prepare accessible, plain information and consent materials; use a qualified human translator/reviewer or interpreter where needed, and give participants a real option to decline, pause or withdraw without enrollment consequences. Avoid staff recruiting their own learners if that creates pressure. Use a non-operational mock notice based only on verified policies; collect only consented notes needed for the research, not registration, contact or financial data. Debrief with the true policy only after the task and clarify that the prototype was not an enrollment action.

Use neutral scenario tasks and report four outcomes separately:
- **Fee comprehension:** participant’s own explanation of whether materials are included, what must be paid, when, and any policy-confirmed condition.
- **Reservation comprehension:** whether a seat is held after submission, the actual next state, how/when confirmation arrives, and what to do if it does not arrive.
- **Task completion:** whether the participant can find the relevant facts and identify the policy-consistent next step, plus misreadings, time, backtracking and assistance needed.
- **Confidence:** participant’s stated confidence, collected separately after each answer and never treated as correctness.

Before sessions, set critical-error rules with the policy owner. A revision decision should favor only changes that preserve policy and improve accurate, independent understanding across the tested language and access contexts. A critical fee or seat-status error means revise and retest before deployment. Do not choose a change solely because fewer people ask staff, nor deploy if a relevant group/context remains untested. Keep staff-question volume, if later validly measured, secondary to comprehension until owners set priorities.
