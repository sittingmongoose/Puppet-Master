## Proposal in brief

Do not rewrite, translate, or publish a notice yet. First establish the exact fee and enrollment rules and trace what happens from form receipt through a confirmed place, wait-list, or other actual end state. Recover the current notice and any available version/translation/channel history. The reported staff questions are an important lead but do not reveal whether readers misunderstand wording, seek reassurance, or encounter inconsistent operations.

After the process and policy are documented, compare (1) the actual unchanged notice, if recoverable, (2) a complete policy-accurate content/information-order variant in the same presentation, (3) the same wording under a different presentation or existing channel, and (4) an existing assisted explanation paired with the unchanged notice. Only consider a combined alternative after these dimensions have been examined. Keep the policy fixed. A missing current notice is a missing baseline, not permission to reconstruct one from memory. No solution is selected by this draft.

The later study should independently measure fee comprehension, reservation-status comprehension, task completion, and confidence. A message that produces fewer staff questions is not automatically better if readers become less informed or less able to act independently.

## What must be established first

A later authorized discovery should ask the policy owner and the people operating enrollment to verify, in writing:

1. The materials-fee rule: whether it is included; the exact amount and unit; who pays; when it is due; any exceptions, waivers, refunds, or course-specific variation; and which source controls if staff explanations differ.
2. The enrollment state machine: what “submit” actually does; whether capacity is checked immediately; when a seat is held; whether there is a pending/requested state or wait-list; who confirms; how and when a reader is told; what happens if a message fails or no response arrives; and any cancellation/deadline consequence.
3. The current and prior notice artifacts that can be recovered: exact print and mobile-message versions, dates, language version, content owner, translator/editor/reviewer, approvals and distribution contexts. Establish whether the intended audience may include minors and the actual eligibility rule. Preserve distinct copies; do not assume a remembered older version is an exact baseline.
4. Current operational paths: how a person asks questions, how staff explain fees/status, whether the explanation varies by staff member or language, and whether an existing assisted enrollment route is available to all likely readers.
5. The people and contexts to include: the actual language pair and relevant varieties; reader-chosen language and reading comfort; literacy/access needs; assistive technology; whether people read on a phone, in print, or through a message preview; and recruitment, accommodation and compensation budget.
6. Decision ownership and thresholds: who validates policy truth, who can approve language and translation, who sets minimum critical-error rules, what must be true before any publication, and how reader understanding is weighed against staff workload.

This work may reveal policy ambiguity, a mismatch between written policy and practice, a content problem, a presentation problem, or multiple causes. If staff report different rules, resolve the operational/policy issue before testing copy. Do not make one language’s source text the presumed authority until the language and policy owners set the source-of-truth process.

## Mechanism hypotheses and how to distinguish them

These are plausible causal explanations, not findings about actual readers:

| Hypothesis | Why it could occur | Discriminating observation in a later study |
|---|---|---|
| Fee inclusion is semantically or visually ambiguous. | A short notice may omit the relation between course price and materials cost, or the fee detail may be hard to find. The actual wording and fee rule are unknown. | Ask a neutral “what would you expect to pay, and when?” task, then compare participants’ answer to the verified fee policy. Record whether the fact was missed, misread or found but not trusted. |
| Form submission is inferred to mean a reserved seat. | Everyday registration conventions can suggest completion even if the service has another step. No local service state is established. | Ask what happens immediately after submitting, whether the place is held, what confirmation to expect and what action to take if it does not arrive. Compare the answer with a documented process map. |
| Shortening and translation revision changed meaning or emphasis. | Two content operations occurred together; differences may reflect omission, terminology, order, dialect/register or the reviewer’s choice. | Compare version lineage and conduct comprehension interviews in each actual language version, with a qualified language reviewer. Do not infer translation equivalence from parallel layout or bilingual staff alone. |
| Hierarchy, typography or screen width hides the facts. | Readers may scan a heading/preview, miss subordinate text, enlarge text, or view a long message on a narrow screen. These contexts are not yet known. | Keep exact content fixed while testing the current and alternative print/mobile renderings in participant-selected contexts. Observe findability and task success separately from interpretation after the text is located. |
| Channel length or delivery timing changes attention. | Mobile preview or message length may omit or delay supporting facts; distribution could reach different contexts than print. | Compare the same verified facts in existing print and message formats; record first exposure/preview and what remains available when opened. Ask about preferred channel rather than assume it. |
| Staff questions signal more than misunderstanding. | A reader may be cautious, want reassurance, lack a reliable status message, or be responding to inconsistent operations. | Record spontaneous help requests and the question’s trigger; independently score the participant’s policy understanding and task outcome. In a separate process walk-through, compare staff explanations to the verified rule. Do not count questions as errors by themselves. |

An explicit policy explanation may be useful if readers need the relationship among already-verified facts made clearer; test it against a concise statement of those same facts, without changing policy. An explicit process statement may be the key intervention if policy is clear but the current confirmation is missing. Conversely, layout changes will not resolve contradictory staff practices. The study therefore tests policy comprehension after a verified process explanation exists and separately records whether service delivery matches that explanation.

## Later study design

### Phase 0: history and policy map

Before drafting stimuli, conduct an authorized records review and a short process walk-through with policy owners and staff. Collect available current/prior notices, language versions, distribution examples, approvals, translation revisions, fee-policy versions and enrollment/confirmation workflow documents. Construct a dated change timeline and mark missing periods. Ask what “materials fee included” and “reserved” mean operationally, with real exceptions. Compare documented rules with the staff explanation and each actual workflow state.

If usable version history is absent, record that; do not invent a baseline. If the owner cannot resolve fee or reservation facts, pause reader testing of those claims and send an owner-decision question rather than letting the researcher choose wording that changes policy.

### Phase 1: formative comparative rounds

**Participants.** First establish who is eligible to enroll in the relevant courses. Recruit actual or likely readers within that confirmed audience; do not assume an adult-only audience. If the service is confirmed adult-only, recruit adults. If eligible readers include minors, obtain an approved age-appropriate protocol covering assent, any required parent/guardian permission, safeguarding, accessible consent and tasks before recruitment. Age eligibility and applicable obligations remain unknown until the service owner and research/ethics lead establish them. Ask directly about reading and speaking comfort in each language, preferred version, literacy support, mobile/print context and assistive needs; do not infer these from ethnicity, nationality, age or household membership. Include readers who use assistive technology or need alternate formats. Involve staff in a separate operational session, not as proxies for readers.

Use two moderated rounds. GOV.UK guidance calls 4–8 typical for a round [S02], so a starting plan is 4–8 readers per round **for each priority language version**, with the actual total dependent on which groups the center serves and what the budget can support. This could mean 16–32 reader sessions for two language versions over two rounds; do not promise it is feasible before pricing recruitment, interpreters, accessibility support and incentives. If resources support less, label the evidence exploratory, record which version/access context is missing, and do not claim balanced or population-representative results. The design is for diagnosing failure modes, not calculating prevalence.

**Stimuli and conditions.** The current notice is the baseline only if its exact current version is recovered. Use matched, non-operational mockups populated only with policy-approved facts. For core content comparison, keep type, hierarchy and channel constant while varying information order/wording. For presentation comparison, keep verified wording constant while varying layout or an existing print/mobile context. If supported by recovered artifacts, actual reading contexts and owner approval, add two separate optional contrasts: concise versus explicit explanation of the same verified policy facts; and co-presented bilingual versus separate language-specific presentation with each language’s wording held constant. Do not invent an explanation, language option, layout, or channel. Mark a contrast not feasible when the actual service context cannot support it. Include a structured staff explanation as an independent assisted condition and retain an unchanged-notice option. Do not include all changes at once in the first test; sequence the contrasts or use staged rounds rather than an overlarge factorial design.

Prepare language versions through a qualified human translator and appropriate reviewer, with policy owner signoff and documented terminology choices. Test each version with readers who use it; do not claim equivalence simply because a back-translation or bilingual reviewer says the sentence matches. A bilingual participant may prefer one language and read the other differently. If participants see multiple variants, counterbalance order, use matched but not repeated answer-revealing scenarios, report first-exposure results separately, and note practice/carryover. If the small sample cannot isolate effects credibly, use staged rounds and present the result as qualitative diagnosis.

**Session and consent.** Plan moderated 30–60 minute sessions, a feasible format in person or remote according to access needs, with interpreters/assistants if requested; the GOV.UK usability guide uses this session range and recommends neutral tasks [S04]. Offer the information sheet and consent in a format/language each person understands. For eligible minors, use the approved age-appropriate assent and parent/guardian permission process where required, with safeguarding and non-coercive recruitment safeguards confirmed before any session. Explain purpose, recording, data use, voluntary participation, withdrawal, incentives, and that the research will not enroll them. Provide an easy way to pause or stop without effect on course access. Do not recruit through a staff member who has power over that person’s enrollment without safeguards. Use no live enrollment form, real contact/financial details or production-system action. Collect only data necessary for the approved study, protect it, and set deletion/retention rules before recruitment [S03].

### Tasks and outcomes

Use believable, neutral scenarios whose facts have been confirmed by the owner; do not hint which answer is expected.

1. Invite the reader to locate course and payment details, explain what they would pay and when, and identify any materials charge or included item.
2. Ask the reader to say what submitting the form does, whether they believe a seat is held, what comes next, how they expect to learn the outcome and what they would do if no confirmation arrives.
3. Ask them to complete a mock “decide what to do next” task using the notice and available process/help route. Record whether they find the facts, select the policy-consistent action, backtrack or need support.
4. Offer an optional standardized staff explanation in the assisted condition and re-ask the task using a matched scenario. Record whether help resolved uncertainty and whether the explanation itself matched policy.
5. After each answer/task, ask the reader how confident they are and why. Record confidence separately from correctness and task performance.

Record observations by condition and participant-chosen language, using a predefined coding sheet:
- **Fee comprehension:** exact fact elements correctly explained, separately (included/excluded, amount/unit, due time and applicable conditions once confirmed).
- **Reservation-status comprehension:** correct immediate state, conditions for a held place, expected confirmation and next step/no-response path.
- **Task completion:** relevant facts found, scenario task completed, policy-consistent next action, elapsed time, backtracking, prompts and assistance.
- **Reader confidence:** self-reported confidence after each outcome, kept as its own measure.
- **Staff/question signal:** whether a spontaneous question is a correction request, reassurance request, missing-information request or process mismatch, based on what the participant says; do not assume intent. Analyze staff observations separately from reader correctness.

Do not create one “comprehension” score that hides a fee error behind a status success or a confidence rating. Compare patterns within each language and access context. Do not interpret raw differences between versions as a language effect unless translation quality, task comparability, audience composition and sample design can support that analysis. This proposed formative work does not estimate population rates.

### Revision decision

Before sessions, the service owner and research lead should define critical misunderstandings in policy terms. A material error about whether a fee is due or whether a seat is reserved is a stop-and-investigate signal, even if average task completion or confidence is high. If readers cannot find correct information, test a presentation remedy; if found but misunderstood, test content/structure and language remedies; if they understand the text but the operation contradicts it, fix the service-process discrepancy through an owner decision. If supported participants complete tasks only after staff intervention, keep that assistance available but do not call the notice independently clear.

Revise only where a change improves accurate task outcomes without changing policy. Retest the changed content/presentation in affected language and access contexts. A combined candidate must be checked end-to-end after isolated findings. Choose no change when evidence supports it; defer deployment where a critical error, policy conflict, or untested priority audience remains. Do not optimize for fewer staff questions without an owner decision on that tradeoff.

## Already covered, rejected, and uncertain

**Covered in this proposal:** plausible mechanisms, policy and operation gates, exact alternatives (wording/structure, presentation/channel, assisted/unchanged, combined/no change), source-backed implementation analogues, study participants, outcomes, accessible consent and a revision rule.

**Rejected for this case/stage:** a finished bilingual notice; automatic translation; a readability score as proof; changing a fee or enrollment rule to fit copy; assuming all readers prefer mobile or one language; using staff questions as a comprehension proxy; selecting an external pattern as the answer; contact/recruitment/deployment; and claiming participant validation.

## Project situation
