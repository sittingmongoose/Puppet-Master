# Multi-collection reuse-information catalogue

## Assignment

A regional museum network wants a search catalogue linking to digitized objects from five collections. Each collection supplies descriptive records and some statement about permitted reuse. Visitors need to see the supplied statement and its source, even when a record is incomplete or changes later. The network does not own all underlying objects or digital reproductions. This is metadata/workflow research, not a legal determination of rights.

You have a 60-minute whole-topic research envelope. Produce a planning proposal, not an implementation. Use public primary documentation, relevant public source code and released issue/fix history where they bear on the proposal. Discover realistic products, mechanisms and alternatives independently; no product, source or conclusion is preselected. A bounded historical example and useful unfamiliar opportunities matter more than an exhaustive catalogue. Clearly distinguish observed external behavior, your inference and a local product choice.

## Exact numbered obligations

1. Compare two aggregation/publishing workflows and an analogous controlled-vocabulary/provenance mechanism.
2. Distinguish supplied rights statement, license, public-domain assertion and local access policy without making independent legal conclusions.
3. Research related URI, attribution, mapping and update conditions as a coherent metadata neighborhood; explain missing/conflicting statements.
4. Investigate a relevant vocabulary/mapping migration or released aggregator behavior change and its collection-specific consequences.
5. Preserve and investigate this supported optional scope: a reuse-help link and downloadable source-attributed metadata record. It is an authorized option, not a mandatory feature or an established technical capability; recommend conditions, retain it when supported, and explain any evidence-based exclusion.
6. Make owner decisions explicit: Each collection registrar owns its supplied statement; the network metadata lead owns mapping and provenance. Legal or policy ambiguity is routed to those owners rather than silently resolved by the catalogue.
7. Preserve negative constraints: Do not grant new licenses, infer reuse permission from a visible thumbnail, scrape restricted collections, or convert an unknown statement into a public-domain claim.
8. Deliver one coherent evidence-backed research proposal covering obligations 1–8, with recommendations, source/version applicability, useful discoveries, unresolved owner inputs, and a validation table separating checks actually executed from checks merely proposed. Do not report research sources or future tests as executed product validation.

## Research boundaries and deliverable

Keep the investigation at the pilot scale above. No installs, accounts, production access, purchases or live writes are needed. Read-only public retrieval is appropriate; any permitted local discriminating check must be disclosed with its actual inputs and result. A check you did not run remains proposed or NOT_RUN. Deliver a complete proposal with a compact comparison, evidence-linked recommendations and prioritized validation; there is no requirement to build a prototype. Owner inputs may remain explicit decisions, but they do not excuse investigating publicly answerable questions. This brief is the complete discovery input.
