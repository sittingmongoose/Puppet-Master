The aggregation choice is a pilot decision: (A) accept the five collections directly through one network-owned adapter/ingest layer, or (B) let collections/registrars deliver to a hub operator that maps and republishes. For either route, support only the feeds each registrar confirms (API, OAI-PMH, file export or IIIF links); no source feed or update schedule was tested. A lightweight local preflight/preview is a useful complement to either route. Keep the supplied record, normalized catalogue record and published view as separately versioned artifacts.

### Rights, statement scope and access

A source's rights statement is evidence about what its supplying institution says for an item; show the exact text/URI and who supplied it. RightsStatements.org describes its statements as high-level descriptions of rights/reuse status, not licenses, and says to use the statement URI in data [S05]. A license such as CC BY is a reuse authorization supplied by a rightsholder; Europeana recommends CC licenses/tools when applicable and says an institution can only apply a CC license when it holds all rights [S06]. CC0 is a public-domain dedication/waiver; the Public Domain Mark is used to mark content asserted to be out of copyright worldwide [S06]. Those are different source categories; the catalogue must not choose among them on its own.

A public-domain mark/assertion is a provider-supplied claim, not a finding by this network. `Copyright Not Evaluated` and `Copyright Undetermined` are distinct RightsStatements.org statements: CNE says the making-available organization has not evaluated status [S21]; UND says it reviewed status but could not conclude and explicitly is not a license [S20]. Do not translate an absent, blank or unsupported value into either statement or into a public-domain claim. Europeana's best-efforts guidance also treats organizational sharing policy as a separate consideration in selecting a rights statement [S07].

Rights claims can concern the underlying work, its digital representation, or both. EDM v5.2.7 defines `edm:rights` for digital objects and permits it on the aggregation or on a `WebResource`; its chosen profile requires one on the aggregation and permits a resource-level statement [S08]. Europeana's later guidance encourages partners to make the rights presentation for physical and digital objects easy to assess, but the pilot should preserve source scope and should not invent a new rights claim [S07]. Record whether each supplied statement is about the work, a named image/file/thumbnail, the whole record or scope unknown. Never infer reuse permission from the presence or appearance of a thumbnail.

A local access rule (for example public preview versus login/restricted original) answers who can retrieve a reproduction; it is not the source's reuse license or rights-status assertion. IIIF Authorization Flow 2.0 defines interaction patterns for access-controlled content resources [S15]. Store and present access status separately from the supplied rights assertion. A visible object or preview does not imply a reuse grant.

For missing or conflicting metadata, keep all source claims with their field/source/record scope and retrieval time. Show the supplied text and its source even if a structured mapping is absent or invalid. Flag missing as “not supplied” and conflicts as “source statements differ”; do not silently prefer the most permissive value, normalize prose into a license, or resolve a policy question. Route the item to its collection registrar.

### Metadata neighborhood, mapping and provenance

Treat these as a connected record, not one rights field: stable source record ID; supplying collection/registrar; source record URI and retrieval/update dates; original rights text/URI and its claimed scope; object/representation links (`isShownAt`/homepage, image/manifest URI); format and access condition; mapped rights URI and mapping rule/version; creator/provider attribution; and the linked subject/type terms. DPLA's public pipeline keeps the original record and uses provider-specific mapping files; its field reference distinguishes a provider, `dataProvider`, a source resource, a digital object, and the original record [S01, S03]. That is useful evidence that the five collections may need separate mappings even when their source schemas look alike.

For vocabulary alignment, use stable concept URIs and retain the submitted label/URI beside the chosen target. Getty AAT records have persistent numeric concept IDs, synonyms, hierarchy, notes and source information; its guidance distinguishes concepts from bare strings and recommends keeping display text separate from individual indexing terms [S17, S18]. W3C SKOS offers `exactMatch`, `closeMatch`, `broadMatch`, `narrowMatch` and `relatedMatch`. Only `exactMatch` is transitive; `closeMatch` deliberately is not, to avoid compound mapping errors [S16]. Do not merge collection terms or infer equivalence from matching strings. Have the network metadata lead approve mappings, with uncertain rows retained and labeled rather than discarded.

For mapping/provenance, preserve original and derived assertions as separate entities. A small PROV-O pattern can record source record/entity, transformation activity, activity time and mapper/ruleset agent, plus `wasDerivedFrom`, `wasGeneratedBy`, `used` and `wasAssociatedWith` links [S19]. In the UI/export, identify the source institution and direct source-record URL; in the audit trail, retain retrieval time, prior/current values, rule version and whether a change came from the registrar or a network mapping. Assign each collection's registrar responsibility for its supplied rights statement; assign the network metadata lead responsibility for mapping and provenance. Ambiguous legal or policy matters go to those owners.

### Migration and released change history

A concrete format migration is IIIF Presentation API 2.1.1 → 3.0.0 (3.0 was formally released in June 2020) [S09–S12]. V2 used `license` and `attribution`. The v3 change log records the breaking `license`→`rights` rename and constrains values to Creative Commons, RightsStatements.org or registered extension URIs. It also renames `attribution` to `requiredStatement`, permits explicit label/value maps, and adds structured `provider` data [S11]. Issue #1287 records the ambiguity: “attribution” in museums can mean artist attribution, while publishers also needed a required visible rights/credit statement [S12]. The v3 cookbook says a `rights` URI may be machine-oriented and may not be visibly rendered; `requiredStatement` must be rendered (possibly initially hidden) [S13].

Collection consequence: a legacy IIIF v2 `attribution` cannot safely be copied wholesale into v3 `requiredStatement` without classifying whether it names creator, credit, rights or several things; a legacy `license` value outside the v3 allowed URI set cannot be silently coerced. Preserve the original manifest and values, crosswalk only known fields, record unresolved mappings, and publish dual or version-negotiated v2/v3 views only if required by actual consumers. IIIF's cookbook demonstrates content negotiation for serving v2 or v3 at one URL, but this is a supported pattern, not a requirement [S14]. The migration is relevant if any of the five source collections publishes IIIF; prevalence is unknown and must be checked per collection.

Separately, DPLA documents a real incremental-update failure in its NARA ingest: from April 2025 through January 2026, delete files were misidentified, so deletes were not applied; a June 2026 fix was documented, and the team left retroactive reprocessing as a decision [S02]. This is an operational history example: an “update feed succeeded” is not enough. Verify insert/update/delete counts and sample affected IDs per collection; keep a source snapshot and an explicit tombstone/remove event. NARA is only a bounded analogy, not evidence about the pilot's collections.

## Evidence-based proposal for the pilot

1. Retain one immutable source snapshot per ingest and a stable source/collection identifier. Store raw source rights text and URI exactly as provided, source field/URI, retrieval time and stated scope. Maintain separate mapped display categories and versioned mapping decisions; never overwrite the source field.
2. Publish a rights panel with the supplying institution, supplied statement and direct URI/source page, scope (work/reproduction/record/unknown), local access condition and last-seen/update timestamp. “Not supplied” and conflicting values remain explicit states. Do not calculate permission, imply legal accuracy, or grant a license.
3. Use per-collection mapping and validation, a report of accepted/unmapped/missing/conflicting values, and an owner resolution queue. Keep ID, source link, provenance, update/tombstone history and vocabulary mappings. A string match alone is not a vocabulary match.
4. Preserve the supported optional reuse-help link if the network owner selects a stable help page and each collection can supply a valid destination when a collection-specific policy applies. Keep this link separate from the machine-readable rights URI. A downloadable source-attributed JSON-LD record is also a supported option only after direct file access, CORS/content type, attribution, provenance, versioning and accessibility are verified. IIIF `seeAlso` can link a machine-readable representation; `rendering` can link an alternative human-consumable download with direct access, label and format; `requiredStatement` carries visible attribution/required text [S10, S13–S15]. These standards support the patterns; they do not prove that any pilot endpoint currently serves the files.
5. Let each collection registrar decide the supplied statement and resolve their own contradictory/missing claim. Let the network metadata lead decide crosswalks, versioning, normalization policy, provenance fields and change management. Route legal/policy ambiguity to those two owners, not to the catalogue.
