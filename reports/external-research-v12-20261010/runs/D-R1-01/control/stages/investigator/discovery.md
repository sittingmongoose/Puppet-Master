# Investigator discovery — D-R1-01-control

## Scope and case boundary

The brief describes a hypothetical cooperative with occasional returned dry baked snacks of poor texture in apparently intact sample bags. The bag supplier and cooling routine changed during the same month. No food or package tests, product formulation, packaging construction, process records, storage/distribution conditions or agreed texture measure were provided. This is not evidence that the product is unsafe, that a pouch failed, or that either simultaneous change caused the reports. No shelf-life, safety, compliance or sale-readiness finding can be made. The question for discovery is how to separate plausible moisture/texture causes and what evidence a controlled pilot should gather.

## Mechanism map

Moisture is a plausible common driver, but it acts in at least two places. A baked piece can have center-to-surface or edge-to-center water gradients after the oven; equilibration during cooling/storage can alter texture and physical integrity even before any package ingress is considered. Bedas et al. tracked biscuit moisture distributions during cooling and subsequent storage with Karl Fischer and NIR methods; different shapes showed different checking outcomes (ER12-S002). This is evidence about those biscuits, not a cooling prescription for the cooperative. Cooling time, product core temperature, bed depth/loading, air temperature/RH, airflow, exposure time before sealing, and the moisture profile at packing are relevant variables to record.

After sealing, a snack can exchange water through the film and through the finished package’s seams or defects. Film transmission values are conditional on material, thickness, temperature and humidity gradient; actual surface area, headspace, fill mass, seam length, sealant layer and seal integrity also matter. In tests of PA/PE and PVDC-coated PET/PE, thermosealed packages transferred more water/oxygen than corresponding films, with regular seals adding about 25% in the tested systems (ER12-S003). A multi-market survey found metallized multilayer BOPP pouches alongside substantial variation in package construction and seal properties; authors inferred possible seal problems in some sampled products, but did not establish the cause for any one snack (ER12-S004). A package looking intact by eye does not answer whether its seal or package-level barrier is adequate.

Product moisture, water activity (aw), crispness, and safety are related but not interchangeable. Equilibrium aw depends on formulation and temperature; an isotherm maps the particular food’s moisture content to aw at a defined temperature (ER12-S009). In a controlled Crackerbread study, force/acoustic crispness features changed across aw 0.113–0.98, with a product-specific critical aw of 0.51–0.59; the article also notes the absence of one standard crispness measure (ER12-S001). A separate corn-cracker paper used a specific formulation, metallized plastic and a critical-moisture model to predict very different shelf lives at 30 °C/75% versus 85% RH (ER12-S005). These numbers are not transferable to the cooperative’s snack. Use both initial moisture and aw, measured at controlled temperature, and define crispness in a product-specific instrument/sensory method.

Food safety is a separate question. FDA identifies chips and crackers among low-moisture ready-to-eat foods addressed by its January 2025 draft sanitation guidance, which is marked draft/non-binding (ER12-S010). FDA also states that drying may prevent Salmonella growth but does not kill it (ER12-S011). Thus a low-moisture texture result, intact-looking pouch, or improved WVTR cannot establish pathogen absence, process adequacy, legal compliance, or sale readiness. Any safety decision requires qualified review of actual formulation, hazards, process controls and sanitation; none is assessed here.

## Comparable approaches and operating history

1. **Characterize a candidate material and the complete package.** Metallized multilayer PP-BO is common in a 2024 market survey; other compared constructions include PP laminated to metallized PP and PVDC-coated PE films in modeled chip packages (ER12-S004, ER12-S008). A study of PLA/CNC blown films tested crackers at 25 °C across RH levels, using 8% critical moisture for its own product; the CNC option delayed its endpoint relative to neat PLA above 50% RH (ER12-S006). A lab CNF/wax/CNF multilayer preserved cracker fracture texture similarly to an original-package control after a one-week, 80% RH challenge, while CNF-only protection did not (ER12-S007). These are leads for material diligence, not qualified cooperative packaging. Request full layer stack, food-contact status, thickness, WVTR method/temperature/RH gradient, sealant identity/window, and actual pouch performance before comparing or purchasing.
2. **Improve seal assurance without changing film.** On the incumbent pouch, document seal width/geometry and use the equipment/material supplier’s qualification process to establish a safe, reproducible window. Compare actual finished-package integrity and seam strength across lots, not just a film datasheet; do not infer or prescribe a jaw setting from published studies. Seal quality can change effective whole-package transfer (ER12-S003, ER12-S004).
3. **Standardize cooling and storage practice using the same film.** Compare the current routine with a documented product-specific cooling/pack condition that controls and records time-temperature-moisture history and the surrounding RH; log finished-goods storage RH/temperature and distribution conditions. Do not choose numerical conditions until the cooperative establishes current practice and realistic exposure. This tests whether initial condition or later environment explains the texture response without changing primary film.
4. **Screen modified atmosphere only if oxidation is shown to matter.** The 2001 potato-chip model compared two multilayers and predicted that changing initial water-vapor content could help if lipid oxidation limited acceptability while hurting when moisture-driven crispness loss dominated (ER12-S008). The cooperative supplied neither formulation nor rancidity evidence, so headspace gas changes are not an immediate path.

## Implementation history and case reconstruction

The one known change history is confounded: bag supplier and cooling routine changed together in a month. The brief does not identify before/after film layers, supplier specifications, roll lots, sealant layers, equipment settings, cooling profile, ambient humidity, or which batches correspond to the returns. Comparable reports show why those details matter: a 2024 commercial survey measured pouches 40–84 µm thick with seam widths 0.6–1.9 cm and reported variable seal outcomes; a 2016 package study compared two multilayer constructions and film-only versus thermosealed packages; a 2019 biscuit study monitored product temperature/moisture distribution through cooling; and a 2021 PLA/CNC cracker study disclosed its 25 °C/RH test conditions and its own critical moisture endpoint (ER12-S002, ER12-S003, ER12-S004, ER12-S006). Published outcomes are tied to their products and operating conditions. No current supplier issue report, fix history, regression, or resolution record was supplied.

First recover the cooperative’s batch timeline and retained-film/retained-product records: recipe and bake lot; cooling start/end and product temperature; exposure time and room RH/T; pack time; supplier, film-roll/lot and declared layer stack; package dimensions/fill/headspace; sealing equipment, recorded settings, operator and seam checks; storage and distribution RH/T and handling; and return/best-before dates. This may identify associations but cannot separate two changes that always co-occurred; a controlled comparison is required for causal attribution.

## Candidate pilot architecture (provisional, before plan release)

Use independent bake batches as blocks. Within each batch, randomly assign equal portions to: (A) incumbent film/current routine baseline; (B) an identified candidate film with seals qualified for that construction and current cooling; (C) incumbent film with a documented controlled cooling/packing routine; and (D) incumbent film with improved seal assurance. Hold recipe, bake lot, fill mass, bag size, handling and common storage conditions constant where an arm does not deliberately change them. If former-supplier film or a candidate is unavailable, the material comparison cannot answer that question; do not substitute an uncharacterized datasheet. Use at least three independent batches as an initial repeatability screen, with sample count per arm determined by pilot variability and access. A separate storage-condition comparison can follow once realistic distribution RH/T is documented.

At pack-out, measure product moisture and aw at controlled temperature and, if feasible, representative center/surface or edge/center moisture; record cooling profile, ambient RH/T, exposure time and packing time. At fixed, preselected storage intervals, evaluate blind sensory crispness against a cooperative-defined scale, instrumental break/fracture and/or acoustic response, product moisture/aw, appearance, and package integrity/seal measures. Test sacrificial packages for integrity at baseline and over storage; do not treat clear appearance as a seal test. Log bag supplier/roll lot, film build, dimensions, seam geometry and actual seal conditions without prescribing a production setting. Trace every unit to batch/arm/date/operator.

Before starting, the cooperative must define its meaningful crispness threshold, minimum useful difference, acceptable package-integrity endpoint, realistic distribution condition/duration, and cost/material tradeoff. A provisional decision rule is to advance the least disruptive arm only if it meets the pre-agreed crispness endpoint across independent batches at the intended pilot duration, shows no worse integrity than baseline, and the measured effect exceeds the pre-agreed useful difference; otherwise retain uncertainty and repeat or revise the study. Passing this rule would justify a larger validation, not establish shelf life, safety, compliance, or sale readiness. Proposed pilot observations above have not been collected. No food/package tests, calculations, or laboratory checks were executed during discovery.

## Unresolved dependencies

Missing ingredients/formulation prevent selecting a transferable aw or critical-moisture target, assessing oxidation as a co-limiter, or completing a food-safety assessment. Missing bag construction and seal equipment prevent comparing WVTR figures or choosing a package/material intervention. Missing cooling details and storage/distribution climate prevent choosing conditions or a duration. Missing batch records prevent associating returns with either change. Missing customer texture endpoint prevents setting a decision threshold. Missing lab access limits instrumentation; a small blocked pilot can still learn whether controlled cooling/handling, same-film seal assurance, or a characterized alternate package shifts texture and package-integrity measures within a short, defined local comparison.
