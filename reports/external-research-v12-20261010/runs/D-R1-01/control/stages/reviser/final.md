## Executive proposal

Do not choose the offered film from its datasheet alone. The brief reports that the supplier and cooling routine changed in the same month, but provides no batch mapping, formulation, material construction, closure measurements, or storage history. Those coincident changes leave the returns confounded. A bag that appears intact has not thereby passed a seal-integrity or package-barrier test, and the report of poor texture does not prove that the bag caused it.

The most useful first step is a bounded record-recovery and baseline characterization effort. Link each returned sample and retained production sample to bake/pack batch, ingredient and film lots, product condition at pack-out, cooling/packing timeline, seal and handling history, and storage/distribution exposure. Obtain the full construction and test conditions for both prior/current and offered films. Measure texture, product moisture/aw, and package integrity as separate outcomes.

If a small pilot is feasible, compare the current configuration against (1) a documented cooling/packing practice using the same film, (2) improved seal assurance using the same film, and (3) a characterized candidate film with a seal qualification appropriate to that film. Keep product, fill, bag geometry, handling, and common storage exposure controlled; randomize arm assignment within multiple independent bake batches. Use package sizes and smaller pilot lots as optional, explicitly costed follow-on arms only when the recovered timeline points to fill ratio, opened-package exposure, or inventory duration. No numerical moisture, RH, time, storage condition, seal setting, or sensory outcome for the cooperative is available or assumed.

Advance only the least disruptive arm that meets a cooperative-defined, product-specific crispness target and minimum useful improvement at a distribution-relevant duration, without worse package-integrity performance and within an owner-defined cost/material/maintenance limit. A positive pilot is evidence to plan a larger validation; it does not establish safe shelf life, safety compliance, or sale readiness.

## Case facts, inferences, and open evidence

The following are case statements only: the cooperative changed bag supplier and cooling routine during the same month; some returns reportedly have poor texture despite bags appearing intact; a supplier has offered another film; and no tests or supporting measurements were supplied. The scenario is hypothetical. I do not treat the returns as representative data or infer an actual package failure.

Plausible, non-exclusive explanations include (a) higher or less uniform product moisture at sealing, (b) post-bake redistribution within a piece or between pieces in the sealed headspace, (c) water-vapor ingress through film or seams under actual temperature/RH gradients, (d) variable seal quality, (e) rough handling or package damage not obvious by visual inspection, and (f) a storage/distribution or elapsed-time difference. Oxidation could matter for some formulations, but this case has no ingredient, rancidity, or flavor evidence. Several mechanisms may coexist.

Moisture is not a single interchangeable number. At equilibrium, aw relates to equilibrium RH; a food-specific sorption isotherm relates moisture content to aw at a specified temperature. Composition and temperature matter (ER12-S009). Moisture redistribution inside a piece during cooling and storage is a plausible mechanism. The upstream S002 record reports different moisture distributions and checking outcomes in thicker round versus thinner pierced biscuits, measured with Karl Fischer and NIR. Neither the investigator nor the critic independently verified the publisher’s underlying claim during this review; treat S002 as a bounded literature lead rather than evidence that selects a condition. Even if accurate, those results concern the study’s shapes and recipe, not this snack or a universal cooling rule. In a separate Crackerbread study, instrumented force/acoustic crispness changed with aw and the critical aw was 0.51–0.59 for that product; it cannot set this cooperative’s threshold (ER12-S001). In another study of four commercial biscuit types using trained and consumer panels, ideal aw varied by product and the apparent crispness-loss point depended on the sensory measure; define the cooperative’s own target before choosing an instrument cutoff (ER12-S015).

Package performance is also a whole-system property. A film’s reported WVTR must be read with the film build, thickness, method, temperature, and humidity gradient. Whole-package ingress also depends on area, fill, headspace, seals, seams, corners, and handling. Paired film/thermosealed-package tests found about a 25% transfer increase from seals in the tested PA/PE and PVDC-coated PET/PE builds; the accessible abstract did not disclose the exact T/RH ranges (ER12-S003). A 2024 commercial survey measured several package forms and seam properties, found substantial variation in headspace oxygen among some pouches, and proposed seal issues as one possible explanation; it did not establish the cause for any individual product (ER12-S004). Appearance alone cannot resolve these hypotheses.

Texture retention, package integrity, and food safety need separate evidence. FDA’s 2025 low-moisture RTE guidance page includes chips and crackers but is explicitly a non-binding draft (ER12-S010). FDA’s general Salmonella page notes that drying can prevent growth without killing the organism (ER12-S011). A texture endpoint or leak test cannot establish pathogen absence or process lethality; a food-safety professional must separately assess the actual formulation, process, sanitation, hazards, and applicable controls.

## Alternatives and provisional path

### First: records and baseline, without a film change

Recover the simultaneous change timeline and determine whether both interventions always co-occurred. Ask for old/current supplier documentation and retained film from each period. Request full layer stack, sealant composition/thickness, total gauge, barrier test method and conditions, food-contact documentation, and any tested pouch-level data. Identify bag dimensions, area-to-fill ratio, fill mass, headspace, roll/supplier lot, seal geometry, and storage/distribution history.

For each returned/retained sample, recover batch, formulation/ingredient lots, bake record, cooling duration and endpoint, product core temperature, bed depth/loading, air temperature/RH/airflow, time exposed before pack, package date, operator, sealer identity/maintenance, recorded settings, seal checks, transit/storage RH/T, handling, and age at return. An association in retrospective records is not causal if bag supplier and cooling routine changed together; it can narrow which contrasts are worth testing.

This is the lowest-cost path and can rule some hypotheses in/out without ordering a film. It is most useful if records and retained samples are traceable. It cannot recreate missing historical measurements, prove microbial safety, or establish that an intact-looking returned bag had a sound closure.

### Second: a small, blocked pilot with distinct interventions

A feasible first pilot uses independent bake batches as blocks. Within every batch, randomize comparable product portions to the following arms; trace each pouch to batch, film/roll lot, operator, time and storage unit:

- **A — Baseline:** current film/current documented cooling and packing routine.
- **B — Cooling/packing practice:** current film and seal practice, with a defined, measured cooling/packing procedure. Use a product-specific, owner-approved endpoint after baseline observation; record time, core temperature, air RH/T, exposure, and pack-out moisture/aw. Do not set a numerical endpoint from another product’s study.
- **C — Same-film seal assurance:** current film and cooling; use the material/equipment supplier’s qualification process and a documented, repeatable seal assurance/check routine. Record all settings, jaw/bar type and condition, seal width/geometry, fragments at the seam, startup rejects, and verification results. Do not adopt literature seal settings. Prefer appropriate package-level integrity testing; destructive seal-strength samples are sacrificial.
- **D — Candidate material:** one fully identified candidate film at current cooling. Qualify that construction’s seal window with the supplier/equipment owner and compare completed pouches, not datasheet figures. Record any process change needed to run it, including adjustment/reject/time burden. If the previous film or offered candidate cannot be sampled and characterized, this material contrast cannot answer the question and should be deferred. This is a practical package-system comparison: a candidate may require different seal settings or other process changes, so its outcome estimates the combined change in film plus qualified closure/process. It does not isolate the film’s barrier contribution. If material-only attribution is needed, use matched closure conditions where technically valid or a larger film-by-seal/process factorial; otherwise report the whole-package-system effect.

Use at least three independent bake batches as an initial repeatability screen; determine per-arm sample count from observed pilot variability, material availability, laboratory access, and the effect size the cooperative considers useful. This is not a claim of statistical power. Hold recipe, bake conditions, portion, fill mass, bag size, handling and common storage environment constant except for the factor an arm deliberately changes. Store all arms under a common condition based on the cooperative’s actual distribution records; monitor RH and temperature continuously. If distribution includes distinct environments, add an explicitly blocked storage factor only if sample volume allows. A material-by-cooling interaction requires a larger factorial study; this small comparison estimates main practical paths, not every interaction.

At pack-out, measure moisture and aw at a defined, controlled temperature; if feasible, sample representative center/surface or edge/center moisture. Record product condition before and after cooling so an initial difference is not mistaken for package ingress. At preselected intervals, assess blind sensory crispness against a scale defined with the cooperative, instrumented fracture/bending and/or acoustic response, moisture/aw, visible condition, and package integrity as separate variables. There is no universal crispness instrument or threshold in the literature (ER12-S001). Use the measure customers value, and verify any instrument’s repeatability on this product before relying on small differences.

Some measures are destructive or alter the unit: moisture/aw sampling opens a pouch; puncture/fracture/acoustic texture methods consume or alter a piece; seal-strength peel and many leak tests destroy or alter a package. Use distinct, randomly assigned sacrificial packs for each destructive measure/timepoint. Repeated non-destructive records can include sealed-pack identity, continuous external RH/T, visual observations, and a validated non-invasive integrity/weight method if available; do not repeatedly open the same pouch and then treat it as a sealed-storage sample. Define sample selection, number, timepoints and missing-data handling in advance. If lab access is absent, process and climate logging plus a structured blinded sensory comparison may still screen alternatives, but cannot quantify package WVTR, aw, leak size, or safety.

### How findings would redirect the work

Use the following as provisional interpretations to test, not as diagnoses:

| Observed in a traceable comparison | Next question | Provisional next step |
|---|---|---|
| Pack-out moisture/aw or within-piece moisture gradient differs by cooling lot before packages enter storage. | Does that difference track the documented cooling exposure and reproduce across bake batches? | Prioritize the same-film cooling/packing arm; do not buy a new film to address an initial-condition difference. |
| Pack-out product conditions are similar, but texture/moisture changes with storage duration/RH and the same-film package passes integrity checks. | Does the complete pouch’s barrier under actual storage conditions explain the trend? | Compare a characterized film/whole-pouch candidate; use measured area, fill, headspace, seams and material conditions. |
| Poor results cluster by seal lot, seam location, trapped crumbs, startup/changeover or handling, with package-integrity differences. | Can a same-film qualified seal/handling routine reduce the variation across independent batches? | Prioritize the same-film seal-assurance arm and inspect the process history; do not presume that nominal film WVTR is the main driver. |
| Results vary mainly with long sealed inventory or opened-package exposure, while process and integrity outcomes are comparable. | Is the exposure time/package-size tradeoff operationally meaningful? | Test FIFO/shorter inventory or a smaller unit as a later no-film intervention; measure area-to-fill, headspace, cost and waste. |
| Texture shifts but moisture/aw and package-integrity measures remain similar. | Does the chosen texture measure track the attribute the customer values, and could bake structure/handling explain it? | Recheck the sensory endpoint and product/process history before increasing film barrier or fitting a moisture-only model. |

A pattern may implicate more than one factor; interactions need a larger factorial design. A lack of traceability or adequate precision leaves the cause unresolved and supports another bounded study rather than a winner claim.

### Third: no-film alternatives and conditional options

- **Inventory duration:** use lot-age/first-in-first-out records and compare the actual holding times with returns. Shortening inventory exposure is worth testing only if longer sealed storage is associated with worse texture; it will not correct high initial moisture or a weak seal.
- **Package resizing:** a smaller unit may reduce time after opening or improve consumption completeness, but does not automatically reduce moisture ingress. It changes film area per product mass, headspace, seals-per-unit, cost and waste. Compare a small number of sizes using the same film and record area/fill/headspace; prefer a second pilot stage unless size is the leading issue in returned samples.
- **Storage practice:** maintain a dry, stable storage location or defined handling limits only after measuring current RH/T and mapping realistic distribution. This leaves the primary film unchanged, but its benefit and cost depend on actual exposure and site capabilities.
- **Modified atmosphere:** defer unless oxidative rancidity is measured as a meaningful quality failure. Published modeling shows a headspace humidity choice can help oxidation while worsen moisture-sensitive crispness (ER12-S008).
- **Emerging materials:** PLA/CNC and CNF/wax approaches merit supplier/lab diligence if an actual barrier need is established (ER12-S006–S007). Do not apply an edible coating or ingredient additive: the assignment excludes additives and the studies do not establish suitability here.

### Decision rule and stop/expand conditions

Before unblinding, the cooperative must name: (1) the customer-relevant texture endpoint and acceptable range; (2) the minimum useful improvement over baseline; (3) target sealed-storage duration and distribution RH/T derived from actual history; (4) acceptable package-integrity result; (5) cost, material, sustainability and operating/maintenance burden limits; and (6) who owns a separate food-safety review.

Advance the least disruptive arm only if its texture outcome meets the pre-agreed target at the intended comparison duration across the independent batch blocks, its estimated improvement exceeds the pre-agreed meaningful difference, and its package-integrity result is not worse than baseline. Compare uncertainty across batches; if the result is inconsistent or precision cannot distinguish a meaningful effect, do not select a winner—repeat or narrow the study. If only the candidate film improves texture but requires unaccepted equipment changes, waste, or cost, it does not meet the decision rule. If a process or seal arm works, test maintenance burden and repeatability over normal startup/changeover before broader use.

A screening pilot can rank plausible causes and candidate paths. It cannot by itself support a declared shelf life, food-safety compliance, product release, or a general barrier specification. Any later shelf-life or safety validation needs a specialist-selected protocol and actual product formulation/process conditions.

## Corrections, optional improvements, and owner decisions

### Corrections to causal framing

- “Bag looks intact” is a visual observation, not a seal-integrity result; it does not prove failure either.
- WVTR for a film under stated conditions is not whole-package performance or a shelf-life prediction. Compare full construction and sealed pouch at relevant temperature/RH.
- Low moisture/low aw and preserved crispness do not demonstrate that pathogens are absent or controlled. FDA distinguishes low-moisture handling from pathogen kill (ER12-S010–S011).
- A two-change timeline is confounded. It is not a natural experiment unless independent before/after contrasts or retained matched samples exist.

### Optional improvements

- Preserve retained samples and unopened reference bags with chain-of-custody, lot IDs, age, and storage history before more are discarded.
- Create one data sheet joining recipe/bake, cooling, packing, film, seal, storage, handling, and return records.
- Begin with smaller, traceable pilot lots to bound cost and loss while keeping all comparison arms represented in each batch.
- If returns point to after-opening exposure, test smaller package sizes; if long sealed hold is implicated, test inventory duration. Neither is presumed to solve a seal or high-initial-moisture cause.
- Track operating burden: adjustment frequency, startup scrap, rejects, cleaning, seal inspection effort, changeover, and downtime for any candidate material or seal control.

### Owner decisions required

1. What crispness does the customer value, how will it be scored, and what is a meaningful difference from baseline?
2. What are the intended sealed shelf duration and real storage/distribution temperature/RH profile?
3. Which prior/current film rolls and product batches are available, and can the cooperative access the supplier’s complete construction and seal data?
4. What package sizes, cost ceiling, material/waste preference, maximum operator burden, and maintenance impact are acceptable?
5. Can an independent food-science/food-safety professional review the formulation/process and define a separate safety assessment if needed?
6. Is qualified laboratory access available for moisture/aw, texture, whole-package integrity and/or package transmission tests?

### Final disposition of already-covered, rejected, optional, and uncertain points

**Already covered:** The proposal compares candidate material and whole-package performance, same-film seal assurance, cooling/packing practice, monitored storage, inventory duration and package resizing. It includes initial moisture and aw, equilibration, RH/T, package construction and geometry, seal variation, handling history, independent batch blocks, traceability, destructive versus repeated observations, and an explicit advancement rule.

**Rejected for this case:** Buying the offered film from a datasheet alone; transferring another product’s aw or critical-moisture limit or predicted shelf life; interpreting an intact-looking bag as verified integrity; attributing the returns to either coincident change; claiming a candidate-film system effect is a film-only effect; prescribing a production seal setting; claiming safety from low moisture, crispness, or better seals; changing headspace without evidence oxidation is limiting; or adding coatings/ingredients (outside scope).

**Optional, owner-dependent paths:** Smaller pack sizes may reduce opened-package exposure but change area-to-fill, cost and waste; shorter inventory may help only if sealed duration is implicated; storage controls may be useful only after actual exposure is measured; modified atmosphere is deferred pending oxidation evidence; emerging PLA/CNC and CNF/wax constructions are exploratory supplier/lab leads, not qualified candidates. Track changeovers, scrap, adjustment frequency, cleaning, inspection effort, downtime, and ongoing tests for any process/material path.

**Uncertain:** Formulation and sorption behavior; valued crispness endpoint; prior/current film stack, sealant, gauge and barrier conditions; seal integrity; cooling/packing history; product and package batch linkage; real distribution RH/T and duration; availability and qualification of any film; supplier evidence; lab access; cost and operator burden. These prevent causal diagnosis, a transferable endpoint, film selection, a safe shelf-life claim, or a complete implementation-history comparison. A modest controlled pilot can still rank practical local mechanisms and paths without making those broader claims.
