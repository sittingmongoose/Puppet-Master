## Case boundary and first read

This is a hypothetical dry baked snack with occasional reported loss of crispness in sealed sample bags. The cooperative changed the bag supplier and cooling routine in the same month. The bags look intact, but appearance is not a leak or barrier measurement. No snack or package samples, process records, supplier construction sheets, moisture readings, water-activity readings, sensory results, or package tests were supplied. Thus no cause, defect, safety status, shelf life, or sale status is established. No experiment or calculation has been run for this case.

Because supplier and cooling changes were simultaneous, a before/after comparison by calendar date would confound package, handling, and possibly batch or season. Reconstruct the transition with dated supplier and film-lot records, package construction/thickness and dimensions, line and seal records, cooling duration and conditions, batch identities, storage/distribution records, and return histories. Do not attribute the complaint to a film or to cooling from the anecdote alone.

## Plausible mechanisms and distinguishing evidence

1. **External water-vapor transfer through film.** A low-moisture product can take up water as the surrounding water-vapor pressure exceeds the product/headspace equilibrium. Moisture content and water activity are related nonlinearly and depend on formulation and temperature; the same moisture gain need not cause the same texture change in different products. Water can plasticize a crisp cereal matrix and change its fracture behavior. Arimi et al. found a critical activity interval of 0.51–0.59 for one commercial Crackerbread product under a specific equilibration and puncture/acoustic method; this is a comparator, not a limit for the cooperative’s unspecified snack [S01]. Hough et al. found that four commercial biscuits differed in preferred crispness activity and that the perceived loss depended on whether assessors judged molar crispness or sound [S02].

2. **Package or seal leakage despite a normal appearance.** The seal region can be a moisture ingress path. A review groups heat-seal leak contributors into process settings, material properties, contamination, and later handling, so the film supplier alone is not a complete package description [S08]. Seal strength and leak detection are separate measurements: ASTM F88/F88M measures force and failure mode on a sealed strip and can support process capability; it does not by itself establish a leak-free package [S10]. ASTM F2096 is a destructive gross-leak check, with a stated 250 μm sensitivity at 81% probability; it is not a water-vapor barrier test and cannot clear smaller defects [S11]. A suitable integrity method must be chosen and validated for the actual pouch format and study question.

3. **Initial moisture and internal equilibration.** Moisture gradients can exist within freshly baked products and continue to redistribute during cooling and storage. In the BRICE project report, round/thick and rectangular/thin pierced biscuits behaved differently; water was mapped at surface and center, and checking was observed within 1–2 days after baking in those products [S07]. A separate cracker study found moisture-sorption kinetics depended on cellular morphology, with fine and coarse structures behaving differently [S03]. The evidence justifies measuring the cooperative product’s initial state and recording geometry and cooling; it does not justify importing a cooling duration, moisture threshold, or texture result from another biscuit.

4. **Humidity, temperature, and handling history.** The humidity and temperature encountered while cooling, waiting for packing, storing, shipping, and opening affect moisture transfer and can alter measured texture. Record actual exposure and handling instead of treating nominal film WVTR as a full shelf-behavior prediction. Establish the customer-relevant texture attribute before choosing an instrumental endpoint.

These mechanisms may coexist. A supplier change can change film structure, surface area-to-product ratio, sealant layer, stiffness, and sealability; a cooling change can change surface exposure, temperature at packing, and product moisture distribution. A barrier film can preserve product moisture yet still leave integrity, texture preference, oxidation, or food safety unresolved.

## Comparable implementations and practical alternatives

- **Higher-barrier film/package candidate.** Baele et al. reported caramelized cookies in flowpacks made from PP, acryl-coated PVdC/PP, or metallized PP, stored at 22 °C/50% RH; the metallized PP package extended textural acceptability in that case [S04]. Balestra et al. compared metalized OPP/paper control packaging with PLA substitution and an OPP/EVA variant on biscuits from one production batch, then stored them at 35 °C/50% RH for 105 days. Packages differed in moisture and water activity, while measured texture did not differ significantly [S05]. Together these show why construction, product, storage condition, and measured endpoint must accompany any claimed material result. Their results do not set a target construction or shelf life for this snack.
- **Alternative low-moisture package.** Jo et al. made 40–45 μm filler-embedded LDPE zipper bags, 25 × 30.5 cm, using a continuous band heat sealer, and compared them with typical LDPE zipper bags at 25 °C/50% RH for 20 days. The reported permeability and texture effects differed among biscuit, shortbread, and puffed-snack products [S06]. This is an example of an actual construction and controlled comparison, but its reclosable zipper format, foods, and test setup differ from the cooperative’s sealed samples.
- **No-film-change option.** Standardize and log cooling/hold time, product temperature at packing, exposed humidity, post-pack storage temperature/RH, and handling while retaining the current verified film and package format. This directly tests the changed practice and is reversible. It does not replace a package-integrity check. Do not invent a target cooling time or RH without the product, line, and supplier evidence.
- **Separate package-integrity option.** Retain the current film while sampling seals for consistent seal-strength and a format-appropriate leak check; record seal contamination, wrinkles, channels, and package damage. Treat a material change as a package-system trial because sealability may change with the film.

ASTM F1249-25 is a current method for film/sheathing WVTR and permeance; its stated scope and significance emphasize agreeing on sampling, test conditions, and acceptance criteria and caution that simple pressure-differential scaling may not apply [S09]. Ask for supplier WVTR values with their temperature, RH differential, conditioning, thickness, and test method; compare like conditions or measure candidate and current films under one agreed protocol. A flat-film value cannot alone characterize a finished bag’s seals, defects, headspace, product loading, or handling.

## Pilot logic to carry forward

A useful modest comparison is a blocked 2 × 2 design across independent production batches: current package/current handling; candidate package/current handling; current package/standardized cooling and storage handling; candidate package/standardized handling. If the specific candidate is not known, the package factor can instead test a controlled seal/integrity intervention with the same film. State clearly which package factor is being tested. Keep ingredients, recipe, bake lot, product size, fill mass, package dimensions, storage schedule, and measurement timepoints matched or recorded; randomize pack assignment within each batch. Use independent packages for destructive or opening-based measurements. Select replication after estimating batch-to-batch variability and available laboratory capacity rather than inventing a power claim.

At baseline and scheduled points, pair moisture content and water activity with the chosen texture measure (instrumental fracture/acoustic measure, and a sensory descriptor if the cooperative can define and support it). Record product temperature and moisture at packing, exposed time/RH, film construction/lot/thickness/dimensions, seal observations and process record, package integrity observations, batch and sample IDs, and logged storage/distribution conditions. Keep texture retention, package integrity, and food safety as separate outcome tracks. A seal-strength result is not a leak result; a moisture/texture result is not a safety result.

Predefine the texture attribute and a practically meaningful improvement with the cooperative before interpreting results. A provisional material path would require a repeatable, practically meaningful texture advantage over current packaging across batches under the same handling, with no worse package-integrity observations and with a full-package explanation of the supplier’s barrier data. A no-film handling path is supported only if it improves the preselected endpoint while current film remains unchanged and package integrity is characterized. If texture does not improve despite lower moisture uptake, do not call the package a crispness solution. If results differ by batch or treatment interaction, retain uncertainty and collect more controlled data before changing procurement.

This proposal cannot establish a safe shelf life, food-safety compliance, or sale readiness. The supplied anecdote and film data sheets are insufficient for those decisions. No food experiment was conducted, no additive is recommended, and no production seal setting is prescribed.

## Unresolved facts and what they prevent

Ingredients/formula, starting moisture and activity, geometry, package construction and dimensions, seal equipment, cooling duration and environment, batch records, storage/distribution history, and the customer-valued texture measure remain unknown. Without the formula and actual initial readings, published critical-activity values or sorption curves cannot be transferred. Without the package design and comparable conditioned film data, no barrier target or sealing method can be selected. Without batch and timeline records, the simultaneous changes cannot be causally separated retrospectively. Without a texture definition, there is no valid acceptance threshold. Laboratory access is unconfirmed; lacking it limits the pilot to traceable process and storage observation and does not provide reliable moisture/activity, instrumental texture, or package-integrity measurements. A small pilot can still identify whether controlled handling or the package system is the more promising next step, but it cannot certify safety or assign commercial shelf life.
