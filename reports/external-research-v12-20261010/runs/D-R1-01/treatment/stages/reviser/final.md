# Revised research proposal: moisture and crisp texture in cooperative dry-snack bags

**Case:** ER12-D-R1-01-FRESH  
**Run / stage:** D-R1-01-treatment / reviser  
**Status:** Complete research proposal for a hypothetical case. No food or package tests, case measurements, performance validation, or safety assessment have been conducted.

## Provisional recommendation

Do not order a pilot film from the complaint alone. First reconstruct the supplier and cooling changes from dated, batch-linked records. Then run a modest, blocked comparison that separates one defined package-system factor from a no-film-change cooling/storage factor while keeping the product and intended storage exposure controlled. The package factor must be selected after the current and offered constructions and seal compatibility are known. If records show seal variation, an intervention on the existing film can be the package factor instead of a new film.

Track three outcomes separately throughout: customer-valued texture retention, package integrity, and food safety. A crispness improvement does not show that seals are sound or that food is safe. A passing gross-leak or vacuum-decay screen does not prove adequate moisture barrier, safe shelf life, or sale readiness. A film WVTR value does not describe all finished-package effects.

The present evidence does not identify a cause or select a material. The same-month supplier and cooling changes are confounded; no sample, defect, batch-linked return history, or test result was supplied. The immediate decision is to recover records and define the product, package, endpoint, and intended distribution before fixing pilot factors or acceptance criteria.

## Case facts and limits

The cooperative reports that some sealed sample bags have returned with poor texture and an intact appearance, and that the bag supplier and cooling routine changed during the same month. This scenario is hypothetical. No food or package samples, production records, construction sheets, moisture or water-activity readings, sensory results, seal records, leak tests, or storage data were provided. Appearance alone is not a leak or barrier measurement. The changes cannot be separated retrospectively without records.

The following remain unknown: recipe and ingredient lots; initial moisture and water activity; piece dimensions and internal moisture gradients; current and offered film layers, gauge, sealant, package area and headspace; seal type and equipment; cooling duration and conditions; time from oven to measurement and packing; storage and transport path; batch identifiers; return age and handling; intended holding period; preferred texture attribute; available laboratory methods and capacity; and pilot budget. Those gaps prevent selecting a film, transferring a published threshold, setting a sample size or storage condition, attributing prior complaints, or making a shelf-life or safety conclusion. A modest pilot could still identify whether controlled handling or the actual package system deserves the next investment.

## Plausible mechanisms and evidence that would distinguish them

The mechanisms may coexist; each is a hypothesis about this case, not a finding.

| Mechanism | Relevant evidence and its limits | Observations that would help distinguish it |
|---|---|---|
| Water-vapor transfer through the film | Water transfer depends on the vapor-pressure difference, temperature, humidity, product sorption behavior, layer structure, gauge, exposed area, and test conditions. Moisture content and water activity are nonlinearly related and formulation-specific. Water uptake can plasticize a crisp matrix and change fracture or acoustic texture, but the relationship depends on product and method. The accessible Crackerbread abstract reports samples equilibrated across aw 0.113–0.98 and a 5 mm force/acoustic puncture method, with a product-specific critical activity interval of 0.51–0.59; four commercial biscuits also differed by product and sensory cue. The interval is not this snack’s threshold [S01, S02, S05]. | Identify the actual layer stack, gauge, and supplier WVTR method/conditions. Compare current and candidate film under agreed common conditions; pair that result with product moisture/activity and texture over the same controlled storage exposure. |
| Moisture gradients and internal redistribution | Biscuit and cracker studies report geometry- and morphology-dependent moisture distribution and sorption. A product can change internally or exchange moisture with headspace after packing without a demonstrated ambient leak [S03, S07]. | At packing, log product temperature and exposed time; measure moisture and activity at baseline and, if meaningful for the piece, separately at surface and center. Use separate unopened packs at scheduled points. Do not import comparator cooling times or rates. |
| Seal path or other package-system variation | Seals can be affected by material properties, process parameters, contamination, later handling, and geometry [S08]. A retail-biscuit study found that the measured water-vapor transfer of some finished packages exceeded film-area estimates where seals were reported as impaired [S12]. | Record seal design, machine, process and maintenance changes, seal contamination/wrinkles/channels, and transport damage. Use a validated package-level leak method suited to the actual package; record seal-strength distribution separately. |
| Cooling, staging, ambient humidity, storage, or handling | Temperature and humidity during cooling, staging, packing, storage, transit, and return handling may change the product’s starting condition or moisture path. A cooling-model thesis identifies product temperature, product depth, and line speed as variables, but supplies no transferable setpoint [S14]. | Reconstruct time-stamped product and environment history. In the pilot, log actual temperature/RH and time at each stage, and compare a defined handling practice while holding material constant. |
| High-humidity response of a barrier layer | If an actual film contains EVOH, a study of four EVOH copolymers at 23 ± 2 °C reports permeability rising by up to two orders of magnitude above aw 0.75. That condition-specific result applies only to identified EVOH structures; it does not identify this film [S13]. A separate powder result is a different product and package from the cookie comparison [S04]. | Obtain the supplier’s full construction and barrier data with temperature/RH conditions. Do not infer that the cooperative’s unidentified film contains EVOH or apply a powder result to the snack. |

A supplier change may alter the layer stack, gauge, sealant, stiffness, sealability, or package area. A cooling change may alter exposed time, temperature at packing, and moisture distribution. Nominal flat-film WVTR alone cannot establish the performance of a finished bag with seals, headspace, product loading, defects, and real handling.

## Proposed controlled pilot

This is a proposed later study. No pilot setup, test, measurement, calculation, or validation described here has been executed.

### Questions and treatment arms

Ask separately whether (1) the package system changes moisture ingress or package integrity under controlled handling and (2) a no-film-change handling practice changes the chosen texture outcome. For the first modest comparison, choose one handling factor: either cooling/hold/staging through packing or post-pack storage/handling. Keep the other stages documented and as constant as practicable; if several practices change together, interpret the arm only as a whole handling-system comparison.

Use a blocked 2 × 2 comparison after naming the package factor:

1. Current package with current, documented handling as the baseline.
2. Candidate package system with the same handling.
3. Current package with one defined, standardized handling practice (cooling/packing or storage).
4. Candidate package system with that same standardized practice.

The package factor is either (a) a candidate material/package system or (b) a defined integrity/seal-control intervention on the same film. Do not change film and sealing practice as two unseparated changes and then attribute the result to film. If a new structure requires a different compatible seal process, state that the treatment is the whole package system. If the candidate structure is not identified or compatible, defer that factor rather than improvise one.

### Blocking, traceability, and replication

Block on independent production batches and split each batch among all four arms. Randomize arm assignment within each batch and retain the randomization record. Treat packages, not multiple pieces from the same bag, as the package-level units. Use separate unopened packages for opening-based or destructive measurements at each timepoint.

The earlier suggestion of three independent batches is only an exploratory planning example, not an evidence-based minimum or power guarantee. Set the batch and package count after obtaining baseline variability, a customer-defined meaningful texture difference, the intended storage envelope, and laboratory capacity. No sample-size calculation was possible or performed from the supplied case. If only a few batches are feasible, report batch-specific estimates and uncertainty; do not infer a stable effect from a nominal batch count.

Keep recipe and ingredient lots, bake conditions, piece geometry, fill mass, package dimensions, packing schedule, and storage schedule matched where possible; record any difference. Trace each sample to production batch, film roll/lot, package, treatment, measurement time, instrument, and operator. Use consistent methods, documented calibration/quality controls, blinded treatment labels for scoring when feasible, and an anomaly log.

### Observations and method selection

Before the pilot, the cooperative must define the texture cue customers value, the practically meaningful improvement, and the intended storage/handling distribution. At each batch baseline, record product mass and dimensions, product temperature at packing, elapsed exposed time, moisture content, and water activity at a specified measurement temperature. If surface/center gradients are meaningful for the product, sample and label those regions separately. Select an instrumental fracture/acoustic measure suited to the product. Add sensory scoring only if the cooperative can define and support it with an appropriate protocol. S01’s reported Crackerbread activity range and S02’s biscuit preferences are comparators, not thresholds for this snack.

Log actual temperature and RH during cooling/staging and storage, and capture time in each stage. Measure texture, moisture, and water activity at baseline and at preselected early, intermediate, and end points within the owner-defined storage window. Use different unopened packs at each opening-based point. If headspace conditions are measured, use separate sacrificial or instrumented packages so the sensor does not compromise the integrity observations. Record supplier and film lot, layer stack, thickness, package area, fill/headspace, seal observations and process records, storage deviations, transport/handling, and package damage.

For a flexible nonporous bag, ASTM F2338-24 vacuum decay is a candidate package-level method because its official scope includes flexible nonporous packages. Its results depend on the actual product/package and setup; the method describes calibration and acceptance criteria based on non-leaking controls and simulated leaks. Validate sensitivity and repeatability on the actual format before relying on it. ASTM F2095-07(2021) is another flexible-package pressure-decay option, but it is destructive and its scope/conditions must fit the actual sample. ASTM F2096-11(2019) is a gross-leak bubble method; although tray and pouch formats are listed, the catalog says sensitivity has not been evaluated for nonporous packaging, so do not assume it is suitable for a plastic snack bag. The cited 250 μm / 81% probability is a method comparator, not a case performance guarantee [S11, R05, R09, C12].

Record package-level leak results by batch, arm, and timepoint, including invalid tests and suspected leak location. Before unblinding, choose a validated method, document its detection limit and limitations, set a fit-for-purpose integrity acceptance criterion for the intended package use against current-package baselines and relevant simulated-leak controls, and set an owner-approved noninferiority margin for candidate versus current package. A candidate must meet the integrity criterion as well as be noninferior; a failing current baseline cannot be made acceptable by comparison alone. No numerical criterion or margin is justified by the supplied evidence. If the method cannot be validated or the owner has not approved an interpretable rule, the pilot cannot pass the integrity gate. A zero observed failures in a small sample is not proof of zero population risk.

Seal-strength sampling may supplement that check. ASTM F88/F88M-23 measures force to separate a test strip and identifies failure mode; use one fixture/technique consistently and treat it as process information, not package leak status. ASTM F1249-25 can support a film/sheet comparison under agreed sampling, conditions, and criteria; its result is not whole-package performance. Supplier WVTR reports must state temperature, humidity differential, conditioning, thickness, and method, and should be compared under matched conditions [S09, S10, R06, R08].

### Decision rule

Before treatment labels are unblinded, record:

- the customer-valued texture attribute and minimum practically meaningful improvement;
- the exact package-system factor and handling factor;
- the intended storage/distribution envelope and planned sampling schedule;
- the package-integrity method, validation evidence, detection limits, baseline, acceptance criteria, and noninferiority margin;
- the batch/package sampling plan and how uncertainty will be reported.

Advance a candidate package system only if it yields a repeatable, practically meaningful improvement in the chosen texture endpoint across batch blocks under matched handling, and passes the separate, predeclared integrity gate. Do not advance a candidate merely because moisture uptake is lower. If current-film standardized handling improves the chosen endpoint and integrity remains characterized, prefer that reversible no-film-change route while keeping material selection open. If treatment effects vary substantially by batch, the package and handling interact, or the required method/criteria are unresolved, retain uncertainty and collect more controlled evidence rather than declare a winner.

Texture retention, package integrity, and food safety are separate conclusions. The proposed pilot is not a food-safety study. Any safety or sale decision requires a separate qualified review of the actual recipe, process, package, and intended handling. Nothing here establishes safe shelf life, compliance, or readiness for sale.
