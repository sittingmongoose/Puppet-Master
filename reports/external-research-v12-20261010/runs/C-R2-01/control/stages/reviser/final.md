# Research proposal: curling and attachment of paper labels on collection sleeves

**Run:** C-R2-01-control  
**Case:** ER12-C-R2-01-FRESH / C-R2-01  
**Stage:** reviser  
**Evidence status:** The setting is invented. No specimen, product identity, room record, failure denominator, or material-performance result was supplied. The mechanisms below are hypotheses, not diagnoses. This proposal recommends no collection-wide change.

## Recommendation

Keep labels in place and defer bulk changes. First map the affected population, preserve the information on annotated labels under owner-approved procedures, reconstruct stock/printer/sleeve/application history, and monitor temperature and relative humidity (RH) where the labels are stored. Then run a bounded, staged comparison on spare labels and spare or documented matching empty sleeves. Do not apply trial materials to collection objects.

Keep three outcomes separate throughout: **paper dimensional stability and curl; attachment reliability; and continued readability of printed and handwritten information.** A good result on one does not compensate for failure on another. The store owner and conservator must set acceptable limits and approve any later pilot.

## Plausible mechanisms and evidence limits

- **Paper movement:** paper absorbs and releases moisture; expansion and shrinkage can be reversible or leave a lasting dimensional change. Larsson et al. measured laboratory sheets made from four commercial pulps through four 50→20→90→50% RH cycles at 23°C, holding each level for at least 24 hours. Response differed with sheet preparation and restraint, including transient first-cycle behavior and irreversible shrinkage in restrained sheets (S01). This supports measuring the actual stock and curl independently. It does not establish how these unidentified label stocks behave, and its chamber cycle is not a default test or storage prescription.
- **Printing and drying:** Maass and Hirn tested two office-paper grades with a one-sided 60 wt% glycerol/water model ink. They observed changing curl and glycerol migration over one week and attributed long-term curl in that model to co-solvent migration (S02). This is relevant only if the case’s ink, stock and printing process are materially comparable. It does not establish that these labels were inkjet printed or explain handwriting or bond failure.
- **Layer and interface behavior:** a face stock, print, adhesive, sleeve surface and application method can interact. Humidity-related paper movement may stress an edge bond; the sleeve polymer, grade, coating or surface treatment can affect contact; pressure, coverage, dwell, temperature at application and contamination can affect an adhesive interface. None is identified here. A 2025 modified-acrylate study on HDPE is only a conditional lead (S07); its detailed temperature result was not independently verified in this review and is not used to select or reject any material.
- **Printing-system compatibility:** NARA’s October 2015 specifications illustrate that the stock, adhesive, printer/ribbon or toner, housing surface and quality checks form a system. One laser specification describes paper face stock with acrylic adhesive and expressly excludes direct application to documents or records; its adhesive is intended to be permanent and difficult to remove. The thermal-transfer specification names polyester or polypropylene face stock, acrylic adhesive and hard-resin ribbon, with a polypropylene preference for curved surfaces (S03–S04). These are procurement conditions for specified housing/furniture uses, not conservation-safe recommendations, sleeve-specific evidence, or acceptance thresholds for this case.
- **Environment:** variable humidity is a lead to measure, not proof of cause. CCI guidance describes RH as local and time-dependent, recommends logged readings near collections, and says a full year may be preferable when assessing seasonal RH transmission into a case. It cautions against changing established climate control without evidence of the current risk (S05). No room target or control change follows from the anecdote.

A contextual SPNHC review distinguishes label substrate, information and attachment, and records unresolved testing questions (S08). National Museums Scotland guidance mentions a Tyvek tag for some fragile or loan objects (S09). Both are context-specific leads, not performance evidence or a validated sleeve design.

## Records and owner decisions needed first

For each stock, adhesive and supply period, request maker, exact product/code, revision, lot and manufacture date; face-stock material and dimensions; paper basis weight, thickness, coating/sizing and grain direction or synthetic polymer/grade; adhesive chemistry and coat weight; liner; technical sheet, SDS, test reports, instructions, storage conditions, reformulations and discontinuance notices. Retain purchase records and any unused stock or packaging.

For the printing history, request printer make/model and process; firmware/settings only where recorded; toner, ink or ribbon type, code and lot; coverage, feed path, stock orientation, drying/fusing settings where relevant, maintenance and commissioning dates. Establish whether the printer change also changed media, or whether handwritten labels ever passed through a printer.

For sleeves and application, record manufacturer, resin/grade, lot/date, documented coating or surface treatment, sleeve age and prior handling; label placement/orientation; work-instruction version, operator, pressure/tool/strokes, temperature/RH, dwell before storage, and any documented cleaning or handling. Do not introduce solvent cleaning. For storage, obtain original logger files, sensor location/calibration/interval, moves, HVAC or water incidents and monitoring gaps. For failures, define the population inspected and record counts by batch, date, location and failure mode; replace “a few” with a denominator where possible.

The owner must decide who may image or transcribe annotations, whether labels can be replaced, which record is authoritative, acceptable failure and readability limits, security/access needs, whether mechanical retention is workable, study resources, and who may authorize a pilot. Missing history stays unknown. A current product with the same generic name may differ from the historical lot or formulation.

## Bounded later study

### 1. Non-interventive baseline and monitoring

After owner approval, assign nonmeaningful IDs and map affected locations without lifting labels. If approved, photograph annotated labels under fixed light and scale and make a checked transcription linked to the original identifier; preserve originals and restrict access as the owner directs. If copying is not authorized, do not copy and record that dependency. Inspect a defined population or stratified sample; count curled, edge-lifted, detached, unreadable and apparently intact labels separately. Do not peel, heat, wet, clean, remove, or destructively sample collection labels.

Use calibrated temperature/RH loggers near the affected shelf and at a comparison location, with placement, model, accuracy, calibration, interval and retrieval dates recorded. A 15-minute interval is a proposed operational setting, not a source requirement. Record at a predeclared monthly inspection interval and log every newly observed failure against the mapped population and last-observation date. Continue monitoring for 12 months when seasonal behavior is the question, consistent with CCI’s guidance (S05). This is proposed observation, not monitoring already performed. Do not change room controls from this anecdote alone.

### 2. Identity gate, sleeve strata and feasibility

Do not start coupon exposure until stock, adhesive, printer/media, sleeve and application identities are established well enough to define the question, or explicitly label the comparison as a newly designed system rather than a reconstruction. First identify materially distinct sleeve polymer/grade/surface-treatment/condition groups. **Treat each distinct group as a separate block or stratum; do not pool different sleeve surfaces.** If the store confirms only one relevant sleeve stratum, state that conclusions apply only to that verified stratum. If resources cannot cover other strata, prioritize one with the owner and leave the others unresolved.

Before choosing a matrix, inventory available lots, compatible supplies, chamber capacity, staff time and owner-defined limits. Use a staged screening design rather than automatically running a 16-cell matrix for every sleeve type. Within each feasible sleeve stratum, compare the identified current system with one candidate selected only after specifications review, holding the verified printer and application constant for the first contrast where possible. Use a stable reference and an exposure based on measured store history and product instructions. Include a bare-sleeve control and an unprinted paper control where they answer a defined question.

If old/new stock and printer/media configurations can be reconstructed, add a balanced contrast for stock-by-printer interaction. Add an application contrast only when the old/current procedure is sufficiently documented to reproduce. Include exposure interactions where the question and resources support them. If enough material and capacity exist, a fuller factorial or fractional-factorial screen can test selected interactions; otherwise choose the highest-priority comparisons before starting, keep omitted factors fixed, and report their effects as unresolved. Do not claim the study separated a factor or interaction it did not vary. Randomize coupon position and blind scoring where feasible. Use six coupons per condition distributed across at least three production lots when available; if only one lot is available, say so. This is exploratory replication, not rare-failure certification. The number of sleeve strata and factor contrasts may be reduced only with that limitation stated.

### 3. Duration and exposure

The sources do **not** establish an adequate duration for these labels. S01 reports four specified RH cycles on laboratory sheets; S02 followed a particular model ink/paper response over one week. Neither supports a 12-week durability claim. Use **12 weeks as a proposed maximum project-planning cap for the coupon screen only**, subject to owner confirmation of capacity, not as an evidence-derived endpoint or proof of adequacy. Before exposure, specify the research question, exact condition, RH/temperature range, ramps and dwell, planned observations, and stop/inconclusive rule. Base any cyclic envelope on actual monitoring and material instructions; do not copy S01’s 20–90% cycle or NARA conditions as defaults. Keep exposure at ordinary/justified test conditions; this proposal includes no heat. If no informative exposure occurs, no failures are observed, the needed condition is not reached, or results conflict by lot/stratum at the cap, report the screen as **inconclusive**. Any extension requires a separate owner decision before the cap. Do not translate accelerated exposure into years in storage. Continue ordinary storage monitoring separately; it is not an accelerated test.

### 4. Measurements, failure log and decision gates

At baseline, weekly, after each declared cycle, and at the end after return to a declared reference condition, record the following independently:

1. **Dimensional stability:** length and width in known sheet directions; curl/flatness using a fixed support, orientation, scale and lighting; dimensional change after conditioning.
2. **Attachment reliability:** edge/corner lift in millimetres, spontaneous detachment, ooze/transfer, face-stock tear and failure plane. Any peel measurement is limited to sacrificial coupons and must specify geometry, angle/rate and conditioning; do not use in-place labels or copy a NARA threshold without owner justification.
3. **Readability:** separately score print and handwriting from consistent images; use a checked double reading of any authorized transcription; test scanning/barcode decoding only if relevant. Any abrasion procedure is coupon-only and predeclared. Never rub or alter in-place annotations.

For each coupon/event retain ID, status (original or replica), product/lot, sleeve stratum, printer/media/settings, application/operator/date, temperature/RH record, condition/cycle, observation time, each endpoint, severity/failure category, photo link, annotation status and reviewer. Keep the original annotation image/transcription linked to, not substituted for, the original record.

Before viewing results, the owner and conservator set separate minimum gates for identity linkage, readability, dimensional behavior, attachment and contact/transfer risk, plus an acceptable failure rate and approval authority. Report results by condition, sleeve stratum and lot, not as one composite score. Any lost identity, unreadable annotation, suspected transfer/contact risk, or broken accession link stops that candidate for review regardless of its average curl or adhesion. If an option meets the gates for the tested sleeve/process, consider only a small owner-approved pilot on empty or matching sleeves, maintain a crosswalk to prior identifiers and inspect at predeclared intervals. Expand only after pilot review. If evidence is mixed, prerequisites are missing, or thresholds are unset, retain current identified materials under monitoring and report uncertainty; do not roll out a collection-wide fix.
