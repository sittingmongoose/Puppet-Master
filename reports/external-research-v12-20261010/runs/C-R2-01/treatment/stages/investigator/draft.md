## Proposal in brief

Do not make a collection-wide material change from the anecdote. Keep existing labels and collection objects in place, start a non-contact condition and readability record, and recover exact material, printer, application, batch and room histories. Compare a matched, non-collection mock-up study only after the sleeve resin/finish, label construction and printer process are known. Keep dimensional movement, attachment, and legibility as separate outcomes.

No product or brand is selected. No adhesive change is established by the brief: it says label stock and printer changed, while adhesive identity is unknown. “Inert sleeve” is not sufficient identification of a resin or surface. Any result remains conditional on what can be documented and reproduced.

## Mechanisms and evidence

**Paper movement and curl.** Cellulose fibers respond to moisture, and paper movement depends on sheet/fiber structure and orientation. The review reports greater hygroexpansion across the machine direction than along it and identifies fiber orientation, sheet structure and process history as influential [S04]. Primary experimental work documents humidity/temperature effects on paper/fiber mechanical and expansional response under tested conditions [S05]. This makes cyclic or transient room humidity a plausible contributor to movement; it does not establish the store's exposure or the case label stock’s magnitude. Record width and length separately and record machine/cross direction only when the stock’s orientation is actually known.

Curl is out-of-plane shape change and must not be confused with edge detachment. One-sided print/coating effects, through-thickness moisture/composition gradients, sheet two-sidedness and manufacturing roll-set can contribute. Maass and Hirn report an inkjet/model-ink experiment and propose delayed co-solvent migration as one mechanism [S06]. It is a conditional hypothesis: the brief does not identify the printer or ink, and the paper/model system differs from adhesive labels.

**Bond and sleeve.** As a mechanical inference, a paper label attached to a flexible polymer surface can experience differential strain as environment changes; this pairing has not been tested in the case. Bond behavior may also vary with polymer/resin and surface finish, contamination, adhesive construction, pressure, contact time, storage/cure, label geometry and position on a flexing sleeve. The word “inert” identifies no resin, additive, surface treatment or finish. The LoC procurement series deliberately separates substrate and printer process and distinguishes bond, curl/lift, print fastness and abrasion [S01–S03]. The laser specification selects a high-permanence acrylic adhesive that is intended to be difficult to remove [S03]; this is a tradeoff to compare against replaceability requirements, not a universal best choice. These are conditions LoC specified for its own procurements, not default instructions for this case.

**Printing and implementation.** A stock change may change the face stock, coating, adhesive, liner or all of them. A printer change may change print mechanism, ribbon/toner/ink, paper path, media profile, settings or handling. A Canon model manual, offered only as a model-specific example, ties curl troubleshooting to damp/unsupported paper, paper type and environment, and notes print-quality/speed tradeoffs for settings changes [S08]. Do not infer those same controls apply to the unknown store printer. The manufacturer’s guide and exact archived settings for the actual printer are needed.

**Manufacturing history.** A commercial newsprint experiment found cross-directional sheet variation by web position that affected print registration [S09]. This is not a label-stock result, but it supports asking for production/batch traceability instead of trusting a repeated product name. The LoC laser-label spec also makes manufacturer, year and run/batch part of package identification [S03].

No source identifies a specific stock, adhesive, printer, sleeve or failure condition in this invented case. No source supports the claim that the adhesive alone caused the issue.

Current application method may be unrecoverable; that uncertainty must remain visible. |

## Bounded later study

### Gate 0 — approvals and two-week baseline

Before testing, the owner/conservator decides: what label failure/association risk is acceptable; whether any label is replaceable; whether and how annotations may be copied; whether affected sleeves may be monitored in place; which records are exceptional/high-value/photo/fragile; and whether mock inserts/holders and any ordinary-condition chamber work are permissible. Resolve current material identity and obtain available vendor instructions. If material identity or annotation policy remains unknown, do not test on the collection.

For two weeks, non-contact map RH and temperature at affected shelf height and a naturally stable comparison location if available. Use identified/check-status loggers, record interval and gaps, and survey current labels in place with scale/color-referenced photos. Make a two-person checked transcription and sleeve crosswalk only if the owner permits copying; otherwise record that the annotation safeguard gate is unresolved. Do not change storage climate before a conservator reviews the measurements.

### Gate 1 — mock-up factor screen

Use empty mock sleeves/coupons made from the verified sleeve resin/finish and no collection contents. Compare the recorded old and new label systems and printers only where actual stock and equipment are available and the combinations are manufacturer-compatible. Use four crossed stock-system × printer cells (old/new × old/new) when possible, and add application as a second crossed factor: current practice faithfully reconstructed on mockups versus supplier-guided standardized practice. If stock and adhesive are separable and supplier can provide matched constructions, add a matched face-stock/adhesive comparison; if they are bundled, call the factor “complete label system” and leave layer attribution open. Never run an incompatible combination merely to fill a factorial cell.

For exposure, use a controlled ordinary-condition comparison only after the two-week logger record establishes a meaningful range: a stable profile and a variable profile bounded to the measured room RH/temperature range, in real elapsed time with no time compression or elevated-temperature aging. If suitable controlled facilities are unavailable, place randomized samples in two naturally occurring monitored locations and treat location as a block, not proof that humidity alone caused a difference. If no meaningful cycle occurs during baseline, humidity exposure remains untested; do not invent one.

A bounded screening cohort of 12 specimens per valid factorial cell, distributed across three independently printed/applied runs (four per run), is a feasibility design to reveal common problems and batch/run patterns. It is not enough by itself to establish a low failure rate. Before an acceptance study, the owner must define an acceptable failure probability and confidence requirement; calculate the necessary sample size and add independent label lots if possible. Randomize positions and retain unlabelled sleeve controls and unbonded paper witnesses to separate sleeve movement and paper movement. Use only supplier/owner-approved conditioning; record all dwell times and conditions rather than borrowing the LoC’s 7-day/23°C/50% RH setup as a universal default [S02].

### Endpoints, records and handling

- **Dimensional stability:** on free paper witnesses, measure length and width change in known directions using calibrated images or a suitable non-contact method; record curl height/shape independently. Note whether direction is known. On mock attached labels, separately record face-stock shape.
- **Attachment reliability:** at fixed time points photograph perimeter/area and record lift length/area, buckling and any spontaneous detachment/time-to-event. Do not pull, peel, probe, or remove a label to create a failure.
- **Readability:** use fixed-light, fixed-distance images and a standard text plus synthetic handwritten annotation on mock labels. Record exact character/annotation readability and correct association to the intended mock sleeve. Do not use collection annotation as an experimental sample.
- **Observation duration:** six months in ordinary, monitored conditions because the report is “after months”; inspect at baseline, weeks 1, 2 and 4, then monthly, and after a recorded excursion. It is an observation window, not a durability guarantee.
- **Failure record:** unique sample ID; full factor values; label and sleeve lots; operator/run; conditioning/application details; image IDs; measurement; date/time; RH/T and data gap; deviation; reviewer; and a fixed code for curl, edge lift, detachment, legibility or association. Preserve failed and missing units in the denominator. No silent exclusions.
- **Handling:** do not deliberately flex, remove or stress the sleeve. Record ordinary handling/relocation as a covariate. Any user retrieval/order comparison for a loose card or index is performed with empty mock sleeves and dummy contents only.

### Gate 2 — defensible decision

Before seeing results, the owner defines separate limits for dimensional change, attachment, handwriting/print readability and correct ID association, and which outcome is a stop condition. Choose among “retain current materials with monitoring,” “revise a process or label system for a limited approved cohort,” “adopt a non-adhesive index/holder after fit/readability review,” or “inconclusive—gather more data.” Do not average unreadable or misassociated records away with good bond scores. A candidate fails if it crosses any owner-defined critical endpoint. If no RH variation, traceable batch, interpretable annotations or adequate sample size is available, report what could not be separated rather than declaring a pass. Any future implementation is staged by one approved low-risk group with records preserved and follow-up inspection; no collection-wide transition follows from this proposal alone.

Accelerated aging may be considered only as a separately approved research question using mock materials and a justified protocol; it is not in this proposal. The LoC 80°C/50% RH/28-day test is explicitly an accelerated test on selected plastic/metal panels. It is not ordinary storage and no equivalence to years is supported here [S02].
