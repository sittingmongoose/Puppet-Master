# Revised research proposal: curling paper labels on collection sleeves

Case: ER12-C-R2-01-FRESH  
Run: C-R2-01-treatment  
Stage: reviser  
Plan release: 2026-10-10T06:01:02.280Z; released-plan SHA-256 a9cfe54c239ea09aeccec070c8754a305206f5565bf7d4cfa92d26b192f491e1.

## Recommendation

Do not change labeling across the collection from this anecdote. Keep existing labels and objects in place. Begin a non-contact condition and association record, recover exact material, printer, application, batch and room histories, and ask the collection owner and conservator to decide annotation-copy permission, replaceability, acceptable risk, and which items need additional safeguards. After those gates, compare candidate systems only on empty mock sleeves or coupons made from an identified sleeve material.

Keep three outcomes separate: paper dimensional stability and curl; attachment reliability; and continued readability and correct association. A curl can occur without bond failure, a label can detach without paper movement, and a readable label can become separated from the sleeve it identifies. Do not select a product or infer that adhesive alone caused the reported failures. No product, brand, adhesive chemistry, or collection-wide intervention is recommended.

## Case facts, hypotheses, and limits

The brief describes an invented store and an anecdote. It reports that a few batches curled or detached after months in a room with variable humidity and that staff remember changing label stock and printer around the same time. It does not identify the affected denominator, onset dates, actual humidity cycle, paper/coating, adhesive, printer/ink, sleeve resin/finish, application method, or failure modes. It does not say that the adhesive changed. These are unverified reports, not observations made in this review.

Plausible mechanisms are competing and may interact:

- **Paper movement:** paper moisture response varies with sheet and fiber structure, machine/cross direction, and manufacturing/drying history. A review reports a general tendency for greater cross-direction than machine-direction hygroexpansion, while primary work documents humidity/temperature effects for tested paper and fiber specimens [S04, S05]. These sources justify measuring directions where known; they do not predict movement of the unidentified stock.
- **Curl:** unequal moisture or composition through the sheet, coatings or one-sided printing, sheet structure, and roll-set may contribute. Maass and Hirn's publisher abstract describes an A4 water/glycerol model-ink experiment and a proposed delayed solvent-migration mechanism for inkjet-printed sheets [S06]. The printer and ink here are unknown, so this is a conditional hypothesis only.
- **Interface and sleeve:** differential movement between a paper face and a polymer sleeve is a mechanical hypothesis, not a demonstrated case mechanism. Adhesion may also depend on the sleeve resin/finish, surface condition, adhesive construction, pressure, contact time, label geometry, and application or storage conditions. “Inert sleeve” does not identify these properties. LoC procurement specifications separately match substrate and printer process and measure bond, curl/lift, and print outcomes [S01–S03, S10]. They are scoped procurement precedents, not product endorsements or direct case evidence.
- **Printing and application:** printer mechanism, media profile, paper path, ribbon/toner/ink, coverage, feed direction, and settings may interact with stock and environmental conditions. Application pressure, contact/dwell, operator, surface state, and placement may vary. The Canon troubleshooting page describes only the LBP5280; it is a lead to recover and consult the actual printer's manual, not a setting recommendation for this unidentified printer [S08].
- **Manufacture and supply:** simultaneous stock and printer changes confound informal before/after reasoning. A newsprint study found cross-machine-direction variation and register differences in its tested paper-machine rolls [S09]; this supports asking about lots, not assuming the same variation in label stock.

Nothing in the supplied case or sources establishes causation, a failure rate, a suitable adhesive, or a safe storage setpoint.

## Records to recover

Build a dated, source-linked chronology with the exact old and new values, source, and “unknown” where evidence is missing.

1. **Label supply:** SKU/product name; face-stock paper/coating and direction if stated; adhesive and liner construction or written confirmation that these are unknown; dimensions; supplier data-sheet revisions; manufacture, purchase and receipt dates; batch/roll numbers; storage conditions; supplier change notices.
2. **Printing:** printer make/model/serial and technology; driver/firmware; media type/profile; relevant heat, darkness, speed, or equivalent settings; feed direction and paper path; ink/toner/ribbon and lot; coverage; template/software changes; dated manuals and change notices.
3. **Application:** operator and date; sleeve surface condition and cleaning actually used; tool/pressure; contact, dwell or cure; room RH/T; repositioning; label geometry and placement; whether a sleeve was flexed. Do not infer undocumented practice.
4. **Sleeve and environment:** supplier; resin/polymer, finish and lot; storage zone and shelf; logger identity and check/calibration status; interval and gaps; RH/T values and timing; moves, handling and documented water/condensation events.
5. **Failures and controls:** unique sleeve/label ID; total affected and unaffected denominator; onset or interval; lot and location; curl shape; edge-lift extent; spontaneous detachment; printed and handwritten legibility; association status; dated standardized images; reviewer; handling or relocation events. Keep separate codes for curl, lift, detach, print degradation, handwriting ambiguity, and misassociation.

A product-family name, supplier claim, or appearance is not proof of the actual lot or formulation used. The LoC examples require printer/substrate selection and package or batch information within their own procurement scope [S01–S03, S10].

## Bounded later study and decision gates

### Gate 0: owner decisions and two-week baseline

Before testing, the owner and conservator decide: whether affected sleeves may be monitored in place; whether any label is replaceable; whether annotations may be copied and by whom; how copies are identified and retained; which items are high-value, photographic, fragile, or otherwise exceptional; what no-contact alternative is permissible; what label/association risk is tolerable; and whether any mock-up or ordinary-condition exposure is permitted. Resolve available material identities and vendor instructions. Do not use collection objects or existing labels as experimental samples.

For two weeks, log RH and temperature at the affected shelf height and, if available, a naturally less-variable comparison location. Record logger identity, check date, interval and data gaps. Survey visible labels in place with standardized, scaled/color-referenced photographs. Make a checked transcription and sleeve/label crosswalk only if copying is authorized; otherwise leave the annotation-permission gate open. Do not change room conditions before the conservator reviews measurements.

Two weeks is a baseline snapshot, not a seasonal characterization. If no meaningful humidity variation is captured, environmental cycling remains untested; do not invent an exposure or infer that humidity is irrelevant.

### Gate 1: mock-up screen

Use only new empty sleeves or coupons with verified matching resin/finish and identified label-stock/system lots. No collection contents or existing collection labels enter the study. If old and new materials/equipment are available and compatible, use a staged 2 × 2 × 2 screen: old versus new complete label system; old versus new printer/process; and documented current versus supplier-guided standardized application. A contrast is run only if the exact stock, equipment, and compatibility can be verified. If the adhesive and face stock are bundled, “label system” is the factor; their separate contributions remain unknown. If a historic printer or method cannot be reproduced, mark that contrast unavailable instead of substituting an assumed equivalent.

Use 12 mock labels per valid combination, spread across three independently printed/applied runs (four per run). The maximum full screen is 8 combinations/96 labels, plus unlabelled sleeve controls and unbonded paper witnesses. This is a feasibility screen for common problems and run patterns, not a powered acceptance study or proof of a low failure rate. Record lots and use independent lots if available; the owner must define a tolerated rate and confidence requirement before calculating a final acceptance sample size.

Observe for six months in ordinary monitored storage because the anecdote says “after months.” This is a bounded first screen, not a seasonal or lifetime test; if the relevant seasonal cycle is absent, say so or extend a later study to cover it. Do not use elevated-temperature aging or accelerated exposures in this proposal. If naturally different locations are available, randomize positions and treat location as a block/covariate; it does not isolate humidity from other location differences. Do not multiply conditions into a large exposure factorial without a separate, owner-approved design.

Use supplier/owner-approved application conditions and record operator, surface state, pressure/tool, room RH/T, print mode/coverage, contact/dwell and deviations. Randomize sample positions and blind photo scoring where feasible. Define failure codes and thresholds before seeing outcomes. Preserve failed, detached, and missing units in the denominator.

### Outcomes, observations, and records

- **Dimensional stability:** measure free paper witnesses' length and width in known directions using a calibrated non-contact method; measure curl height/shape separately. Record direction only when identified. On attached mock labels, measure face-stock shape separately from bond state.
- **Attachment reliability:** at each inspection, map lifted perimeter/area and buckling from fixed images; record spontaneous detachment and time-to-event. Do not pull, peel, probe, or remove collection labels.
- **Readability and association:** use fixed-light/distance images of standardized print and synthetic handwritten annotations on mock labels. Record exact character/annotation readability and correct mock-sleeve association separately. Do not use collection annotations as test material.
- **Schedule:** baseline, weeks 1, 2, and 4, then monthly through month 6 and after a documented environmental excursion. Include every sample at every point, even after failure; note missing data and reasons.
- **Per-unit record:** unique ID, label/sleeve lots, every factor, printer/operator/run, application and conditioning details, image IDs, measurements, date/time, RH/T and gaps, deviations, failure code, and reviewer.

### Gate 2: decision

Before reviewing results, the owner defines separate limits for dimensional change, attachment, printed/handwritten readability, and correct ID association, with any critical stop condition and required sample size/confidence. Choose among retaining current materials with monitoring; a limited, approved process or system revision; a no-adhesive index/holder after fit and retrieval checks; or “inconclusive—gather more data.” Do not average a wrong association or unreadable annotation away with a good bond score. Missing material traceability, an unobserved humidity range, ambiguous annotations, incompatible cells, or inadequate sample size means “unresolved/inconclusive,” not “pass.” Any later change is staged on an approved low-risk cohort with originals retained unless a separate conservation decision authorizes otherwise. No result from this proposal justifies a collection-wide change by itself.
