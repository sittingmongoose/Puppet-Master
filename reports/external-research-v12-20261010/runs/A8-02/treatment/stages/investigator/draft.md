## Recommendation

Use a small append-first archive with two separately controlled intake paths: continuous gateway events for stations that have a compatible radio/network feed, and an optional documented staff-reviewed CSV backfill for outages. Preserve each received event and original file, record arrival separately from the station's observation timestamp, and append review/correction records that point to the original observation. Make chart points and summaries replaceable projections of that history. Do not promise exactly-once observation delivery or auto-delete late readings.

For an event-time chart, sort and aggregate by the best station observation time whose quality is declared; retain the original timestamp, timezone/offset and quality declaration. Keep server receipt/arrival times for provenance and freshness. If observation time is missing or untrusted, do not silently substitute arrival time as the observation time; mark it uncertain and route it to an owner-defined display/review policy. Backfills may add points in the past and should trigger projection recomputation according to an explicit policy.

For this pilot, PostgreSQL 18 staging and append-oriented tables are a plausible shared archive/import choice, not a selected dependency. A validated non-null source identity is required before a uniqueness constraint or conflict action can automate replay suppression. PostgreSQL defaults permit repeated NULLs in a unique key; DO NOTHING and DO UPDATE express different policies, and UPDATE would erase useful raw-history distinctions if applied to the raw table [S03](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/sql-copy.html), [S04](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/ddl-constraints.html), [S05](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/sql-insert.html). Preserve incoming event bytes/fields, source, batch or delivery identity, receipt time and ingestion decision. Store corrected values as appended revisions with actor/reviewer, reason, time, and a reference to the prior record; never silently rewrite or discard the evidence.

| Authorized CSV backfill into the same archive | It tolerates outages and stations without a stream, but is manual, delayed, format-sensitive and susceptible to repeated files, ambiguous timezones, duplicate rows and silent correction. CSV permits quoted newlines and has NULL/empty and whitespace subtleties; server-side COPY and client-side psql \\copy have different file-access boundaries [S03](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/sql-copy.html). | Retain as an optional documented path because the brief authorizes it. Preserve the original file, stage rows with file hash/batch ID and row number, validate schema/units/timezone, show duplicate/correction candidates to staff, record reviewer decisions, and append accepted readings and correction lineage. Do not claim a product capability until compatibility with actual exports and the selected database/client is verified. |

Do not select Beam as a pilot dependency. Apache Beam's event-time windows and watermarks are a useful delayed-event processing analogue: triggers can emit on event-time progress and late arrivals; an explicitly configured allowed-lateness horizon controls whether an old window accepts later elements and when state is cleaned up [S06](https://beam.apache.org/releases/javadoc/2.70.0/org/apache/beam/sdk/transforms/windowing/Window.html). That is a finite projection/window policy, not permission to discard a late raw weather observation. The club can use a simpler scheduled recomputation or per-station chart projection unless scale/operations justify Beam.

## Time, identity, replay, and correction policy

Keep at least these distinct: (a) station observation time as claimed by device/CSV, with timezone/offset and quality; (b) gateway/network receipt time when supplied; (c) archive arrival time assigned by the receiving service; and (d) staff correction/review time. Do not collapse them. The event may be hours old when delivered. Sort historical readings by trusted observation time; compute freshness from receipt of the latest acceptable reading and the station's expected reporting interval, not by relabeling receipt as measurement time. Missing clock-quality inputs are an explicit uncertainty.

Treat radio retransmission, network multi-gateway deduplication, broker delivery/QoS, adapter acknowledgement, application replay, archive conflicts and chart deduplication as separate layers. MQTT QoS does not establish unique physical observations [S01]. TTS's duplicate-uplink window concerns copies heard by multiple gateways, and its ABP counter-reset behavior is LoRaWAN/TTS-specific [S11], [S14]. TTS best-practice docs say LoRaWAN devices should preserve session/frame-counter state across ordinary power cycles and expect packet loss; this must not be generalized to other radio types or presumed true of these ten devices [S12]. A repeated counter after a reboot may collide with a prior reading; a packet/message identifier may also only be transport-scoped.

Ask the station steward to declare sensor identity, clock behavior/quality and what persists across reset. If a stable boot/session epoch plus monotonic reading counter exists, the archive maintainer can evaluate it with station identity as a candidate key. Validate reset, wraparound, replacement and firmware behavior before automatic dedup. If there is no stable non-null key, preserve ambiguous arrivals and send candidate matches to review rather than asserting identity. A payload/time fingerprint may help staff find candidates but is not proof of a unique observation. Transport retransmissions may be recorded as delivery metadata while mapping to one reading only under an explicit, validated application rule.

Keep raw observation records immutable. A correction is an appended record referencing the superseded observation, with the corrected field/value, source, reason, reviewer and correction time. Replay policy should say whether a byte-identical event is retained as another delivery record, linked to an existing observation, or rejected from a projection; CSV duplicate and correction decisions should have the same audit trail. PostgreSQL ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING can suppress a conflicting insert and DO UPDATE mutates a row, but neither is a neutral policy; NULLS DISTINCT means nullable keys do not suppress repeated NULL-key rows [S04], [S05]. InfluxDB OSS v2 is an alternative chart projection only: its point key is measurement+tag set+timestamp, same-key field sets merge and new conflicting fields win, while coarse timestamp precision can collide readings. This does not supply raw-row identity or correction lineage, and the cited rolling v2 docs do not establish behavior for InfluxDB 3 [S09].

## Released history and useful discoveries

A bounded released event-time failure chain was found in Apache Beam. Issue #36470 reports a Spanner Change Stream reader stall when partition watermark updates may have been reordered; the issue describes watermark caching/filtering added in 2.67 as worsening context. The issue page's milestone says 2.70.0, while issue activity says fixed in 2.69.0. Beam's official 2.69.0 release notes (tag commit 2ca7d21, published 2025-10-17) list the backward-moving partition-watermark reader stall fix [S07](https://github.com/apache/beam/issues/36470), [S08](https://github.com/apache/beam/releases/tag/v2.69.0). No implementation commit was identified in the reviewed history, so patch mechanics and metadata discrepancy remain unresolved. This is a useful warning that ordering/progress metadata can itself arrive out of order, and a basis for testing late/out-of-order input and monotonic-progress assumptions. It is specifically a Beam Spanner connector incident, not a defect claim about the club, MQTT, TTS or radio devices.

A second, distinct TTS issue (#4502) describes a 3.14.0 ABP frame-counter reset case and maintainer-described duplicate windows/cooldown, then closes the proposed exception as intended behavior. No linked release or fix commit was found; do not present it as a released fix. It shows why actual device protocol, counter persistence and network duplicate policy are owner/device dependent [S14]. TTS docs also expose receipt metadata and finite storage retention, but their rolling/version and extraction limits are recorded in S10-S15.

### Reconciliation with the released draft plan

- Corrected its assumption that gateway arrival is observation time: preserved separate device, network, archive and review times.
- Rejected discarding a reading merely because its event time precedes the chart's current point: retain raw data, then define projection recomputation/late-update policy.
- Rejected the presumed globally unique sequence across reboot: identity and counter persistence require steward/device evidence.
- Kept The Things Stack and generic MQTT as conditional investigation leads, not chosen winners; TTS MQTT's documented QoS 0 limitation and finite/edition-specific storage materially change the lead's fit.
- Rejected the assumption that network deduplication removes application duplicates. TTS multi-gateway dedup, MQTT transport delivery, application replay and archive/chart policy have distinct identities and boundaries.
- Kept CSV backfill as explicitly authorized optional scope but rejected blind append without staging, identity review or correction lineage. Evidence does not justify excluding it; its feasibility is unverified.
- Rejected a connected demo as a requirement and as incompatible with the negative constraint. Use offline fixtures/simulated arrivals only if later authorized; none were run here.
- Replaced the plan's “no released history” gap with the bounded Beam #36470 / 2.69.0 chain, preserving its Spanner-specific applicability and source-history uncertainty. TTS #4502 is behavior history, not a fix claim.
- Preserved the assigned owner roles and prohibitions. The plan's option is not silently made mandatory and its local presence is not evidence of capability.
