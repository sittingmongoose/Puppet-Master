## 1. Complete brief and scope

### Assignment

A volunteer weather club aggregates temperature and rainfall observations from ten stations. Some use a low-power radio gateway; others upload CSV after an outage. The club publishes a historical chart with an explicit freshness indicator and preserves raw observations for correction. Devices may reboot or buffer readings for hours. The system is informational and must never control equipment or issue hazard warnings.

This is a planning proposal, not an implementation. The research considers public primary documentation, relevant public source code and released issue/fix history where useful. No product, source, or conclusion was preselected. A bounded historical example and useful unfamiliar alternatives are included without claiming exhaustive coverage. External behavior described below is separated from inference and local product decisions.

## 2. Recommendation

Use an append-first observation archive as the source of truth and generate the historical chart and freshness view from it. Provide two separately controlled intake routes into that archive:

- **Gateway stream:** use a broker/application adapter only after the actual station radio, gateway, protocol, payload, buffering and service configuration are identified. For LoRaWAN using The Things Stack (TTS), its documented MQTT integration is MQTT 3.1.1 and QoS 0 only, so it is a conditional network-ingress lead, not a durable queue or archive. A generic MQTT v5 gateway route is a distinct alternative with configurable QoS/session behavior. Telegraf is one possible adapter, subject to exact version, payload, output and acknowledgment configuration.
- **Optional CSV backfill:** retain the brief-authorized, documented staff-reviewed path for outages and stations without a compatible stream. It is optional scope, not a mandatory feature or an established club capability. Preserve each source file and stage its rows for review before accepting them into the archive.

For either route, preserve each received envelope/file and record its arrival independently from the station’s observation timestamp. Store normalized values and quality declarations alongside unchanged source representations. Append replay links, review decisions and corrections rather than silently rewriting raw evidence. The chart is a replaceable projection of accepted interpretations; a late reading may cause a historical projection to be recomputed, but must not be deleted from the raw archive because of its age.

PostgreSQL 18 staging and append-oriented tables are a plausible archive/import design, not a selected dependency. A uniqueness rule or conflict action can automate suppression only after the archive maintainer has a validated, stable, non-null event identity. InfluxDB OSS v2 could be evaluated as a chart projection, with its documented point-key merge and timestamp-precision collision behavior; it is not a substitute for row-level raw history or correction lineage. Apache Beam provides a useful event-time/late-data analogy, not a recommended pilot dependency.

## 3. Intake routes and tradeoffs

| Route | Evidence-backed fit and tradeoffs | Pilot recommendation and limits |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Low-power gateway stream** | MQTT is designed for constrained M2M/IoT use. Generic MQTT QoS 0 can lose messages; QoS 1 can redeliver; QoS 2 adds protocol exchanges for exactly-once delivery within the MQTT protocol exchange. Session expiry, persistence and client/broker behavior still matter. TTS’s named MQTT server is narrower: its official docs specify MQTT 3.1.1 and QoS 0 only. TTS network-side multi-gateway deduplication is not archive-level replay handling. Its Storage Integration may provide a bounded retrieval aid, but it is an Application Package enabled for an application or end device; Cloud/Sandbox use and Enterprise configuration differ. Retention is 30 days on Cloud, 24 hours on Sandbox and configurable on Enterprise; Cloud stores uplinks only. Writes are batched and can be delayed, so this is not a real-time freshness source. [S01, S10–S13] | Use a stream only if inventory confirms the station/gateway protocol, payload, service/edition, session configuration, spool limits and clock behavior. For TTS, treat Storage Integration as a conditional recovery aid only when enabled for the relevant application/device and after checking edition, retention and retrieval behavior. Independently archive received events and expose gaps. Generic MQTT QoS 2 does not prove exactly-once sensor observations or archive persistence. |
| **B. Optional staged CSV backfill** | A file route accepts delayed readings from outages or stations without a stream, but requires people and is vulnerable to repeated files, schema/unit/timezone ambiguity, malformed rows, duplicate candidates and corrections. PostgreSQL 18 `COPY FROM` appends rows; conversion errors stop by default, while `ON_ERROR ignore` can discard rows. CSV permits quoted embedded newlines; null/empty conventions and quoted whitespace require care. Server-side `COPY` and client-side `psql \copy` have different file-access boundaries. [S03–S05] | Keep it as a documented option, not a mandatory product capability. Preserve the original file, record a file hash/import ID/row locator and parser/schema version, stage parse outcomes, bind station/time/units only from declared facts, and present ambiguous duplicates/corrections to staff. Append approved readings or correction records with reviewer and reason. Hold rows with unresolved identity or time facts for steward review rather than guessing or silently skipping. Verify compatibility against actual exports and the selected DB/client before claiming it works. |

A small pilot can start with either route while keeping both compatible with the same archive model. Do not choose TTS just because it appears in the draft plan; first establish whether any station is LoRaWAN and whether the selected deployment is TTS. Likewise, generic MQTT is not assumed available on the gateway. The actual radio and export inventory can change the route choice.

### Other useful alternatives

- **Telegraf MQTT consumer:** the rolling v1 documentation describes a possible adapter and tracking-metric acknowledgments to an input after output handling. Its documented defaults include QoS 0 and `persistent_session=false`; offline resumption depends on a stable client ID, persistent session, and compatible QoS 1/2 on both publishing and subscribing sides. Pin the binary/configuration and test its buffer and output semantics before relying on it. [S02]
- **InfluxDB OSS v2 projection:** the v2 FAQ defines a point by measurement, tag set and timestamp; same-key writes union fields and newer conflicting values win. Coarse timestamp precision can collide distinct readings. This can be useful for chart reads only if raw observations and revisions live elsewhere. The cited rolling v2 page does not establish InfluxDB 3 behavior. [S09]
- **Apache Beam:** its event-time windows are a useful analogy for late chart updates, but their finite lateness/state rules must not govern raw-archive retention. A scheduled recomputation or simple per-station projection is likely more proportionate at ten stations. [S06]

## 4. Time, identity, replay and correction model

Store distinct time and provenance facts rather than replacing one with another:

1. **Observation time:** timestamp claimed by the station or supplied in a CSV, preserved in its original text/offset/precision. Store normalized UTC separately only when the parsing rule is known, with a station-steward declaration of clock quality.
2. **Gateway/network receipt time:** retain it when the gateway or network service supplies it. It explains delivery stages, not when the sensor measured the weather.
3. **Archive arrival time:** time the receiving service accepts an event, or time a reviewed import enters the archive. This is an appropriate input to freshness, not a replacement observation timestamp.
4. **Review/correction time:** time a staff member makes and records an adjudication.

For a TTS uplink example, the documented outer `received_at` is Application Server receipt; nested `uplink_message.received_at` is Network Server receipt; `rx_metadata.time` is gateway receipt. These are useful delivery-layer evidence but do not establish the station’s sensor observation time. The page is a rolling format example, not a version-pinned payload contract, and absent fields may be omitted. The actual station payload remains unknown. [S15]

Use a trusted observation time for historical ordering when its quality permits. If the clock is missing or untrusted, flag that uncertainty and use an owner-defined display/review rule; never silently relabel receipt as measurement time. Freshness should be derived from arrival of the latest acceptable reading and an editor-approved expected reporting interval/threshold. Wording should say when data was recorded or received and must not imply live measurement.

Treat the following as separate stages: radio retransmission; network or multi-gateway deduplication; broker QoS/session delivery; adapter acknowledgment; application replay; archive conflict/idempotency policy; and chart projection/deduplication. MQTT packet identifiers are transport handshake IDs that can be reused; its DUP flag describes a PUBLISH packet, not a unique physical observation. A repeated application message can arrive with DUP=0 and a new packet ID. QoS is not a durable observation ID and cannot guarantee exactly-once sampling or chart persistence. [S01]

Do not assume a restarted device’s sequence counter remains globally unique. The station steward must declare sensor identity, replacement history, clock quality, counter width/wrap/reset behavior and what persists across ordinary reboot and power loss. If the actual device supplies a stable event ID, or a validated epoch plus monotonically increasing counter, the archive maintainer can consider a non-null key scoped to station identity. Test reset, wrap, firmware and replacement semantics before automatic suppression. Without a proven key, preserve ambiguous arrivals and show candidate matches for review. Timestamp/value fingerprints may help locate candidates but are not proof that two observations are the same.

Preserve raw received envelopes and input files. If an application replay is identified under an explicit, validated rule, record the relationship between delivery occurrence and logical observation; let the archive maintainer decide whether the chart projection suppresses a duplicate. Do not deduplicate solely on `(station, timestamp, value)`. PostgreSQL unique constraints only enforce the selected key; by default NULLs are distinct, so nullable keys can coexist. `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` suppresses a conflicting insert, while `DO UPDATE` mutates the conflicting row; neither is neutral policy and neither belongs on immutable evidence without explicit review. [S04, S05]

Represent a correction as an appended revision or annotation pointing to the prior observation, carrying corrected field/value, reason, source, reviewer/actor, correction time and approval state. Keep the raw message/file/row, prior interpretation and all revisions queryable. Build the historical chart from the latest accepted interpretation and recompute projections when approved backfill changes historical event time. This is a local proposal, not a product guarantee.

## 5. Delivery boundary and event-time analogue

There are three relevant layers:

- **Radio/network:** actual radio framing, loss, buffering, retransmission, gateway spool and multi-gateway network deduplication. TTS documents a duplicate uplink as one heard by multiple gateways where a late copy arrives outside its deduplication window and is dropped. This concerns TTS/LoRaWAN network handling, not application archive replay. TTS device guidance about packet loss and saving LoRaWAN session/frame-counter state across ordinary power cycles applies only if the club actually uses compatible LoRaWAN devices; it does not establish these stations’ firmware behavior. [S11, S12]
- **Application/archive:** MQTT QoS, broker sessions, adapter acknowledgments, event IDs, persisted raw arrivals, replay links, import batches, conflict actions and correction review. These are separate policies. The TTS ABP counter-reset issue is a product/protocol-specific maintainer discussion, not a released fix or general radio rule. [S01, S02, S14]
- **Historical chart:** event-time ordering, late update/rebuild policy, selected accepted corrections and an editor-approved freshness indicator. The chart may change when an approved old reading arrives; archive retention does not inherit a chart window’s cutoff.

Apache Beam 2.70.0 documents windows based on element event time; its default trigger emits when the watermark passes a window end and again for late arrivals. An explicitly configured `withAllowedLateness` duration governs how late elements are accepted downstream and how long old-window state is kept; beyond the watermark-defined boundary, elements are dropped and state is cleaned. Its JavaDoc also shows `Duration.ZERO`, so the zero-duration example is consistent with the same rule, not an exception to it. These are Beam window/grouping semantics, not a raw archive policy. [S06]

## 6. Bounded released failure/fix history

Apache Beam issue #36470 reports a Spanner Change Stream reader stalling after a partition watermark moved backward. The issue describes possible reordering of metadata-table watermark updates and watermark caching/filtering introduced in Beam 2.67 as contributing context. The official Beam 2.69.0 release notes list a fix for Spanner Change Stream reading stuck because a partition watermark moved backward. The issue’s displayed milestone is 2.70.0, while issue activity/release notes say fixed in 2.69.0; the reviewed history did not identify the actual patch commit, so the metadata discrepancy and patch mechanics remain unresolved. This is evidence of a released Beam connector progress/order failure only, not a defect in this club’s radio or devices. It supports testing ordering, monotonic progress and recovery if a future implementation uses a comparable event-time engine. [S07, S08]

The separate TTS issue #4502 describes an ABP frame-counter reset report and maintainer discussion of network-server duplicate handling, including the then-current default 200 ms deduplication window followed by a 1 second cooldown. The issue is about a specific TTS/LoRaWAN behavior, not application archive replay; it is not evidence of a released fix. Its value is to show why device counter-reset behavior and a network receiver’s bounded duplicate policy must be investigated for the actual device/service. [S11, S14]

## 9. Owner decisions and unresolved inputs

These are matters the public sources cannot settle. They remain open decisions rather than assumed device capabilities.

1. **Station steward:** identify each station’s radio/protocol and firmware; sensor identity/replacement rules; source event IDs or sequence/boot epochs; counter wrap/reset/persistence on ordinary reboot and power loss; observation timestamp meaning, timezone, synchronization and quality flags; payload/schema; expected cadence.
2. **Archive maintainer:** select gateway/broker/server/adapter/archive versions and editions; define the durable intake boundary, acknowledgment and spool limits; choose whether raw deliveries and logical observations have separate records; validate any candidate idempotency key and replay horizon; decide replay/backfill policy; define CSV schema, units/timezone/parser, staff roles and duplicate/correction approval; set projection recomputation and retention/export rules.
3. **Club editor:** choose freshness language, threshold and stale/unknown display treatment so “received recently” is not represented as a live measurement. No safety or hazard messaging.
4. **Pilot operations/owners:** expected outage duration and acceptable delay; volume; existing station connectivity and CSV samples; review capacity; raw-data retention, privacy and access needs. These do not change the brief’s required owner assignments.

Before selecting TTS or applying S10–S15, establish whether a station is LoRaWAN and identify the service/deployment/edition and payload version. Before treating rolling Telegraf/TTS documentation or InfluxDB v2 behavior as product fact, pin the actual build/configuration and test its scoped behavior in an authorized offline environment.
