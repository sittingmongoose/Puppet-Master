## Findings by required class

### Material wrong

None identified in the consequential factual claims checked.

The ISO records support the stated scope limits and lifecycle caveats: ISO 16283-1 is airborne room-to-room insulation in its stated room-volume/frequency range and lists Amendment 1:2017; ISO 16283-3 is airborne façade insulation and is marked “to be revised”; ISO 12354-1 extends its calculation to 50 Hz only when element and junction data are available. ISO 1996-1 does not set environmental-noise limits, while ISO 1996-2 leaves effort and resulting uncertainty to the user. The draft does not present those methods as executed checks or as a site diagnosis. Sources: [ISO 16283-1](https://www.iso.org/standard/55997.html?browse=ics), [ISO 16283-3](https://www.iso.org/standard/59748.html), [ISO 12354-1](https://www.iso.org/standard/70242.html), [ISO 1996-1](https://www.iso.org/standard/59765.html), [ISO 1996-2](https://www.iso.org/standard/59766.html).

The primary research records support the draft’s bounded summaries. Hopkins and Turner’s publisher abstract supports corner plus central microphone positions for 50/63/80 Hz room-average estimates with improved short-term repeatability, but only for airborne room-to-room measurement. The LSBU record reports airbed testing and reduced low-frequency reverberation time in Henry Wood Hall, not outdoor isolation. The UCL abstract describes absorber-area/location effects in steady-state coupled dwelling layouts, not this building. Gjers’ 2024 paper reports a 5 dB Dw difference for its 180 mm slab comparison, changing contacts/placements, mixed vibration bands, and a need for further testing. The SR 11 sheet is load-, shape-factor-, frequency-, and amplitude-dependent and disclaims guaranteed performance. Sources: [Hopkins & Turner](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0003682X0500068X), [LSBU record](https://researchportal.lsbu.ac.uk/en/publications/flexible-control-of-acoustic-conditions-in-a-music-rehearsal-spac-3/), [UCL record](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10138595/), [Gjers 2024](https://www.acoustics.asn.au/conference_proceedings/AAS2024/papers/p36.pdf), [Getzner SR 11 sheet](https://www.getzner.com/media/914/download/Data%20Sheet%20Sylomer%20SR%2011%20EN.pdf?v=6).

### Material incomplete

1. **Path inference from no detected vibration**  
   **Locator:** draft.md, “Distinguish paths without assuming one,” bullet beginning “Airborne transmission becomes more plausible if … without corresponding receiver-surface vibration evidence.”  
   **Evidence:** the listed gear is only a recorder and basic sound meter. FHWA §7.0 says indoor sound alone often cannot separate airborne from groundborne contribution and describes vibration as the usual indirect measure; §§7.1.2 and 17.2 require suitable transducer placement/coupling and frequency capability ([FHWA-HEP-18-065](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/Environment/noise/measurement/handbook.cfm)).  
   **Assessment:** a lack of detected vibration does not weigh against structural coupling unless a suitable synchronized vibration channel was available, correctly coupled and located, and sensitive over the relevant bands. The draft already says the inference is only suggestive and elsewhere calls for properly mounted accelerometers, so this is a caveat in one diagnostic sentence rather than a flaw in the pilot’s overall logic. Keep the first source-level comparison as a source-linked test; reserve path attribution for adequate paired sound and vibration evidence. Do not imply that the supplied meter/recorder can establish the path.

### Unsupported

No consequential unsupported site claim or predicted benefit found. The mechanical/path explanations are framed as hypotheses. The panel and product examples are bounded by their source conditions, and the draft explicitly refuses to predict a courtyard result from room absorption or manufacturer data.

### Minor locator/wording

1. **Emslie and Hanson comparison conditions**  
   **Locator:** draft.md, “Real implementation histories and what to investigate,” Emslie and Hanson paragraph.  
   **Evidence:** the 2008 paper’s pre-build boundary readings and post-build outdoor reading are not a same-position, same-source matched before/after experiment. It reports baseline boundary positions and later vigorous-drumming measurements outside the completed studio, including a 3 m façade position and an elevated neighbor-side deck result. It is explicitly not peer reviewed ([Emslie & Hanson, AAS 2008](https://www.acoustics.asn.au/conference_proceedings/AAS2008/papers/p64.pdf)).  
   **Assessment:** the draft names the different locations and says the case’s numbers are not this site’s target or forecast, so it does not make a false transfer. A short explicit statement that these are not controlled matched before/after readings would make the evidence boundary easier to follow.

The first pilot is conditional on access, suitable instruments, representative events, and resident agreement.
