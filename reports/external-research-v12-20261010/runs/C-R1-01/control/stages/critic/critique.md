## Findings

### CI-01 — Material incomplete: separate structural excitation from airborne transmission

**Draft locators:** “Recommendation in brief”; “Air/structure interaction [S08–S09]”; “Proposed pilot and comparison”; “Likely confounders and limits”; decision bullets about synchronized vibration.

The draft proposes simultaneous indoor/outdoor sound readings and optional synchronized contact accelerometers near the source and at a receiver-side structural point. It also warns that airborne sound may excite surfaces and that correlated vibration alone is not proof. Those are sound cautions. Yet the pilot's single A–B–A lower-output change reduces the source excitation generally, so a lower courtyard microphone level and lower surface vibration would still leave the route ambiguous. The S08 publisher abstract describes coupled airborne/structure behavior in wall/window tests; S09's SINTEF abstract says transmitted low-frequency sound excites floor vibration in its tested lightweight building. Neither supports treating vibration correlation by itself as a path identifier.

**Why it matters:** The brief asks for an observation plan that could distinguish airborne transmission, structural coupling, and unrelated background events. The proposal has a useful screen for unrelated activity and can detect whether a source setting changes the courtyard result, but does not yet outline a controlled comparison with enough path discrimination to answer the structural-versus-airborne part.

**What a reviser should add:** Keep the source-level pilot as a reversible response test, and add a proposed diagnostic branch for an acoustician if path attribution matters. Under safe and permitted conditions, compare repeatable airborne-dominant excitation (for example, a loudspeaker with no deliberate mechanical connection to the floor/wall) against a contact/impact-dominant condition with the amplified source silent, while synchronously recording source-room pressure, courtyard pressure, and vibration at accessible structural points. Match or document the airborne source level and spectrum where feasible; note that even non-contact airborne sound may excite building surfaces, so the comparison can reveal mixed paths but cannot prove a unique route without suitable sensors, access, and analysis. If that equipment or access is unavailable, explicitly leave path attribution unresolved and describe the A–B–A pilot as an outside-outcome screen only. This is a proposed measurement design, not an instruction to alter the building.
