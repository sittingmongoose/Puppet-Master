## Recommended sequence and comparison

1. **Contain and characterize before choosing equipment.** Map each rinse/overflow destination, document the material inventory and the existing extra-bucket history, measure work-event/daily water volumes and peak patterns, and agree on safe interim containment. Keep unidentified and high-residue materials separate if the local partner says this is practicable. If zero discharge cannot be accepted while tests are pending, do not begin a pilot.
2. **Compare an improved batch-settling option.** Test the actual stream’s settling behavior under partner-controlled conditions. Candidate features for evaluation are a contained batch, controlled inlet/equalization, sufficient undisturbed rest, and non-disturbing decant that leaves bottom solids captured. The benefit is passive capture of settleable particles with modest process complexity. The expected limitation is residual fines/colloids and any dissolved constituents; rushed inflow or careless decant can resuspend solids. Bucket or tank geometry and rest time must be measured, not borrowed from a factory report.
3. **Compare a filter only after capture of larger solids.** A coarse screen followed by locally selected replaceable depth/cartridge media is a candidate, not a specified product. Screen/media grade must be selected from measured feed and a contained partner test. Record volume treated, head loss/flow decline, breakthrough, service interval, operator minutes, used media and any wash liquid. If dissolved ions prove material, a plain particle filter does not answer that issue; membrane/electrodialysis studies are a more complex separate question, with pressure, fouling, cleaning and concentrate burden.
4. **Keep source segregation as a real alternative, not an afterthought.** Evaluate dry cleanup/reclaim before rinsing, separate first/high-residue glaze rinses from more dilute clay/tool rinse if inventory and partner agree, reduce avoidable water, and contain material for a facility that accepts its profile. University reclaim-bucket and settling-tank practices show operational precedents, not a universal disposal classification. This option may avoid an uncertain release path but can consume labor and storage space.
5. **Do not assume combinations outperform their parts.** Settling before filtration is a testable sequence because upstream solids may affect filter loading, but it must be compared at matched feed and recorded maintenance burden. Chemical coagulation/flocculation is excluded by the brief.

The 2018 tile-factory jar study reported 41.97% TSS reduction after 100 minutes’ settling before coagulant addition. That supports measuring rest-time response, not choosing 100 minutes for this workshop. The 2025 pilot used a stabilized industrial feed and a sedimentation-plus-filtration train; its reported effluent cannot be attributed to settling alone. Its physical stage also missed the paper’s reuse pH target, while sludge disposition remained further work. The 2021 ceramic membrane study demonstrates how changing feed and fouling can change flux/rejection and require cleaning; its factory-scale cross-flow equipment is not a modest shop recommendation. EPA’s older granular-filter review adds failure modes—clogging/headloss, surges, downtime and maintenance—that a candidate filtration trial must measure. See S01–S04 for source locators and limits.

**S02 reporting caveat:** the publisher abstract says the pilot was tested through “four experimental runs,” while the main methods describe three sequential two-week stages and the results are presented as stages 1–3. I could not resolve this internal reporting inconsistency. Retain the main-text description as three sequential stages with the ambiguity stated; do not portray four distinct or matched conditions. In any event, the 102 mg/L stage-1 value is from the sedimentation-plus-filtration line, not settling alone.

## Transfer and performance criteria

Do not accept an earlier report based on a photograph or a percent-removal headline. Require, at minimum:

- The exact site, craft/process step, feed/material/glaze inventory and SDS basis; what is excluded or mixed.
- Influent pH/conductivity, TSS/turbidity, particle-size distribution, relevant chemistry, and their ranges over shifts/seasons or high/low work periods.
- Actual event volumes, average and peak flow, intermittency, residence time, temperature, mixing and solids resuspension conditions.
- Tank/flow path, effective volume, filter model/media/pore rating, loading, headloss/pressure, breakthrough, cleaning/backwash, replacement, bypass and uptime.
- Paired influent/treated samples with analytical methods, detection limits, sample timing, QA/QC, replicates and handling; results for peak and ordinary operation, not only one favorable run.
- Mass/volume and characterization of sludge, filter media, wash water and any retentate; documented receiving facility/route and required acceptance profile.
- Operator minutes, training, cleanout frequency, storage footprint, energy/consumables, downtime, repair history and operating cost under local workload.
- Intended endpoint and criteria: reuse process and its specific requirements, or discharge destination, authority and written limits. Do not transfer a process-reuse criterion into a discharge conclusion.

## Proposed validation, separate from executed work

**Baseline proposal:** after local review, observe multiple representative work periods and explicitly include quieter and busier periods and the actual range of glaze/tool tasks. Log work/activity, products/materials, rinse-water volume, bucket additions, overflow, rest/decant, cleanouts and labor. Recover any dates or notes about the earlier extra bucket to evaluate whether loading differed. Use matched, well-mixed paired samples at the rinse input and current captured overflow/supernatant where safe and authorized. The partner lab chooses holding times and QA. For variable event flows, use an event/flow-representative composite where appropriate; use separate immediate grabs for pH and extreme/short-lived conditions. Characterize TSS, turbidity and particle size; measure pH and conductivity; choose constituents from the actual inventory and specify the analytical method, filtration cutoff, and units with the qualified lab; compare total and lab-defined filtered fractions only when the method supports that interpretation. Analyze captured solids/media separately if required for handling decisions.

**Later controlled comparison:** only after a local partner accepts the sampling and residuals plan, split a characterized, homogenized batch for contained settling-time/decant and candidate-filter screening. If screening justifies a field pilot, compare present collection, improved equalized batch settling, settling plus selected filter, and source segregation/collection across matched or alternated work blocks. Record task/material mix and volume to avoid repeating the quieter-week confound. Keep all effluent, sludge and media contained pending results and an accepted route; no chemical additions and no discharge.

**Pre-agreed stop/escalation:** pause immediately for an unrecorded glaze/material, near-capacity container, leak/overflow/bypass, loss of containment, absent receiving route or labor above the owner’s limit. Pause a filter if it blocks, flow falls sharply, breakthrough appears, or the partner-defined particle criteria fail. Escalate to source segregation/authorized collection or a broader qualified assessment if relevant dissolved constituents remain, solids/media have no accepted route, or local authorization for the intended endpoint is absent. Numeric thresholds, analytes and hold times must be set by the partner/lab from real information; this case supplies none.

## Already-covered, rejected, and uncertain points

**Already covered by the scenario:** the site already uses settling buckets; a second bucket was tried once, but under lower activity; staff have observed both clear-looking overflow and visible fines. These facts justify investigating particle escape and collecting operating history, but they do not establish why it changed or whether the overflow is suitable for any destination. The project objective is a decision about a later controlled pilot, not a chosen treatment device.

**Rejected from the proposal:** treating appearance as an endpoint; counting the quieter-week extra-bucket anecdote as an effectiveness trial; transferring a factory removal percentage, local reuse criterion, or sewer statement to this case; applying a numeric settling time or filter rating without feed data; prescribing coagulant doses; choosing an analyte list from an assumed glaze composition; calling captured solids harmless; or naming a sewer, trash, evaporation, or other disposal route before characterization and local acceptance. Potable-water use, discharge compliance, and treatment operation are outside scope.

**Still uncertain:** actual particle-size distribution and settleability; total and dissolved constituents; exact glaze/stain/clay/cleaner inventory and changes by task; bucket dimensions and hydraulic paths; work-event and daily volumes, peaks, and existing cleanout interval; destination and applicable authority; residuals profile and receiving facility; available space, budget, training, operator time; and whether the supervisor accepts complete containment/no discharge while characterization is pending. These remain questions rather than assumed values.

## Corrections, optional improvements, owner decisions

**Corrections to weak interpretations:** the extra bucket is not causal evidence because workload changed; a filter/settler that makes water look clear has not shown dissolved constituent control; reported factory removal is not a workshop efficiency; Hamed et al.’s sedimentation result is for a train with filtration; its sewer statement is not the local workshop’s permission; WCU’s hazardous-solids rule is its own institutional program, not this case’s classification.

**Optional improvements for the owner to consider:** reclaim/dry-clean before rinsing; capture high-residue first rinses separately; add a measured settling-rest/decant step only if safe containment and labor allow; test a replaceable filter after settling only if baseline particle data and waste handling justify the extra maintenance. Any combined treatment remains a comparison arm, not a presumed winner.

**Owner/local-partner decisions:** acceptance of no discharge while pending; actual destination and responsible authority; available work time and footprint; permitted/accepted interim storage and final waste receiver; inventory boundaries for materials brought by users; analysis budget and locally meaningful acceptance limits; whether preventing release is the objective or whether a permitted reuse/discharge endpoint is being pursued.
