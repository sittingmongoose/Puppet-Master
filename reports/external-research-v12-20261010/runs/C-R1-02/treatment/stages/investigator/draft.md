## Proposal in brief

Treat the visible overflow and the extra-bucket anecdote as a question to investigate, not an effectiveness result. The extra bucket coincided with a quieter week; lower inflow, different cleanup tasks, longer settling time, a changed glaze mix, or other conditions could explain the observation. Recover any dates, bucket arrangement, cleaning frequency, staff observations, and workload records before using it as history. It is not already a matched trial. [S01]

A sensible proposal is staged: first map and segregate the streams and characterize actual flow and materials; compare the current practice with a controlled, flow-managed settling concept; keep a particulate filtration/recirculation option only for a reliably segregated clay-only stream; and retain closed collection with qualified off-site handling as a no-treatment alternative if the owner accepts preventing discharge. Do not select or install a permanent system yet. None of the reviewed examples validates the workshop’s effluent, removes every dissolved constituent, assigns a legal discharge route, or establishes a safe solids route.

Particle removal and dissolved-constituent management are separate. Gravity and particulate filters transfer some suspended matter into collected solids. Fine clays may settle slowly or remain suspended, and intermittent inflow can resuspend settled material or reduce effective residence time. Filter capture depends on the medium and loading; fouling can reduce flow and cause bypass or poor capture. Dissolved constituents are not shown to be removed by those mechanisms. Glaze-derived metals may partition between particles and water, so the actual fraction must be measured if relevant. Clear appearance, turbidity alone, or a vendor’s “recycling” label cannot establish treatment success, potability, safe discharge, regulatory compliance, or harmless residuals. The 1975 EPA contractor survey is historical draft context, not a current rule or a validated small-shop design. [S01, S05, S06]

### Relevant implementation evidence and transfer conditions

The historic EPA contractor report reviewed 14 pottery plants and described variable clay/glaze wastewater, intermittent flows, settling, flocculation, clarification, and filtered underflows. One small earthenware art pottery recycled water after settling; another plant’s treatment train filtered underflows, recycled filtrate, and landfilled filter cake. Some reported effluent data lacked heavy-metal analysis. This establishes that such operations have been tried in other settings; it does not make any case a success example for this workshop. The report is a 1975 draft contractor document, explicitly not an official EPA publication or EPA-reviewed finding. Its industrial scale, age, formulations, incomplete metal data, unknown local permissions, and incomplete matching data all limit transfer. [S01]

The current ROHDE TA 85 is a useful gravity-separator comparison, not a verified outcome: manufacturer data list 85 L internal volume and 70 L fill volume. The current manual describes three calm compartments and says most suspended particles settle. It limits connection to one sink and warns that a sudden gushing inflow, such as dumping a full 20 L sink, can overflow it; it requires regular cleanout. Its instructions show a sewer connection, but that is not a local authorization. It does not report dissolved constituents or independent removal performance. [S07, S08]

A targeted version check found the official July 2025 TA 85 manual as well as the current March 2026 English file. Both editions include the one-sink/full-20-L-sink overflow warning, three-compartment settling, and regular cleanout. The older manual lists “volume 85 L,” 587 × 387 × 523 mm, and 12 kg; the current one distinguishes 85 L internal from 70 L fill volume and presents dimensions/weight differently. The current manual says technical changes can lead to corrections in subsequent editions and is not automatically updated; no release note explains whether the specification differences reflect a physical revision or revised reporting. Confirm the current serial/model and usable fill volume before any future sizing. [S08, S12]

Cink 2 illustrates a small productized filtration/recirculation approach but is explicitly restricted by its own manual to water-soluble clay materials. The product page’s 1 µm statement and “recycling” description do not override the manual’s exclusion of glazes or prove an effluent result. Its captured water and material are subject to local requirements, and it is not to be connected to sewer or septic. The manual’s maintenance details are especially relevant to the brief’s labor concern: empty buckets, clean bag and string filters, allow settling, refresh water, and respond to falling flow/cloudiness or seal issues. [S09, S10]

The manufacturer’s detailed Cink 2 page calls the system “updated” and lists design changes (flexible nozzle, foot switch, bucket dollies/cabinet, extra filters), but supplies no dated change log or issue-to-fix record. Its cautions state that it is not intended for glaze capture/reuse and that mixed clay types are incompatible for reuse. The page lists a 190 lb unit and palletized shipment, so footprint/handling and total delivered cost deserve review for any conditional clay-only comparison. It also hosts one customer review reporting motor noise that made classroom conversation difficult; this is one unverified experience, not evidence of a general defect, but noise should be logged if the unit is ever considered in a shared workspace. No independent treatment-performance data accompanies these claims. [S11]

For any earlier implementation or product considered, request all of the following before transferring its result: (1) actual source tasks and glaze/clay product inventory; (2) feed particle-size distribution, TSS/settleable solids, and relevant total and dissolved chemistry; (3) event and daily volumes, peak flow/pulses, batch size, tank geometry, effective volume, and residence time; (4) influent and outlet samples collected under stated representative conditions, methods, detection limits, QA/QC, and matched workload; (5) which glaze families were included and how variability was handled; (6) actual cleanout/filter-change intervals, labor minutes, clogging, bypass, overflow, leaks, odors, downtime, consumables, power, and water use; (7) captured wet/dry solids quantity, characterization, storage, and documented destination; (8) water reuse/discharge route and the authority or permit conditions; (9) study date, scale, operating history, and independent verification. A clear-water photograph, untethered removal percentage, or uncharacterized residue route does not meet this bar.

## Proposed investigation and pilot design — not executed

### Gate 1: scope and permission

With the supervisor, clarify whether the primary objective is less visible fines, less plumbing blockage, lower rinse-water generation, zero discharge, or another outcome. These goals are not interchangeable. Recover the extra-bucket chronology, cleaning schedule, operator, workload, rinse types, and any overflow notes so the anecdote can guide baseline logging without being called a trial. Map the current drain route and floor drains. Inventory each glaze/clay product and supplier information; do not assume all glaze residues are alike. Record actual bucket/sink geometry, daily/event volumes, space, and acceptable labor. Ask the actual local wastewater control authority whether any proposed discharge or pilot is acceptable. If the receiving route is a U.S. publicly owned treatment works (POTW), federal pretreatment prohibitions and POTW-specific limits or best-management practices may matter [S02, S03], but jurisdiction, receiving route, regulated status, and local permission are unknown. Preserve stream identity and composition history; if U.S. hazardous-waste rules apply, a generator determination is made before mixing and uses process knowledge and/or testing [S04]. That is a reason to characterize first, not a classification of this workshop’s waste. Ask a qualified waste partner/lab about representative sampling and residual characterization. If location/authority is still unknown, keep collected water contained and do not discharge as part of the pilot.

### Gate 2: baseline and causal comparison

Before changing the process, log representative quiet and busy operation and different glaze-use events. Per event, record date/time, type of work, product/recipe class, whether the stream is clay-only, glaze-contact, floor/mop, or mixed/unknown, amount of rinse water, duration/flow pattern, overflow/visible fines, cleaning and maintenance time, and existing bucket configuration. Include any available dates/cleanout/workload records from the temporary extra bucket. The key comparison is workload-matched: compare similar tools, tasks, rinse volumes, and glaze/clay mix before and after a proposed change. If activity is lower during the changed-bucket period, do not treat clearer overflow as causal evidence.

### Gate 3: supervised, prespecified comparison

After characterization and local approval, a qualified partner can compare: (A) baseline practice; (B) source segregation and flow-managed settling on an allowed stream; (C) a filtration/recirculation configuration only on a stream within that product’s intended use; and (D) closed collection/off-site handling without treatment if the owner selects the no-discharge objective. Define the endpoints, comparison schedule, sample locations, methods, operating limits, and stop rules before the pilot. Keep all pilot flows contained until permission and an accepted path are documented. Avoid changing multiple factors at once where possible, and record when changes cannot be separated. The pilot is not a home or unsupervised wet experiment, and it includes no chemical dosing.

### Sampling and operating measurements

Use paired representative inlet/outlet samples for each approved condition across different workload periods and relevant stream classes. For short pulses, coordinate event-based samples or a lab-approved event/flow composite; do not choose a single clear sample. Record volume so concentration changes can be interpreted as mass loading. A qualified lab should select matrix-appropriate TSS/settleable-solids methods and analytes after reviewing product inventory. Turbidity can supplement but not replace solids and chemistry results. Where inventory or local partner indicates metal concern, request separate total-recoverable and operationally dissolved fractions, with the lab managing filtration, preservation, method quality, and detection limits. EPA Method 200.8 is one reference that distinguishes 0.45 µm filtered dissolved from unfiltered total-recoverable fractions; it does not select analytes or acceptance criteria for this workshop. [S05, S06]

Track flow, settling/dwell time, volume retained/reused, dry solids mass or another lab-agreed solids quantity, residual identity, cleaning time, bucket/sludge fill level, filter changes, flow reduction, media breakthrough, visible carryover, resuspension, clogging, bypass, overflow, leaks, odors, downtime, and water refresh frequency. Use representative samples, blanks/duplicates, chain of custody, suitable holding/preservation, and lab QA/QC. Choose sample count and duration with the partner based on event variability and detection needs; the brief provides no data from which to invent a count, duration, threshold, or flow capacity.

### Stop and escalation rules

Set endpoint and action levels with the local authority and qualified partner before operation. Stop and contain flow immediately if there is a leak, overflow, bypass, unexpected glaze entering a clay-only stream, unsafe handling, or a filter/settler failure that defeats collection. Pause the pilot if lab results fail an agreed endpoint, dissolved constituents are outside the chosen treatment concept, or maintenance/solids burden exceeds what the workshop can sustain. Unknown glaze or sludge classification goes to the local waste authority/qualified professional. No discharge follows from a clear appearance or a particulate-removal result. If the partner has not confirmed an acceptable route, continue the owner-approved closed-collection alternative rather than release water.
