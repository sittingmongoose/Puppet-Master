# Independent discovery: recurring rehearsal calendar distribution

Run: A4-02-control · Stage: investigator  
Research boundary: the complete brief at `control/inputs/brief.md`; no exact plan was available or sought during this discovery. Research was limited to public primary documentation, standards, a released open-source issue/fix chain and its released code. No accounts, installs, purchases, member calendars, production systems or live calendar writes were used. Source IDs and version scope are in [`sources/index.md`](sources/index.md) and `source-map.json`.

## Initial recommendation

For a one-season pilot, publish one bounded, read-only HTTPS iCalendar (`.ics`) feed from a source schedule the secretary controls. Give members the subscription URL and steps for their own calendar client. A feed is the best fit for recurring events and later amendments because subscribers can refresh from the same source; it remains a periodic pull, so never promise immediate updates. Offer a separate downloadable one-season `.ics` snapshot only as an optional fallback for people who cannot or do not want to subscribe. It is a one-time copy and will not track changes. Do not encourage importing both copies into the same calendar without testing for duplicates.

This is a planning recommendation, not a claim that a feed or snapshot has been built or tested. Google’s URL route requires a public Google calendar; Outlook.com accepts an online iCal URL. No product source reviewed guarantees identical handling of moved/cancelled recurrence instances or preservation of member-specific reminder edits. Those are acceptance tests, not assumptions. [S02–S05]

## Calendar identity, recurrence and time zones

- Give each season’s rehearsal series a persistent `UID`. The secretary owns it and retains it across corrections and exports; do not mint a replacement UID because a rehearsal moved, a room changed, or the file was regenerated. RFC 5545 defines UID as a persistent globally unique component identifier and describes a recurrence set as one UID plus instance identifiers. Increment `SEQUENCE` for significant revisions if used. [S01]
- Express the normal weekly rehearsal as a bounded `VEVENT` series: a `DTSTART`/`DTEND` in the venue’s local time zone, one weekly `RRULE`, and a finite season boundary. Use the actual IANA time-zone ID (for example, `America/Chicago` only if that is the venue’s zone), include the matching `VTIMEZONE`, and keep the rule’s transitions valid across the season. Do not use floating local times: RFC 5545 says local `DATE-TIME` without `TZID` is floating and can mean different actual times to recipients in different zones. Do not encode a recurring local-clock rehearsal as a fixed UTC time unless the owner says the absolute instant, rather than the venue’s wall-clock time, is the policy. [S01]
- Members who travel should see the venue-fixed instant rendered in the time zone currently used by their client. That consequence follows from a time-zoned event plus each client’s display settings; it is an inference and needs a representative-client check. A `TZID` is a rule set, not a permanent numeric offset. RFC 5545 requires an applicable `VTIMEZONE` for each referenced ID and sufficient rules for every recurrence instance. [S01]
- For a moved single rehearsal, keep the series UID and identify the instance by a `RECURRENCE-ID` equal to its *original scheduled start*; set the exception’s new `DTSTART`, end and location to the amended values. RFC 5545 explicitly says the recurrence identifier stays at the original time even if, for example, Friday is moved to Thursday. Do not use a “this and future” shift for one isolated move. [S01]
- For one cancellation, encode the original instance as an `EXDATE` on the master series, using the same date/time form and `TZID`. The standard defines `EXDATE` as an exclusion from the recurrence set. A client may retain an already-cached item until it fetches/reconciles a changed feed; verify that behavior before relying on it. [S01, S02, S05]
- A season update should preserve UID and original recurrence anchors, change only affected series/exception values, update revision metadata consistently, and republish a complete canonical season. Avoid replacing a single recurring series with regenerated unrelated IDs; imports/subscriptions may then duplicate events or fail to match changes. This duplicate/reconciliation risk is a product inference that must be checked in clients. [S01]

## Routes and useful alternatives

| Route | Observed operation and boundary | Pilot fit / uncertainty |
|---|---|---|
| Google Calendar, public calendar URL feed | On a computer browser, add another calendar → “From URL”. Google says the source calendar must be public; it appears under “Other calendars”. Google documents setup through a computer, not the Android/iOS app. The public iCal address itself works only when that calendar is public. [S02, S03] | Familiar route, but public visibility is a material condition. If the orchestra uses this route, the manager must first decide what room text is public. A public calendar is not a private member-only roster. Help pages do not name a Calendar backend build or refresh interval; no recurrence-exception compatibility claim is established here. |
| Outlook.com, online iCal subscription | Sign in on the web, Add calendar → “Subscribe from web”, paste the URL. Microsoft distinguishes that from Upload from file: the subscribed calendar refreshes, while a file import is a snapshot. The Outlook.com page says refresh is normally about every 3 hours but can take more than 24 hours. [S05] | A clear route for a maintained source feed and a strong warning against promising immediate delivery. Applies to the documented Outlook.com web operation; do not generalize its interval to other Outlook editions or other feed clients. |
| Apple Calendar / iCloud subscription (additional native-client opportunity) | Apple documents a web-address subscription on iOS/iPadOS 26 and later, earlier iOS/iPadOS 18 flows, and Mac. To see the subscribed calendar across devices, use the same Apple Account and choose iCloud for the account/location in the listed flows. [S06] | Useful evidence for desktop/mobile client reach. Apple’s page does not state an update interval, so none is inferred. Having Apple devices is not a reason to require account migration. |
| CalDAV with collection sync (analogous server-sync mechanism) | CalDAV is calendar-aware access to calendar objects in a WebDAV collection. RFC 6578 adds a sync token and a `sync-collection` report that lets a client ask for additions, changes and deletions since its previous token. This differs from repeatedly fetching one static `.ics` URL. [S07, S08] | Technically useful when a pilot needs a server, user accounts, client-side editing or richer synchronization. It requires server provisioning and account configuration beyond a read-only season feed; it is not justified by this brief alone. No CalDAV deployment was tested. |
| Nextcloud 36 Calendar (unfamiliar alternative) | The current Nextcloud 36 manual documents read-only public links and read-only subscriptions from another iCal URL; its documented default subscription refresh is weekly, and an administrator may change it. [S09] | A small organization already operating Nextcloud could avoid introducing a new calendar vendor. The weekly default is a poor fit if members expect timely room changes; it is still periodic feed polling, not an instant update guarantee. The manual’s URL is `/server/latest`, so its alias can move; the page was labeled Nextcloud 36 when read. |

**Keep these terms distinct.** A downloaded `.ics` file imported into an account copies events at that moment; Microsoft says it will not refresh. A subscription points to an online calendar and refreshes periodically, under the recipient’s service/client schedule. Server synchronization such as CalDAV exchanges calendar objects and change state using a calendar-aware server/protocol. None should be described as the same operation. [S04, S05, S07, S08]

Google’s import documentation adds a specific caveat: recurrence imported from CSV may become one-time events. It is not evidence that an iCalendar subscription loses recurrence, so do not overgeneralize it; use `.ics` for the optional calendar snapshot and test the actual output. [S04]

## One-season snapshot option

Keep the brief’s snapshot option. It is authorized but optional, and this research did not establish that a generated snapshot already exists. If the owner chooses it, publish a distinct finite-season `.ics` download from the same schedule source, label the covered season and “one-time import; later changes do not update this copy,” and validate weekly recurrences, the moved instance, and the cancelled instance in supported clients. Outlook.com explicitly treats file import as a snapshot; Google documents `.ics` file import from a computer. [S04, S05]

The snapshot should not set a reminder policy. Avoid feed-level `VALARM` defaults; members own their local reminders. Whether a client preserves a member’s reminder customization when a subscribed event is later changed is not established by the documentation reviewed. Test that exact update path; if a client overwrites it, document the client limitation and do not claim the policy is met there.

## Release/fix history and season-update consequence

A bounded historical recurrence/time-zone example is Python `dateutil`’s `rrulestr` parser. Issue #614, opened 2018-01-18, reported that the parser rejected a `TZID` parameter on `DTSTART`; PR #624 was merged 2018-03-11; the dateutil 2.7.0 release on that date records support for `TZID` on `DTSTART` in `rrulestr`. The tagged 2.7.0 implementation extracts the `TZID`, looks up a time zone, attaches it to a local parsed start, and rejects multiple time zones. This is a version-specific parser fix, **not** evidence about Google, Microsoft or Apple client behavior. The issue’s example ends its start time with `Z` while also using `TZID`; RFC 5545 says `TZID` must not be applied to UTC values, so that example is not a valid production fixture. A proper test must use local date-time with `TZID` and no `Z`. [S10–S13, S01]

Current time-zone data also changes. IANA’s release index reported 2026e (2026-09-29) as the latest release and summarized a Manitoba move to permanent UTC−05 beginning 2026-10-31. That matters only if the venue or a relevant member zone uses the affected rule; the orchestra location is not given. Recheck the actual venue zone and the dates covered by the season, and record the time-zone data version used for any generated `VTIMEZONE`. Google/Apple/Outlook’s embedded time-zone data versions were not disclosed by the reviewed pages. [S14, S15]

**Inference for a season update:** schedule identity and local wall time should remain owned by the secretary; regenerate or review time-zone definitions when legal time rules change; then compare expansion and display around each affected transition in target clients. A feed fetch may be delayed after a source update (especially Outlook.com), so issue a human-readable notice for urgent moved/cancelled rehearsals rather than treating calendar refresh as an alerting channel. The notification recommendation follows from documented refresh delay, but no notification product is proposed or tested. [S05, S14, S15]

## Owner decisions / unresolved questions

1. Secretary confirms the source-of-truth schedule, stable series identity, venue time zone, season start/end and who approves amendments. These are explicit ownership choices, not client defaults.
2. Orchestra manager decides whether room details may be public. Until then, do not put those details in a public calendar URL or public snapshot. Google’s iCal sharing route requires a public calendar; do not imply link secrecy is access control. [S03]
3. Members choose their own calendar client and keep their own reminders. Agree which client/OS versions are in the pilot acceptance set; documentation pages do not expose exact Google/Outlook SaaS builds or prove recurrence/exception compatibility.
4. Decide whether to offer the optional snapshot after reviewing download/import experience and duplicate risk. No account migration or access to members’ calendars is required for this proposal.
5. Set a practical notice policy for same-week changes. Google’s source has no interval, Outlook.com can exceed 24 hours, Apple does not state one, and Nextcloud’s documented default is weekly. No route supports a blanket promise of immediate refresh. [S02, S05, S06, S09]
