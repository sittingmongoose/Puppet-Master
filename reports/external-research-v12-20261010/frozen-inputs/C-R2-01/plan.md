## Project situation

A volunteer collection store uses printed paper labels on inert sleeves. A few batches have curled or detached after months in a room with variable humidity. Staff remember changing both the label stock and printer around the same time. Some labels have handwritten annotations that must remain readable. We want a research proposal before altering the labeling practice across the collection. The setting and anecdote are invented for this case; no specimen or monitoring data are supplied.

## Request

Use a full 60-minute research window to develop a proposal for investigating the curling and attachment problem. Explain plausible mechanisms, useful alternatives, and how manufacturing or implementation history could help separate material behavior from application practice. Recommend a bounded later study and a defensible decision process rather than a collection-wide fix.

## Requirements to preserve

- Treat dimensional stability, attachment reliability, and continued readability as distinct outcomes.
- Compare changing paper or adhesive with at least one useful attachment or environmental-practice alternative, including retaining current materials while improving monitoring if appropriate.
- Consider interactions among humidity cycling, printing, adhesive application, sleeve material, and the simultaneous supply changes; do not assume the adhesive alone is responsible.
- Include implementation history and material specifications as research leads while distinguishing accelerated tests from ordinary storage experience.
- Propose controls, observation duration, failure recording, and a way to preserve annotations; identify proposed validation separately from any executed desk analysis.

- Do not recommend solvents, heat, destructive sampling, or removal of labels from collection objects in this assignment.
- Do not name a stock or adhesive as conservation-safe solely because it is advertised as archival.
