# A repeating brightness pattern in a teaching telescope archive

Case ID: ER12-D-R2-03-FRESH
Slot ID: D-R2-03
Domain: observational astronomy instrumentation

## Project situation

A teaching observatory has a set of nightly images in which several stars appear to show a repeating brightness pattern. The camera was rotated and the image-processing routine was revised during the observing term. Students suspect a variable star, but some comparison stars appear to drift too. A nearby illuminated sports field is mentioned in staff notes. No images, calibration frames, pipeline code, or confirmed variable-object catalog entries are supplied. This is a synthetic research-planning scenario.

## Request

Use the full 60-minute research window to prepare a proposal for investigating instrumental, environmental, processing, and astronomical explanations. Explain mechanisms, compare useful analysis and acquisition alternatives, and investigate calibration or implementation history. Design a later reanalysis or observation pilot that can discriminate explanations. We need no observatory software and no announcement of a new variable object.

## Requirements to preserve

- Distinguish repeatable measured brightness changes, calibrated photometric performance, and evidence for intrinsic source variability.
- Compare calibration or reduction changes with observing or comparison-field strategy and a useful independent-data or no-new-observations alternative.
- Address camera orientation, detector position, exposure and timing, background contamination, atmosphere, and comparison-star behavior without assuming any one cause.
- Investigate processing and instrument history and the operating details of relevant prior implementations, including what evidence makes them transferable.
- Propose controls, reanalysis provenance, independent comparisons, and a decision about further observation; separate planned validation from any desk calculation or search actually performed.

## Boundaries

- Do not claim a discovery, identify a real star as variable, or fabricate a period, light curve, calibration accuracy, or significance.
- Do not operate the telescope, alter the archive, install a reduction pipeline, or schedule live observations.
- Do not treat a visually periodic plot or repeated pipeline output as independent confirmation.

## Still unresolved

The field, filters, camera behavior, timing conventions, exposure history, calibration procedure, reduction revisions, comparison selection, and weather records are unknown. The instructor has not decided whether the first goal is pipeline reliability or astrophysical classification. Preserve these uncertainties in the proposal.
