# Confusion over a bilingual community-course notice

Case ID: ER12-D-R2-04-FRESH
Slot ID: D-R2-04
Domain: language and accessibility research

## Project situation

A community center offers short courses and prints a bilingual enrollment notice. Staff say readers often ask whether materials fees are included and whether a place is reserved after submitting the form. The notice was shortened when the translation was also revised, and distribution moved partly to mobile messages. There are no response logs, participant interviews, or stable older notices available in this packet. This is a fictional community-service setting.

## Request

Use the 60-minute research window to develop a proposal for investigating the communication problem before the center rewrites or deploys anything. Explain plausible comprehension and presentation mechanisms, compare meaningful useful alternatives, and research relevant implementation history. Give us a later study with realistic participants, outcomes, and ethical boundaries, not a website, a finished translation, or a ready-to-publish notice.

## Requirements to preserve

- Separate comprehension of fees, comprehension of reservation status, task completion, and reader confidence.
- Compare wording or information-structure changes with presentation or channel changes and a useful assisted or unchanged-notice alternative.
- Address language proficiency, literacy, mobile viewing, and staff explanation without treating either language group as homogeneous.
- Investigate notice and service-process history and comparable implementations, including how differences in audience and delivery limit transfer.
- Propose a comparison study, accessible recruitment and consent, meaningful task measures, and a revision decision; clearly distinguish proposed participant validation from desk checks actually executed.

## Boundaries

- Do not recruit or contact participants, collect personal data, publish a notice, deploy a form, or contact the community center.
- Do not invent comprehension scores, participant preferences, translation equivalence, or representative audience demographics.
- Do not change fee or reservation policy to make the wording easier, and do not claim accessibility or legal compliance from a readability score.

## Still unresolved

The language pair, policy details, audience distribution, assistive-technology use, reading contexts, current notice, and feasible recruitment budget are not supplied. There is no agreed priority between fewer staff questions and better reader understanding. State what must be established and which provisional comparisons remain useful.
